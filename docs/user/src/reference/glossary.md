# Glossary

**Arm** — Pick the track that records. On the Arrange tab, tap a track's name, or hold **MAIN** and hit the track's pad.

**Arrange** — The tab where clips are recorded, kept as takes and launched, and laid out as a song.

**Audio track** — A track in the track list that records sound from the INPUTS (or from inside mpfree) onto the song's timeline, under the clip lanes.

**BPM** — Beats per minute: the tempo.

**Browser** — MPFree's file manager, opened with BROWSE: projects, samples, kits and the rest of the stick.

**Capture** — Keep what you just played as a clip, even though you weren't recording.

**Chain** — The effects on one mixer strip, in the order the sound goes through them.

**Chance** — How likely a note is to play each time round, from 1 to 100 %.

**Chop** — Cut a sample into slices, one per pad: the Sample tab's **Slice** tool does it.

**Clip** — A recorded or programmed part (a pattern, a bass line) on one track.

**Comping** — Recording a part several times, then playing the best bits of each take, from the take lanes.

**Condition** — A rule for when a note plays, such as every second time round (1:2) or only during a fill (FILL).

**Crossfade** — Blending from one sample into the next where two zones or velocity layers meet, instead of a hard switch.

**Cursor** — In the note editor, the line across the roll that the data wheel moves a snap at a time; **Pads write** puts notes there.

**CV/gate** — Voltages that play a modular synth: CV sets the pitch or moves a value, the gate says when a note is held.

**Edit pad** — The Pads tab's page for a pad's deep settings: its layers, envelope, filter, keygroup, scenes and slices. Tap Edit pad in the pad's settings, or press PROG EDIT on the Pads tab.

**Envelope** — How a sound's volume rises and falls each time it plays: attack, hold, decay, sustain, release.

**Euclidean fill** — Spreading a number of hits as evenly as they go over a number of steps, a quick way to write a groove.

**Faint clips** — Other clips shown faintly behind the one you edit in the piano roll, picked with its **Show** chip, so you can write a part against another.

**Flatten** — Recording a pad as it sounds now, with all its settings, into a new sound.

**Fold** — The note editor's chip that hides rows: show every row, only the rows with notes, or only the clip's scale.

**I/O view** — The mixer's view of where everything goes: audio, MIDI, sync and CV.

**Insert** — An effect a sound goes through on its own mixer strip.

**Instrument** — What a track plays: its kit, one of MPFree's synths, or a plugin synth.

**Keygroup** — A pad that plays one sample across a range of notes, higher and lower, like a keyboard instrument.

**Kit** — A set of drum sounds, one per pad, across the pad banks.

**Latch** — Keep Note Repeat or the arpeggiator playing after you let go of the pads. A sampled pad also has a Latch of its own: a hit starts it and the next hit stops it.

**Layer** — One recording in a loop. The first pass is the first layer; every overdub adds another. A sampled pad's velocity layers are its different sounds for soft and hard hits.

**Lock lane** — A row under a sound on the Sequencer's Steps that sets one of its settings step by step, such as its filter or a send.

**Loop** — A few bars of audio that play round and round, in time, recorded by the looper.

**Macro** — A knob that moves several settings at once. There are eight.

**Master safety** — The limiter at the end of the master that keeps the output from distorting harshly by accident.

**MIDI** — The language keyboards, synths and drum machines use to send notes, knob moves and timing to each other.

**MIDI clock** — Timing pulses one device sends so another plays at the same tempo.

**MIDI Learn** — Teaching MPFree which knob, fader or pedal on a controller moves a value on the screen.

**Mute group** — Pads that cut each other off, like an open and a closed hi-hat.

**Note editor** — The Sequencer tab's **Notes** view: a clip's notes as a piano roll, a list or a tracker, to draw, pick, move and change them.

**Note Repeat** — Hold NOTE REPEAT and a pad, and the pad repeats in time.

**Nudge** — Move a note a little early or late.

**Overdub** — Record on top of what is there, keeping it.

**Pad bank** — One set of 16 sounds on the pads. There are eight: A to H.

**Pad layout** — How the pads play a synth: Chromatic, In key, or Chord pads.

**Pads tab** — The tab where you put sounds on pads and set each pad's level, tuning, effects and what it plays.

**Pads write** — The note editor's switch that makes each pad you press write its note at the cursor, one step at a time.

**Parameter lock** — A setting changed for one step only, such as a filter that opens on one hit.

**Patch** — A way of setting up your keyboard for a song: up to eight zones, each playing one of the song's instruments on its own keys and velocities, so you can split and layer. A song keeps its patches, and you switch them while you play. See [Patches](../making-music/patches.md).

**Pattern** — A clip shown as steps: the rows on the Play page, and the Sequencer's Steps.

**Perform set** — Eight pages of action pads (loop keys, throws, mutes, scenes, macros) that can take over the pads, set up on the Pads tab.

**Piano roll** — A view of notes as bars against a keyboard: higher notes higher up, time left to right. MPFree's is the note editor on the Sequencer tab.

**Pick** — Choose notes in the note editor (a tap, a box, the Select menu) so the Q-Links, a drag, **Delete** or a tool act on them. The chosen notes are the **picked** notes.

**Pin** — A hit in a warped sound fixed to a beat with the Warp tool; the sound between pins stretches to fit.

**Plugin** — An effect or synth made by someone else, in the CLAP format or as an MPC plugin, that MPFree can load from its stick.

**Prefs** — MPFree's settings, opened with SHIFT + MENU.

**Project** — One song, saved with everything it needs: clips, kits, sounds, the mixer, loops and audio tracks.

**Q-Link** — One of the four knobs beside the screen. What they control depends on the page.

**Ratchet** — A step that plays its note several times in quick succession.

**Region** — A block of recorded audio on an audio track.

**Resample** — Record MPFree's own sound (the whole mix, a track or a pad) back onto a pad or into a loop.

**Scene** — A row of the clip grid, launched all at once. The Sample tab also has two scenes of the whole kit to morph between.

**Scene card** — The card for one row of the clip grid: its scene's name, what plays after it, its tempo, mutes and program changes, and Capture, Copy row, Insert row and Delete row. Hold the row's number to open it.

**Send** — How much of a sound goes to one of the four shared effects (send returns A to D).

**Sequencer** — The tab after Play: the armed track's clip as steps (**Steps**) or as notes in the note editor (**Notes**). **STEP SEQ** opens it.

**Set** — A list of songs (your projects) to play in order at a gig or a rehearsal, made in BROWSE's **Sets** place and played on the Show view. See [Set lists](../making-music/set-lists.md).

**Show view** — The Arrange tab's view for playing a set: the song playing, what comes next and whether it's ready, moved on with a footswitch, the wheel or a tap.

**Skip back** — Keeping what had just come in without having pressed Record: Sample Recall, with **From** set to the recorder's Source.

**Snap** — The grid that notes you draw or move land on in the note editor. **Auto** follows how far you have zoomed in.

**Snap (loop)** — When you close a loop a little off the downbeat you played, the chip that moves the loop's end onto that downbeat. **Use my tap** puts it back where you tapped. See [Recording loops](../making-music/looping.md).

**Song** — Clips laid out on a timeline on the Arrange tab, one lane per track, with the audio tracks under them.

**Song list** — The song as a list of parts, each playing a scene so many times ("Intro × 2, Verse × 4"), played in order on the bar. See [The song list](../making-music/arranging.md#the-song-list).

**Status chip** — The CPU and RAM numbers at the top right of every page. They turn yellow or red when something needs you; tap them for System status.

**Step inspector** — The panel on the Sequencer's Steps that shows and sets the picked step's velocity, length, chance, condition, ratchet and nudge.

**Submix** — A mixer strip that groups several sounds, so one fader and one set of effects serve them all.

**Swing** — Delaying every other step a little, for a shuffled feel.

**System status** — The page that says what went wrong, what it means for your music and what to try. See [When something goes wrong](when-something-goes-wrong.md).

**Take** — One recording. UNDO takes back the newest; SHIFT + UNDO brings it back.

**Theme** — MPFree's colours and the style of its panels, picked in **Prefs > Appearance**, or made from a short text file. See [Making your own theme](making-a-theme.md).

**Throw** — A short performance effect that plays while you hold it.

**Track** — A lane of clips that plays one clip at a time, with its own sound.

**Tracker** — A view of a clip as a list of steps from top to bottom, every step's settings in a row.

**Tunings page** — The page with the song's tuning and every instrument's: what each synth and kit plays in, the song's or its own. Open it from the help of the Synth tab's Tuning row.

**Tuning file** — A Scala file (`.scl`, with an optional `.kbm`) that tunes the synths and the kits' keygroups to a scale of your own.

**Velocity** — How hard a pad is hit, from 1 (softest) to 127 (hardest).

**View** — One of a page's layouts: the Sequencer tab's Steps and Notes, or the Arrange tab's Clips grid, Song view and song list.

**Warp** — Playing a sample in time with the project's tempo: Stretch keeps its pitch, Repitch changes it like a record.

**XY layout** — What the XY pad moves: one of the ten ready-made layouts, or one of yours with up to eight settings across and eight up. See [Your own XY layouts](../making-music/xy-layouts.md).

**Zone** — One part of a patch: a range of keys and velocities that plays one instrument. Two zones over the same keys layer.

**16 Levels** — One sound spread over all 16 pads, each a step of velocity, tune, cutoff, decay or start.
