## Goal

- **The SD card:** MPFree reads samples, kits and projects from the card in the MPC's SD slot, and saves its own files there. The same card keeps working in Akai's system and in a computer.
- **The internal drive (the eMMC):** MPFree can read the musician's files on it, **read-only**, when asked: Akai's Internal drive (partition 8) and Akai's factory content (partition 7). Nothing on the eMMC is ever written.
- **Off unless asked for:** both stay switched off at every start, as today. MPFree switches one on when the musician opens it, and off again afterwards and at shutdown.

## Where we are today

- **Every MPFree start switches both off before anything touches them.** On the soft-reboot route the stick unbinds the eMMC controller (`ff0f0000`) and the SD controller (`ff0c0000`) first and powers the MPC off if it can't ([how](soft-reboot-boot.md)). On the chain the stick's device tree disables both controllers, so the second kernel never sees them ([step 7](stick-autoboot.md#the-chain-to-the-sticks-own-device-tree-step-7-opt-in)).
- **One read-only window exists already: the first-start backup** on a creator stick (`tools/mpc-one/stick/setup-backup.sh`, [how](soft-reboot-boot.md)). It binds the eMMC controller alone, marks every eMMC disk read-only as it appears (a udev rule in `/run`, the kernel's `force_ro`), checks the partition table and the block layer's write and discard counters, copies the eMMC to the stick, checks the counters did not move, and unbinds the controller again. The SD controller stays off. It runs on Akai's kernel (the soft-reboot route), where the controllers are in the device tree; on a `--chain` stick's second kernel they are not, so the backup can't run there.
- **MPFree already reads ext4 without the kernel:** the USB creator's ext4 reader (`rust/mpfree-diskimage`) copies Akai's root filesystem out of the update file.
- **The plan for our own kernel said "no MMC driver at all".** That is corrected below: our kernel has the drivers, and nothing starts them unless MPFree asks.

What we know about Akai's side, from earlier rounds:

- Akai's disk service (`edisksd`) mounts **every partition it can read** on a removable disk, **read-write**, at `/media/<label or UUID>` (`nosuid,nodev,noexec,nosymfollow`); it did that to all of a stick's partitions, ext4 included ([record](stick-autoboot.md)).
- Akai's `no-reserved-blocks@.service` runs `tune2fs` on **every ext4 partition** as it appears, which writes that filesystem's superblock. It started for the SD card on 2026-11-15 ([record](stick-autoboot.md#second-power-on-2026-11-15-zip-092220z-mpfree-started)).
- The owner's card is a 64 GB card with one exFAT partition.
- Step S0 answered the rest on 2026-11-23, below. Still not known: what Akai's system does with a partition it cannot read (the owner's card is readable).

### What S0 found (2026-11-23)

The owner ran [block S0.1](#step-s0-block-for-the-owner) on Akai's 3.10.1 (kernel `6.18.44-az01`) with their card in the slot. The report stays in the project, since it lists the card's top folder.

- **Filesystems:** `vfat`, `msdos`, `exfat`, `ntfs3`, `ext2`/`ext3`/`ext4`, `hfsplus` and FUSE are built into Akai's kernel (`=y`, not modules), with code page 437, ISO 8859-1 and UTF-8 for names. The stick preloads nothing for FAT or exFAT. FAT's names default to ISO 8859-1 there (`CONFIG_FAT_DEFAULT_IOCHARSET`), which no name outside ASCII survives, so S1's window mounts FAT with `utf8`, and exFAT with `iocharset=utf8` (its default already, and how Akai mounts it).
- **The owner's card:** SDXC, 64 GB (59.5 GiB), an MBR with one partition from sector 32768, exFAT 1.0: the window reads it as `mmcblk1p1`.
- **How Akai mounts it:** `edisksd` mounts it at `/media/<UUID>` about 9 s after power-on, **read-write**: `rw,nosuid,nodev,noexec,relatime,nosymfollow,fmask=0022,dmask=0022,iocharset=utf8,errors=remount-ro`. The udev rule `65-sdcard.rules` also wants `sdcard.mount`, which systemd skips: its condition, the device tree's `inmusic,internal-sd-fitted`, isn't met on the MPC One. `no-reserved-blocks@` starts for the card too and leaves exFAT alone.
- **Akai's disk tools:** `fsck.exfat`, `mkfs.exfat`, `blkid`, `e2fsck` and `tune2fs` are there; `fsck.vfat`, `fsck.fat`, `dosfsck` and `mkfs.vfat` are not.
- **The slot:** `rockchip,rk3288-dw-mshc` at `ff0c0000`, with card detect (`cd-gpios`) and the card's lock switch wired (`wp-gpios`, not inverted, `disable-wp` absent), so MPFree can read the switch. The device tree offers no high-speed or UHS mode (`cap-sd-highspeed` and every `sd-uhs-*` absent), so the card runs at 25 MHz, 4 bits, legacy timing, 3.3 V: at most 12.5 MB/s on the bus. The card answered at 1.2 s into the boot.
- **Not safely removed:** Akai's kernel said "Volume was not properly unmounted. Some data may be corrupt. Please run fsck." as it mounted the owner's card, before anything of MPFree's ran that boot. Both Linux drivers keep a mark they find at mount (Linux 6.18: FAT's `fat_set_state` changes nothing while `sbi->dirty`, exFAT keeps it in `vol_flags_persistent`), so a clean unmount never clears it; only a check that repairs it on a computer does. So it may be from any cut while a system had the card mounted and had written to it, since the card was last repaired. S1 opens the card read-only and says so; S2 won't write to it until it is checked.
- **The stick's route and the mark:** on the stick's route the soft-reboot starts at about 6.6 s, before `edisksd` mounts the card (9.0 s in this boot), and the 15 Nov record shows only the content, data and root partitions unmounting at the switch ([record](stick-autoboot.md#second-power-on-2026-11-15-zip-092220z-mpfree-started)). So Akai's system most likely never has the card mounted when MPFree starts, and the mark most likely comes from Akai's own use: a cut while it had the card mounted read-write. Not proven: the startup switch-off waits for ext4 and f2fs on the eMMC or the card to be let go, not for FAT or exFAT, which keep no entry in `/sys/fs`. To settle it once the card has been repaired on a computer: start the stick, then Akai's system without it, and see whether the warning is back.

## How the SD card is shared with Akai's system

**The owner chose A on 2026-11-17 (00:24Z).** The three ways that were offered:

| | **A. A folder on the card as it is** (recommended) | B. A partition of MPFree's own | C. A second card just for MPFree |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The card | Unchanged: its one FAT32 or exFAT partition, as Akai or a computer made it | Akai's partition made smaller, plus an ext4 partition for MPFree | A card MPFree formats (ext4) |
| What MPFree writes | Only inside one top folder, `MPFree/` | Only its own partition | The whole card |
| Akai's system | Sees MPFree's folder; its samples are ordinary WAV files Akai can load | Mounts MPFree's partition too, read-write, and runs `tune2fs` on it | Mounts it if it is in while Akai runs |
| A computer | Reads everything | Can't read the ext4 partition (Windows, macOS) | Can't read it (Windows, macOS) |
| A power cut while saving | FAT and exFAT keep no journal, so a cut can damage the filesystem, the same risk Akai's own saves carry. MPFree lowers it: whole files written then renamed, synced, the card checked before writing | ext4's journal protects it | ext4's journal protects it |
| Getting there | Nothing to do | Repartitioning a card that has data on it means copying everything off and back (there is no safe exFAT shrink); never done without a typed confirmation | Formatting a card; never done without a typed confirmation |

A keeps one card that works everywhere, with nothing to repartition. B or C can be added later for musicians who want a journal.

## How MPFree switches them on and off

| | On Akai's kernel (soft-reboot route; the chain until our kernel) | On our own kernel (chain) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At every start | Both controllers unbound first, as today | The MMC drivers are modules that nothing loads at start; both controllers are held (`driver_override`) so that loading the driver binds neither. Nothing probes the eMMC or the card at all |
| SD card on | Bind the SD controller alone (`drivers_probe`), as the backup does for the eMMC | Release the SD controller's hold, load the driver, bind it alone |
| Internal drive on (read-only) | The backup's window: a udev rule in `/run` marks every eMMC disk read-only as it appears, the kernel's `force_ro` is checked on every disk and partition, the write and discard counters are recorded | The same, and our kernel makes every eMMC disk read-only when it is created, by a small patch, and refuses to make it writable again, so no window exists at all |
| Off | Unmount, unbind, check the block devices are gone; for the eMMC, check the counters did not move. If a controller won't let go, the MPC powers off, as the backup does | The same, then the hold goes back on |

The kernel needs: the MMC drivers (as modules on our kernel), `vfat` and `exfat` (both in mainline; Akai's has both built in, with `ntfs3`, `ext4` and `hfsplus`, [S0](#what-s0-found-2026-11-23)). Our kernel's card set-up must match Akai's (bus speeds, cache): step 1's driver surveys show Akai's MMC settings, and ours start from those (S0: 25 MHz, 4 bits, legacy timing).

### The internal drive is read with MPFree's own ext4 reader, never mounted

Mounting Akai's ext4 even read-only can write to it: the kernel replays an unfinished journal at mount time. So MPFree never mounts the eMMC. It opens the partition read-only and reads files with the ext4 reader the creator already uses, the way the workstation tools read the eMMC backup with `debugfs`. The kernel's read-only flag is underneath as a second guard.

- **What it reads:** partition 8 (Akai's Internal drive: projects, samples, kits) and partition 7 (factory content). Partition 8 also holds Akai's writable `/etc` and `/var` layers (`/data/system`); MPFree doesn't list those.
- **Factory content** stays on the MPC: MPFree loads it, never copies it to the stick or anywhere else ([factory content policy](../project/factory-content-policy.md)).
- **If Akai's last shutdown was cut off** (journal not clean), MPFree says the drive may show older files, and still only reads.

### Rules for writing to the SD card (layout A)

- MPFree writes only inside `MPFree/` at the top of the card. Anything else on the card it only reads.
- Before the first write after the card goes in, a read-only check (`fsck.exfat -n` or `fsck.vfat -n`). If the card isn't clean, MPFree uses it read-only and says to check it on a computer. MPFree never repairs or formats a card on its own: only the SD card round asks the write window for a repair (on the owner's go, [S2a](#the-write-window)).
- Each file is written whole to `NAME.part`, synced, then renamed, as on the stick's data partition ([storage spec](../design/storage-spec.md)).
- Mounted `nosuid,nodev,noexec`, as Akai mounts it. The power card's Shut down and Restart unmount the card first. A card taken out while mounted is noted, and MPFree says to put it back or check it.

## Step S1: the read-only window

[`setup-sd-card.sh`](../../../tools/mpc-one/stick/setup-sd-card.sh) follows the backup's eMMC window ([`setup-backup.sh`](../../../tools/mpc-one/stick/setup-backup.sh)): root runs it when the pad player asks (`/run/mpfree-app/sd-card.request`), and it keeps the card open until the pad player asks to close it (`sd-card.close`). It never writes to the card.

1. **Checks first:** the startup switch-off finished, no `mmcblk` device is there, neither the SD nor the eMMC controller has a driver, the first-start backup isn't running and the internal drive isn't open for Akai's kits, and nothing is mounted at `/run/mpfree-sd`. So the eMMC's window and the card's never overlap; if both were asked for at the same moment, each would see the other's disk and close (both mark every `mmcblk` disk read-only).
2. **The window:** the same udev rule in `/run` that marks each `mmcblk` disk read-only as it appears, then only the SD controller (`ff0c0000`) is bound. Every `mmcblk` device must be under that controller's folder in sysfs, and none may be a boot or RPMB area, which only an eMMC has: anything else closes the window at once, and an eMMC disk that stays turns the MPC off. The card is identified by where it sits, never by its name (`mmcblk1` with the eMMC beside it, perhaps `mmcblk0` alone). The disk and each partition must read `ro = 1`, `force_ro = 1`. An empty slot closes the window and says so.
3. **The filesystem:** one partition, or none (a card formatted without a table). Its first sector, read once with `dd` from a disk the kernel already holds read-only into a file in `/run`, must say exFAT (offset 3) or FAT32 (82) or FAT12/16 (54); NTFS and anything else are named and left alone. The same copy says whether the card was **safely removed** last time: Linux marks a FAT filesystem as in use when it mounts it for writing (bit 0 of byte 65 on FAT32, of byte 37 on FAT12/16) and an exFAT one at its first write (bit 1 of VolumeFlags, byte 106; bit 2 there means a disk error was seen), and clears the mark at the unmount, so a mark still set means a cut or a card pulled out while mounted. A mark already there at the mount stays, whatever the unmount (FAT's `sbi->dirty`, exFAT's `vol_flags_persistent`), until a check on a computer repairs it, so it may be old: the owner's card has one ([S0](#what-s0-found-2026-11-23)). A read-only mount leaves the mark as it is (Linux 6.18, Akai's kernel: `fat_set_state` in `fs/fat/inode.c` returns at once on a read-only mount, and `exfat_set_vol_flags` in `fs/exfat/super.c` changes nothing on disk). The card still opens, read-only, with `dirty=yes` in its status and a line saying to check it on a computer. Other systems may keep their own mark elsewhere (Windows also uses the FAT's second entry), which this doesn't read. The kernel must already have that filesystem in `/proc/filesystems`: after the soft-reboot no module can load. Akai's kernel has `vfat` and `exfat` built in ([S0](#what-s0-found-2026-11-23)), so the stick preloads nothing for them.
4. **Mounted read-only** at `/run/mpfree-sd`: `ro,nosuid,nodev,noexec,noatime`, files root's and readable by the pad player's group (`gid=960,fmask=0137,dmask=0027`), names as UTF-8 (`utf8` on FAT, `iocharset=utf8` on exFAT: Akai's kernel turns FAT's names into ISO 8859-1 otherwise).
5. **While open,** once a second: the card's write and discard counters (stat fields 5-7 and 12-14, disk and partitions) must not move and the disk must stay read-only; the card taken out closes the window. The pad player moves a counter in `/run/mpfree-app/sd-card.alive` while it uses the card, as it does for the content window; once the counter stands still for 120 s (the pad player stopped), the window closes as if asked.
6. **Closing:** the counters again, and the kernel's warnings. On a 6.x kernel the block layer lets a write to a read-only disk through and only warns, once per disk (`Trying to write to read-only block-device mmcblk1p1`). Nothing can open a read-only disk for writing or mount it read-write, so a write would have to come from inside the kernel, but the window counts those warnings too, and one that names an `mmcblk` disk fails it. Then it unmounts (after 10 s of the pad player still holding a file, lazily: the card is read-only), unbinds the controller, waits for every `mmcblk` device to go, and removes the rule, stale `/dev/disk` links and the mount point. A controller that won't let go turns the MPC off, as the backup's does.

Its state is in `/run/mpfree-setup/sd-card-status` (`checking`, `opening`, `open` with the card, filesystem, size, mount point and whether it was left marked as in use, `closing`, `closed`, `none`, `failed` with why). Every status gives back, as `ask`, the hex word the pad player wrote in its request (at most 32 hex digits, nothing else from the file), so the pad player knows an answer is to its own request and not an earlier opening's. Its notes are in `sd-card-log.txt` there and the kernel log. A stop from the system closes it cleanly.

**Tests:** [`test_sd_card_window.py`](../../../tools/mpc-one/tests/test_sd_card_window.py) runs it under dash with only its `NEEDS` programs, against a fake kernel whose sysfs answers the bind and unbind as the controller and udev do (the `/sys/block` entry a link into the controller's folder, `ro` following `force_ro`). It covers FAT32, FAT16 on a card without a table and exFAT; each with its in-use mark set and clear (and exFAT's disk-error bit, and other bits that don't count); an empty slot; NTFS, ext4, two partitions and a kernel without exFAT, each left unmounted; each check before the window; a boot area under the SD controller, an eMMC disk (the MPC turns off), a card that stays writable, udev missing the rule, a failed mount; writes while open, a kernel warning (and that another disk's, or one from before, doesn't count), the card taken out, a stop from the system, the pad player slow to let go, the pad player's counter standing still (and one left from an earlier opening), and a controller that won't let go. Static checks: the one `dd` read, the one read-only `mount`, no other disk tool, and sysfs written only for `force_ro`, the bind and the unbind.

### On the stick

### The pad player's side

[`mpfree-app/src/sd_card.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/sd_card.rs) is the pad player's half, used by the Browser's "SD card" place ([below](#the-browsers-sd-card-place)).

- `SdCard::open` asks with a new word of 32 hex digits in `sd-card.request` (written whole under a hidden name and renamed in) and waits up to 60 s for a status that gives that word back: `open` gives the `Card` (its filesystem, size, mount point, and `dirty` when it wasn't safely removed last time), `none` and `failed` say why (`NotOpened`). A request nobody picked up in time is taken back, so the card never opens later unasked. A stick without the service says it needs making again.
- While the card is open, a thread of its own writes the counter to `sd-card.alive` every 2 s (never through a link: a counter that can't be written lets the window close by itself after 120 s).
- `SdCard::close` asks with `sd-card.close` and waits up to 60 s for the service's last word; dropping an `SdCard` asks without waiting.
- A pad player started again while its earlier window is still open (the status says `open` and its own mounts list the card) takes that window over, counter and all, rather than asking again: a request made while a window is open would open the card again once it closed.

- `Slot` is what the Browser's place uses: `Slot::open` asks for the card on a thread of its own (a close still running finishes first), and `Slot::tick`, once a frame, gives what happened as `Heard` (`Open` with the `Card`, `NotOpened` with why, `Closed`, and lines for the log). While the card is open it looks every 2 s whether the window is still open (the card taken out, or the system closed it: the service's last word is the reason), and once nothing has used the card for 30 s (`PUT_AWAY`: the browser hidden and its worker idle) it closes it on a thread. A storm test (`the_place_keeps_to_one_window_through_a_storm_of_taps`) taps, hides and shows at random against a stand-in window that answers at random and has cards taken out: every window it opens is the slot's, heard once and closed, none is left for the quiet close, and what the slot says always matches what it holds.

Tests (`sd_card::tests`) run it against a stand-in service thread: open, the counter moving, close; an earlier opening's status (an empty slot, or open without its mount) not taken for this one's; an empty slot and a card it doesn't read; a request nobody answers, taken back; a stick without the service; a window taken over; a drop that closes; the counter and the close never written through a link; the slot opening on its thread, staying open while used and put away once not, hearing the card taken out (and asking nothing of a window that is over), an empty slot and a stick without the service.

Its writes are all in its own `/run/mpfree-app`, and both review checks name them line by line: `test_app_mpc.py`'s write check, and CI's static no-write step (`mpc-inventory.yml`), which allows the app's file deletes only in the Plugins store and in `sd_card.rs`'s two (a request taken back, and its own half-written file). ### The write window

1. **The read-only window's checks and opening** (S1's steps 1 and 2): the eMMC's controller stays unbound and every `mmcblk` disk must be under the SD controller and read-only.
2. **Refused** (exit 3, the card never written) unless the card is one exFAT filesystem (one partition, or none); its in-use mark (byte 106, bit 1) and disk-error bit (bit 2) are clear; Akai's `fsck.exfat` is there (S0 found it; it is checked at the window's start, not in `NEEDS`, so the creator still makes a stick without it) and `fsck.exfat -n` on the disk the kernel holds read-only exits 0; and the card's lock switch is off (`ro` must follow `force_ro 0`). FAT cards wait for S2b's own read of the FAT.
   - **The repair, only when asked for** (the request's second line `repair`, which only `mpfree-disktest --sd-card --repair-card` writes, for the round; the pad player never asks): a card that is marked, has the disk-error bit or fails `fsck.exfat -n` is not refused but repaired in the MPC. `force_ro 0` for the card's disk alone (a locked card is refused, untouched), `fsck.exfat -y` (it mends what it found and clears the mark), `force_ro 1`, the first sector read again (both bits must be clear) and `fsck.exfat -n` again (clean); only then the opening below. Anything else is refused with what the repair left, and the card stays read-only. A card that checks clean is never repaired. Akai's system mounts the card read-write when it starts, and the stick's soft reboot doesn't unmount it first, so the card is marked whenever Akai's system wrote to it (Linux marks exFAT at the first write, not at the mount): it was at the first round and wasn't at the second. The repair is what lets a marked card stay in the MPC instead of going to a computer. **On the MPC (2026-11-24):** Akai's `fsck.exfat` is exfatprogs 1.2.2. On the owner's marked card, `-n` answered clean (it doesn't look at the mark, which is why the window reads the mark itself), `-y` exited 0 and cleared the mark, and `-n` stayed clean (4,490 files). If a repair doesn't leave the card clean, the round's write steps are refused and the rest runs.
3. **Mounted for writing, `MPFree/` alone writable:** `force_ro 0` for the card's disk only, mounted `rw,nosuid,nodev,noexec,noatime,dirsync` (files root's, the pad player's group may write: `uid=0,gid=960,fmask=0117,dmask=0007`, `iocharset=utf8`, `errors=remount-ro`) at a private `/run/mpfree-sd-write` (0700). `MPFree/` is made if it isn't there. Then, in this order: that mount is made read-only (`remount,bind,ro`), the whole card is bound read-only at `/run/mpfree-sd` (`mount --bind`, then `remount,bind,ro`), the private mount goes, and only then `/run/mpfree-sd/MPFree` is bound onto itself and made writable (`remount,bind,rw`). So the kernel itself refuses a write anywhere else on the card. The order matters because systemd makes `/run` a shared mount: each mount there is copied to the other mount namespaces (a service's own), and a bind of a shared mount is a peer of it. Bound while the private mount was still there, `MPFree/` was copied inside the private mount too, which then couldn't be unmounted ("target is busy", the first round on the MPC); and a copy takes its mount's options as they are at that moment (a later remount changes one mount alone), so the private mount is read-only before anything is bound from it. Nothing is made private, so every unmount reaches the copies.
4. **Closing:** `sync`, both binds unmounted, never lazily (a writable mount must really go). A program still holding a file gets 10 s, then TERM and KILL; a mount that still won't go turns the MPC off cleanly, which unmounts everything. Then `force_ro 1`, the first sector read again (the mark must be clear after a clean unmount), `fsck.exfat -n` again, the MiB written (the disk's sector counter before and after), the controller unbound. A problem gives `failed` with what it was ("check it on a computer"); clean gives "Closed cleanly: N MiB written; the card checks clean." The card taken out while open is said too.

## Step S2: saving to the card (design, not built)

What the kernel does when MPFree writes to a FAT or exFAT card, from Linux 6.18's own code (Akai's kernel is `6.18.44-az01`), and the rules that follow. S2a above builds rules 1 to 3 for exFAT as a window the round's test uses; the app's own saves (S2b, rule 4) are not built. S2 changes the [storage spec](../design/storage-spec.md)'s rule zero ("the SD card is never written"), so it is merged only on the owner's go.

**What the kernel does** (`fs/fat/`, `fs/exfat/`, v6.18):

- **The in-use mark.** FAT is marked in use as soon as it is mounted for writing, and exFAT at its first write; both clear it at a clean unmount (`fat_set_state`, `exfat_set_volume_dirty`), unless it was already set at the mount: then both keep it until a repair ([S0](#what-s0-found-2026-11-23)). exFAT writes its mark with a cache flush and FUA. A cut while mounted leaves the mark, and the next mount warns "Volume was not properly unmounted. Some data may be corrupt. Please run fsck." S1 already reads the mark ([above](#step-s1-the-read-only-window)).
- **fsync reaches the card.** Both drivers' `fsync` write the file and the FAT (or the whole disk's buffers for exFAT) and then flush the card's cache (`blkdev_issue_flush`).
- **A rename to a new name isn't atomic on FAT.** `vfat_rename` adds the new name's entry, points it at the file's clusters, then removes the old entry. A cut between the two leaves two names on one file's clusters (cross-linked), and deleting either later frees clusters the other still uses: real damage, beyond a lost file. exFAT renames in place when the new name needs no more directory entries than the old (`NAME.part` to `NAME` usually does: up to 15 characters per entry), so the change is one entry set, mostly one sector; a longer name takes the same two steps as FAT.
- **Mount options that help:** `dirsync` (directory changes written as they happen, both drivers), `flush` (FAT only: writes data sooner, so less is cached when a card is pulled), `errors=remount-ro` (both drivers' default: an error found while writing stops the writing).

**The rules, from that:**

1. **Opened for writing only when it is safe to.** The card's in-use mark must be clear, and a read-only check must pass first (S0 found `fsck.exfat` on Akai's system and no `fsck.vfat`: so `fsck.exfat -n` on exFAT, on the disk the kernel still holds read-only, if its version takes `-n` (unverified), and MPFree's own read of the FAT and the folder's entries on FAT). Otherwise the card opens read-only and says to check it on a computer. MPFree never repairs a card on its own; the SD card round asks S2a's window for a repair in the MPC (the owner's card is marked whenever Akai's system has had it).
2. **A second window, the same checks.** The read-only window's checks, then `force_ro` is switched off for the card's disk alone, after it is found under the SD controller (the eMMC controller still has no driver), and it is mounted `rw,nosuid,nodev,noexec,noatime,dirsync` (plus `flush` on FAT). The eMMC's controller stays unbound throughout, as in S1.
3. **Only inside `MPFree/`,** as layout A says, each file whole: written to `NAME.part`, `fsync`, renamed, the folder synced. On FAT the rename's moment is the risk above: the in-use mark is set the whole time the card is open for writing, so a cut there is always seen at the next opening, which is read-only until the card is checked. Whether a hidden-attribute publish (write under the final name with the hidden attribute, then clear it: one entry rewritten in place, `FAT_IOCTL_SET_ATTRIBUTES`) is safer on a real card is measured first (below).
4. **Open for writing only while saving.** The pad player opens the write window for a save and closes it straight after, so the card spends seconds marked in use, not the session. A clean close clears the mark; the power card's Shut down and Restart close it first.

**Tested before the MPC:** the [pull test on FAT](../design/disk-streaming.md#wp-on-fat-the-pull-test-for-saving-to-the-sd-card) (`mpfree-disktest --cut-test --fat`, or `--fat-hidden` for the hidden way, built) on a spare FAT32 and a spare exFAT stick on a computer. Each run writes a stream of whole files and is pulled mid-write. MPFree's check then reads the stick without writing, and the computer's own `fsck -n` checks it too. The runs show how often a pull leaves a cross-link or a lost file with each way of publishing. Then on the MPC with the owner's card, with S1's checks around it and the boot-area hashes unchanged.

**What S0 settled for S2** ([above](#what-s0-found-2026-11-23)): Akai's system has `fsck.exfat` and no `fsck.vfat`. It mounts the card read-write with the same exFAT driver (`errors=remount-ro`), so its own cuts leave the same mark, and the owner's card has one.

## Step S3: the internal drive, read only (design, not built)

The MPC's own **Internal** drive (partition 8, Akai's `/data`, also mounted by Akai's system at `/media/az01-internal`) as a place in the Browser: the musician's projects, samples and kits from Akai's side, read only. Partition 7, the factory content, already reads this way for Akai's kits without a backup (setup-backup.sh's step 3c and [`mpfree-factory`](../../../rust/mpfree-factory/src/import.rs)); S3 does the same for partition 8, for as long as the browser uses it.

**Never mounted.** Mounting ext4, even read-only, replays an unfinished journal ([above](#the-internal-drive-is-read-with-mpfrees-own-ext4-reader-never-mounted)). The pad player opens the partition's device read-only and reads it with [`mpfree_diskimage::ext4`](../../../rust/mpfree-diskimage/src/ext4.rs), which never writes, never replays a journal and bounds every count it reads.

**The window,** a third mode beside the backup's copy and its step 3c, with S1's protocol rather than 3c's (3c ends with a "done" file and an hour's limit; a place stays open while the browser uses it):

- `/run/mpfree-app/internal-drive.request` with a word; every status gives it back as `ask=`, as S1's do.
- The eMMC controller is bound alone, under the backup's udev rule (every `mmcblk` disk read-only as it appears) and its checks: the startup switch-off finished, no other window open (the SD card's included: both windows mark every `mmcblk` disk read-only, and each sees the other's disk and closes), `ro = 1` and `force_ro = 1` on the disk, its partitions and boot areas, the counters recorded.
- Only partition 8's node becomes root's and the pad player's group's, 0440, in `/dev` (RAM), as 3c does for partition 7. Nothing is mounted. The status names the partition and the journal's state (below).
- While open, once a second: the write and discard counters must not move and every disk must stay read-only, or the window fails and closes. The pad player moves `internal-drive.alive` every 2 s; 120 s still, and the window closes as if asked. `internal-drive.close` closes it.
- Closing is the backup's: counters, the node's owner and mode back, unbind, wait for every `mmcblk` device to go, remove the rule. A controller that won't let go turns the MPC off.

Probably one script, `setup-internal-drive.sh`, sharing the backup's window functions, with the same kind of fake-kernel tests as `test_sd_card_window.py`. The creator puts it on the stick with a `.path` and `.service` pair like S1's.

**Akai's journal.** Akai mounts partition 8 read-write. After a clean shutdown its journal is empty. After a cut, ext4 marks it `needs_recovery` (feature_incompat bit `0x4`, which the reader accepts and reports as `Ext4::needs_recovery()`), and the newest changes may be only in the journal. MPFree reads the filesystem as it stands, without them. So when the bit is set, the window's status says `journal=unclean`, the place opens anyway, and the line under the list says "The MPC wasn't shut down cleanly last time: the newest files may be missing here. Start Akai's MPC software once to tidy it." A file whose blocks don't read as a sound, a kit or a project fails to load and says so. Nothing is ever written.

**A damaged drive.** The reader bounds every count it reads, so a damaged filesystem gives an error, not a panic or a hang. Its host tests hold it to that: `a_damaged_image_gives_an_error_not_a_panic_or_a_hang` changes a small image's superblock, group descriptors, used inodes and folder blocks at random, 3000 images a run, and reads each as the place would (270,000 such images with other seeds, in debug and release, gave no panic and no hang; the last 60,000 read every file as a stream too); `a_folder_linked_back_to_the_root_ends_the_walk_with_an_error` shows a folder linked back to the root twice ends a walk at 64 folders deep, at once. The reader also refuses a superblock whose last block's place in bytes wouldn't fit in 64 bits, so no offset it works out can overflow, however a damaged inode points.

One limit is the caller's: a damaged inode can claim a size its blocks don't hold, and the reader reads what isn't held as zeros. It must, because a file with holes is no sign of damage: `mke2fs -d` itself stores a block of silence as a hole. So refusing holes would refuse real sounds. Instead the place treats a sound on the drive as the Sample page treats a file. It gets the same length check: `mpfree_sampler::wav::MAX_SAMPLES` × 8 bytes, plus 1 MiB. It gets the same memory promise: `memory::reserve` with `decode_peak`, from the sound's header, read first through `Ext4::open_file`. So a claimed size never takes memory the MPC hasn't got, and at worst a damaged sound loads as silence.

**The place.** The library is built on real folders. The internal drive is an image read through the reader, so it can't be a library folder.

- **Listing:** the place gets its rows from the reader, as the Plugins place gets its rows from the app: `mpfree-library` gains a read-only `Volume` behind `Place::Internal`, with `list(loc)` from `read_dir` and the inodes' sizes and times, and `open(loc)` from `open_file`: a stream (`Read` and `Seek`, holes as zeros), so the loader reads a sound's header, promises its memory, then reads it whole. `check_writable` refuses `Place::Internal`, as it does the SD card.
- **Hidden:** Akai's own folders are left out of the top folder: `system` (Akai's `/etc` and `/var` layers), `lost+found`, and names starting with a dot. Links and special files are never shown or followed.
- **Load, Audition and Copy** read the bytes through the volume on the library's worker: a sound is read into memory, once its memory is promised (above), and handed to the loader as bytes (`LoadSample` with the bytes instead of a path), so nothing is copied anywhere first. Copy, then Paste in a library place, writes the bytes with `Library::write_new`.
- **Akai's own files:** the MPC's projects (`.xpj`) and programs (`.xpm`) open with [`mpfree-mpc-import`](../../../rust/mpfree-mpc-import/src/lib.rs) (sounds and patterns only, decisions), reading each sample the program names through the same volume. That is S3's second part.
- **Its look:** the SD card place's (read-only path line, Copy only, no stars). Its own screens go to Jay first, as the SD card's did.

**Unverified, to settle before it is built:**
- Which top folders Akai's browser shows on the Internal drive.
- Whether Akai's partition 8 uses features the reader refuses (`INCOMPAT_KNOWN`).
- Whether its folders and files all read with MPFree's reader, and how many sounds have holes.

The owner's eMMC backup answers all three on the workstation, without the MPC, through [`mpfree-ext4-survey`](../../../rust/mpfree-diskimage/src/bin/mpfree-ext4-survey.rs). `mpfree-ext4-survey --newest-backup` opens the newest `~/mpfree-backups/mpc-one-emmc-*/mmcblk0.img` read-only and finds the partition named `data` by its GPT. It reads that partition with the reader the place will use and reports:
- its feature words, by name, and whether the reader opens it;
- the journal's state;
- the top folder, marking what the place leaves out;
- counts of files, folders, hashed folders and links, and how deep the folders go;
- the largest folder and how long it took to list;
- the files' endings and the sounds against the place's limit;
- how many WAVs start with a WAVE header;
- the files with holes;
- every file read whole as a stream, with the time and any error.

It prints names and counts, never a file's contents. Its tests run it on a GPT disk image whose `data` partition `mke2fs -d` made like the Internal drive, with:
- 600 sounds in a hashed folder;
- a sound with silence stored as a hole;
- Akai's `system` folder;
- a link and a hidden file.
- How long the reader takes to list a folder of a few thousand samples on the eMMC (htree folders are read whole). On the host, `lists_a_hashed_folder_of_thousands_of_files` lists a hashed folder of 5000 files whole, each with its size, in about 3 ms from an image the host has cached. The eMMC's own reads are what is left to measure, on the MPC.

**Passes when,** on the MPC with Jay's go and a rehearsed block: the place lists the Internal drive, a sound from it loads and plays, Copy and Paste put it in the library, the counters stay unchanged, and the boot hashes are the same before and after.
