# Looper: audio loops on the transport

**Status:** engine crate (roadmap step T4), 2026-11-11, on main. STOP keeping the first pass and the pads recorded with their effects (16 Nov) have not run on the device yet. The pad player runs it, with a Looper tab and the line input ([below](#in-the-pad-player-round-6)), and round 7 (cc7f06d) ran the one-loop version on the MPC One, from the pads and from the line input (see [Line input](#line-input-on-the-mpc-one) below). Several loops, per-layer mixing, the edits, merge, layers moved between loops and the faster playback described here have not run on the device yet.

Up to 8 stereo loops on the transport, recorded from the line input or the pad player's own sound, playing together and sharing one memory. Each follows the transport's bars, so it stays in time with the clips, the arp and the metronome.

## How it plays

- **The first pass** starts on the next bar line after Record, and ends after the set number of bars (`SetBars`; the engine's own default is 2, the app's is free), or, with free length, on the bar line after Record is pressed again. Its place in the song is a whole number of bars from `origin`.
- **STOP during the first pass keeps it** (Jay, 16 Nov: STOP stops at once and keeps the take). On the transport's `Mark::Stopped`, `Loop::stop_first` ends the pass on whole bars: up to the next bar line, with the take cut where STOP came and silence after it; or, when STOP came within a 1/16 note after a bar line (pressed a moment late), back on that bar line, giving back the blocks recorded past it (`Store::shrink`). The next bar line's frame is the one the transport would have reached at the tempo then (`Span::frame_of`, kept at each bar line), so the loop has the frames a pass ending there would have had. Nothing after STOP is recorded except the input still on its way (`latency`). The take fades out over up to `FADE` frames at the cut (written, `Loop::close_first`), and the loop point's seam fades from the silence after it into what was heard just before the first bar line, as at any loop point; the slot's `valid` range covers what was kept before the pass and the take, so the silence is never read from stale blocks, and nothing after STOP is kept ([Boundaries](#boundaries-the-first-pass-kept-around-its-loop)). `Status::cut` gives the take's frames. No overdub waits for PLAY after it (`After::Overdub` is skipped). STOP before a 1/16 note was played throws the pass away (`Stats::aborted`), as a jump or locate still does.
- **Playback follows the song position.** The loop's position is the song position's place in the loop: `(tick - origin) mod len_ticks`, scaled to frames and read between frames. It stays on the bars through stops, locates and transport loops. At the tempo it was recorded at it plays sample for sample; at another tempo it plays faster or slower, like tape, until a print for that tempo is in ([Prints](#prints-another-tempo-the-same-pitch)): then it keeps its pitch.
- **Overdubs** are layers on top of the first pass, from the moment Overdub is pressed until it is pressed again, over as many passes as you like. Overdub pressed during the first pass starts the overdub exactly where the first pass ends. Undo fades out and removes the newest layer; Redo brings it back, until a new overdub starts.
- **After recording** (`Command::SetAfter`, the Looper tab's setting): `After::Play` (the default) plays the loop when its first pass ends; `After::Overdub` goes straight into an overdub, as if Overdub had been pressed during the first pass. Overdub during the first pass cancels it for that pass. A resample's first pass never goes into an overdub.
- **Fade old** (`Command::SetFadeOld(keep)`, 0 to 1, the default 1 fades nothing): each time the loop comes round while overdubbing, the older layers keep `keep` of their sound (0.88 keeps 88 % a pass), and the layer being recorded keeps that share of its own earlier passes, so old material dies away as new comes in, like a tape loop's feedback. The older layers turn down by a gain of their own, gliding over a few milliseconds at the loop point; their level knobs stay as set, and `LayerInfo::faded` shows how far each is down. Undo of the overdub turns them back up to where they were, and Redo down again. Below -80 dB a layer is silent.
- **Each layer has its own mix.** Level (0 to 2), pan (a balance, -1 to 1), mute and solo. While any layer is soloed, only soloed layers play.
- **Any layer can be deleted**, the first pass too; the last layer stays (Clear removes the loop). A delete fades the layer out, and Undo brings it back in its place. Undo and Redo walk one history of overdubs and deletes, newest first.
- **Clear has an undo** (Looper tab step 3a). Once a cleared loop has faded out, `Loop::read` copies the whole loop (`Store::keep`: its layers, history, settings, `Store::kept[n]`, a preallocated `Loop` a loop) and marks the blocks its slots hold (`kept_mask`); the loop resets. Undo on that empty loop (`Loop::unclear`) puts it all back, with the loop's current latency and launch, and fades in where the song has got to. The kept blocks stay the loop's until it records again (the bar line its first pass starts on), loads or takes a layer, or `Command::Forget` (as Clear, with no undo: `LoopHost::tend_open` uses it for Open and New); when another loop needs memory, `Store::grow` drops the oldest kept loop first (`kept_at`). `Status::used` leaves kept blocks out and `Memory::used` counts them as free (`Store::available`). A clear during an overdub undoes the overdub first (`Loop::undo_open_overdub`), so Undo brings the loop back as it was before it and Redo then brings the overdub: a loop key held to clear started one on its press. `Status::kept` says Undo can bring a loop back.
- **Silent overdubs leave nothing.** An overdub whose input never reached `SILENT` (0.001, -60 dB) and that turned no older layer down (Fade old) is dropped when it closes (`Loop::drop_overdub`): no layer, no history step, its blocks back at once.
- **Overdubs: Add, Replace or Once** (`Command::SetOverdubs`, `Status::overdubs`). Add records until Overdub again. **Replace** (step 3b) records as Add, and its slot keeps its fade-in and fade-out lengths (`Slot::replace`): wherever it was written, the layers before it in the play order are silent, crossfading over those fades, as the complement of its own gain there (`Slot::cover`, read through the Replace layer's view, so it follows the edits). `Loop::ducks` works out each layer's share at a frame, newest first; `sample_at`, `render_layers` (merge, bounce, prints) and `read_layer` (a save: the layers under it are saved as heard, so a loaded loop sounds the same with the Replace as an ordinary layer) use it. A loop with a Replace layer plays frame by frame (`Loop::still` is false). Its strength (`Slot::duck`) moves to 0 a step a frame while the Replace layer is muted or left out by a solo, and fades with it when it is undone or deleted, so the old part fades back in. A second pass replaces its own first, crossfading back into it as it fades out; a Replace of silence stays (it silences those frames), and a copy of a Replace layer to another loop is an ordinary layer. Once punches out by itself after one pass: it closes `latency` frames before its start comes round, so its fade-out (the seam fade, at least a frame, instead of the punch's `FADE`) ends where its fade-in began, and the pass has no seam.
- **Speed** (`Edit::Speed(-1 | 0 | 1)`, step 3c): like tape. It changes only the loop's length in ticks (twice for half speed, half for double; refused if the ticks would not divide or would fall under a 1/16 note), so the same frames play slower or faster against the song position, an octave down or up; the position jumps within the loop and crossfades, as other edits do. The shape keeps the speed shown (`Status::speed`) and `Status::rate`, the octaves the stored audio plays faster than recorded (half speed -1; ½× straight to 2× is two steps). It is an edit: Undo and Redo swap the shape. A rate other than 0 always wants a print (below): the print resamples the bounce by its octaves first (`print::octave`, a 64-tap Kaiser-windowed sinc taken round the loop, so it joins up) and stretches only if the tempo also differs, so the print plays as it is. **Overdub at another rate waits for that print** (`Loop::print_wait`, `Status::overdub_next`; a second press or Undo calls it off), then fits the loop to it and starts, as over a tempo print; the fit sets `rate` to 0 and keeps `speed`, and a speed change after it is relative to the fitted audio. Back at rate 0 with no print playing, a waiting overdub starts at once. Fitting at half speed doubles the frames; in a full memory the new overdub's memory comes from forgetting the oldest history, as ever. A copy into an empty loop and a load take the speed and rate; the save adds `speed=` and `octaves=` to a loop's line when either is not 0.
- **Loop solo** (`Command::Solo(bool)`): while any loop with sound is soloed (`process` works it out each period after the commands), the loops not soloed play silent in their places (`Status::solo_out`), fading as a mute does. Clear lets go of a loop's solo.
- **Layer ids.** A layer keeps its id while it exists, whatever comes and goes around it, so a screen can name it safely.
- **Silent while stopped or counting in.** Overdub pressed while stopped waits for Play. Stopping ends an overdub, with a fade. STOP pressed while already stopped lets go of an overdub waiting for Play and disarms an armed loop, on every loop (`LoopHost::stop_waiting`, the app's `LoopAction::StopWaiting`), so no light is left blinking.

## Several loops

- **8 loops** (`MAX_LOOPS`), each with its own first pass, length, origin, layers, history, level and mute. They all play at once, each on its own bars: a 1-bar loop and a 3-bar loop recorded later both stay on the song's bar lines.
- **The selected loop takes the commands.** `select_loop(n)` is queued with the other commands, in order, so `select_loop(2)` then `Record` arms loop 3 and nothing else. A selection waits behind a command that is waiting for a fade, so no command reaches the wrong loop. `status()`, `layer()` and `layer_info()` show the selected loop; `loop_status(n)`, `loop_layer(n, k)` and `loop_layer_info(n, k)` show any loop. A loop number past the last is counted in `Stats::no_layer`, and the selection stays.
- **One recording at a time.** The line input goes to one loop: when a loop arms, starts an overdub or waits to start one, the others stop recording. An overdub punches out (with its fade); a first pass ends on its next bar line, so a free-length pass keeps what was played; an armed loop disarms. Arming a loop while another records its first pass hands over on the bar line: the first pass ends where the next one starts. Starting an overdub on one loop while another records its first pass lets that pass run to its bar line, so both take the input until then.
- **One memory.** All loops share the memory `Looper::new` asks for, in blocks of 1024 frames (`BLOCK`, 8 KiB of stereo). A layer holds whole blocks: a first pass takes them as it records (with `KEEP_BEFORE` frames before it), and when it ends the blocks for `KEEP_AFTER` frames after it; an overdub takes a whole pass's worth when it starts. Clear gives a loop's blocks back once it has faded out, and so do Undo and Redo once what they could bring back is gone. The pool is a little larger than the seconds asked for: one block per layer of one loop more, so whole layers fill what the seconds promise.
- **Launches** (for [Loop tracks](loop-tracks.md)). `launch(n, on, at)` turns loop `n` on or off as a clip launches: from the bar line at song tick `at`, on the frame that line falls on, or with `None` at once. An off loop fades out as a mute does and is silent, but its place runs on with the song, so a launch brings it back where it would have been had it never stopped. A launch waits in the loop (`Status::cue`) for its bar line; the bar line handler takes it while the transport plays, on that line or the first one after it if the song jumped past. A new launch replaces one waiting. `Status::on` says whether the loop is launched; a loop starts on, and arming a first pass turns it on. It doesn't wait for the selected loop's fades. A loop number past the last is counted in `Stats::no_layer`.
- **The gauge.** `memory()` gives `used` and `capacity` in frames for all loops together; `Status::used` is what one loop holds, and `Status::capacity` is the shared total. A first pass that outgrows the free memory is thrown away and counted in `Stats::aborted`; an overdub that does not fit is refused and counted in `Stats::refused`. Neither affects the other loops.

## The history

`history()` (or `loop_history(n)`) gives what the Looper tab's undo history shows: `done()`, the steps Undo takes back, oldest first, and `redo()`, the steps Redo brings back, the next one first. Each `Step` is `Overdub(id)`, `Delete(id)`, `Edit(edit)`, `Merge(id)`, `Fit(id)`, `Added(id, from)` or `Moved(id, to)` ([Layers between loops](#layers-between-loops)), with layers named by id as in `LayerInfo`. It holds the last `HISTORY` (64) steps of a loop. The first pass is not a step: Clear takes it away. It is a plain value with no allocation, so the audio loop can send it to the screen.

## Edits

The edits change how a loop plays, never its audio:

| Edit | What it does |
|---|---|
| `Half` | Keeps the first half (down to a 1/16 note). |
| `Double` | Plays the loop twice. Overdubs can then make the two halves differ. |
| `Reverse` | Plays the loop backwards. |
| `Nudge(n)` | Moves the audio by `n` 1/16 notes, later (above 0) or earlier, going round the loop. |
| `Trim { from, to }` | Keeps ticks `from..to` of the loop. That becomes the loop, and it stays where it was in the song (`origin` moves by `from`). |
| `SeamFade(frames)` | The crossfade at every cut the edits make and at a first pass's loop point, up to `MAX_SEAM` (8192). 0 is a straight cut; 256 (`FADE`) until changed. |
| `Move(frames)`, `Resize(frames)`, `Turn(frames)` | Move the first pass's loop in the audio it kept, its end, or its bar 1 ([Boundaries](#boundaries-the-first-pass-kept-around-its-loop)). Only while the first pass plays alone, unedited. |

- **How they work.** Each layer plays its audio through a view (`src/view.rs`): up to 8 pieces of its audio, each forwards or backwards. An edit makes new views for every layer and keeps the old shape (length, place, seam, views) for Undo. So an edit takes no memory and no time, and Undo brings back exactly what was there. Edits go into the same history as overdubs and deletes.
- **The loop around an edit.** Every layer of the loop is edited together. Overdubs recorded after an edit fit the edited loop. An edit, its Undo and its Redo crossfade from the loop before over 256 frames.
- **Seams.** Where playback jumps from one piece of audio to another (a trimmed loop's loop point, a nudge inside a trimmed loop, a halved loop's end), the last `seam` frames of the piece crossfade into the audio just before the next piece, which flows on into it. A first pass's loop point is one of these cuts too (since 0A): its loop is a region of what it kept, and the audio just before the region is what was heard before the pass.
- **When.** Edits apply while the loop plays and nothing records. Otherwise, or when an edit cannot be made (shorter than a 1/16 note, or more than 8 pieces in a view), they are counted in `Stats::refused`. Like Overdub, an edit waits for a fade or an overdub's tail.

## Boundaries: the first pass kept around its loop

The smart loop end plan's step 0A (22 Nov): the groundwork for moving a loop's start and end after it closes, with nothing rewritten.

- **What a first pass keeps.** Its layer's audio is `[before][loop][after]`: `KEEP_BEFORE` (`MAX_SEAM`, 8192) frames of what was heard before its first frame, copied in from `Heard` as it comes (`Loop::render_before`, silence where nothing was heard yet), the loop as before, and up to `KEEP_AFTER` (2 x `MAX_SEAM`) frames of what the writer hears after the end while the loop plays (`Loop::keep_after`, `Loop::tail`). The memory for both is taken with the pass (the after part when it ends, in `finish_len`), so a loop's memory is known from its close: about 24 blocks, 196 KB, half a second at 44.1 kHz. With no room for the after part, the loop keeps nothing after it and `Stats::kept_short` counts it. STOP keeps nothing after it. A resample keeps the last 256 frames its writer took before it (the other loops, not what was heard), with silence before.
- **The loop is a region.** The layer's view is `View::region(size, start, len, src)`: `len` frames from `start`, played from `src` (bar 1) round to `start`. The loop point is a cut in that view, so its seam is the edits' seam (`piece_frame`), read as the loop plays and never written in: `Loop::seal` and its 5.8 ms fade written into the audio are gone, and `SeamFade` sets the first loop's seam like any other. The view's audio is `MAX_SEAM` frames longer than what was kept, never written, so a seam that reaches past what was kept reads silence instead of going round to the other end. A pickup (`lead`) is a region whose `src` is `lead` frames after its `start`.
- **The boundary pair.** `Shape::region` (and `Loop::region`) holds the region's `start` and `src`, with the length in `Shape::len`, so it is kept for Undo with every edit; `Loop::captured` is where the pass began and closed. The names follow the audio tracks plan's recordings and regions, agreed with that thread: frames at the app rate from the first frame kept, `src` the frame that plays on bar 1, the length in frames, `captured` a mark on the recording, not a region field. When loops join that table, a loop layer maps onto a recording and a region with no second mechanism.
- **Move, Resize, Turn.** `Edit::Move(d)` shifts both boundaries (the length stays, what plays changes; a smart start), `Edit::Resize(d)` the end (the length changes and the bars stay, so the loop plays a little faster or slower until the tempo follows it, which is step 0C's timing change; a smart end), `Edit::Turn(d)` bar 1 inside the same audio (what plays stays; a pickup). They are edits: Undo, Redo, the 256-frame crossfade and the history (`Step::Edit`) as for any other. They apply only while the loop is its first pass alone and playing that region (`Loop::bounds_now`: one layer, no other live slot, the view still the region, but for a seam fade or a speed), within what was kept, and at least a 1/16 note long; otherwise they are refused (`Stats::refused`). Half, Double, Reverse, Nudge and Trim end the region (Undo brings it back). `Status::bounds` gives `Bounds { src, start, captured, kept }` while they can be made, and the undo history names them "Move → 12 ms", "End ← 5 ms", "Bar 1 → 20 ms". Nothing in the app makes them yet: smart loop ends (steps 1 to 3) and the screens (step 2) will.
- **What it changes.** A Merge of a first pass alone, unturned, is still nothing to merge. A reversed first loop's loop point is now at its end and fades into what was heard after the pass, backwards. A still first pass plays through its own fast path in `still_run` (one forward piece: frames straight from the audio but for the seam's), so it costs what the whole-view path did (see [Cost](#cost)). While the after part is being written the loop runs frame by frame, about half a second after each close.

## Merge

`Command::Merge` bounces the loop's layers into one layer, as heard: through their edits, at their levels, pans, mutes and solos. The loop's own level and mute stay outside.

- **In the background.** It takes the memory for one layer and renders `MERGE_SPEED` (2) frames for each frame that plays, while the loop plays on unchanged. A 2-bar loop at 120 BPM (4 s) is merged in 2 s. `Status::merging` gives the progress, 0 to 1.
- **Taking over.** The merged layer takes the layers' place on the frame by which it is surely rendered: the period the merge started in, plus half the loop. That frame is the same at any period size. It sounds the same, so the change is instant. The test compares the whole session against one without the merge: the output differs by less than 0.00001.
- **Undo** crossfades back to the layers, in their places with their mixes, and Redo crossfades to the merged layer again. Undo while it renders stops it and gives the memory back.
- **While it renders,** the loop's other commands wait (Record, Overdub, Redo, edits and every layer command), so the layers it reads cannot change under it. Undo, Clear, Mute and the loop's level still apply.
- **Refused** (counted in `Stats::refused`) when the loop is not playing, is recording, has nothing to merge (one plain layer at its own level), or there is no memory for one more layer.
- **Room.** A loop plays at most 15 layers (`MAX_LAYERS` - 1), so a merge always has a slot. When every slot is held (the merged layer, and the layers its Undo would bring back), a new overdub forgets the history up to the oldest delete or merge.

## Bounce and resample

Step (d) of the Looper tab: a loop can leave the looper as audio, for a pad or a WAV file, and a new loop can record the others.

- **Bounce.** `Looper::bounce(n, buf)` renders one pass of loop `n` as heard (its layers through their edits and mixes, at the loop's level, not muted) into `buf`, in the background at `MERGE_SPEED` like a merge. The app makes `buf` off the audio thread with room for the loop (twice its frames, stereo) and moves it in; the looper never grows it, so nothing allocates. `bounce_progress()` gives 0 to 1. When it is done, `take_bounce()` moves the `Bounce` out: `audio`, `frames`, the loop's `ticks` and `origin`, and `complete` (false if Clear stopped it, with silence after what was rendered). Off the audio thread, `Bounce::wav(rate)` makes a 32-bit float stereo WAV, which the sampler's `parse_wav` reads, so the app can put it on a pad or save it with `mpfree-store`.
- **While it renders** the loop plays on, unchanged, and its commands wait (Mute and the loop's level still apply; Clear stops it). One bounce at a time. `bounce` gives `buf` back when the loop is not playing, is recording or merging, `buf` is too small, or a finished bounce has not been taken.
- **Resample.** `Command::Resample` on an empty loop is Record, but the first pass records the other loops as they are heard (after their levels and mutes), sample for sample, instead of the input. There is no latency to make up for, because the audio never leaves the box. The loop then plays and takes overdubs like any other. Resample again during the first pass acts as Record; on a loop that has a first pass it is refused. In the app it is a source, not a button: with the Looper tab's Records from set to Loops (`LoopHost::resample`, `LoopAction::RecordLoops`), Record on an empty loop sends `Command::Resample`, and Record on a loop that plays overdubs from the input as usual.
- **Tests.** A bounce matches the layers as heard, through their mixes and at the loop's level (to 0.000001), and its WAV has the sampler's format (a WAV from `Bounce::wav` was also read back through the sampler's `PreparedSample::parse_wav`, bit for bit, once by hand); a resampled loop matches what the other loops played, sample for sample; a resample runs through the busy three-loop session at every period size; the random-press test bounces and resamples; the no-alloc test bounces again and again, with its buffer going round.

## Layers between loops

`Looper::copy_layer(from, id, to, keep)` gives layer `id` of loop `from` to loop `to`: the Looper tab's drag and drop (layout spec 4h). It is queued with the commands and applies at the start of the next period.

- **Where it can go.** Onto a loop with the same length in ticks that plays with nothing changing its layers (no first pass or overdub being written, merge, fit, load, bounce or fade) and has a free slot (fewer than `MAX_LAYERS` - 1 layers); or into an empty loop (not clearing, loading or taking another layer), which then takes the source loop's frames, ticks and origin. The source loop must play with nothing changing its layers too. Anything else is counted in `Stats::refused`, and a layer that is not there in `Stats::no_layer`. The app checks the same first, so the screen can say why (`LoopHost::copy_layer`, `looper_page::drop_spots`).
- **Copied in the background.** Into a free slot (or, for an empty loop, memory for the source's length, taken at once), at `COPY_SPEED` (64 frames for each frame that plays) while both loops play on unchanged. It reads the layer as it plays, through its loop's edits and seam, at gain 1; the layer's level, pan, mute and Fade old turn-down go with it as settings (not its solo).
- **In time with the song.** The copy's frame 0 is where the source layer is at the target loop's start: `shift = ((target origin - source origin) mod ticks) × source frames / ticks`. A loop recorded at another tempo has another length in frames for the same ticks, so the layer is read at `source frames / target frames` frames a frame, between frames (linear), and lands at the target's length.
- **Joining.** The layer comes in on the frame `start + frames / COPY_SPEED` (rounded up), which does not depend on the period size, fading in over `FADE` frames as an `Added` step in the target's history. Into an empty loop, the loop fades in as a whole and starts playing on that frame. For a move (`keep` false), the source fades the layer out on the same frame as a delete, so the sound is the same throughout (the test holds the biggest step from one frame to the next under 0.0001). A loop's last layer stays, so a move from a one-layer loop is a copy.
- **The history.** The target lists `Step::Added(id, from)`, and for a move the source lists `Step::Moved(id, to)`. While each is the last step of its loop, and both loops play with nothing changing, Undo in either loop takes back both together (the layer fades out of the target and back into the source), and Redo brings both again. Otherwise each is its own loop's step, like an overdub or a delete. Undoing an `Added` that is its loop's only layer clears that loop.
- **While it copies,** the commands that would change either loop's layers wait (Record, Overdub, Redo, edits, merges, layer commands, and Undo on the source). Undo on the target, or Clear on either loop, calls the copy off and gives the memory back; Mute and the loop's level still apply. `load` refuses a loop that is taking a layer. `Status::copying` gives the progress, 0 to 1.
- **Cost.** A copy reads two layer frames and writes one for each of 64 frames a played frame, about as much as a merge for a 32nd of the loop's length. Nothing allocates.

## No clicks

| Where | What the looper does |
|---|---|
| The loop point | A seam the loop plays, never written into the audio: the last `seam` frames (256 until changed) crossfade into what was heard just before the first pass, which flows on into the first frame. A sine that does not fit the loop would jump about 0.13 at a raw splice; the test holds it under 0.008. |
| Punch in and out | The overdub writer fades in and out over 256 frames (`FADE`, about 5.8 ms). After a full pass it adds, so the fade-in and the next pass join without a seam. An overdub that takes over from the first pass fades in over 32 frames (`HANDOFF_FADE`), because the input runs on without a break there; that keeps the downbeat's attack. |
| Start, stop, mute, clear | The loop's gain ramps over 256 frames. |
| Undo, redo and delete | The layer's gain ramps over 256 frames. |
| A layer's level, pan, mute and solo | Its left and right gains move one 256th of full scale a frame toward the settings. |
| A jump in the song position | A locate, a transport loop that is not a whole number of loops, or a restart right after a stop: the old place and the new one crossfade over 256 frames. |
| Edits | An edit, its Undo and its Redo crossfade from the loop before over 256 frames. Each cut an edit makes has a seam fade (`SeamFade`). |
| Merge | The merged layer takes over without a fade (it is the same sound). Undo and Redo of a merge crossfade over 256 frames. |
| Resample | The first pass records the other loops after their own fades, and its loop point crossfades like any first pass, into the last 256 frames the writer took before it (what the other loops played, not what was heard). |
| Commands during a fade | Record, Overdub, Undo, Redo, edits, merges and Delete that would cut a fade short wait for it (at most 256 frames), in order. Pressing Undo twice removes two layers. A new overdub waits while the last one's tail is written (the latency, then 256 frames), so that tail still fades out. |
| Stale audio | A reused layer slot only plays the frames the new overdub wrote. |

## Prints: another tempo, the same pitch

A loop follows the song position, so at another tempo it would play faster or slower, like tape. A **print** keeps its pitch: one pass of the loop's layers as heard, stretched to the loop's length at the new tempo with `mpfree-stretch` (the sampler's phase vocoder, which keeps hits sharp).

- **Made on a worker.** `Looper::bounce_print(n, buf)` renders the layers as heard, without the loop's own level and mute, at `PRINT_SPEED` (8 frames for each frame that plays, so a 2 s loop is ready in a quarter of a second). `print::PrintJob` (or `print::print`) stretches the `Bounce` to `print::frames_at(ticks, bpm, rate)` frames on a worker thread, in steps. A loop goes round, so the job stretches it with two stretch windows wrapped on before and after and keeps the middle; the print's start is crossfaded over `print::SEAM` (1024) frames from what the stretch made after its end, so it joins up at its loop point with no click.
- **Handed in.** `Looper::print(n, Some(print))` hands it to loop `n` without allocating; it takes over once the print playing (if any) has faded out. It plays in place of the layers, crossfading in and out over `FADE` frames, while the loop is still as it was when bounced: the same `Status::version` (which moves on with every overdub, undo, edit, delete or layer mix change), length in ticks and start. Any change stops it until a new one comes. The print it replaces waits in `take_print(n)` for the host to drop away from the audio thread. `Status::printed` says a print plays; `print_info(n)` gives its frames, ticks and version.
- **Where it plays** is worked out from the song position, as the loop's is: `(tick - origin) mod ticks`, scaled to the print's frames. So it stays on the bars through locates and transport loops, and a jump crossfades as the layers' does.
- **In the app**, `LoopHost` runs a print worker (the `mpfree-print` thread): each period it looks at the tempo, and a playing loop whose length at that tempo is more than a frame off its own (within the stretch's 10x) gets a print made and handed in. A print that comes back for a loop that has changed meanwhile is dropped (counted as "too late"). Buffers are made and dropped on the worker, so the audio loop never allocates. `describe()` counts prints made, too late and failed.

## Fit

Overdubbing over a print would record at the new tempo onto layers made at the old one. So **Overdub (or Record) while a print plays first fits the loop to the tempo**: the print becomes the loop's one layer, at the print's length (the loop is then sample for sample at the new tempo), and the overdub starts on the frame it takes over. The copy runs at `FIT_SPEED` (64 frames for each frame that plays, a plain copy), so the overdub waits about a 64th of the loop; a second press while it copies calls the overdub off. `Command::Fit` fits without overdubbing. Undo takes the fit back as one step (`Step::Fit`): the loop dips out over `FADE` frames, its layers come back as they were, and it fades in again; Redo fits again. `Status::fitting` gives the copy's progress. A fit is refused when no print plays.

## Capture: what was heard

Capture (Looper tab step 4a, `Command::Capture(bars)`) turns the last bars played into a loop, whether Record was pressed or not: [`heard.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-looper/src/heard.rs).

- **The heard ring.** `Heard` keeps the last `HEARD_SECONDS` (60; 30 before the capture window, 20 Nov) of the looper's input, whatever the loops do: interleaved stereo, frame `f` at `f % cap`, stamped with the device frame each period starts on (`Block::start_frame`), and copied in before the commands apply. It is made once in `Looper::new` (none when the memory is 0 s), so it never allocates; `bytes_for` counts it (about 21 MB at 44.1 kHz). It keeps 64 bar lines, more than 60 s hold at any tempo played. `Command::CaptureTo(tick, bars)` takes bars ending on any bar line still kept in the newest run (`Heard::line_at`), as `Capture` does from the newest; `Looper::capture_take_to` says what it would take. `NewInput` (the app sends it when Records from changes) forgets what came before: a take never starts before it.
- **Bar lines.** For each span the song plays or records, `Heard::hear` logs the bar lines in it (`Span::lines`): their frame and tick, in a ring of 32, more than the 8 bars of the longest capture needs. A run of lines is one unbroken stretch of song: a stop, a count-in, a tick that does not follow on from the last span's (a locate, a transport loop's jump) or a gap in the frames starts a new run, and a take never spans two. So the bars between two lines are exactly what was heard between them, whatever the tempo did in between.
- **The take.** `Heard::take(end, bars, latency)`: `bars` whole bars ending on bar line `end`, halving (8, 4, 2, 1) until they fit: the start line must be in the same run, the frames must have been heard since `NewInput`, and the ring must still hold all of them, with the copy's time and `SLACK` (8192 frames) to spare. The take is read `latency` frames late, as a first pass is written, so what was played on the bar line lands on the loop's first frame. Its ticks are the lines' difference and its origin the start line's tick, so the loop lands on the song's bars.
- **When.** `Capture` into an empty loop takes the bars up to the bar line just passed. In a bar's last beat (`Heard::in_last_beat`, `Looper::capture_waits`) it waits for the line coming, and takes the bars up to it; if the song stops first, it takes them up to the last line. Refused (counted in `Stats::refused`) into a loop that is not empty or is busy, with no whole bar heard, or without memory for the frames.
- **The copy** reuses a load's machinery: the loop takes its memory at once (dropping kept clears and reclaiming as a recording does), then copies the frames in at `LOAD_SPEED` (64 frames for each frame that plays: 2 bars at 120 BPM in about 60 ms), silent; then it fades in, in time with the song, on a frame that does not depend on the period size (from `max(now, start + frames)`). The loop point fades into the `FADE` frames heard just before the take's start, so it joins up as a first pass does. If the ring has moved past the frames before the copy reads them, it is thrown away and counted in `Stats::aborted`.
- **Calling it off.** Capture again, Undo, Clear or Forget while it waits or copies calls it off and gives the memory back. Other commands to that loop wait until it plays. `Status::capturing` is `Some(0.0)` while it waits for its line, then the copy's progress, 0 to 1.
- **For the screen.** `capture_take(bars)` says what a Capture into the selected loop would take now (none: nothing yet), `heard_lines(out)` gives the newest run's bar lines (tick and frame) and `heard_at()` the frame after the last one heard, for **What you played**.

## A beat's warning before the bar

Jay's device notes (20 Nov): a tap on an empty ring seemed to start at once, so a loop could land before he was ready. The engine always waited for the next bar line, but that line could be a few milliseconds away.

- **The lead.** `Command::Record` on an empty loop sets `Loop::just_armed`; at the start of the next period `Looper::process` notes, while the song plays (`State::Playing | Recording`), the tap's song tick and the tick `LEAD_BEATS` (1) beats after it (`Loop::tap_lead`). `Loop::starts_on` lets no bar line strictly between the two start the first pass (`Starts::Bar` and resamples), so a tap in the last beat before a line starts on the line after. A line on the tap's own tick still starts it (the song starting with the tap). Armed while stopped or counting in there is no lead: the loop starts on the bar PLAY starts on, as REC and PLAY together always did (SHIFT + Arm too). A stop, locate, transport loop or panic forgets the lead (`Loop::marker`).
- **The countdown.** `Status::starts_on` is the bar line (song tick) the armed loop starts on while the song plays: the first bar line after the tap, or the one after the lead. The Looper tab's ring (`LooperPage::refresh_deck`) says the bar ("13", "REC on bar"), and in the last bar before it the beats left ("3", "beats to REC"; "1", "beat to REC"). Play's loop row draws the same channels.
- **Tests:** `a_tap_in_the_last_beat_waits_for_the_bar_after`, `a_tap_with_a_beat_to_spare_starts_on_the_next_bar`, `a_loop_armed_while_stopped_starts_with_play` (tests/looper.rs), `a_waiting_loop_counts_the_beats_in_its_last_bar` (looper_page.rs).

## Length: Pattern

The Looper tab's Length has **Pattern** first, the app's default (20 Nov): a new loop is as long as the pattern playing, so it fits the beat. The engine never sees it; the app sends `Command::SetBars(Some(PATTERN_BARS))` (0, looping.rs), and `LoopHost::command` turns that into `SetBars(pattern_bars)` and keeps `LoopHost::match_pattern`. Any other Length lets go of it.

- **The pattern's length.** `pattern_bars` in main.rs: the longest clip playing (`TimeHost::now_playing(t).song_len`) on a notes track (a Loop track's cells are loops, not patterns), rounded up to whole bars. The pad player tells `LoopHost::set_pattern_bars` every 32 periods; while Pattern is on and the picked loop is empty (not capturing), its bars follow it. Nothing playing, or longer than `PATTERN_MAX_BARS` (32): `SetBars(None)`, free (and so Tempo from the first loop works with Pattern too).
- **Record** on an empty loop with Pattern on sends the bars first, so a loop picked and tapped in the same period has them.
- **Capture** takes the Length's bars, so with Pattern it takes the pattern's length.
- `LooperView::match_pattern` and `pattern_bars` let the tab light Pattern. Test: `length_pattern_follows_the_pattern_playing` (looping.rs).

## Listen: a loop on its own while stopped

Jay's device notes (20 Nov): a play button to hear a loop or a layer while main playback is stopped.

- **`Looper::listen(n, layer)`** plays loop `n` alone from its first frame, round and round, while the song is stopped: the whole loop as heard (`Loop::heard_gains`: its layers' levels, pans, mutes, solos and Fade old), or with `layer` (a layer id) that layer alone at its level and pan, even when muted (`Loop::listen_gains`). It goes through the loop's level, at the speed it was recorded (a print made for another tempo is not used). Refused (false) while the song plays, or for a loop that isn't playing (empty, armed, recording, overdubbing) or is merging.
- **`Looper::play_listen`**, each period after the commands: `Loop::render_layers_at` (the render a bounce and a merge use, with Listen's gains) a `RUN` of frames at a time into a stack buffer, faded in over `FADE` frames and added to the output. It stops at once if the loop changes under it (empties, records, merges, the layer goes), and fades out over `FADE` frames when the song starts or on `Looper::stop_listening`. No allocation. `Looper::listening` gives the loop, the layer and the position, 0 to 1.
- **In the app.** `LoopAction::Listen(Some(layer))` (the header's Listen: `Some(None)`; a lane's ▶: `Some(Some(id))`) goes to `LoopHost::listen`, which toggles (the same again stops), and says what it did or why not on the hint line. `LooperView::listening` lights the button (Stop) or the lane's ▶, and moves the editor's playhead. While the music plays both are faint, and a tap says Listen plays while the music is stopped.
- **Tests:** `listen_plays_a_loop_or_a_layer_alone_while_the_song_is_stopped` (tests/looper.rs), `listen_plays_the_picked_loop_while_stopped` (looping.rs), `listen_plays_the_loop_or_a_layer_while_the_music_is_stopped` (gui.rs).

## When I play: starting on your note

Starts: When I play (Looper tab step 4b, `Command::SetStarts(Starts::Play)`, per loop like After recording): an armed loop waits for your first note instead of the next bar line.

- **The note.** A loop listening (`Status::listening`: armed, `Starts::Play`, not a resample, no note yet) takes its first note one of two ways. By ear (`Command::StartsByEar(true)`, which the app sends while Records from is Line in): the first input sample at or over `PLAY_LEVEL` (-40 dB), checked in the first pass's writer, which sees every input frame while armed, and placed `latency` earlier, when it was played. Told (`Command::Played(frame)`, the app's pad hit): the pads' mix has the song's own sound in it, so a level would start it on the drums; a pad hit is the note, on the frame it was read. Either way the note waits in `Loop::hit` for the next period's start (`Looper::place_hits`, after the commands), so where it starts never depends on the period size.
- **The song playing: the nearest bar line.** `Heard::around(p)` gives the bar lines of the run the song is playing now on either side of the note (a run starts at a stop, a count-in, a locate or a transport loop's jump, as for Capture). A note in the first half of a bar starts the pass on that bar's line; it has passed, so `Loop::begin_first` starts it now with `rec_from` on the line, and the frames from the line to now come in from the heard ring (`Fill`, at `LOAD_SPEED`; what was heard before the line is kept before the pass, `Loop::render_before`), while the writer takes the input from now on. A note in the second half is a pickup into the next line: the pass starts on that line (`pending`, placed in `Loop::starts_on` at the line), but records from the note (`rec_from` = the note, `lead` = the frames between), and when it closes, `finish_len` makes the first layer's region start its bar 1 `lead` frames in (`View::region`, as `Edit::Turn` would), so the loop's frame 0 is the line and the pickup plays in the loop's last `lead` frames, leading into bar 1 each time round. The loop point is between the end of the pass and the note, and fades into what came before the note, as any first pass's does. A pickup heard after its line passed (a long period) starts now from the heard ring the same way. A pickup still waiting when the song stops or jumps is forgotten (`Loop::marker`), and the loop listens again.
- **Counting in.** A note during the count-in is a pickup into bar 1 if it is no more than half a bar before it, else it is forgotten when bar 1 comes and the loop listens again.
- **The song stopped.** The loop asks the host to start the song: `Looper::take_start` gives the loop once a note, and the host starts the transport on the next period's first frame (`TimeHost::start_at`, below). The pass starts on bar 1's line, `rec_from` on the note (`starts_on`: the note is the loop's frame 0, so bar 1 is where you started; the frames between come in from the heard ring); a note a quarter of a bar or more before the song started is too stale, and the pass starts on the line instead. If the host can't start the song it calls `Looper::start_refused`, and the loop listens again. A pad hit needs none of that: the host starts the song on the pad's own frame before the transport runs (pads are read at the top of the period), and tells the looper `Played` on that frame, which the song is playing by the time it is placed.
- **Cost.** One comparison a frame while a loop listens by ear, in the writer that already runs for an armed loop; a placement once a note; the fill copies at most half a bar plus a period at `LOAD_SPEED`. `place_hits` allocates nothing (`tests/no_alloc.rs`). The first pass's writer runs every frame, so what it does once a pass stays out of it: with the start's copy of the input before the pass (`keep_pre`) inline, CI's instruction gate measured the bench's overdubbing case 5.9 % above main (26 565 to 28 145 instructions a period); with it out of line, counted with callgrind on a copy of that case, the rise is about 2.5 %, from `place_hits`' check of each loop once a period and the writer's longer path.
- **In the app.** The Looper tab's **Starts** (`leditor/starts`: On the bar, When I play; faint for Records from: Loops, whose tap says why) sends `SetStarts`; the host keeps the last one sent (`LoopHost::starts`) and sends it to an empty loop when it is picked (`LoopHost::select`) or recorded (`LoopHost::command`), so the choice sticks for every empty loop; a loop with sound, or armed, keeps its own. `LoopHost::record` keeps `StartsByEar` matching Records from. A listening ring says "Play to start". main.rs's pad paths (the screen's and the hardware's, and the smoke `Session`'s) call `pad_starts_loop` before the pad plays: if a loop listens for the pads (`LoopHost::listens_for_pads`), with the song stopped it starts the song on the coming period's first frame (`start_song_on_note`, refused while an export runs), then sends `Played` on that frame. After `LoopHost::run`, `loop_starts_song` takes a line-input start (`LoopHost::take_start`) and starts the song on the next period's first frame. A take armed and ready (SHIFT + Arm) records from the same frame. A refusal is counted (`LoopHost::starts_refused`, in the status line), logged and said on the screen ("Loop 1 heard you, but the song can't start: ...").

## Tempo from the first loop

Looper tab step 4c (`Command::TempoFromLoop(bool)`, for every loop, off by default; the app sends it on while the Looper tab's MENU **Tempo from the first loop** is on and the song can take a tempo from a loop): with the song stopped, a first loop recorded in your own time sets the tempo.

- **Free.** `Looper::frees(k)`: the setting is on, the loop is not a resample, and every other loop is empty (nothing loading, copying in or capturing). Then, with the song stopped at the period's start (`Looper::stopped`), Record on the empty loop (On the bar) starts the first pass at once, on the period's first frame (`Looper::begin_free`, from the Looper-level apply, since there is no bar line to wait for), and a note placed while stopped (When I play, `place_hits`) starts it on the note, the frames since copied in from the heard ring as for 4b. `Loop::begin_free` sets `free`: no origin, no bars, no bar lines; the writer runs as for any first pass (`write_first`), and what the loop point fades into is what was heard just before the start, kept from `Heard` as for any first pass. A loop armed while the song played that then stops keeps waiting for PLAY: only a Record or a note while stopped starts a free pass. `Status::free` says so (also for an empty or listening loop that would record free now), and the screen and the said lines use it.
- **Closing.** Record during a free pass sets `ending` as for any first pass; `Looper::close_free` (each period, after the commands and the placed notes) closes it on the next period's first frame (`end` = the block's end), so the song can start where the loop comes round, once `read_bars` can read it: until then it keeps recording and closes as soon as it can. The loop's origin is the start of the bar the song is stopped in (`pos - pos.rem_euclid(bar)`, as `TimeAudio::start_at` locates), `len_ticks` the bars, and `finish_len` closes it as for any first pass (the writer still finishes the latency tail). `Looper::take_tempo` gives the host the loop, its `Reading` and that frame, once. `close_free` looks at the loops only while a free pass may be recording (`free_open`, set by `begin_free`), so with none it is one check a period: the bench's overdubbing case, counted with callgrind on a copy of it, costs 33 instructions a period more than 4b (0.1 %).
- **The reading** (`reading.rs`, 22 Nov, the smart loop end plan's step 0B; it replaced `free_tempo`'s 80 to 160 BPM window). `read_bars(len, rate, bar, said, hint)` gives a `Reading`: the bars, the tempo `60 rate (bar / PPQ) bars / len` that makes them exactly `len` frames, how sure (`Sure::Clear` or `Sure::Ambiguous`), the close runner-up (`other`) and the bar count Length said (`said`). With Length set (`said`), the loop is that many bars at the tempo played: nothing is read, unless that tempo is under `LOOP_MIN_BPM` (30), when the length is read as on Free and `Reading::overruled` says so. On Free, every power of two whose tempo is in the app's range (`LOOP_MIN_BPM` to `LOOP_MAX_BPM`, 30 to 300, the MPC's) is scored: a soft preference, `-(ln(T / 113.14))^2 / (2 x 0.35^2)`, centred on the old window's middle (`PREFERRED_BPM`, the square root of 80 x 160) so that with nothing else to go on the pick is the old rule's (ties to fewer bars, as before; a property test checks every length the old rule took), plus the song's tempo before the loop as evidence (`TempoHint`): `2.0 exp(-(ln(T / hint))^2 / (2 x 0.06^2))` when it was set by hand since the project opened (`TempoKind::Set`, `Command::TempoMeant`), 0.3 times that when it was left from before (`TempoKind::Earlier`). The best is `Clear` when it scores at least `MARGIN` (0.5) above the next, else `Ambiguous` with the next as `other`: with no hint, two counts an octave apart are a close call when the slower is between about 73 and 87 BPM. The shortest free loop is a bar at 300 BPM (0.8 s in 4/4), or Length's bars at it. The beat inside the loop joins as evidence (the plan's step 1c for pad and MIDI notes, step 3 for line-in hits): `read_bars_with(len, rate, bar, said, hint, fit)` takes each count's fit, from `ends::InnerBeat::fit`: the notes after the opening hit (the first within the reach of the press, its own notes left out) against that count's beat grid from the hit, each on a bell a twentieth of a beat wide (10 to 30 ms), weighted by accent, 0 to 1; None with fewer than `MIN_BEAT_NOTES` (4), when the length alone decides. It adds `W_BEAT` (1.5) times the fit to each power of two's score, and 3, 5, 6 or 7 bars (`OTHER_COUNTS`) join only when their fit leads the best power of two's by `FIT_LEAD` (0.2): twice the bars fit about as well as the bars played (every beat of the slower count is on the faster grid), so a power of two never wins on the beat alone, and the preference keeps its say. `Reading::beat` keeps the winner's fit, and the log says what the beat said (`looping::reading_words`: ", from the beat you played" for a count that isn't a power of two, ", which the beat you played fits" at a fit of 0.6 or more, else "fits only loosely"). `Looper::note` takes the notes whatever Smart loop end is set to, so the reading uses the beat with it Off too. Halve and Double are Read as (`Snap::Bars`, below), the loop point view's choices (step 2 screens, below).
- **The song.** The host starts the song on that frame at that tempo (`TimeHost::start_at(at, Some(bpm), take)`: Locate, `SetTempo`, Play, the take). At that tempo `bars` bars are exactly `len` frames (to 1e-9 of a frame), so the loop plays sample for sample and wants no print (`want`'s one-frame tolerance). The app takes the tempo as it is, not rounded (`App::set_tempo` at the next period, before `follow_tempo`, which `tempo_asked` keeps quiet meanwhile), and a project keeps it exactly: `Meta`'s `tempo = {}` writes the shortest text that reads back as the same `f64`.
- **What stops it.** The song starting under a free pass (`Mark::Started` or `CountIn`: PLAY, or REC counting in) throws the pass away (`aborted`), since the song would play at the old tempo; `Panic` does too, as for any first pass. A start the host can't make (`start_at`'s refusals, an export) is counted (`LoopHost::tempos_refused`, in the status line), logged and said, and the loop plays at the song's tempo when it starts (like tape, until a print is in). The app turns the setting off for the looper while another device's clock leads (`followed_bpm`, `clock_waits`), the song has more than one tempo (`tempo_map().segments().len() > 1`) or an export runs (`tempo_from_loop_allowed`, every period).
- **In the app.** MENU's **Tempo from the first loop** (`Item::LoopTempo`, `Settings::loop_tempo`, kept as `loop-tempo`). main.rs and the smoke `Session` call `LoopHost::set_tempo_from_loop` and `LoopHost::set_tempo_meant` (from `App::tempo_by_hand`: true once the tempo is tapped, typed, turned or set from a remote, through `App::set_tempo_by_hand`; false again when a project opens or a loop sets the tempo) before `LoopHost::run`, and `loop_sets_tempo` after it (the line "looper: Loop 1 is 1 bar at 96.00 BPM: the song starts at that tempo", with ", as Length set" after the tempo when Length gave the bars; `looping::reading_words`). A close call or a Length the reading couldn't keep adds its note in brackets and goes on the screen too (`looping::reading_note`: "(a close call: it could be 1 bar at 79.00 BPM)"); a Length overruled is counted (`LoopHost::readings_overruled`, in the status line). `pad_starts_loop` doesn't start the song for a loop that records free. The ring says **Free** and the seconds (**closing** while it waits to be long enough); the picked loop's line says "recording free · its length sets the tempo", or "recording 2 bars at the tempo you play · ..." with Length set (`looping::free_words`). Tests: `with_length_set_the_first_loop_is_its_bars_at_your_tempo` and `tempo_from_the_first_loop_sets_the_tempo_and_starts_the_song` (smoke, phases.rs).
- **Bars in any meter** (22 Nov, the smart loop end plan's step 0B). The app counts a loop's bars by the transport's bar (`Meter::bar_ticks`, which `LoopHost` keeps as `bar_ticks` from each period's first span): `take_tempo`'s bars, the bar a launch starts from, the layer-move lines, the log's lengths (`ticks_text`), the live wave's bar and, through `LooperView::bar_ticks`, the Looper tab's lengths (`looper_page::bars_of`) and the deck's bar and beat. `LooperView::frames_per_beat` is a beat of the meter (an eighth in 6/8), so `beats_per_bar` of them make a bar. Before this the host counted beats times a quarter note, so in 6/8 a bar was 5 760 ticks instead of 2 880 and a one-bar loop said 0 bars (the engine already used the meter's bar). Tests: `a_free_loop_counts_its_bars_by_the_meter` (looping.rs), `a_loops_bars_count_by_the_meter` (looper_page.rs).

## Smart loop end

The smart loop end plan's step 1 (approved 22 Nov; plan https://claude.ai/artifact/SxMixk5nzvTBK9mogZDqjn): when a free first pass that sets the tempo closes, the looper weighs the notes played around the closing tap and can put the end on the downbeat the player meant, and the start on their first hit when it came just after REC. Step 3 (below, **By ear**) does the same on the line input and mixes, from the hits it hears.

- **The setting.** `Command::SetSmartEnd(SmartEnd)`, for every loop: `Off` (closes on the tap, as before), `Suggest` (closes on the tap, then offers the end found) or `Auto` (listens on to the end of the reach, then closes on the end found). The app sends the Looper tab's MENU **Smart loop end** (`Item::SmartEnd`, `Settings::smart_end`, Suggest at first, kept as `smart-loop-end`) through `LoopHost::set_smart_end` every period, whatever the source; `LoopHost::record` tells it to listen by ear (`Command::SmartByEar`) while the take's source is the line input or a mix. It applies only where Tempo from the first loop makes the pass free.
- **The notes.** The time engine keeps every note started in a period, live on a track (as the arps give them) or by a route, with its frame on the song's frame clock, its track, note and velocity (`TimeAudio::note_started`, at most `time::audio::NOTES`, 64, a period). main.rs and the smoke `Session` hand them to `LoopHost::note` before `LoopHost::run` (`TimeHost::take_notes`); one the time engine had no room for gives its frame to `LoopHost::notes_lost` (counted, in the status line), and the looper treats it like a note its log dropped. Notes are keyed on what was played, the track and the pad or note, not on an instrument (agreed with the instrument redesign thread). `Looper::note` keeps them only while they could matter (on with Tempo from the first loop, whatever Smart loop end is set to, since the reading weighs the beat too, or a decision waiting); `begin_free` keeps those from a reach before the pass on, and the period's end forgets them when nothing needs them.
- **The log** (`ends::NoteLog`): the pass's first `HEAD` (128) notes, its opening, kept for the whole pass, and the last `TAIL` (128) round a ring. A note dropped from the ring, or lost before it, is counted and marks the newest frame lost, so a decision whose reach it falls in can't be made (`Verdict::Lost`). Fixed size, allocation-free.
- **Deciding** (`ends::decide`). The reach is 100 ms either side of the tap, or a 1/16 note if that is shorter (`ends::reach`). The opening hit is the first notes within 30 ms of each other (`TOGETHER_MS`) within the reach of the pass's start; `Decision::lead` is its frames from the start (0 when no note came that soon). Ends are measured from the opening hit: every hit within the reach of the tap is a candidate (`Decision::end`, its frame), the loop it would make running from the opening hit to it; from the press, the same length ends `lead` earlier. Each is scored `W_ONSET onset + W_RHYTHM rhythm + W_CONTEXT context + W_PRIOR prior`:
  - **onset**: its notes against the opening's, the same track and key, by the opening note's accent and how alike the two velocities are;
  - **rhythm**: the loop's notes after the opening hit up to the reach, against the beat grid that length (from the opening hit) gives (bars as the tap's reading counts them), each on a bell a twentieth of a beat wide (10 to 30 ms), weighted by velocity squared (the opening's own notes are on every grid, so they don't count);
  - **context**: whether the notes after the opening come round again after the candidate, within 20 ms (`CONTEXT_MS`), as far as has been heard;
  - **prior**: nearness to the tap.

  The tap is scored too, as a candidate with no hit. The best is taken only when it scores at least `THRESHOLD` (0.9: the opening hit played again as it was is enough on its own, a softer one needs the rhythm too) and leads everything else, the tap included, by `MARGIN` (0.4); otherwise the tap stands: `NoMatch` ("no note near it"), `NotClear` ("no clear match near it") or `Lost` ("too many notes to follow"). The weights are set on synthetic performances for now and get tuned on real takes in the device sessions (step 4).
- **Auto.** `close_free` keeps recording past the tap (`Loop::tap`, `Status::closing`, the ring says **closing**) until the reach has been heard, then decides with the bars fixed to the tap's reading, and closes on the end found (re-read at the same bars, `reread`, within the tempo range; a length outside it keeps the tap, "too short a loop"). The loop is already `end - stop` frames past its end, so `Looper::take_tempo` also gives `into`, those frames in ticks of the loop, and the host starts the song that far into its first bar (`TimeHost::start_at(at, bpm, take, into)`), in step with the loop; anything due before it is skipped, never played late. Before deciding, the bars are re-read with the beat (`read_bars_with`, the tap's length); the decision keeps them.
  - **A smart start** (step 1c). With a lead, the loop is the length from the opening hit to the end found, and starts on the opening hit: `finish_len` closes it at that length, then `Loop::start_on` moves its region `lead` frames on in what the pass kept, its length kept. That needs what was heard to `end` kept after it, so the loop's memory grows first (`Store::grow`); without the room, the same length runs from the press (the end `lead` earlier than found). With no clear end, a lead still starts the loop on the first hit, ending on the tap. `SmartNote::start` says how far the start moved, in the log line (", and started it on your first hit, 80 ms after REC"), counted in `Stats::starts_found`.
  - **The handoff into an overdub** (step 1c). With Then: Overdub, the overdub opens as the pass closes, `end - stop` frames after the loop's end, and what was played in that time would be lost. A pass that closed after its end has already written it: `finish_len` marks it as the start of what the pass keeps after its end (`Slot::valid`, up to `KEEP_AFTER`). At the overdub's first frame (the overdub writer's fresh-layer branch, so no other frame pays), `Loop::hand_off` writes those frames once, just before the overdub's place, where it would have put them, with the overdub's fade-in, and the overdub goes on from them at full gain (`Stats::handoffs`). Nothing is handed on when the pass kept too little.
- **Suggest.** It closes on the tap as Off does and waits (`Loop::smart_wait`) until the reach has been heard, then decides. ## Free loops

The free-running loop thread's step F1 (23 Nov, engine only; design doc with mockups https://claude.ai/artifact/To8p1MNkUyZx9JcUgJ5Am2, waiting for the owner's OK; the screens are step F2). Each loop keeps time with a clock: the song's (`Clock::Song`, the transport, as everything above) or the looper's one **free clock** (`Clock::Free`, the Looper tab's **Sync: Free**, `Command::SetClock`, taken only while the loop is empty and sticky like Starts). A free loop plays on when the song stops, keeps its place when the song locates or loops and its tempo when the song's changes, and records with the song stopped, at once or in your own time. It is the MPC's Sync off (MPC 3.10.1 guide, pp. 339-342), with one difference: every free loop keeps time with the same free clock, so free loops recorded over each other stay together.

- **The free clock** (`FreeClock`, a `Copy` value; `Looper::free_clock`). An anchor (`anchor_frame`, `anchor_tick`), `bpm`, `rate`, `meter`, `running` and `top`, its bar 1. While it runs, tick `t` falls on frame `anchor_frame + round(frames from anchor_tick to t)`, as on the transport's spans: `FreeClock::tick_at` is `Span::tick_at`'s sum, bit for bit, and `FreeClock::span` makes a `Span` of it, so a free loop goes through the same three doors a song loop does (`Loop::bar_line`, `Loop::marker`, `Loop::run`) and nothing in `Loop` knows which clock it has. `FreeClock::next_bar` finds its next bar line as `Span::lines` does. It never locates and never changes tempo; it only stops (back to `top`) and starts (from `top`). It lives in `FreeTime` (`Looper::free_time`) with `gen`, how many clocks have been set: each free loop keeps the one it was made on (`Loop::clock_gen`).
- **Set from the song.** A free loop armed while the song plays and no free clock runs waits for the song's bar line as a song loop does (`Loop::on_free` is false for an armed loop while the free clock doesn't run). On that line `FreeTime::set_on_song` makes the free clock a copy of the song's clock in that span (its anchor, tempo, rate and meter; `top` the line's tick) and the loop begins on it. So the loop plays sample for sample as a song loop would, until the song stops, locates or changes tempo, and then plays on as if it hadn't (`tests/free_loops.rs`). A stopped free clock instead starts there from its top, keeping its tempo, and the stopped free loops come in with it.
- **Set in your own time.** With the song stopped and no free clock, Record on an empty free loop records free at once, whatever Tempo from the first loop says (`Looper::frees`: a free loop with no free clock, not resampling; song loops don't count against it, nor it against them), and a note starts it on the note (When I play). The pass is the [tempo from the first loop](#tempo-from-the-first-loop) pass (`begin_free`, `close_free`, the reading, smart loop end), but closing sets the free clock instead of asking for the song's tempo: `top` 0 on the loop's start, running from the closing frame at the reading's tempo (its bars exactly its frames), in the song's meter. `take_tempo` gives nothing and the song's tempo stays as it was. STOP as the closing tap (`Loop::stop_close`) sets the clock stopped.
- **What runs.** `process` works out `any_free` once a period: a free loop with audio, starting or loading. With none, nothing below runs and the song's loops run exactly as before. With one, each run also gets the free clock's place for each frame (`free_ticks`, a buffer in the looper so nothing is cleared per run) and a free loop runs on `free_run` (the same frames, those ticks, playing while the clock runs); the free clock's bar lines cut the runs while a free loop is armed, recording or cued (`free_done`: lines handed out), and so does a clock set to start on a frame to come. `tend_free` (once a period, after the commands) drops the clock once no free loop holds audio (`Loop::holds_free`), keeping it for Undo (`FreeTime::kept`), and starts a free loop armed with nothing playing.
- **The song's marks.** A free loop on the free clock hears none of them; `FreeTime::mark` makes of each what the free loops should hear. `Panic` (STOP with the song stopped) stops the free clock (`FreeTime::stop`): each free loop hears it as a song loop hears STOP (a `Stopped` at the clock's tick: a first pass keeps what was played to the next bar line, an overdub ends, they fade out), one armed on the bar lets go, one listening for your note listens again, and a pass in your own time takes it as its closing tap. `Started` (PLAY) starts a stopped free clock from its top on PLAY's frame, so PLAY starts the song and the stopped free loops together. The song stopping lets go of a free loop armed to start on the song's bar line (nothing will play it in) and starts free loops a Play waits to start. A locate or the song's loop lets that Play start on the next bar line.
- **Stop and Play for the free loops** (`Command::FreeStop`, `Command::FreePlay`; F2 puts them on Play's header in Listen's place). Stop stops the free clock at once, back on its bar 1. A loop keeps its `origin`, so it waits at its place on bar 1 and starts again from there: a two-bar loop that started on the clock's bar 2 waits half way through, and the free loops come back together as they played. Play starts it from bar 1: at once with the song stopped, or on the song's next bar line a beat or more on ([LEAD_BEATS](#a-beats-warning-before-the-bar), `FreeTime::cue`) while it plays. REC or OVERDUB on a free loop while the clock is stopped starts it first.
- **Armed free loops.** While the free clock runs: on its bar lines, a beat or more after the tap counted on it (`Loop::tap_lead`, `Status::starts_on` in its ticks). Stopped, with the song playing: on the song's bar line, the free clock starting there from its top. Nothing playing: at once, the clock starting from its top with it (`Looper::begin_free_loop`), or with none, in your own time. When I play: a note while the clock runs starts it on the clock's nearest bar line (`free_around` gives the lines either side, `Looper::place_on` places it as for the song's, the frames since copied in from what was heard); nothing playing, on the note.
- **Undo.** Clearing the last free loop takes the clock with it; Undo brings both back if no other clock was set since (`clock_gen == gen`), else it is refused (`Stats::refused`), since the loop's place means nothing on another clock.
- **Not yet (step F3).** Capture into a free loop, snaps (`snap_plan`) and Suggest's wait for a free loop are refused; a layer copies only between loops on the same clock (an empty loop takes the other's). The app's tempo printer must leave free loops alone (they keep their own tempo): that comes with the screens (F2).
- **Cost.** Song loops pay for the checks above: against the same code without free loops, counted with callgrind on this machine, the bench's overdubbing case is 27 222 to 27 473 instructions a period (+0.9 %), 8 loops playing +0.1 %, half edited +0.1 %, the free pass by ear +0.4 %. The per-frame position (`fill_ticks`) is out of line (`#[inline(never)]`): inline, the code round it grew and the compiler stopped vectorising it, which cost every looper case about 3 150 instructions a period (+12 % on the one-loop case). The bench's "4 free loops of 2 layers playing on, the song stopped" case is 129 196 instructions a period here (CI's first run sets its baseline).
- **Tests** (`tests/free_loops.rs`, against a real transport): a free loop recorded while the song plays is a song loop sample for sample until the song stops, then plays on exactly as if it hadn't; song loops sound the same to the bit beside a free loop through a locate, a tempo change, the song's loop, STOP and PLAY; a free loop keeps its tempo and place through the song's tempo changes, locates and loop; a second STOP stops it at its top and PLAY starts it there with the song; a free loop in your own time sets the free clock (its bars exactly its frames), not the song's tempo, and plays at once; one recorded over it starts on the free clock's bar line a beat on and lines up with its bars; STOP during a free pass keeps it to the next bar line, and the free loops wait in their places at bar 1; Undo brings the last free loop back with its clock until another clock is set; Play while the song plays waits for its bar line a beat on, and with the song stopped Stop and Play act at once; a note starts a free loop on the free clock's nearest bar line; saved free loops open stopped at their tops; and the free clock copied from a song span gives the song's ticks, spans and bar lines bit for bit at four tempos and meters, through a locate and a tempo change. `tests/no_alloc.rs` runs all of it at every period size without allocating.

## Saved with the project

A loop goes into a project as its settings, each layer's mix, and each layer's audio as it plays (through the loop's edits and their seam fades, at gain 1): [`save.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-looper/src/save.rs).

- **Saving.** `Looper::loop_file(n)` gives a `LoopFile` (frames, ticks, origin, level, mute, bars, After recording, Starts (`starts=play`, left out for On the bar), Fade old, and each layer's level, pan, mute, solo and Fade old turn-down) for a loop that is playing with nothing changing its layers (no first pass or overdub being written, merge, fit or load). It allocates the list of layers. `Looper::read_layer(n, k, from, out)` reads a piece of layer `k` without allocating, and gives the loop's version, so a save read across many periods can tell that the layers changed.
- **The files.** `save::to_text` and `save::from_text` are the looper's part (`parts/looper.txt`): `key=value` lines, one a loop and one a layer, with each layer's audio named by its file. Unknown keys and lines are skipped, so a newer build's text still loads. `save::wav` and `save::from_wav` write and read each layer as a 32-bit float stereo WAV, which the sampler reads too.
- **Loading.** `Looper::load(n, file)` puts a saved loop back into an empty loop. It takes its memory at once (refused if there is not enough), sets the settings, and copies the layers in at `LOAD_SPEED` (64 frames for each frame that plays) while the loop stays silent; then the loop fades in, in time with the song, on a frame that does not depend on the period size. Its commands wait while it copies, except Clear (which calls it off and gives the memory back), Mute and the settings. `Status::loading` gives the progress. The `LoopFile` waits in `take_loaded(n)` for the host to drop away from the audio thread. A loaded loop has no history: Undo cannot go back past it.
- **In the app.** `LoopHost::save()` waits until no loop is recording, overdubbing, merging, fitting or loading, has the worker make room for every layer, then reads the layers as they play at `SAVE_SPEED` (64 frames for each frame that plays), with no glitch; a loop that changes while it is read starts the save again (`save_restarts`). `take_saved()` gives the result: the project keeps its text (`save::to_text`, each layer naming its file) and a WAV a layer (`save::wav`). Opening goes back through `save::from_text` and `save::from_wav`, and `LoopHost::load` loads each loop into its place. Nothing here writes a file: the project browser writes the part and the WAVs into the project through its own reviewed save (`gui.rs` `save_project`, each layer named `loop-<id>.wav` for its audio, see [browser.md](browser.md#the-parts)).
- **What saving keeps** (the smart loop end plan's step 0A). Saving is as it was: each layer as it sounds, a first pass's loop point and seam fade included (`tests/bounds.rs` checks the saved frames against the fade worked out by hand). What a first pass kept around its loop, and where its bounds were, are not saved: within a session nothing is lost, saves included, and a reopened loop sounds the same, with `Status::bounds` None, as every edit and the history already are. Keeping a loop's recording and its bounds across reopening comes with the audio tracks thread's shared recordings (its phase 4), not a file format of the looper's own.
- **Open and New.** `LoopHost::clear_all` stops a save under way and queues Clear for every loop (once the command queue has room for all eight). The opened project's loops come from a reader thread (`project::loops_open`) through `LoopHost::open`, and `LoopHost::tend_open`, between periods, loads them once every loop is empty and the clears have applied (`settle`: a load before then would be cleared with them). New project only clears.
- **Free loops** ([below](#free-loops)). A free loop's line says `clock=free` (left out for the song's clock), and the free clock is the part's last line, `free-clock bpm=... beats=... unit=... top=...` (`save::ClockFile`, `Saved::free_clock`; written in full, every bit of the tempo). Opening calls `Looper::open_free_clock` before the free loops load (`load` refuses a free loop with no free clock), so they open stopped at their tops. An older build skips both (unknown words and lines), and its loops open as song loops.

## Latency

The player hears the loop late (the output buffer) and their playing reaches the input late (the input buffer and the converters). `SetLatency(frames)` makes up for both: input that arrives `latency` frames after a moment belongs to that moment, so it is written that much earlier in the loop. The demo checks that the first pass lines up with what was played, to the frame, with 640 frames of latency.

On the MPC One the latency is not measured yet. The playback buffer is 384 frames and the capture buffer was 256 frames while Akai's service ran; the converters add an unknown amount. It will be measured with a cable from an output to an input, and that needs the owner's go (see below).

## The API

```rust
let mut looper = Looper::new(44_100, 60);   // 60 s of stereo for all loops: the only allocation
looper.command(Command::SetLatency(640));   // for every loop
looper.command(Command::Record);            // loop 1 armed: records from the next bar line
// in the audio callback, after the transport:
looper.process(&block, &line_input, &mut mix); // adds every loop to the mix
looper.select_loop(1);                      // the commands after this go to loop 2
looper.command(Command::Record);
let s = looper.status();                    // the selected loop: phase, layers, redo, position, memory, ...
let all = looper.memory();                  // the gauge: used and capacity, all loops
let one = looper.loop_status(0);            // any loop
let wave = looper.layer(0);                 // a layer's audio, in play order, for the waveform: wave[2 * frame + channel]
wave.read(from, &mut frames);               // or many frames at once, a block at a time
let so_far = looper.first_pass(0);          // the first pass as far as it has recorded (Status::recorded frames)
let info = looper.layer_info(1);            // its id, level, pan, mute, solo, audible, recording
looper.layer_command(info.unwrap().id, LayerCommand::Mute(true));
looper.bounce(0, buf);                      // loop 1 out to a pad or a WAV: buf made off the audio thread
if let Some(b) = looper.take_bounce() { /* send b off the audio thread: b.wav(44_100) */ }
looper.bounce_print(0, buf);                // for a print: the layers as heard, without the loop's level
let p = print::print(&bounce, 44_100, print::frames_at(ticks, bpm, 44_100))?; // on a worker
looper.print(0, Some(p));                   // plays while the loop is as it was; take_print(0) to drop the old one
let file = looper.loop_file(0);             // saving: settings and mixes (allocates the list)
looper.read_layer(0, k, from, &mut piece);  // a layer as it plays, a piece at a time
looper.load(2, file);                       // into an empty loop; take_loaded(2) once in
looper.copy_layer(0, id, 1, false);         // a layer of loop 1 moves to loop 2 (true: a copy)
looper.launch(2, false, Some(4 * 3_840));   // loop 3 off from bar 5's line (Loop tracks); None: at once
looper.capture_take(4);                     // what Capture(4) into the selected loop would take now
looper.listen(1, None);                     // stopped: loop 2 plays on its own (Some(id): one layer)
looper.stop_listening();                    // fades out
looper.command(Command::Capture(4));        // the last 4 bars heard become the selected (empty) loop
looper.command(Command::SetClock(Clock::Free)); // the selected (empty) loop keeps time with the free clock
let c = looper.free_clock();                // the free clock while a free loop holds audio: tempo, meter, bar 1, running
looper.command(Command::FreeStop);          // the free loops stop, back to their bar 1 (FreePlay starts them)
```

- `Command`: `Record`, `Overdub`, `Undo`, `Redo`, `Clear` (Undo brings it back), `Forget` (Clear with no undo), `Mute(bool)`, `Solo(bool)`, `SetOverdubs(Overdubs)`, `SetBars(Option<u32>)`, `SetAfter(After)`, `SetFadeOld(f32)` ([How it plays](#how-it-plays)), `SetLatency(u32)`, `SetLevel(f32)` (0 to 2), `Edit(Edit)` ([Edits](#edits)), `Merge` ([Merge](#merge)), `Resample` ([Bounce and resample](#bounce-and-resample)), `Fit` ([Fit](#fit)), `Capture(u32)` and `NewInput` ([Capture](#capture-what-was-heard)), `SetClock(Clock)`, `FreeStop` and `FreePlay` ([Free loops](#free-loops)). Commands apply at the start of the next period.
- `Status` also has `starts_on` ([A beat's warning before the bar](#a-beats-warning-before-the-bar)), `seam` (the seam fade in frames), `merging` (a merge's progress, 0 to 1, while it renders), `resampling` (armed or recording a resample), `after`, `fade_old`, `version` (moves on whenever the layers' sound changes), `printed`, `fitting`, `loading`, `copying`, `capturing` and `clock` (Song or Free, [Free loops](#free-loops): a free loop's `origin`, `ticks`, `starts_on` and `cue` count the free clock's ticks).
- `history()` and `loop_history(n)`: [The history](#the-history).
- `LayerCommand`, sent with a layer's id: `Level(f32)` (0 to 2), `Pan(f32)` (-1 to 1), `Mute(bool)`, `Solo(bool)`, `Delete`. Deleting the overdub being recorded throws it away, as Undo does. A command for a layer that is not there is counted in `Stats::no_layer`.
- `LayerInfo` (`layer_info(k)`, in play order, the first pass first): `id`, `level`, `pan`, `muted`, `solo`, `audible` (not muted, not left out by a solo), `recording` and `faded` (Fade old's turn-down; the layer plays at `level * faded`).
- `Phase`: `Empty`, `Armed`, `Recording`, `Playing`, `Overdubbing`.
- `layer(k)` and `loop_layer(n, k)` give a `LayerView`: index it like the slice it used to be (`wave[2 * frame + channel]`, `wave.len()` samples). It reads through the layer's edits, so frame `f` is what plays at frame `f` (without the seam fades). Frames an overdub did not reach read as silence, so a waveform never shows stale audio from a reused slot. `read(from, out)` gives the same values for many frames at once, copying whole blocks where the layer plays unedited and fully written; a reader of many frames (the waveform) uses it, since indexing frame by frame looks up the block for every sample.
- `first_pass(n)` gives loop `n`'s first pass as far as it has recorded while it records (`Status::recorded` frames, 0 otherwise), so the screen can draw it as it grows; None before its first frame and once it ends.
- **Stopped:** once a loop has faded out after a stop, its position waits at the song position's place in it, so the playhead stops with the transport and shows where it will start again (a second STOP takes the song, and the playhead, back to the start). Before, the position ran on while stopped.
- `Stats` counts what went wrong, never silently: commands that did not fit the queue, overdubs, edits and merges refused, first passes thrown away (the transport jumped during the first pass, STOP came before a 1/16 note of it, or it outgrew the memory).
- The memory is shared by every loop and layer: a 2-bar loop at 120 BPM is 4 s, so 60 s holds a first pass and 14 overdubs, or 8 loops of a first pass and two overdubs at 2 s each. The app sizes it from Prefs "Loop memory" (1, 2, 5, 10 or 20 minutes, 5 by default; `LoopMemory` in `looping.rs`), made once at the start and locked in RAM with the rest of the process, so a new size applies from the next start. `bytes_for(rate, seconds)` gives what a size costs (the sound plus the block tables, about 8.4 KB a block, and the 60 s Capture keeps: 131 MB for 5 minutes at 44.1 kHz, 458 MB for 20). Block numbers are `u16`, so the most a looper holds is 65 535 blocks, about 25 minutes at 44.1 kHz; 20 minutes fits at 48 kHz too. At most 15 layers a loop (16 slots, one kept for a merge). `Status::used` is the memory a loop holds, in whole blocks: its layers and the ones Undo or Redo can bring back. A deleted layer keeps its memory while Undo can bring it back; when the memory is full, a new overdub takes the oldest deleted layer's memory, and the history up to that delete is forgotten.
- `process`, `command`, `select_loop`, `layer_command`, `copy_layer`, `launch`, `history`, `bounce`, `bounce_progress`, `take_bounce`, `print`, `take_print`, `read_layer`, `load` and `take_loaded` never allocate, lock or panic. `Bounce::wav`, `loop_file`, the `print` and `save` modules allocate and belong off the audio thread.

## Cost

Measured with `mpfree-bench --filter looper` on the workstation (128-frame periods at 44.1 kHz, 2902 µs; the looper's budget is 3 % of a period, 87 µs, judged on the worst period on the MPC):

| Case | Before this change (mean / p99) | Now (mean / p99) |
|---|---|---|
| One 2-bar loop, layer after layer overdubbed | 11 to 15 µs / 31 to 45 µs | 7 to 9 µs / 16 to 23 µs |
| 8 one-bar loops of 3 layers, all playing, an overdub on one after another | about 100 µs / 165 to 210 µs (multi-loop build, before the speed-ups) | 29 to 34 µs / 53 to 74 µs (1.8 to 2.5 %) |

The workstation is several times faster than the MPC One, so these are for comparing, not for the budget: the device numbers come from the pad player's status lines and from `mpfree-bench` run on the MPC. Round 7 on the device (the one-loop version before this change): with the pads and three layers, looper mean 1.6 %, p99 3.1 %, worst 5.6 % of a period; with the line input, mean about 1.3 %, p99 4.0 %, worst 10.6 %. Both are over the budget on the worst period, which is why playback got cheaper first.

What made it cheaper, with the output unchanged to the bit (the demo's hash and the period-size test):

- **A still loop runs layer by layer.** While a loop plays at full gain with no writer, fade, mix change or jump, `process` works out each frame's position for a run of up to 128 frames, then adds each layer over the whole run. The sums happen in the same order as frame by frame, so the bits are the same. Anything that moves (a first pass, an overdub, a fade, a mute, a jump at a span's start) runs frame by frame as before.
- **Runs.** A period is cut into runs at bar lines and transport markers, so the loops run one after another inside a run, and each frame's song position is worked out once for all loops.
- **Two walls in `Loop::run`** (smart loop end step 1c, 22 Nov). `still_run` is out of line (`#[inline(never)]`: called once a run, so the call costs nothing that counts), so its layer-by-layer loop keeps its own registers whatever the frame-by-frame path around it grows, and the frame-by-frame path takes the run's ticks and input as slices exactly the run's length, so a frame's index needs no check. Before this, a small change on the frame-by-frame side (1c's overdub handoff) cost the playing cases up to 3 % through register spills alone; after it, against the baseline before step 0C, 8 loops playing are -0.1 %, half edited +0.4 % and the overdubbing case +2.0 % (`instr_gate.py`).
- **No `fmod` per frame.** The loop's position is `(tick - origin) mod length`; `f64::rem_euclid` calls `fmod`, a loop of integer steps in software, which was an eighth of the looper's time with 8 loops. `rem_ticks` gives the same bits with one division (tested against `rem_euclid` on a million values, on x86-64 and ARMv7).
- **A still mix costs nothing.** Each layer's gains are only stepped while one of them is moving.

**Edits and merges.** The workstation's timings drift by a third during the day on the cloud VM, so these were compared by counting instructions (callgrind, 2000 periods of 128 frames, 8 one-bar loops of 3 layers):

| Case | Instructions per period |
|---|---|
| 8 loops, no edits, before the edits existed | 277 000 |
| 8 loops, no edits, with the edits built in | 292 000 (+5 %) |
| 8 loops, half of them edited (a quarter reversed, a quarter trimmed and nudged: two pieces and a seam) | 431 000 |

An edited layer finds its pieces with a cursor that starts from the last one (one compare a frame, not a search). It reads each audio frame once where one frame's second sample is the next one's first, and a frame's place in the audio needs no division. The first version of the edits cost 609 000 for the edited case. A merge adds two frames of every layer per frame while it renders: about as much again as playing the loop, for half its length. The bench has an "8 loops of 3 layers playing, half edited" case.

**History, After recording and Fade old.** The history is a fixed list read by the screen, so it costs nothing while playing. Fade old adds one multiply to each layer's level and, once a pass, one to each frame the overdub writes over; the bench's "8 loops of 3 layers playing" case went from 526.7 to 532.8 million instructions for the whole run (+1.2 %), with the demo's hash unchanged.

**Boundaries.** A first pass now plays a region of the audio it kept, not all of it, so it no longer takes the whole-audio path. It has one of its own: a run that ends before the seam reads straight from the audio at the region's first frame (one add a frame), and only a run that reaches the seam checks each frame. Positions only go forward in a run, so its first and last frames tell whether it reaches the seam. Counted by `tools/ci/instr_gate.py` (instructions per period):

| Case | Before | With boundaries |
|---|---|---|
| 8 loops of 3 layers playing | 294 259 | 297 524 (+1.1 %) |
| 8 loops of 3 layers playing, half edited | 417 853 | 429 335 (+2.7 %) |
| One loop, overdubbing | 26 754 | 27 414 (+2.5 %) |

The first version, which read every first-pass frame through its pieces, cost 12 % more with 8 loops playing.

## In the pad player (round 6)

[`looping.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/looping.rs) runs the looper in the pad player's audio loop: the loop plays into the mixer's Loop track and the line input into its input strip, which is heard while Monitor is on ([`mixing.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/mixing.rs)), and [`looper_page.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/looper_page.rs) is its **Looper** tab ([the guide](../guides/mpfree-app.md#round-6-the-looper-the-synth-and-the-sampler-in-one-pad-player)).

- **What it records.** By default the pads (Jay, 20 Nov: the default must not resample the drum pattern or the master; his rule: instruments generate sound, tracks organize and record performances, the pads are controllers that play instruments). `main.rs` `pads_instrument` is the one place that says which instrument the pads play, as a `looping::Instrument`, never a track: the shared synth while it is chosen for the pads (`App::pads_chose_synth`, #722 and the instrument redesign's step 3), else the selected track's: MIDI out, its own instrument, its kit, or the shared synth. The mixer says where it sounds, separately (`MixHost::instrument_strip`): a track's own instrument in that track's channel, the Synth strip for the shared synth, Drums for kit 1 and a kit no track homes, or the channel of the first track that plays a kit (`TrackKits::homes`, as `Pads` and export's stems route it); None for MIDI out. `MixHost::loop_take` takes it after its fader, in time like a mix. A pattern on another instrument stays out; the instrument's own clips share its channel and go in with it, which the screen's name says ("Pads: what the pads play, and its clips"). Only tagging the voices the pads start can separate them (the design doc's What I play). The send returns are left out (they mix every track's sends). `main.rs` `loop_input` (which the test session runs too) tells the looper the instrument each period (`LoopHost::follow_pads`) and takes the take's source from the mixer. **A take holds its source** (the design review of 20 Nov, point 6): `LoopHost::settle` keeps the source and the instrument a take started with until it ends (a first pass, an overdub, or a Capture waiting: `LoopHost::taking`), whatever Records from is picked meanwhile (`LoopHost::set_source`) or wherever the pads move. A wait is logged once and said on the screen (`looper: this take keeps recording the pads (Kit 1); the master from the next take`), and the Looper tab's chip shows it (`LooperView::taking_from`: "THIS TAKE · NEXT: MASTER"; empty rings' feet and What you played name the take's source, since a loop started then records it too). Then the new source takes over (`looper: records ...`) and sends `Command::NewInput`, so What you played starts again and a Capture never spans two sources. Pads on MIDI out record silence, said once (`looping::PADS_ON_MIDI_OUT`). A source that can't be had (`loop_take`'s errors) is silence, said (`looper: records silence, ... can't be had: ...`), never another sound in its place (the dry engines' stand-in went on 20 Nov, after a review), and `LoopHost::refuse` turns down Record or Overdub that would start a take on it, on the screen. Lost while a take records, it is a gap of silence in the take: counted (`LoopHost::dropouts`, in the looper's stats line), logged and said on the screen (`looper: the take records silence from here, ...`), and its return too (`looper: the take hears ... again`). **Master and the loop's own sound:** the returns and the submixes play into the master, so `loop_take` refuses Master (silence, `MixHost::loop_echo`) while the Loop track sends to a return (`LOOP_SENDS_HEARD`, a send above `SEND_OFF_DB`, -60 dB, the Play page's off), goes to a submix (`LOOP_IN_SUBMIX`) or goes to one that sends to a return, and after each comes down for as long as the effects there ring with it (`LOOP_ECHOES_RING`, `LOOP_SUBMIX_RINGS`): `MixHost::watch_loop_echoes` runs each period after the mixer and counts down each return's and submix's tail, from its inserts' `Node::tail_frames` and latency (`MixHost::rings`; a tail that never ends, a frozen delay, holds until it does). A plugin that reports no tail can't be waited for; a master without the loop (a second pass of the returns) is the full fix, after the device round's CPU numbers. The Play page's off send still sends at -60 dB (about 0.1 %): an echo that small reaches the master. Before 20 Nov Pads was `MixHost::pads_wet`, which `loop_take(None)` still is (with Line in picked, the capture point rests there): it takes the master's input from a capture point (`LOOP_CAPTURE`, before the master's inserts and fader) and subtracts the Loop track, the Audio track and the line input, each from its own capture point after its fader. What is left is every track played, after its inserts and fader, with what the send returns add; never the click (added after the mixer) or the loops. The master's own effects (Perform FX's throws, Master safety) and the Level come after it, as they do for the loop when it plays, so they apply once. The capture points hold the last period (the looper runs before the mixer, whose Loop track it feeds), so the pads come `PERIOD` frames late plus the capture point's latency; `LoopHost::take_pads_as` makes the looper make that up, and the app's `SetLatency` (the line input's) does not reach the pads. For Master, a strip that is sounding but can't be taken out (the Loop or Audio track or the line input going to a submix, or a plugin's latency putting it out of step with the master's input) is silence with a `looper: records silence, the mix can't be had: ...` line, and `looper: records the mix again` when it can again. The Play page's "off" Delay send on the Loop track is -60 dB (about 0.1 %), so at off a loop's echo in the master is that small. Nothing on the MPC is opened. With the line input on, the line input, with the latency estimate below.
- **A mix** (`LoopSource::Mix(StripId)`, the Looper tab's Records from picker, `App::loop_mix`): each period main.rs hands `LoopHost::source`'s strip to `MixHost::loop_take`, which moves `LOOP_CAPTURE` when the choice changes (that period is silence; the next block takes from the new point). The master is the master's input less the Loop track only (its capture point after `LOOP_CAPTURE`), so the audio tracks and the line input stay in and the master's effects and Level still come once, on playback; any other strip, a track or a submix, is its own capture point after its fader, with nothing subtracted, found again each period (`Mixer::set_capture` allocates nothing) so a strip that moves stays the one taken. Never the Loop track, nor a submix the Loop track goes to: both record silence and say why (`looper: records silence, the mix can't be had: ...`, once), as does a strip no longer in the mix, which puts the point back on the master's input. A mix is in time like the pads (latency 0 for `SetLatency`, the capture point's own made up by `take_pads_as`), and Starts: When I play waits for a pad hit. The picker's mixes are `mixing::throw_strips` less the Loop track, asked for when it opens (`FromUi::LoopMixesWanted`, answered by `ToUi::ThrowStrips`); the setting isn't kept (Pads at the next start), and Prefs' Record from says "A mix (Looper tab)" and steps to the pads or the line input.
- **The line input.** `alsa.rs` opens the ACVA card's capture device without waiting (`O_NONBLOCK`) at the settings Akai's app used (stereo S32_LE, 44.1 kHz, 128-frame periods, a 256-frame buffer), starts it, and reads whatever has arrived each period into a FIFO of 8 periods. A period with too little input gets silence and is counted (after the first 4); more than 3 periods waiting are dropped to 2, counted; an overrun prepares and starts it again, counted; more than 3 read errors in a row close it. It is off at every start; the row comes back Off whenever it closes.
- **Monitor** adds the line input to the outputs (about 9 ms late at 3 periods).
- **Latency estimate**: the output buffer plus one input period, `(periods + 1) × 128` frames (512 at 3 periods). The converters add an unknown amount: measure it with a cable from an output to the input and set it in the Looper options.
- **Logging**: a `looper:` line for each press and change (with the song position), `line input:` lines when it opens, closes or fails, and every 30 s `status: looper: ...` with the render time.
- **Dragging a layer** (`LoopAction::CopyLayer`, from the Looper tab's drag and drop): `LoopHost::copy_layer` checks what the engine will accept (same length in ticks, both loops free, room for a layer) and gives the `looper:` line the screen shows, the reason when it refuses ("loop 2 is 2 bars and Layer 2 1 bar: a layer goes only onto a loop of its length"). Undo and Redo of a move say which loop the layer goes back to or comes from. The screen side is in `looper_tab.rs` (the card under the finger, the wheel), `looper_deck.rs` (the deck's loop channels light where the layer can land) and `looper_page.rs` (`drop_spots`, `drop_words`, `sync_drag`). - **The waveform** (`LoopHost::wave_step`, on the audio thread, once a period). The Looper tab draws the selected loop from a `LoopWave` (Play's loop row draws the rings, as the deck does): min, max and RMS of the layers summed and each layer's peak, in 600 columns. It is built a slice a period: `WAVE_BUDGET` (2 048) layer-frames shared by the layers, read with `LayerView::read` and folded into the columns run by run. That is about 6 µs a period on the build workstation, roughly 1 to 2 % of a period on the MPC; a two-bar loop's wave takes a quarter of a second, and a minute of loop under 4 s (one loop filling all 20 minutes of the largest Loop memory: about 80 s, the wave growing as it goes, never costing more a period). It starts again when the loop's length, layers or phase change, or another loop is picked, and every `LOOP_WAVE_PERIODS` while overdubbing. While the selected loop's first pass records, a live wave takes its place: the frames recorded since the last period go into columns drawn against the pass's length (its bars at the tempo, or two bars for a free length, doubling each time the pass gets there), and a wave goes to the screen every 12 periods (about 35 ms) with `recorded` set, so the strip and the tab draw it growing and put the playhead at its end. The waves come from a pool of four made at the start: a wave is filled in place once the screen has let go of it (`Arc::get_mut`), so making one never allocates or frees on the audio thread. If the screen holds all four, the wave waits a period, counted in `describe()` (it should stay 0). Once the selected loop's wave is done, the same budget makes each loop's small wave for its cells on the Clips grid (`CellWaves`, [loop-tracks.md](loop-tracks.md) part 3): 24 columns, each the peak of every layer's frames there, a loop at a time, whenever its frames, layers or `version` change (a loop recording or overdubbing keeps the one it had), into fixed arrays sent with the view (`LooperView::cell_waves`). Before this, the builder indexed every sample of every layer, 8 192 frames a period, and on the MPC that cost more than a period: the device log from the 12 Nov live test shows underruns climbing from 6 to 100 while the looper was in use, which is the choppy, clicking playback heard then.

## The footswitch (Learn's Looper card)

The design's hands-free row ("every looper action learnable over MIDI, for a footswitch"); built with the MIDI thread's points of 18 Nov. The MPC One has no footswitch jack, so this is MIDI only: a footswitch comes in through a controller or a MIDI pedal.

- **The ids:** `Owner::Looper` (`looper.N` in text), its ids in `mpfree_param::looper`, fixed and never renumbered since a rig keeps its footswitches by them: `PICKED_KEY` 0, `PICKED_MUTE` 1, `NEXT_LOOP` 2, `UNDO` 3, `REDO` 4, `CAPTURE` 5, `CLEAR` 6, Loop n's key `0x100 + n` and its mute `0x200 + n` (`key(n)`, `mute(n)`, n from 0). Locks, macros and the mod matrix never target it.
- **Values to acts** (`remote::Remote::looper_write`, with `remote::Now`'s `picked` and `loops_muted` from `desk::looper_now`): a key has both edges, as a pad does: down past the middle it is `LooperAct::Key(n)` (the picked loop's key takes the loop picked then), and `Remote::loop_holds` clears it after `perform_pads::CLEAR_HOLD_S` held, once (the main loop and the smoke session call it each pass, as they do the pads' `Held`); its let-go only ends the hold. A mute is state: on from the middle up, acting only when that differs from where the loop stands, counting a mute asked in the last 0.25 s the looper hasn't taken yet, and set with `LoopAction::Mute(n, on)` (`LoopHost::set_mute`), so two moves before the looper takes the first can't undo each other. Next loop, Undo, Redo, Capture, Clear, Snap and Use my tap fire once on the down edge (`Remote::looper_write`'s held bits are a `u16`, bit `id`). Snap and Use my tap (`LoopAction::Choose`, `LoopHost::choose`, smart loop end step 2d) answer the hint line's question while it asks (Snap anyway, Not now), else go to that side as the loop point view's Snap and Use my tap do (`LoopHost::side`, keep false), and say so when the picked loop has no snap to choose. *Test:* `a_footswitch_snaps_and_puts_the_loop_back_on_your_tap`. A controller pulled out never sends its let-go (`MusicIn::unplugged` lets go of the router's notes only, and a CC has no end), so `MusicIn::take_unplugged` tells the app and `Remote::let_go` drops every held key and press without acting: a footswitch down when its cable comes out clears nothing, and its next press is a press. The transport's held presses and the Clips grid's go with them: `let_go` hands back each transport press still down, which the app lets up through `App::remote_press(id, false)` (a REC or TAP TEMPO held by a pedal comes back up as the MPC's own button would), and a Clips press is forgotten, so the next one fires. *Test:* `an_unplugged_controller_lets_the_transport_and_the_clips_go`.
- **What they do** (`desk::looper_remote`, on every page): a key is `desk::perform_pad(PadAction::LoopKey(n))`, the pad's own path (it picks the loop, arms the loop for REC and takes its next step), with "from a learned control" on its line; Next loop is `LoopAction::Select` of the next loop, round after Loop 8; Capture is `LoopAction::Capture`; Undo, Redo and Clear are `App::looper_tap` of the Looper tab's buttons, on the picked loop.
- **Learning:** the Learn section's Looper card (`learn_section.rs`; `mixer/learn-looper` in the [controls catalogue](../guides/controls.md)). A press is learned as `Mode::Momentary` whatever sends it (`midi_in::learned_mode`), so a footswitch's CC goes down and comes up; a key on a mute is a toggle (`key_toggles`). Looper learns are kept in the rig, as the transport's are. `learn_describe` reads a mute's on or off (`remote::describe_looper`), and `learn_label` names each "Looper · Loop 3 key", "Looper · picked loop's key" and so on.
- **Tests:** `remote::tests` (a key's edges and its hold, the presses once each, a mute set only where the loop isn't, an unplugged footswitch letting go and clearing nothing), `the_loopers_footswitch_is_named_and_read_for_learn`, `smoke::phases::tests::a_footswitch_plays_the_looper_from_any_page` (mpfree-app), `the_loopers_ids_are_fixed_and_each_says_one_thing` (mpfree-param), `a_looper_footswitch_reads_back_from_the_rig` (mpfree-midi), `the_looper_card_arms_learn_on_a_loops_key_and_mute_and_the_presses` (mpfree-midi-ui), and the `mixer-io-learn-looper` golden screen.
