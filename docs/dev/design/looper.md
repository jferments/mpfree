# Looper: audio loops on the transport

**Status:** engine crate (roadmap step T4), 2026-11-11, on main. STOP keeping the first pass and the pads recorded with their effects (16 Nov) have not run on the device yet. The pad player runs it, with a Looper tab and the line input ([below](#in-the-pad-player-round-6)), and round 7 (cc7f06d) ran the one-loop version on the MPC One, from the pads and from the line input (see [Line input](#line-input-on-the-mpc-one) below). Several loops, per-layer mixing, the edits, merge, layers moved between loops and the faster playback described here have not run on the device yet.

Up to 8 stereo loops on the transport, recorded from the line input or the pad player's own sound, playing together and sharing one memory. Each follows the transport's bars, so it stays in time with the clips, the arp and the metronome.

## How it plays

- **The first pass** starts on the next bar line after Record, and ends after the set number of bars (`SetBars`; the engine's own default is 2, the app's is free), or, with free length, on the bar line after Record is pressed again. Its place in the song is a whole number of bars from `origin`.
- **STOP during the first pass keeps it** (Jay, 16 Nov: STOP stops at once and keeps the take). On the transport's `Mark::Stopped`, `Loop::stop_first` ends the pass on whole bars: up to the next bar line, with the take cut where STOP came and silence after it; or, when STOP came within a 1/16 note after a bar line (pressed a moment late), back on that bar line, giving back the blocks recorded past it (`Store::shrink`). The next bar line's frame is the one the transport would have reached at the tempo then (`Span::frame_of`, kept at each bar line), so the loop has the frames a pass ending there would have had. Nothing after STOP is recorded except the input still on its way (`latency`). The take fades out over up to `FADE` frames at the cut, and the loop point's fade from the input just before the first bar line goes over the silence, as at any loop point; the slot's `valid` range then covers the take and that fade, so the silence is never read from stale blocks. `Status::cut` gives the take's frames. No overdub waits for PLAY after it (`After::Overdub` is skipped). STOP before a 1/16 note was played throws the pass away (`Stats::aborted`), as a jump or locate still does.
- **Playback follows the song position.** The loop's position is the song position's place in the loop: `(tick - origin) mod len_ticks`, scaled to frames and read between frames. It stays on the bars through stops, locates and transport loops. At the tempo it was recorded at it plays sample for sample; at another tempo it plays faster or slower, like tape, until a print for that tempo is in ([Prints](#prints-another-tempo-the-same-pitch)): then it keeps its pitch.
- **Overdubs** are layers on top of the first pass, from the moment Overdub is pressed until it is pressed again, over as many passes as you like. Overdub pressed during the first pass starts the overdub exactly where the first pass ends. Undo fades out and removes the newest layer; Redo brings it back, until a new overdub starts.
- **After recording** (`Command::SetAfter`, the Looper tab's setting): `After::Play` (the default) plays the loop when its first pass ends; `After::Overdub` goes straight into an overdub, as if Overdub had been pressed during the first pass. Overdub during the first pass cancels it for that pass. A resample's first pass never goes into an overdub.
- **Fade old** (`Command::SetFadeOld(keep)`, 0 to 1, the default 1 fades nothing): each time the loop comes round while overdubbing, the older layers keep `keep` of their sound (0.88 keeps 88 % a pass), and the layer being recorded keeps that share of its own earlier passes, so old material dies away as new comes in, like a tape loop's feedback. The older layers turn down by a gain of their own, gliding over a few milliseconds at the loop point; their level knobs stay as set, and `LayerInfo::faded` shows how far each is down. Undo of the overdub turns them back up to where they were, and Redo down again. Below -80 dB a layer is silent.
- **Each layer has its own mix.** Level (0 to 2), pan (a balance, -1 to 1), mute and solo. While any layer is soloed, only soloed layers play.
- **Any layer can be deleted**, the first pass too; the last layer stays (Clear removes the loop). A delete fades the layer out, and Undo brings it back in its place. Undo and Redo walk one history of overdubs and deletes, newest first.
- **Clear has an undo** (Looper tab step 3a). Once a cleared loop has faded out, `Loop::read` copies the whole loop (`Store::keep`: its layers, history, settings, `Store::kept[n]`, a preallocated `Loop` a loop) and marks the blocks its slots hold (`kept_mask`); the loop resets. Undo on that empty loop (`Loop::unclear`) puts it all back, with the loop's current latency and launch, and fades in where the song has got to. The kept blocks stay the loop's until it records again (the bar line its first pass starts on), loads or takes a layer, or `Command::Forget` (as Clear, with no undo: `LoopHost::tend_open` uses it for Open and New); when another loop needs memory, `Store::grow` drops the oldest kept loop first (`kept_at`). `Status::used` leaves kept blocks out and `Memory::used` counts them as free (`Store::available`). A clear during an overdub undoes the overdub first (`Loop::undo_open_overdub`), so Undo brings the loop back as it was before it and Redo then brings the overdub: a loop key held to clear started one on its press. `Status::kept` says Undo can bring a loop back.
- **Silent overdubs leave nothing.** An overdub whose input never reached `SILENT` (0.001, -60 dB) and that turned no older layer down (Fade old) is dropped when it closes (`Loop::drop_overdub`): no layer, no history step, its blocks back at once.
- **Overdubs: Add, Replace or Once** (`Command::SetOverdubs`, `Status::overdubs`). Add records until Overdub again. **Replace** (step 3b) records as Add, and its slot keeps its fade-in and fade-out lengths (`Slot::replace`): wherever it was written, the layers before it in the play order are silent, crossfading over those fades, as the complement of its own gain there (`Slot::cover`, read through the Replace layer's view, so it follows the edits). `Loop::ducks` works out each layer's share at a frame, newest first; `sample_at`, `render_layers` (merge, bounce, prints) and `read_layer` (a save: the layers under it are saved as heard, so a loaded loop sounds the same with the Replace as an ordinary layer) use it. A loop with a Replace layer plays frame by frame (`Loop::still` is false). Its strength (`Slot::duck`) moves to 0 a step a frame while the Replace layer is muted or left out by a solo, and fades with it when it is undone or deleted, so the old part fades back in. A second pass replaces its own first, crossfading back into it as it fades out; a Replace of silence stays (it silences those frames), and a copy of a Replace layer to another loop is an ordinary layer. Once punches out by itself after one pass: it closes `latency` frames before its start comes round, so its fade-out (the seam fade, at least a frame, instead of the punch's `FADE`) ends where its fade-in began, and the pass has no seam.
- **Speed** (`Edit::Speed(-1 | 0 | 1)`, step 3c): like tape. It changes only the loop's length in ticks (twice for half speed, half for double; refused if the ticks would not divide or would fall under a 1/16 note), so the same frames play slower or faster against the song position, an octave down or up; the position jumps within the loop and crossfades, as other edits do. The shape keeps the speed shown (`Status::speed`) and `Status::rate`, the octaves the stored audio plays faster than recorded (half speed -1; ½× straight to 2× is two steps). It is an edit: Undo and Redo swap the shape. A rate other than 0 always wants a print (below): the print resamples the bounce by its octaves first (`print::octave`, a 64-tap Kaiser-windowed sinc taken round the loop, so it joins up) and stretches only if the tempo also differs, so the print plays as it is. **Overdub at another rate waits for that print** (`Loop::print_wait`, `Status::overdub_next`; a second press or Undo calls it off), then fits the loop to it and starts, as over a tempo print; the fit sets `rate` to 0 and keeps `speed`, and a speed change after it is relative to the fitted audio. Back at rate 0 with no print playing, a waiting overdub starts at once. Fitting at half speed doubles the frames; in a full memory the new overdub's memory comes from forgetting the oldest history, as ever. A copy into an empty loop and a load take the speed and rate; the save adds `speed=` and `octaves=` to a loop's line when either is not 0.
- **Loop solo** (`Command::Solo(bool)`): while any loop with sound is soloed (`process` works it out each period after the commands), the loops not soloed play silent in their places (`Status::solo_out`), fading as a mute does. Clear lets go of a loop's solo.
- **Layer ids.** A layer keeps its id while it exists, whatever comes and goes around it, so a screen can name it safely.
- **Silent while stopped or counting in.** Overdub pressed while stopped waits for Play. Stopping ends an overdub, with a fade. STOP pressed while already stopped lets go of an overdub waiting for Play and disarms an armed loop, on every loop (`LoopHost::stop_waiting`, the app's `LoopAction::StopWaiting`), so no light is left blinking.

## Several loops

- **8 loops** (`MAX_LOOPS`), each with its own first pass, length, origin, layers, history, level and mute. They all play at once, each on its own bars: a 1-bar loop and a 3-bar loop recorded later both stay on the song's bar lines.
- **The selected loop takes the commands.** `select_loop(n)` is queued with the other commands, in order, so `select_loop(2)` then `Record` arms loop 3 and nothing else. A selection waits behind a command that is waiting for a fade, so no command reaches the wrong loop. `status()`, `layer()` and `layer_info()` show the selected loop; `loop_status(n)`, `loop_layer(n, k)` and `loop_layer_info(n, k)` show any loop. A loop number past the last is counted in `Stats::no_layer`, and the selection stays.
- **One recording at a time.** The line input goes to one loop: when a loop arms, starts an overdub or waits to start one, the others stop recording. An overdub punches out (with its fade); a first pass ends on its next bar line, so a free-length pass keeps what was played; an armed loop disarms. Arming a loop while another records its first pass hands over on the bar line: the first pass ends where the next one starts. Starting an overdub on one loop while another records its first pass lets that pass run to its bar line, so both take the input until then.
- **One memory.** All loops share the memory `Looper::new` asks for, in blocks of 1024 frames (`BLOCK`, 8 KiB of stereo). A layer holds whole blocks: a first pass takes them as it records, an overdub takes a whole pass's worth when it starts. Clear gives a loop's blocks back once it has faded out, and so do Undo and Redo once what they could bring back is gone. The pool is a little larger than the seconds asked for: one block per layer of one loop more, so whole layers fill what the seconds promise.
- **Launches** (for [Loop tracks](loop-tracks.md)). `launch(n, on, at)` turns loop `n` on or off as a clip launches: from the bar line at song tick `at`, on the frame that line falls on, or with `None` at once. An off loop fades out as a mute does and is silent, but its place runs on with the song, so a launch brings it back where it would have been had it never stopped. A launch waits in the loop (`Status::cue`) for its bar line; the bar line handler takes it while the transport plays, on that line or the first one after it if the song jumped past. A new launch replaces one waiting. `Status::on` says whether the loop is launched; a loop starts on, and arming a first pass turns it on. It doesn't wait for the selected loop's fades. A loop number past the last is counted in `Stats::no_layer`.
- **The gauge.** `memory()` gives `used` and `capacity` in frames for all loops together; `Status::used` is what one loop holds, and `Status::capacity` is the shared total. A first pass that outgrows the free memory is thrown away and counted in `Stats::aborted`; an overdub that does not fit is refused and counted in `Stats::refused`. Neither affects the other loops.

## The history

`history()` (or `loop_history(n)`) gives what the Looper tab's undo history shows: `done()`, the steps Undo takes back, oldest first, and `redo()`, the steps Redo brings back, the next one first. Each `Step` is `Overdub(id)`, `Delete(id)`, `Edit(edit)`, `Merge(id)`, `Fit(id)`, `Added(id, from)` or `Moved(id, to)` ([Layers between loops](#layers-between-loops)), with layers named by id as in `LayerInfo`. It holds the last `HISTORY` (64) steps of a loop. The first pass is not a step: Clear takes it away. It is a plain value with no allocation, so the audio loop can send it to the screen.

## Edits

The edits change how a loop plays, never its audio:

| Edit | What it does |
|---|---|
| `Half` | Keeps the first half (down to a 1/16 note). |
| `Double` | Plays the loop twice. Overdubs can then make the two halves differ. |
| `Reverse` | Plays the loop backwards. |
| `Nudge(n)` | Moves the audio by `n` 1/16 notes, later (above 0) or earlier, going round the loop. |
| `Trim { from, to }` | Keeps ticks `from..to` of the loop. That becomes the loop, and it stays where it was in the song (`origin` moves by `from`). |
| `SeamFade(frames)` | The crossfade at every cut the edits make, up to `MAX_SEAM` (8192). 0 is a straight cut; 256 (`FADE`) until changed. |

- **How they work.** Each layer plays its audio through a view (`src/view.rs`): up to 8 pieces of its audio, each forwards or backwards. An edit makes new views for every layer and keeps the old shape (length, place, seam, views) for Undo. So an edit takes no memory and no time, and Undo brings back exactly what was there. Edits go into the same history as overdubs and deletes.
- **The loop around an edit.** Every layer of the loop is edited together. Overdubs recorded after an edit fit the edited loop. An edit, its Undo and its Redo crossfade from the loop before over 256 frames.
- **Seams.** Where playback jumps from one piece of audio to another (a trimmed loop's loop point, a nudge inside a trimmed loop, a halved loop's end), the last `seam` frames of the piece crossfade into the audio just before the next piece, which flows on into it. That is the same trick as the first pass's loop point.
- **When.** Edits apply while the loop plays and nothing records. Otherwise, or when an edit cannot be made (shorter than a 1/16 note, or more than 8 pieces in a view), they are counted in `Stats::refused`. Like Overdub, an edit waits for a fade or an overdub's tail.

## Merge

`Command::Merge` bounces the loop's layers into one layer, as heard: through their edits, at their levels, pans, mutes and solos. The loop's own level and mute stay outside.

- **In the background.** It takes the memory for one layer and renders `MERGE_SPEED` (2) frames for each frame that plays, while the loop plays on unchanged. A 2-bar loop at 120 BPM (4 s) is merged in 2 s. `Status::merging` gives the progress, 0 to 1.
- **Taking over.** The merged layer takes the layers' place on the frame by which it is surely rendered: the period the merge started in, plus half the loop. That frame is the same at any period size. It sounds the same, so the change is instant. The test compares the whole session against one without the merge: the output differs by less than 0.00001.
- **Undo** crossfades back to the layers, in their places with their mixes, and Redo crossfades to the merged layer again. Undo while it renders stops it and gives the memory back.
- **While it renders,** the loop's other commands wait (Record, Overdub, Redo, edits and every layer command), so the layers it reads cannot change under it. Undo, Clear, Mute and the loop's level still apply.
- **Refused** (counted in `Stats::refused`) when the loop is not playing, is recording, has nothing to merge (one plain layer at its own level), or there is no memory for one more layer.
- **Room.** A loop plays at most 15 layers (`MAX_LAYERS` - 1), so a merge always has a slot. When every slot is held (the merged layer, and the layers its Undo would bring back), a new overdub forgets the history up to the oldest delete or merge.

## Bounce and resample

Step (d) of the Looper tab: a loop can leave the looper as audio, for a pad or a WAV file, and a new loop can record the others.

- **Bounce.** `Looper::bounce(n, buf)` renders one pass of loop `n` as heard (its layers through their edits and mixes, at the loop's level, not muted) into `buf`, in the background at `MERGE_SPEED` like a merge. The app makes `buf` off the audio thread with room for the loop (twice its frames, stereo) and moves it in; the looper never grows it, so nothing allocates. `bounce_progress()` gives 0 to 1. When it is done, `take_bounce()` moves the `Bounce` out: `audio`, `frames`, the loop's `ticks` and `origin`, and `complete` (false if Clear stopped it, with silence after what was rendered). Off the audio thread, `Bounce::wav(rate)` makes a 32-bit float stereo WAV, which the sampler's `parse_wav` reads, so the app can put it on a pad or save it with `mpfree-store`.
- **While it renders** the loop plays on, unchanged, and its commands wait (Mute and the loop's level still apply; Clear stops it). One bounce at a time. `bounce` gives `buf` back when the loop is not playing, is recording or merging, `buf` is too small, or a finished bounce has not been taken.
- **Resample.** `Command::Resample` on an empty loop is Record, but the first pass records the other loops as they are heard (after their levels and mutes), sample for sample, instead of the input. There is no latency to make up for, because the audio never leaves the box. The loop then plays and takes overdubs like any other. Resample again during the first pass acts as Record; on a loop that has a first pass it is refused. In the app it is a source, not a button: with the Looper tab's Records from set to Loops (`LoopHost::resample`, `LoopAction::RecordLoops`), Record on an empty loop sends `Command::Resample`, and Record on a loop that plays overdubs from the input as usual.
- **Tests.** A bounce matches the layers as heard, through their mixes and at the loop's level (to 0.000001), and its WAV has the sampler's format (a WAV from `Bounce::wav` was also read back through the sampler's `PreparedSample::parse_wav`, bit for bit, once by hand); a resampled loop matches what the other loops played, sample for sample; a resample runs through the busy three-loop session at every period size; the random-press test bounces and resamples; the no-alloc test bounces again and again, with its buffer going round.

## Layers between loops

`Looper::copy_layer(from, id, to, keep)` gives layer `id` of loop `from` to loop `to`: the Looper tab's drag and drop (layout spec 4h). It is queued with the commands and applies at the start of the next period.

- **Where it can go.** Onto a loop with the same length in ticks that plays with nothing changing its layers (no first pass or overdub being written, merge, fit, load, bounce or fade) and has a free slot (fewer than `MAX_LAYERS` - 1 layers); or into an empty loop (not clearing, loading or taking another layer), which then takes the source loop's frames, ticks and origin. The source loop must play with nothing changing its layers too. Anything else is counted in `Stats::refused`, and a layer that is not there in `Stats::no_layer`. The app checks the same first, so the screen can say why (`LoopHost::copy_layer`, `looper_page::drop_spots`).
- **Copied in the background.** Into a free slot (or, for an empty loop, memory for the source's length, taken at once), at `COPY_SPEED` (64 frames for each frame that plays) while both loops play on unchanged. It reads the layer as it plays, through its loop's edits and seam, at gain 1; the layer's level, pan, mute and Fade old turn-down go with it as settings (not its solo).
- **In time with the song.** The copy's frame 0 is where the source layer is at the target loop's start: `shift = ((target origin - source origin) mod ticks) × source frames / ticks`. A loop recorded at another tempo has another length in frames for the same ticks, so the layer is read at `source frames / target frames` frames a frame, between frames (linear), and lands at the target's length.
- **Joining.** The layer comes in on the frame `start + frames / COPY_SPEED` (rounded up), which does not depend on the period size, fading in over `FADE` frames as an `Added` step in the target's history. Into an empty loop, the loop fades in as a whole and starts playing on that frame. For a move (`keep` false), the source fades the layer out on the same frame as a delete, so the sound is the same throughout (the test holds the biggest step from one frame to the next under 0.0001). A loop's last layer stays, so a move from a one-layer loop is a copy.
- **The history.** The target lists `Step::Added(id, from)`, and for a move the source lists `Step::Moved(id, to)`. While each is the last step of its loop, and both loops play with nothing changing, Undo in either loop takes back both together (the layer fades out of the target and back into the source), and Redo brings both again. Otherwise each is its own loop's step, like an overdub or a delete. Undoing an `Added` that is its loop's only layer clears that loop.
- **While it copies,** the commands that would change either loop's layers wait (Record, Overdub, Redo, edits, merges, layer commands, and Undo on the source). Undo on the target, or Clear on either loop, calls the copy off and gives the memory back; Mute and the loop's level still apply. `load` refuses a loop that is taking a layer. `Status::copying` gives the progress, 0 to 1.
- **Cost.** A copy reads two layer frames and writes one for each of 64 frames a played frame, about as much as a merge for a 32nd of the loop's length. Nothing allocates.

## No clicks

| Where | What the looper does |
|---|---|
| The loop point | The last 256 frames crossfade into the input from just before the first pass, which flows on into the first frame. A sine that does not fit the loop would jump about 0.13 at a raw splice; the test holds it under 0.008. |
| Punch in and out | The overdub writer fades in and out over 256 frames (`FADE`, about 5.8 ms). After a full pass it adds, so the fade-in and the next pass join without a seam. An overdub that takes over from the first pass fades in over 32 frames (`HANDOFF_FADE`), because the input runs on without a break there; that keeps the downbeat's attack. |
| Start, stop, mute, clear | The loop's gain ramps over 256 frames. |
| Undo, redo and delete | The layer's gain ramps over 256 frames. |
| A layer's level, pan, mute and solo | Its left and right gains move one 256th of full scale a frame toward the settings. |
| A jump in the song position | A locate, a transport loop that is not a whole number of loops, or a restart right after a stop: the old place and the new one crossfade over 256 frames. |
| Edits | An edit, its Undo and its Redo crossfade from the loop before over 256 frames. Each cut an edit makes has a seam fade (`SeamFade`). |
| Merge | The merged layer takes over without a fade (it is the same sound). Undo and Redo of a merge crossfade over 256 frames. |
| Resample | The first pass records the other loops after their own fades, and its loop point crossfades like any first pass. |
| Commands during a fade | Record, Overdub, Undo, Redo, edits, merges and Delete that would cut a fade short wait for it (at most 256 frames), in order. Pressing Undo twice removes two layers. A new overdub waits while the last one's tail is written (the latency, then 256 frames), so that tail still fades out. |
| Stale audio | A reused layer slot only plays the frames the new overdub wrote. |

## Prints: another tempo, the same pitch

A loop follows the song position, so at another tempo it would play faster or slower, like tape. A **print** keeps its pitch: one pass of the loop's layers as heard, stretched to the loop's length at the new tempo with `mpfree-stretch` (the sampler's phase vocoder, which keeps hits sharp).

- **Made on a worker.** `Looper::bounce_print(n, buf)` renders the layers as heard, without the loop's own level and mute, at `PRINT_SPEED` (8 frames for each frame that plays, so a 2 s loop is ready in a quarter of a second). `print::PrintJob` (or `print::print`) stretches the `Bounce` to `print::frames_at(ticks, bpm, rate)` frames on a worker thread, in steps. A loop goes round, so the job stretches it with two stretch windows wrapped on before and after and keeps the middle; the print's start is crossfaded over `print::SEAM` (1024) frames from what the stretch made after its end, so it joins up at its loop point with no click.
- **Handed in.** `Looper::print(n, Some(print))` hands it to loop `n` without allocating; it takes over once the print playing (if any) has faded out. It plays in place of the layers, crossfading in and out over `FADE` frames, while the loop is still as it was when bounced: the same `Status::version` (which moves on with every overdub, undo, edit, delete or layer mix change), length in ticks and start. Any change stops it until a new one comes. The print it replaces waits in `take_print(n)` for the host to drop away from the audio thread. `Status::printed` says a print plays; `print_info(n)` gives its frames, ticks and version.
- **Where it plays** is worked out from the song position, as the loop's is: `(tick - origin) mod ticks`, scaled to the print's frames. So it stays on the bars through locates and transport loops, and a jump crossfades as the layers' does.
- **In the app**, `LoopHost` runs a print worker (the `mpfree-print` thread): each period it looks at the tempo, and a playing loop whose length at that tempo is more than a frame off its own (within the stretch's 10x) gets a print made and handed in. A print that comes back for a loop that has changed meanwhile is dropped (counted as "too late"). Buffers are made and dropped on the worker, so the audio loop never allocates. `describe()` counts prints made, too late and failed.

## Fit

Overdubbing over a print would record at the new tempo onto layers made at the old one. So **Overdub (or Record) while a print plays first fits the loop to the tempo**: the print becomes the loop's one layer, at the print's length (the loop is then sample for sample at the new tempo), and the overdub starts on the frame it takes over. The copy runs at `FIT_SPEED` (64 frames for each frame that plays, a plain copy), so the overdub waits about a 64th of the loop; a second press while it copies calls the overdub off. `Command::Fit` fits without overdubbing. Undo takes the fit back as one step (`Step::Fit`): the loop dips out over `FADE` frames, its layers come back as they were, and it fades in again; Redo fits again. `Status::fitting` gives the copy's progress. A fit is refused when no print plays.

## Capture: what was heard

Capture (Looper tab step 4a, `Command::Capture(bars)`) turns the last bars played into a loop, whether Record was pressed or not: [`heard.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-looper/src/heard.rs).

- **The heard ring.** `Heard` keeps the last `HEARD_SECONDS` (30) of the looper's input, whatever the loops do: interleaved stereo, frame `f` at `f % cap`, stamped with the device frame each period starts on (`Block::start_frame`), and copied in before the commands apply. It is made once in `Looper::new` (none when the memory is 0 s), so it never allocates; `bytes_for` counts it (about 10 MB at 44.1 kHz). `NewInput` (the app sends it when Records from changes) forgets what came before: a take never starts before it.
- **Bar lines.** For each span the song plays or records, `Heard::hear` logs the bar lines in it (`Span::lines`): their frame and tick, in a ring of 32, more than the 8 bars of the longest capture needs. A run of lines is one unbroken stretch of song: a stop, a count-in, a tick that does not follow on from the last span's (a locate, a transport loop's jump) or a gap in the frames starts a new run, and a take never spans two. So the bars between two lines are exactly what was heard between them, whatever the tempo did in between.
- **The take.** `Heard::take(end, bars, latency)`: `bars` whole bars ending on bar line `end`, halving (8, 4, 2, 1) until they fit: the start line must be in the same run, the frames must have been heard since `NewInput`, and the ring must still hold all of them, with the copy's time and `SLACK` (8192 frames) to spare. The take is read `latency` frames late, as a first pass is written, so what was played on the bar line lands on the loop's first frame. Its ticks are the lines' difference and its origin the start line's tick, so the loop lands on the song's bars.
- **When.** `Capture` into an empty loop takes the bars up to the bar line just passed. In a bar's last beat (`Heard::in_last_beat`, `Looper::capture_waits`) it waits for the line coming, and takes the bars up to it; if the song stops first, it takes them up to the last line. Refused (counted in `Stats::refused`) into a loop that is not empty or is busy, with no whole bar heard, or without memory for the frames.
- **The copy** reuses a load's machinery: the loop takes its memory at once (dropping kept clears and reclaiming as a recording does), then copies the frames in at `LOAD_SPEED` (64 frames for each frame that plays: 2 bars at 120 BPM in about 60 ms), silent; then it fades in, in time with the song, on a frame that does not depend on the period size (from `max(now, start + frames)`). The loop point fades into the `FADE` frames heard just before the take's start, so it joins up as a first pass does. If the ring has moved past the frames before the copy reads them, it is thrown away and counted in `Stats::aborted`.
- **Calling it off.** Capture again, Undo, Clear or Forget while it waits or copies calls it off and gives the memory back. Other commands to that loop wait until it plays. `Status::capturing` is `Some(0.0)` while it waits for its line, then the copy's progress, 0 to 1.
- **For the screen.** `capture_take(bars)` says what a Capture into the selected loop would take now (none: nothing yet), `heard_lines(out)` gives the newest run's bar lines (tick and frame) and `heard_at()` the frame after the last one heard, for **What you played**.

## When I play: starting on your note

Starts: When I play (Looper tab step 4b, `Command::SetStarts(Starts::Play)`, per loop like After recording): an armed loop waits for your first note instead of the next bar line.

- **The note.** A loop listening (`Status::listening`: armed, `Starts::Play`, not a resample, no note yet) takes its first note one of two ways. By ear (`Command::StartsByEar(true)`, which the app sends while Records from is Line in): the first input sample at or over `PLAY_LEVEL` (-40 dB), checked in the first pass's writer, which sees every input frame while armed, and placed `latency` earlier, when it was played. Told (`Command::Played(frame)`, the app's pad hit): the pads' mix has the song's own sound in it, so a level would start it on the drums; a pad hit is the note, on the frame it was read. Either way the note waits in `Loop::hit` for the next period's start (`Looper::place_hits`, after the commands), so where it starts never depends on the period size.
- **The song playing: the nearest bar line.** `Heard::around(p)` gives the bar lines of the run the song is playing now on either side of the note (a run starts at a stop, a count-in, a locate or a transport loop's jump, as for Capture). A note in the first half of a bar starts the pass on that bar's line; it has passed, so `Loop::begin_first` starts it now with `rec_from` on the line, and the frames from the line to now come in from the heard ring (`Fill`, at `LOAD_SPEED`, the input before the line taken for the loop point as `pre`), while the writer takes the input from now on. A note in the second half is a pickup into the next line: the pass starts on that line (`pending`, placed in `Loop::starts_on` at the line), but records from the note (`rec_from` = the note, `lead` = the frames between), and when it closes, `finish_len` rotates the first layer's view by `lead` (`View::rotate`), so the loop's frame 0 is the line and the pickup plays in the loop's last `lead` frames, leading into bar 1 each time round. The loop point is between the end of the pass and the note, and fades into what came before the note, as any first pass's does. A pickup heard after its line passed (a long period) starts now from the heard ring the same way. A pickup still waiting when the song stops or jumps is forgotten (`Loop::marker`), and the loop listens again.
- **Counting in.** A note during the count-in is a pickup into bar 1 if it is no more than half a bar before it, else it is forgotten when bar 1 comes and the loop listens again.
- **The song stopped.** The loop asks the host to start the song: `Looper::take_start` gives the loop once a note, and the host starts the transport on the next period's first frame (`TimeHost::start_at`, below). The pass starts on bar 1's line, `rec_from` on the note (`starts_on`: the note is the loop's frame 0, so bar 1 is where you started; the frames between come in from the heard ring); a note a quarter of a bar or more before the song started is too stale, and the pass starts on the line instead. If the host can't start the song it calls `Looper::start_refused`, and the loop listens again. A pad hit needs none of that: the host starts the song on the pad's own frame before the transport runs (pads are read at the top of the period), and tells the looper `Played` on that frame, which the song is playing by the time it is placed.
- **Cost.** One comparison a frame while a loop listens by ear, in the writer that already runs for an armed loop; a placement once a note; the fill copies at most half a bar plus a period at `LOAD_SPEED`. `place_hits` allocates nothing (`tests/no_alloc.rs`). The first pass's writer runs every frame, so what it does once a pass stays out of it: with the start's copy of the input before the pass (`keep_pre`) inline, CI's instruction gate measured the bench's overdubbing case 5.9 % above main (26 565 to 28 145 instructions a period); with it out of line, counted with callgrind on a copy of that case, the rise is about 2.5 %, from `place_hits`' check of each loop once a period and the writer's longer path.
- **In the app.** The Looper tab's **Starts** (`leditor/starts`: On the bar, When I play; faint for Records from: Loops, whose tap says why) sends `SetStarts`; `LoopHost::record` keeps `StartsByEar` matching Records from. A listening ring says "Play to start". main.rs's pad paths (the screen's and the hardware's, and the smoke `Session`'s) call `pad_starts_loop` before the pad plays: if a loop listens for the pads (`LoopHost::listens_for_pads`), with the song stopped it starts the song on the coming period's first frame (`start_song_on_note`, refused while an export runs), then sends `Played` on that frame. After `LoopHost::run`, `loop_starts_song` takes a line-input start (`LoopHost::take_start`) and starts the song on the next period's first frame. A take armed and ready (SHIFT + Arm) records from the same frame. A refusal is counted (`LoopHost::starts_refused`, in the status line), logged and said on the screen ("Loop 1 heard you, but the song can't start: ...").

## Tempo from the first loop

Looper tab step 4c (`Command::TempoFromLoop(bool)`, for every loop, off by default; the app sends it on while the Looper tab's MENU **Tempo from the first loop** is on and the song can take a tempo from a loop): with the song stopped, a first loop recorded in your own time sets the tempo.

- **Free.** `Looper::frees(k)`: the setting is on, the loop is on Free length (`bars` None), not a resample, and every other loop is empty (nothing loading, copying in or capturing). Then, with the song stopped at the period's start (`Looper::stopped`), Record on the empty loop (On the bar) starts the first pass at once, on the period's first frame (`Looper::begin_free`, from the Looper-level apply, since there is no bar line to wait for), and a note placed while stopped (When I play, `place_hits`) starts it on the note, the frames since copied in from the heard ring as for 4b. `Loop::begin_free` sets `free`: no origin, no bars, no bar lines; the writer runs as for any first pass (`write_first`), and with no fill the ring the loop point fades into is the input heard just before the start, taken from `Heard`. A loop armed while the song played that then stops keeps waiting for PLAY: only a Record or a note while stopped starts a free pass. `Status::free` says so (also for an empty or listening loop that would record free now), and the screen and the said lines use it.
- **Closing.** Record during a free pass sets `ending` as for any first pass; `Looper::close_free` (each period, after the commands and the placed notes) closes it on the next period's first frame (`end` = the block's end), so the song can start where the loop comes round, once the pass is a bar at `FREE_MAX_BPM` (160): until then it keeps recording and closes as soon as it is. `free_tempo(len, rate, bar)` gives the bars and the tempo: the fewest bars (1, 2, 4 ...) whose tempo `60 rate (bar / PPQ) bars / len` is `FREE_MIN_BPM` (80) or more, which is under 160 once it takes 2. The loop's origin is the start of the bar the song is stopped in (`pos - pos.rem_euclid(bar)`, as `TimeAudio::start_at` locates), `len_ticks` the bars, and `finish_len` closes it as for any first pass (the writer still finishes the latency tail). `Looper::take_tempo` gives the host the loop, the tempo and that frame, once. `close_free` looks at the loops only while a free pass may be recording (`free_open`, set by `begin_free`), so with none it is one check a period: the bench's overdubbing case, counted with callgrind on a copy of it, costs 33 instructions a period more than 4b (0.1 %).
- **The song.** The host starts the song on that frame at that tempo (`TimeHost::start_at(at, Some(bpm), take)`: Locate, `SetTempo`, Play, the take). At that tempo `bars` bars are exactly `len` frames (to 1e-9 of a frame), so the loop plays sample for sample and wants no print (`want`'s one-frame tolerance). The app takes the tempo as it is, not rounded (`App::set_tempo` at the next period, before `follow_tempo`, which `tempo_asked` keeps quiet meanwhile), and a project keeps it exactly: `Meta`'s `tempo = {}` writes the shortest text that reads back as the same `f64`.
- **What stops it.** The song starting under a free pass (`Mark::Started` or `CountIn`: PLAY, or REC counting in) throws the pass away (`aborted`), since the song would play at the old tempo; `Panic` does too, as for any first pass. A start the host can't make (`start_at`'s refusals, an export) is counted (`LoopHost::tempos_refused`, in the status line), logged and said, and the loop plays at the song's tempo when it starts (like tape, until a print is in). The app turns the setting off for the looper while another device's clock leads (`followed_bpm`, `clock_waits`), the song has more than one tempo (`tempo_map().segments().len() > 1`) or an export runs (`tempo_from_loop_allowed`, every period).
- **In the app.** MENU's **Tempo from the first loop** (`Item::LoopTempo`, `Settings::loop_tempo`, kept as `loop-tempo`). main.rs and the smoke `Session` call `LoopHost::set_tempo_from_loop` before `LoopHost::run` and `loop_sets_tempo` after it (the line "looper: Loop 1 is 1 bar at 96.00 BPM: the song starts at that tempo"); `pad_starts_loop` doesn't start the song for a loop that records free. The ring says **Free** and the seconds (**closing** while it waits to be a bar at 160 BPM); the picked loop's line says "recording free · its length sets the tempo".

## Saved with the project

A loop goes into a project as its settings, each layer's mix, and each layer's audio as it plays (through the loop's edits and their seam fades, at gain 1): [`save.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-looper/src/save.rs).

- **Saving.** `Looper::loop_file(n)` gives a `LoopFile` (frames, ticks, origin, level, mute, bars, After recording, Starts (`starts=play`, left out for On the bar), Fade old, and each layer's level, pan, mute, solo and Fade old turn-down) for a loop that is playing with nothing changing its layers (no first pass or overdub being written, merge, fit or load). It allocates the list of layers. `Looper::read_layer(n, k, from, out)` reads a piece of layer `k` without allocating, and gives the loop's version, so a save read across many periods can tell that the layers changed.
- **The files.** `save::to_text` and `save::from_text` are the looper's part (`parts/looper.txt`): `key=value` lines, one a loop and one a layer, with each layer's audio named by its file. Unknown keys and lines are skipped, so a newer build's text still loads. `save::wav` and `save::from_wav` write and read each layer as a 32-bit float stereo WAV, which the sampler reads too.
- **Loading.** `Looper::load(n, file)` puts a saved loop back into an empty loop. It takes its memory at once (refused if there is not enough), sets the settings, and copies the layers in at `LOAD_SPEED` (64 frames for each frame that plays) while the loop stays silent; then the loop fades in, in time with the song, on a frame that does not depend on the period size. Its commands wait while it copies, except Clear (which calls it off and gives the memory back), Mute and the settings. `Status::loading` gives the progress. The `LoopFile` waits in `take_loaded(n)` for the host to drop away from the audio thread. A loaded loop has no history: Undo cannot go back past it.
- **In the app.** `LoopHost::save()` waits until no loop is recording, overdubbing, merging, fitting or loading, has the worker make room for every layer, then reads the layers as they play at `SAVE_SPEED` (64 frames for each frame that plays), with no glitch; a loop that changes while it is read starts the save again (`save_restarts`). `take_saved()` gives the result: the project keeps its text (`save::to_text`, each layer naming its file) and a WAV a layer (`save::wav`). Opening goes back through `save::from_text` and `save::from_wav`, and `LoopHost::load` loads each loop into its place. Nothing here writes a file: the project browser writes the part and the WAVs into the project through its own reviewed save (`gui.rs` `save_project`, each layer named `loop-<id>.wav` for its audio, see [browser.md](browser.md#the-parts)).
- **Open and New.** `LoopHost::clear_all` stops a save under way and queues Clear for every loop (once the command queue has room for all eight). The opened project's loops come from a reader thread (`project::loops_open`) through `LoopHost::open`, and `LoopHost::tend_open`, between periods, loads them once every loop is empty and the clears have applied (`settle`: a load before then would be cleared with them). New project only clears.

## Latency

The player hears the loop late (the output buffer) and their playing reaches the input late (the input buffer and the converters). `SetLatency(frames)` makes up for both: input that arrives `latency` frames after a moment belongs to that moment, so it is written that much earlier in the loop. The demo checks that the first pass lines up with what was played, to the frame, with 640 frames of latency.

On the MPC One the latency is not measured yet. The playback buffer is 384 frames and the capture buffer was 256 frames while Akai's service ran; the converters add an unknown amount. It will be measured with a cable from an output to an input, and that needs the owner's go (see below).

## The API

```rust
let mut looper = Looper::new(44_100, 60);   // 60 s of stereo for all loops: the only allocation
looper.command(Command::SetLatency(640));   // for every loop
looper.command(Command::Record);            // loop 1 armed: records from the next bar line
// in the audio callback, after the transport:
looper.process(&block, &line_input, &mut mix); // adds every loop to the mix
looper.select_loop(1);                      // the commands after this go to loop 2
looper.command(Command::Record);
let s = looper.status();                    // the selected loop: phase, layers, redo, position, memory, ...
let all = looper.memory();                  // the gauge: used and capacity, all loops
let one = looper.loop_status(0);            // any loop
let wave = looper.layer(0);                 // a layer's audio, in play order, for the waveform: wave[2 * frame + channel]
wave.read(from, &mut frames);               // or many frames at once, a block at a time
let so_far = looper.first_pass(0);          // the first pass as far as it has recorded (Status::recorded frames)
let info = looper.layer_info(1);            // its id, level, pan, mute, solo, audible, recording
looper.layer_command(info.unwrap().id, LayerCommand::Mute(true));
looper.bounce(0, buf);                      // loop 1 out to a pad or a WAV: buf made off the audio thread
if let Some(b) = looper.take_bounce() { /* send b off the audio thread: b.wav(44_100) */ }
looper.bounce_print(0, buf);                // for a print: the layers as heard, without the loop's level
let p = print::print(&bounce, 44_100, print::frames_at(ticks, bpm, 44_100))?; // on a worker
looper.print(0, Some(p));                   // plays while the loop is as it was; take_print(0) to drop the old one
let file = looper.loop_file(0);             // saving: settings and mixes (allocates the list)
looper.read_layer(0, k, from, &mut piece);  // a layer as it plays, a piece at a time
looper.load(2, file);                       // into an empty loop; take_loaded(2) once in
looper.copy_layer(0, id, 1, false);         // a layer of loop 1 moves to loop 2 (true: a copy)
looper.launch(2, false, Some(4 * 3_840));   // loop 3 off from bar 5's line (Loop tracks); None: at once
looper.capture_take(4);                     // what Capture(4) into the selected loop would take now
looper.command(Command::Capture(4));        // the last 4 bars heard become the selected (empty) loop
```

- `Command`: `Record`, `Overdub`, `Undo`, `Redo`, `Clear` (Undo brings it back), `Forget` (Clear with no undo), `Mute(bool)`, `Solo(bool)`, `SetOverdubs(Overdubs)`, `SetBars(Option<u32>)`, `SetAfter(After)`, `SetFadeOld(f32)` ([How it plays](#how-it-plays)), `SetLatency(u32)`, `SetLevel(f32)` (0 to 2), `Edit(Edit)` ([Edits](#edits)), `Merge` ([Merge](#merge)), `Resample` ([Bounce and resample](#bounce-and-resample)), `Fit` ([Fit](#fit)), `Capture(u32)` and `NewInput` ([Capture](#capture-what-was-heard)). Commands apply at the start of the next period.
- `Status` also has `seam` (the seam fade in frames), `merging` (a merge's progress, 0 to 1, while it renders), `resampling` (armed or recording a resample), `after`, `fade_old`, `version` (moves on whenever the layers' sound changes), `printed`, `fitting`, `loading`, `copying` and `capturing`.
- `history()` and `loop_history(n)`: [The history](#the-history).
- `LayerCommand`, sent with a layer's id: `Level(f32)` (0 to 2), `Pan(f32)` (-1 to 1), `Mute(bool)`, `Solo(bool)`, `Delete`. Deleting the overdub being recorded throws it away, as Undo does. A command for a layer that is not there is counted in `Stats::no_layer`.
- `LayerInfo` (`layer_info(k)`, in play order, the first pass first): `id`, `level`, `pan`, `muted`, `solo`, `audible` (not muted, not left out by a solo), `recording` and `faded` (Fade old's turn-down; the layer plays at `level * faded`).
- `Phase`: `Empty`, `Armed`, `Recording`, `Playing`, `Overdubbing`.
- `layer(k)` and `loop_layer(n, k)` give a `LayerView`: index it like the slice it used to be (`wave[2 * frame + channel]`, `wave.len()` samples). It reads through the layer's edits, so frame `f` is what plays at frame `f` (without the seam fades). Frames an overdub did not reach read as silence, so a waveform never shows stale audio from a reused slot. `read(from, out)` gives the same values for many frames at once, copying whole blocks where the layer plays unedited and fully written; a reader of many frames (the waveform) uses it, since indexing frame by frame looks up the block for every sample.
- `first_pass(n)` gives loop `n`'s first pass as far as it has recorded while it records (`Status::recorded` frames, 0 otherwise), so the screen can draw it as it grows; None before its first frame and once it ends.
- **Stopped:** once a loop has faded out after a stop, its position waits at the song position's place in it, so the playhead stops with the transport and shows where it will start again (a second STOP takes the song, and the playhead, back to the start). Before, the position ran on while stopped.
- `Stats` counts what went wrong, never silently: commands that did not fit the queue, overdubs, edits and merges refused, first passes thrown away (the transport jumped during the first pass, STOP came before a 1/16 note of it, or it outgrew the memory).
- The memory is shared by every loop and layer: a 2-bar loop at 120 BPM is 4 s, so 60 s holds a first pass and 14 overdubs, or 8 loops of a first pass and two overdubs at 2 s each. The app sizes it from Prefs "Loop memory" (1, 2, 5, 10 or 20 minutes, 5 by default; `LoopMemory` in `looping.rs`), made once at the start and locked in RAM with the rest of the process, so a new size applies from the next start. `bytes_for(rate, seconds)` gives what a size costs (the sound plus the block tables, about 8.4 KB a block, and the 30 s Capture keeps: 120 MB for 5 minutes at 44.1 kHz, 448 MB for 20). Block numbers are `u16`, so the most a looper holds is 65 535 blocks, about 25 minutes at 44.1 kHz; 20 minutes fits at 48 kHz too. At most 15 layers a loop (16 slots, one kept for a merge). `Status::used` is the memory a loop holds, in whole blocks: its layers and the ones Undo or Redo can bring back. A deleted layer keeps its memory while Undo can bring it back; when the memory is full, a new overdub takes the oldest deleted layer's memory, and the history up to that delete is forgotten.
- `process`, `command`, `select_loop`, `layer_command`, `copy_layer`, `launch`, `history`, `bounce`, `bounce_progress`, `take_bounce`, `print`, `take_print`, `read_layer`, `load` and `take_loaded` never allocate, lock or panic. `Bounce::wav`, `loop_file`, the `print` and `save` modules allocate and belong off the audio thread.

## Cost

Measured with `mpfree-bench --filter looper` on the workstation (128-frame periods at 44.1 kHz, 2902 µs; the looper's budget is 3 % of a period, 87 µs, judged on the worst period on the MPC):

| Case | Before this change (mean / p99) | Now (mean / p99) |
|---|---|---|
| One 2-bar loop, layer after layer overdubbed | 11 to 15 µs / 31 to 45 µs | 7 to 9 µs / 16 to 23 µs |
| 8 one-bar loops of 3 layers, all playing, an overdub on one after another | about 100 µs / 165 to 210 µs (multi-loop build, before the speed-ups) | 29 to 34 µs / 53 to 74 µs (1.8 to 2.5 %) |

The workstation is several times faster than the MPC One, so these are for comparing, not for the budget: the device numbers come from the pad player's status lines and from `mpfree-bench` run on the MPC. Round 7 on the device (the one-loop version before this change): with the pads and three layers, looper mean 1.6 %, p99 3.1 %, worst 5.6 % of a period; with the line input, mean about 1.3 %, p99 4.0 %, worst 10.6 %. Both are over the budget on the worst period, which is why playback got cheaper first.

What made it cheaper, with the output unchanged to the bit (the demo's hash and the period-size test):

- **A still loop runs layer by layer.** While a loop plays at full gain with no writer, fade, mix change or jump, `process` works out each frame's position for a run of up to 128 frames, then adds each layer over the whole run. The sums happen in the same order as frame by frame, so the bits are the same. Anything that moves (a first pass, an overdub, a fade, a mute, a jump at a span's start) runs frame by frame as before.
- **Runs.** A period is cut into runs at bar lines and transport markers, so the loops run one after another inside a run, and each frame's song position is worked out once for all loops.
- **No `fmod` per frame.** The loop's position is `(tick - origin) mod length`; `f64::rem_euclid` calls `fmod`, a loop of integer steps in software, which was an eighth of the looper's time with 8 loops. `rem_ticks` gives the same bits with one division (tested against `rem_euclid` on a million values, on x86-64 and ARMv7).
- **A still mix costs nothing.** Each layer's gains are only stepped while one of them is moving.

**Edits and merges.** The workstation's timings drift by a third during the day on the cloud VM, so these were compared by counting instructions (callgrind, 2000 periods of 128 frames, 8 one-bar loops of 3 layers):

| Case | Instructions per period |
|---|---|
| 8 loops, no edits, before the edits existed | 277 000 |
| 8 loops, no edits, with the edits built in | 292 000 (+5 %) |
| 8 loops, half of them edited (a quarter reversed, a quarter trimmed and nudged: two pieces and a seam) | 431 000 |

An edited layer finds its pieces with a cursor that starts from the last one (one compare a frame, not a search). It reads each audio frame once where one frame's second sample is the next one's first, and a frame's place in the audio needs no division. The first version of the edits cost 609 000 for the edited case. A merge adds two frames of every layer per frame while it renders: about as much again as playing the loop, for half its length. The bench has an "8 loops of 3 layers playing, half edited" case.

**History, After recording and Fade old.** The history is a fixed list read by the screen, so it costs nothing while playing. Fade old adds one multiply to each layer's level and, once a pass, one to each frame the overdub writes over; the bench's "8 loops of 3 layers playing" case went from 526.7 to 532.8 million instructions for the whole run (+1.2 %), with the demo's hash unchanged.

## In the pad player (round 6)

[`looping.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/looping.rs) runs the looper in the pad player's audio loop: the loop plays into the mixer's Loop track and the line input into its input strip, which is heard while Monitor is on ([`mixing.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/mixing.rs)), and [`looper_page.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/looper_page.rs) is its **Looper** tab ([the guide](../guides/mpfree-app.md#round-6-the-looper-the-synth-and-the-sampler-in-one-pad-player)).

- **What it records.** By default the pads and the synth as they are heard, with their effects (Jay, 16 Nov: the final round heard an echo on the drums that the loop left out): `MixHost::pads_wet` takes the master's input from a capture point (`LOOP_CAPTURE`, before the master's inserts and fader) and subtracts the Loop track, the Audio track and the line input, each from its own capture point after its fader. What is left is every track played, after its inserts and fader, with what the send returns add; never the click (added after the mixer) or the loops. The master's own effects (Perform FX's throws, Master safety) and the Level come after it, as they do for the loop when it plays, so they apply once. The capture points hold the last period (the looper runs before the mixer, whose Loop track it feeds), so the pads come `PERIOD` frames late plus the capture point's latency; `LoopHost::take_pads_as` makes the looper make that up, and the app's `SetLatency` (the line input's) does not reach the pads. A strip that is sounding but can't be taken out (it goes to a submix, or a plugin's latency puts it out of step with the master's input) makes the looper record the engines' sound dry, in time, with a `looper: records the pads dry, without their effects: ...` line, and `looper: records the pads with their effects again` when it can again. Known leak: a loop's own sends go into the returns, so a loop sent to the delay is recorded with its echo (the Play page's "off" Delay send is -60 dB in the mixer, about 0.1 %). Nothing on the MPC is opened. With the line input on, the line input, with the latency estimate below.
- **The line input.** `alsa.rs` opens the ACVA card's capture device without waiting (`O_NONBLOCK`) at the settings Akai's app used (stereo S32_LE, 44.1 kHz, 128-frame periods, a 256-frame buffer), starts it, and reads whatever has arrived each period into a FIFO of 8 periods. A period with too little input gets silence and is counted (after the first 4); more than 3 periods waiting are dropped to 2, counted; an overrun prepares and starts it again, counted; more than 3 read errors in a row close it. It is off at every start; the row comes back Off whenever it closes.
- **Monitor** adds the line input to the outputs (about 9 ms late at 3 periods).
- **Latency estimate**: the output buffer plus one input period, `(periods + 1) × 128` frames (512 at 3 periods). The converters add an unknown amount: measure it with a cable from an output to the input and set it in the Looper options.
- **Logging**: a `looper:` line for each press and change (with the song position), `line input:` lines when it opens, closes or fails, and every 30 s `status: looper: ...` with the render time.
- **Dragging a layer** (`LoopAction::CopyLayer`, from the Looper tab's drag and drop): `LoopHost::copy_layer` checks what the engine will accept (same length in ticks, both loops free, room for a layer) and gives the `looper:` line the screen shows, the reason when it refuses ("loop 2 is 2 bars and Layer 2 1 bar: a layer goes only onto a loop of its length"). Undo and Redo of a move say which loop the layer goes back to or comes from. The screen side is in `looper_tab.rs` (the card under the finger, the wheel), `looper_deck.rs` (the deck's loop channels light where the layer can land) and `looper_page.rs` (`drop_spots`, `drop_words`, `sync_drag`). A layer dropped on an empty loop's ring makes it a loop of its own.

## The footswitch (Learn's Looper card)

The design's hands-free row ("every looper action learnable over MIDI, for a footswitch"); built with the MIDI thread's points of 18 Nov. The MPC One has no footswitch jack, so this is MIDI only: a footswitch comes in through a controller or a MIDI pedal.

- **The ids:** `Owner::Looper` (`looper.N` in text), its ids in `mpfree_param::looper`, fixed and never renumbered since a rig keeps its footswitches by them: `PICKED_KEY` 0, `PICKED_MUTE` 1, `NEXT_LOOP` 2, `UNDO` 3, `REDO` 4, `CAPTURE` 5, `CLEAR` 6, Loop n's key `0x100 + n` and its mute `0x200 + n` (`key(n)`, `mute(n)`, n from 0). Locks, macros and the mod matrix never target it.
- **Values to acts** (`remote::Remote::looper_write`, with `remote::Now`'s `picked` and `loops_muted` from `desk::looper_now`): a key has both edges, as a pad does: down past the middle it is `LooperAct::Key(n)` (the picked loop's key takes the loop picked then), and `Remote::loop_holds` clears it after `perform_pads::CLEAR_HOLD_S` held, once (the main loop and the smoke session call it each pass, as they do the pads' `Held`); its let-go only ends the hold. A mute is state: on from the middle up, acting only when that differs from where the loop stands, counting a mute asked in the last 0.25 s the looper hasn't taken yet, and set with `LoopAction::Mute(n, on)` (`LoopHost::set_mute`), so two moves before the looper takes the first can't undo each other. Next loop, Undo, Redo, Capture and Clear fire once on the down edge. A controller pulled out never sends its let-go (`MusicIn::unplugged` lets go of the router's notes only, and a CC has no end), so `MusicIn::take_unplugged` tells the app and `Remote::let_go` drops every held key and press without acting: a footswitch down when its cable comes out clears nothing, and its next press is a press.
- **What they do** (`desk::looper_remote`, on every page): a key is `desk::perform_pad(PadAction::LoopKey(n))`, the pad's own path (it picks the loop, arms the loop for REC and takes its next step), with "from a learned control" on its line; Next loop is `LoopAction::Select` of the next loop, round after Loop 8; Capture is `LoopAction::Capture`; Undo, Redo and Clear are `App::looper_tap` of the Looper tab's buttons, on the picked loop.
- **Learning:** the Learn section's Looper card (`learn_section.rs`; `mixer/learn-looper` in the [controls catalogue](../guides/controls.md)). A press is learned as `Mode::Momentary` whatever sends it (`midi_in::learned_mode`), so a footswitch's CC goes down and comes up; a key on a mute is a toggle (`key_toggles`). Looper learns are kept in the rig, as the transport's are. `learn_describe` reads a mute's on or off (`remote::describe_looper`), and `learn_label` names each "Looper · Loop 3 key", "Looper · picked loop's key" and so on.
- **Tests:** `remote::tests` (a key's edges and its hold, the presses once each, a mute set only where the loop isn't, an unplugged footswitch letting go and clearing nothing), `the_loopers_footswitch_is_named_and_read_for_learn`, `smoke::phases::tests::a_footswitch_plays_the_looper_from_any_page` (mpfree-app), `the_loopers_ids_are_fixed_and_each_says_one_thing` (mpfree-param), `a_looper_footswitch_reads_back_from_the_rig` (mpfree-midi), `the_looper_card_arms_learn_on_a_loops_key_and_mute_and_the_presses` (mpfree-midi-ui), and the `mixer-io-learn-looper` golden screen.
