P0's measuring tools are built: the streaming survey (D0) and the read-only stick test (D1), both run on the MPC on 22 Nov ([the numbers](#d1-on-the-mpc-22-nov)), and the test under load (D2b, the owner's ask on 22 Nov: [below](#d2b-the-test-under-load)). Nothing streams or records with it in the app yet: the audio tracks' engine is still the 12 Nov one, switched off. The later phases (P3 to P6) were approved on 2026-11-22 after two reviews, as eight steps; step 1's base, the shared memory check (promises before big loads, a sound file's peak from its header), is built, and the Sample page's file loads use it: see [profiling.md](profiling.md#memory-floor-refuse-a-load-rather-than-be-killed). Step 2's rules are built in the crate: what a sound is and what follows from it, the streaming ledger, the cache made for the musician's budget and the limits ([below](#p3-what-a-sound-is-and-the-streaming-ledger)). So is step 3: files at their own rate, resampled ahead for stems and clips and read band-limited by voices ([below](#p3-files-at-their-own-rate)). Step 5 is built against a pretend card handle: the storage manager, insertion numbers and what happens when the card is pulled ([below](#p4-the-storage-manager)); the real handle is the hardware project's. And step 7: FLAC as a compressed source with its seek index ([below](#p6-flac-as-a-compressed-source)). The Storage kinds of System status and the watch that turns this layer's states into them are built too; the audio tracks' phase 3 connects them ([below](#what-the-musician-is-told-the-storage-kinds)).

**Owner:** the disk streaming thread. What plays and when belongs to the audio tracks ([audio-tracks.md](audio-tracks.md)); this layer makes sure it is in RAM in time. The SD card's switching on and its safety testing (steps S0 and S1) belong to the [SD card plan](../hardware/sd-card-and-internal-drive.md) and the owner's hardware project.

## Goal

Play long audio from disk on a stage without a dropout: backing stems, audio track takes, audio clips, a set list's next song and, later, long samples. **The target:** 16 stereo stems at 24-bit for a 60-minute set while 2 tracks record, with no counted miss and the rendered sound matching a reference, on the owner's stick, inside a RAM budget the musician picks (64, 128 or 256 MB, 128 MB by default).

The order of what matters: recordings are never lost silently, then steady timing, then speed.

## Today, and what a review found

`mpfree-audiotracks` (12 Nov) already streams takes from partition 4 through a RAM cache of 128 KiB float chunks, switched off and never run on the MPC. A review on 22 Nov found seven problems, all confirmed on main at `829e1568`:

1. **The sound thread does disk bookkeeping.** `AudioTrackHost::period()` calls `tend()` ([host.rs](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/host.rs)), whose `tend_disk()` builds file names with `format!` and whose `tend_save()` builds the whole save (`save_file()`) every period while settled.
2. **A short read becomes silence counted as success.** `fill_part` ([lib.rs](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/lib.rs)) zero-pads whatever didn't arrive and marks the chunk clean.
3. **A failed write is only counted.** `write_failed` adds to `write_errors`; the take carries on as if saved.
4. **One serial disk thread.** `serve()` (the old `disk.rs`) handles one job at a time, so reading a whole file or building peaks blocks the playback reads behind it.
5. **Replies are matched by take and chunk.** Take numbers are reused after a project change, so an old reply can land in a new take.
6. **Importing refuses while streaming** (`ImportRefused::Streaming`).
7. **The WAV header and the sync come only at the end.** A cut mid-take leaves a file with no usable header. `finish()` also computes the data size as a `u32`, which overflows after about 3 h 23 min of stereo float.

The cheap fixes go into the old engine with failing tests first (P0.5); the deep ones are designed out of the new layer (P1). After P0.5, findings 2, 3 and 4 and the `u32` length of 7 are fixed in the old engine; 1, 5, 6 and the header and sync of 7 each have an ignored test that fails until P1 ([below](#p05-the-old-engines-fault-fixes)).

## The design

One shared stream layer, a new crate `mpfree-stream` grown out of `mpfree-audiotracks`, plays every long sound from disk. It is foundational, like the mixer, not a feature of audio tracks.

### The sound thread's side

- Each player reads RAM through its own cursor and writes recordings into capture memory. It talks to the rest through fixed-size single-producer, single-consumer queues made at start: it posts where each source is playing and which launches are cued, and takes ready chunks off a queue, copying them into its cache within a fixed budget per period (about 16 KB a period at 16 stems). It handles at most a set number of messages a period. File names, opening files, errors and save data all happen on the coordinator thread.
- **Who owns a chunk.** In P1 the sound thread owns its cache outright and the coordinator owns only buffers in flight, passed by message: no shared memory, safe Rust. Handing cache slots over without a copy comes only if the worst-period tests show the copy matters, and then under written rules: a slot goes Free, Filling, Ready, Published, Retired, Free; a published slot never changes; it is fully written before it is published (release and acquire ordering); a retired slot is reused only after the sound thread has passed a period boundary, because cursors look chunks up afresh each period. No reference counting on the sound thread.
- P1 must show that a source can be unloaded, replaced or invalidated mid-play with no use-after-free, stale or half-written audio, allocation or blocking on the sound thread: stress tests, loom checks of the queue protocol, sanitizer runs on the workstation.

### Sources and requests

- Everything that plays is an **audio source**: an id for this run, a generation, the project's lasting id for it, its format, sample rate, channels, length and where it is stored. Tracks, clips, the sampler and the set list point at sources; none owns a file. Several cursors can read one source at once, sharing its cached chunks. Looping, sample-accurate seeks, start offsets, speed and cancelling are tested abilities of every cursor.
- **Every request says who asked:** the project's generation, the source, the source's generation, a request number, and the frames it expects. A reply for an older generation is dropped. A short read inside the sound is a read error; zeros go only past the sound's real end.

### Memory, heads and the coordinator

- **Memory or disk is decided by budget.** A sound loads into RAM when it fits next to what is playing; otherwise it streams. Kit pads and short drum sounds load whole when they fit; "Keep in memory" pins any sound. No switch, no restart, no fixed length rule.
- **Heads depend on the kind of sound** (table below). When room runs short: playing now and capture memory first, then cued launches, heads of the playing song, then the next song inside its own budget. The layer answers "next song ready", naming any file whose head is missing.
- **The coordinator plans by deadline.** It works out which frames every playing source needs and by when, wrapping round loops and scaling by speed. Playback reads go earliest deadline first. A deadline belongs to the whole job: for a FLAC file or one at another rate the read, the decoding and the conversion each get a share of the time. Decoding has its own bounded queue, apart from background work (peaks, copies).
- **Recording pressure.** A recording's deadline is the moment its capture memory would fill. Below a quarter full, playback reads come first; past half, recording writes move up; past three quarters, background work stops; past nine tenths, the screen warns that recording may stop. These are starting points for the device tests. If the stick stalls longer than any buffer covers, the engine says so.
- **The window comes from measurement, and admission decides what fits.** A stream wants 2 x the stick's measured worst shortfall + 1 s of read-ahead, starting at 8 s and never under 4 s. Each armed recording reserves its data rate times the longest stretch the stick couldn't take writes. The coordinator adds capture reserves, playing audio, heads, cued launches and the wanted read-ahead; if that fits the budget the workload is admitted, otherwise MPFree trims prefetching it doesn't need and says plainly that the workload isn't covered. It never shrinks the margin quietly while calling a set ready. (A stick with a 20 s stall wants 41 s ahead, about 231 MB for 16 float stems: it doesn't qualify for 16 stems in 128 MB.)
- **Small units, joined into bigger reads.** The cache works in about 64 KiB of file; the I/O thread joins neighbouring requests into reads of up to 512 KiB when no other deadline would be missed meanwhile. Files are read in their own format (16 or 24-bit, float); the cache holds float.
- **I/O threads only do I/O,** one per device (the stick, later the SD card, reads only). Decoding, rate conversion, peaks, imports and copies run on worker threads in small steps that yield. The I/O threads start at normal priority on cores 0 to 2; a different priority or core is kept only if D3 shows a better worst period and worst read.

| Kind of sound | What stays in RAM |
|---|---|
| Short drum and kit sounds | The whole sound, when it fits the budget |
| Long one-shots | A short start (tens to hundreds of ms, from the measured stall); the rest streams |
| Keygroup samples | Small starts, inside the instrument's own budget |
| Backing stems | A large read-ahead window, to ride out a stall of several seconds |
| Loops | The start and the next loop point |
| Cued clip launches | Fetched by the launch time |
| The next song | Its own capped budget |

| At 44.1 kHz, stereo | 16-bit | 24-bit | 32-bit float |
|---|---|---|---|
| One stem, from disk | 176 kB/s | 265 kB/s | 353 kB/s |
| 16 stems, from disk | 2.8 MB/s | 4.2 MB/s | 5.6 MB/s |
| A 4-minute song of 16 stems, all in RAM | 677 MB | 1.0 GB | 1.35 GB |

### Recording

- **Recordings survive a cut.** A take is written as `NAME.wav.recording` with its WAV header written at the start. The header carries a reserved `JUNK` chunk that can become RF64's `ds64` (EBU Tech 3306), so a take past 4 GB never moves its audio. Beside it, `NAME.wav.checkpoint` holds two alternating fixed-size records: format version, the recording's id, a sequence number, frames and bytes committed, the audio format, and checksums of the record and of the last committed block. Every few seconds: the new audio is written and synced, then the older record is overwritten with the next sequence number and synced. The folder is synced once both files exist and again after the rename to `NAME.wav` at stop. At the next start MPFree picks the newest valid record whose audio checks out, rebuilds the header from it, and offers to recover the take to there.
- **The exact protocol gets its own short spec page, reviewed before any of it is built** (P1), including what happens after every step that can be cut off.
- Takes record as 32-bit float by default (a take from inside the mix keeps anything over 0 dBFS); 24-bit line takes are an option the device tests can switch on.
- **A failed write has a plan.** Each recording shows one state on its lane: Recording, Saving, Saved, At risk or Stopped. Saved means MPFree finished every sync it needs; whether a stick really keeps synced data through a power cut is a separate fact about that device, shown in its profile once it passes a cut test (D6). Passing errors are retried a few times; a full, pulled or failing stick each get their own message. Unsaved audio stays in capture memory, which playback can never evict.
- **Linux can't pile up a big flush.** MPFree may cap how much unwritten data the stick holds (`strict_limit` and `max_bytes` on its `bdi`, about 32 MB), in the running system only, and only after D4 shows it helps.

### Playing

- **Launches wait together.** PLAY or a locate to a place not in RAM waits up to about a quarter of a second, in the transport, never in the sound thread. If any stem of a launch isn't ready by its boundary, the whole launch moves to the next boundary and the screen says so; it never starts 14 of 16 stems. A launch holds its stems' starts until the new clip has started and the old one faded; for a group every hold succeeds or none does, inside the budget, and holds are released on request.
- **One rule for speed.** Native (read as stored, decoded ahead on a worker), Prepared (a 44.1 kHz copy made ahead for a live set) and RAM. In every mode a cursor moves at (file rate / output rate) x the musical speed: a 48 kHz file at normal speed moves 1.088 file frames per output frame and keeps its length and pitch. Streamed sources use a band-limited read for any rate change (48 to 44.1 kHz is 1.47 semitones, under the sampler's two-semitone line in `mpfree-sampler`'s `bandlimit`). A Prepared copy is offered when a set is checked, never forced; originals never change.
- **Performance snapshots.** Preparing a set records which version of each file it plays (the staged copy, its size and hash); during the show those files are fixed.
- **Linux's file cache** is RAM the kernel can take back, on a 2 GB machine with no swap. Reads stay ordinary buffered reads; D3 measures the cache under memory pressure.
- **Every dropout is shown,** tied to its source: "The stick fell behind: 0.3 s of Drums stem went silent at bar 34." Audio track lanes show their counters and Dev › Disk the numbers. A disk chip in the top bar would change the [layout spec](layout-spec.md), so it is a separate proposal.

### Storage places and files

- A small **storage manager** knows three places: the stick's data partition (on at every start), the SD card (off until asked, read-only first) and the internal drive (off, read-only on a separate explicit ask, never needed for streaming). Opening the SD card browser can never switch on the internal drive.
- **Measured profiles:** each stick and card that passes a device test keeps its result under its own identity ("16 stems and 2 recordings passed, 22 Nov"): what was measured, not that the device is healthy now.
- **Three kinds of file.** Managed: made by MPFree in the project's folder; only these can be cleaned up. External: the SD library or files elsewhere; read and relinked, never deleted. Derived: fitted or converted copies and peaks, disposable, except copies prepared for a set. **Save order:** media written and synced, then the project file through a temp file, sync, rename and folder sync; only then is the save committed and cleanup allowed. A recording finishing is a separate step from a project save.

## The SD card

For streaming the card starts as a **read-only library**: browse it and copy stems onto the stick, which plays them. Partition 4 is ext4, which Windows and macOS can't write, so a computer writes the card and MPFree copies from it; a card pulled mid-song can't stop a stem that plays from the stick.

**Protection comes before the card switches on.** Akai's disk service, `edisksd`, still runs under MPFree's boot: it isn't among the units the stick switches off, and in the 14 Nov stick test it mounted the stick's partitions read-write ([firmware-3.10.1-stick-test.md](../hardware/firmware-3.10.1-stick-test.md)). The order:

1. Check that both the SD controller and the internal drive's controller are off.
2. Keep `edisksd` away from SD disks (switched off on the stick, or a rule it is proven to obey), and put a rule in `/run` that marks every SD disk read-only as it appears.
3. Switch on the SD controller alone.
4. Check the disk hangs off the SD controller (`ff0c0000`), not the internal drive's (`ff0f0000`), whatever its `mmcblk` number.
5. Check the disk and every partition are read-only; record the write and discard counters (`/sys/block/<card>/stat`).
6. Mount the chosen partition `ro,noatime,nosuid,nodev,noexec`, plus `noload` on an ext4 card so a journal is never replayed.
7. Check the mount options and the counters again, then let the library see the card.

Any failed check leaves the card unusable to MPFree and says why. The card has a life cycle (Off, Switching on, Checking, Ready, Fault, Removing); every insertion gets a new number its sources carry, and the card's and the volume's serials are checked so a swapped card is never taken for the first. A pulled card is a lost source: new reads are refused, reads in flight cancelled, its streams go silent and are counted, and nothing needs repairing because nothing was written. The counters are read again when the card is switched off; any change stops reading.

**A copy is a transaction:** read from the card and hashed on the way, written to `NAME.part` on the stick, synced, read back with `O_DIRECT` (dropping pages is only advice) and hashed again, renamed, the folder synced, and only then added to the library. It runs at the lowest priority and pauses when recording pressure rises. It proves the stick returns the right bytes through a fresh read, not that its cache reached flash; that is part of qualifying a device.

**Playing straight from the card comes later,** chosen per set: Performance-ready (the set's files staged on the stick and checked, the default for a gig) or Direct library (stream from a card that passed D5 and has a profile, an explicit opt-in for a live set). Musicians should use an A1 card rated U3 or V30.

### Two kinds of card

MPFree and Akai's system never run at once, so "shared" means taking turns.

| | A card just for MPFree | A card shared with Akai |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Formatted for MPFree (the SD plan's layout C), on a workstation at first | The card as Akai or a computer made it, usually one exFAT partition (layout A) |
| Filesystem | ext4 with a journal (recommended), or exFAT if it must open on a computer | exFAT or FAT32 as it is, never reformatted |
| Reading and streaming | Yes, after gate G and D5 | Yes, read-only, after G and D5 |
| Recording, saving | Yes, once the card passes a write test and a cut test | No: record to the stick; shared-card writing is a separate design (the SD plan's S2) with its own approval |
| A cut while writing | The journal restores the filesystem's records (not the audio); MPFree's checkpoints restore a take to its last checkpoint; whether synced data survived depends on the card | No journal: a cut mid-write can damage records every file shares, Akai's files too |
| When Akai's system sees it | `edisksd` mounts it read-write and runs `tune2fs`: take it out before starting Akai's system, or MPFree treats it as possibly changed and checks it (`e2fsck -n`) next time | Used by Akai as normal; Akai may change or delete files MPFree knows about |

A card just for MPFree holds nothing bootable (whether the MPC's U-Boot looks at the SD card isn't known, [mpc-one-boot-architecture.md](../hardware/mpc-one-boot-architecture.md)), carries a label and a marker file with a random id, and is mounted read-write only after a clean `e2fsck -n` (with `errors=remount-ro`). A shared card stays read-only: its "in use" flag is reported, never cleared; a changed file drops its cached audio outside a prepared set. **Recommendation:** keep a shared card read-only; to record or save to a card, use one just for MPFree, and the strongest gig setup plays stems from one device and records to the other.

**What a qualified workload must show:** the rendered sound matches the reference within a stated tolerance; no underrun; no capture frame lost or overwritten; every checkpoint reported committed comes back after a cut; no period past its deadline (the worst period and the slowest 0.1% reported); the cache and every buffer inside their limits; every error counted and tied to a source or device; no stale audio or invalid access when unloading mid-play; acceptable CPU with the sampler, mixer, screen and effects busy. Each run records its files, rates, stream count, recording format, cache size, filesystem state and device identity.

**The bench gate stays as a tripwire.** CI's 5% gate counts instructions per 128-frame block on the sound path, so moving work off the sound thread lowers it. The main performance measure is the worst period on the MPC, from D3.

## P0: the measuring tools

Both run on the test build (MPFree's system on the stick, port 2222) through [`tools/mpc-one/host_disktest_mpc.py`](../../../tools/mpc-one/host_disktest_mpc.py), which talks only to the newest stick build's host key, so Akai's system is refused before anything runs. Each run on the MPC waits for the owner's go; D0 and D1 ran on 22 Nov ([below](#d1-on-the-mpc-22-nov)).

### D0: the streaming survey

`host_disktest_mpc.py --survey-only` (**read-only**, no opt-in, nothing copied) runs the test build's usual checks, `df -k /run/mpfree-data`, then [`read-only-stream-survey.sh`](../../../tools/mpc-one/read-only-stream-survey.sh): the kernel and its CPU settings, partition 4's mount and free space, the stick's block queue (scheduler, `nr_requests`, request sizes, read-ahead, write cache) and I/O counters, the USB path (speed, the storage driver, `usb-storage` or `uas`, the host controller), the writeback settings (`/proc/sys/vm/dirty_*` and the stick's `bdi`), memory, the USB interrupts with their CPUs and the threads that serve them (policy and priority), the pad player's real-time threads, the CPUs and governors, which file systems and disk modules the kernel has, what else from a disk is mounted, whether `edisksd` runs or is masked, and the kernel's messages about the stick. It reads procfs, sysfs, unit files and the kernel log only; it prints no serial number, MAC or UUID. The other two modes run the same survey in their step 2.

### D1: the read-only stick test

`mpfree-disktest --read-only` ([`src/bin/disktest/read.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/bin/disktest/read.rs)) reads files already on partition 4 and writes nothing:

- **What it reads:** the audio files (WAV, AIFF, FLAC) under `library/` and every file under `backup/` (a creator stick's eMMC backup, one long file), 64 KiB or more, regular files only, never through a link, never off partition 4, at most 5,000 files. Longer files are read as 64 MiB pieces so one file can feed many streams. Only the pieces about to be read are dropped from the cache (`posix_fadvise64 DONTNEED` on their range), so every read reaches the stick.
- **Safety:** on the stick it refuses (exit code 3) unless `/run/mpfree-data` is ext4 labelled `MPFREE-DATA` on partition 4 of a USB disk, mounted `noatime` or read-only (so a read changes no access time), with no MMC device present. Files are opened read-only with `O_NOFOLLOW` and checked to be on partition 4. It prints counts and sizes, never a name, and checks at the end that every file it saw is as it was.

### D2b: the test under load

`mpfree-disktest --under-load` ([`src/bin/disktest/load.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/bin/disktest/load.rs)) asks what D1 can't: how the stick and the processor hold up when playback's streams compete with heavy reads and writes, a busy processor and heavy task switching (the owner's ask, 22 Nov).

- **The steps,** about 45 s each: 2 s for the load to settle, then 8 streams of 24-bit with 128 KiB reads (the engine's chunk) and 24 with 512 KiB reads (its joined reads), 20 s each, reading the library and backup as D1 does; a set that can't keep up is stopped at 60 s and says so (its shortfall is then a floor). A recording runs through every step: 3.1 MB/s (8 stereo float tracks) in 128 KiB blocks to a new file, `fdatasync` every second. What each step adds: (1) nothing; (2) a heavy write, a copy as fast as the stick takes it, `fdatasync` every second; (3) a heavy read of other pieces, 512 KiB at a time, as fast as they come; (4) both; (5) a busy thread pinned to each core, filtering a 256 KiB buffer; (6) heavy task switching: 8 pairs of threads passing a byte back and forth and 32 threads sleeping 200 microseconds at a time; (7) 5 and 6; (8) everything. Every thread is ordinary (`SCHED_OTHER`, nice 0), like the I/O threads' starting place, so the pad player's real-time thread and the interrupt threads still come first.
- **Heat:** the temperature (`/sys/class/thermal`, read-only) is read every half second. The busy and switching threads stop at 75 °C, or at the lowest trip point the kernel gives if lower (trip points under 40 °C are left aside), and the load steps after are left out. On the MPC, with no temperature to read, steps 5 to 8 are left out from the start; on a desktop they run unwatched. It prints each zone's trip points, which nobody has read on the MPC yet.
- **Safety:** everything D1 and D2 refuse, it refuses (exit code 3): partition 4 only, mounted `noatime`, no MMC device, at least 64 MiB free. It writes only `rec.bin` and `copy.bin` in its folder, each new for a step and deleted after it, together at most the write run's room (448 MiB, half the free space); a file that fills is written again from its start. The library and backup are only read, and checked at the end to be as they were. A stop (Ctrl-C, the session closing, an error) stops every thread before the folder is deleted.
- **What it reports,** per step: the CPUs' busy and disk-wait shares and the task switches a second (`/proc/stat`), the temperature and each core's clock, how late a 1 ms sleep wakes, each stream set's read times, worst shortfall and the plan's window, the recording's write and sync times and how far behind it fell, the heavy jobs' speeds and times; then a summary table.
- **On the MPC:** `MPFREE_ACCEPT_DISKTEST=YES python3 tools/mpc-one/host_disktest_mpc.py --under-load` (the program goes to RAM as for D1). About 7 minutes, up to 17 if the stick can't keep up.
- **On a desktop:** `mpfree-disktest --desktop-test DIR --under-load [--quick]`; `MPFREE_DISKTEST_THERMAL=DIR` names a stand-in for `/sys/class/thermal` (desktop tests only).
- **Tests:** `tests/disktest.rs` (a quick run reports every step, leaves nothing and changes no library file; refused off the stick), the unit tests in `load.rs` (the steps, the heat stop, reading the thermal zones and `/proc/stat`), and the host script's tests.

## P0.5: the old engine's fault fixes

The fault tests were written first and failed against the old engine; these changes make them pass. The audio tracks' phase 3a (22 Nov) then removed the old engine's streaming whole, with these tests: its takes record through `mpfree-stream`'s writer ([audio-tracks.md](audio-tracks.md#recording-to-files-phase-3a)), and play from their files in phase 3b, whose first three steps, the engine reading takes through the layer's player, the host running the coordinator and opening the files, and the app's Dev switch, are built, with every region's and the loop's start held in the cache (step 4's first part) ([audio-tracks.md](audio-tracks.md#playing-from-files-phase-3b)). What follows is the record of P0.5.

- **Short reads (finding 2).** A chunk that comes back shorter than the take's audio there is counted (`Stats::short_reads`), never played, and asked for again when next wanted. A failed read is too, as before.
- **Failed writes (finding 3).** A failed write is asked for again (`Io::Rewrite`, always into the take's own file, never a new one, even for chunk 0), and the file finished again once the take is recorded; a failed finish is tried again too. After `WRITE_TRIES` (3) tries, or when a request is lost to a full queue, the take is **at risk**: its unwritten chunks stay dirty in memory, so it still plays and nothing else takes their room.
- **Recording states.** `AudioTracks::save_state(id)`: Recording, Saving, Saved, AtRisk. A fit waits for Saved, because it reads the take's file. The audio tracks rethink will make a save stop with a reason when a take is at risk, rather than wait.
- **Long jobs (finding 4).** The disk thread reads a whole file (a fit, or the waveform table of a saved take with no `.peaks` file) 512 KiB at a time, answering up to 8 other requests between steps, so playback reads and recording writes never wait behind it.
- **The WAV length (finding 7, in part).** A finish whose length doesn't fit a WAV header (`WAV_MAX_DATA`, about 3 hours of stereo at 48 kHz) fails instead of writing a wrong length; the sizes are worked out in 64 bits, so a 32-bit MPC never wraps them.
- **Fault knobs.** `DiskStats` has `fail_writes`, `fail_finishes` and `short_reads` (the next so many of each fail) and counts the long jobs' steps.
- **Tests.** `tests/faults.rs` (a fake disk) and `tests/host_faults.rs` (the real disk thread, its knobs, and a take file cut short under it) check every sample played against what was recorded; the disk thread's unit tests cover the long jobs and the WAV limit. Ignored, each naming its finding, until P1 fixes it: the host's period allocates (1, in `tests/no_alloc.rs`), an old reply lands in a new project's take (5), a sound can't be brought in while streaming (6), a recording's file has no usable header until it ends (7).

## P1: the stream layer

`rust/mpfree-stream`, built a piece at a time. The first part plays files from disk through a cache the sound thread owns, and sounds held whole in memory through the same cursors; recording (after its spec page is reviewed) comes next, and decoding workers with the formats that need them (FLAC, rate conversion). The 12 Nov audio tracks engine is not moved onto the layer: the [audio tracks rethink](audio-tracks.md) builds its tracks and clips on it directly (agreed between the two threads, 22 Nov), so P1 is done when its harness passes against the layer.

- **`format`**: where a WAV, RF64 or AIFF file's audio starts, how long it is and how it is stored (8, 16, 24 or 32-bit, float or double, either byte order), so it is read in its own format and turned into float as it arrives. A file cut short is read to its end and says so; anything it can't stream (more than 2 channels, a rate outside 8 to 192 kHz, a compressed AIFF) is refused with its reason.
- **`msg`**: what goes between the sound thread and the coordinator. Every message says who asked (`Tag`: the project's generation, the source, the source's generation).
- **`player`**: the sound thread's side. `begin` takes at most a set number of messages a period and copies at most a set number of samples into the cache; `read` copies a source's frames out (silence where a unit isn't ready, counted as missed); `playing` and `end` post where each cursor plays once a planning round.
- **`cursor`**: one voice's place in a source: exact seeks, a speed up to 4 file frames an output frame, loops (a cursor before a loop plays into it; one past its end plays on), an end frame (a region that is part of a take: silent from there, not counted, nothing read past it), several cursors on one source. It gathers the frames it will pass round the loop, with the neighbours its read looks at, then reads them: band-limited by default since P3 ([below](#p3-files-at-their-own-rate)), or with 4-point Hermite; at speed 1 from a whole frame, the file's own samples bit for bit.
- **Sounds in memory.** `Coordinator::open_memory` hands a sound held whole in memory (a take just recorded, a short sound) to the sound thread, which reads it straight from there and gives it back when it is closed or replaced; it never touches the disk or the cache.
- **`coord`**: the coordinator, on its own thread. It opens files, gives each source a new generation, plans reads earliest deadline first over a window ahead of every cursor (round its loop once, scaled by its speed), asks an I/O thread that only reads, decodes, and sends fills. A cursor not heard of for two rounds has stopped. A read that fails or comes back short is counted and asked for again; nothing short is sent to be played.

### Who owns a chunk

These are the rules the code keeps, and the tests check:

1. **The sound thread owns its cache outright.** No other thread reads or writes it. There is no shared memory and no unsafe code (`#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`).
2. **The coordinator owns only the buffers in flight.** A buffer goes to the sound thread inside a fill and always comes back (`FromPlayer::Back`), whether the fill was used or refused. Neither side frees a buffer the other made. If the way back is full, the sound thread holds the buffer in a queue made at start, big enough for every buffer there is. A memory sound is shared (`Arc`), but the sound thread never lets go of its share: it sends it back (`FromPlayer::Resident`) on a close, a replace, a new project or a refusal, so the last share is always dropped off the sound thread.
3. **The coordinator chooses every slot, and keeps a mirror of the cache.** Only its own messages change the cache, and the sound thread applies them in the order sent, so the mirror is exact. A slot goes to a unit that is free, else to the one wanted longest ago and not in the last round.
4. **A slot goes Free, Filling, Ready.** A fill marks it Filling and takes it out of what plays at once; the copy can take several periods; only when every sample is in does it become Ready. A read of a Filling slot is silence, counted as missed: never half a unit.
5. **Generations only go up.** A source's new generation (a replace) or a close drops its cached units and any fill being copied, on the sound thread, before anything else is applied. A fill or a reply for an older generation, or an older project, is dropped and counted on whichever side sees it first; both sides check.
6. **Neither side blocks or allocates on the channels.** Both are bounded std channels made at start; the sound thread only uses `try_send` and `try_recv`, and the coordinator only polls, so a send never has to wake a waiting thread.

Handing slots over without the copy (Published and Retired states) comes only if the worst-period tests show the copy matters, under the rules in [The sound thread's side](#the-sound-threads-side).

### The interface for the sampler and the launcher

A player that wants a sound from disk needs four things: `Coordinator::open(source, path)` or `open_memory(source, channels, data)` (each gives the source's `Tag`) and `close(source)` on the coordinator's side; a `Cursor::new(source, id)` made off the sound thread; and `Cursor::play(&mut player, out)` once a period on the sound thread, with `seek`, `set_speed`, `set_loop` and `set_end` between periods. A start partway into a period is a call with the rest of the period's buffer. The sound thread calls `Player::begin` at the start of each period and `Player::end` at its end, once for all the cursors.

An engine built on the layer reuses its tests through the `testkit` feature (`mpfree-stream = { path = "../mpfree-stream", features = ["testkit"] }` in its dev-dependencies): marked sound files and memory sounds, whose every sample says which sound and frame it is, and `Expect`, which checks a period's output against what the sound thread has now.

### Tests

- `tests/format.rs`: every kind of WAV and AIFF built byte by byte and decoded exactly.
- `tests/stream.rs`: every format played back exactly through the coordinator and the cache; loops crossing a unit's edge, seeks into a unit, speeds 0.5, 1.5 and 2 landing on the right frames; two cursors sharing a source; a cold start silent and counted, never wrong; a device that fails, comes up short and answers out of order, never played; and **the unload and replace test**, in one thread (6,000 periods of random replaces, closes, new projects, seeks, loops and moves over a cache too small for what plays) and with real threads (the coordinator and its I/O thread replacing and closing sources while this thread plays). Every sample of every file says which file and frame it is, so each frame played is checked: silence, or exactly the frame it should be of the generation the sound thread has now. Planting either bug (a replace that keeps the old units, or a slot Ready before it is copied) makes both fail.
- `tests/no_alloc.rs`: the sound thread's calls never allocate or free, through a busy run with periods from 1 to 4096 frames.
- The unload and replace test also opens every fourth sound in memory, sets and moves cursors' ends, and holds and releases parts as it goes.

## P1.5: planning reads and admission

In `mpfree-stream`'s coordinator, tested in `tests/plan.rs`:

- **Deadlines.** Each unit a cursor wants has a deadline in seconds: the output frames before it plays, at the cursor's speed, round its loop, over the output's rate (`Plan::rate`). Reads go earliest deadline first.
- **Joined reads.** A read takes the next units of the same file with it, up to 512 KiB (`Plan::join_bytes`), while the time it would take at the device's measured speed, after the reads already queued, stays within half the time left before the next other deadline. A joined read that comes back short keeps the units it brought whole and counts the rest as short.
- **Cancelling.** Each round, a read none of whose units are wanted any more (its cursor jumped, its source was closed or replaced) is marked cancelled, and the I/O thread skips it if it hasn't started; one wanted again before then is unmarked.
- **Holds.** `hold_all(parts, by, tier)` keeps parts of sources in the cache whether or not anything plays them: a start, a loop point, a cued launch, fetched by a deadline or in the background. A group succeeds whole or not at all, and only if it fits beside what plays (at the least safe window), what is reserved and what is held at its tier or above; a refusal says how many units it needed and how many were left. A close, a replace or a new project lets a source's holds go.
- **Who gives way.** Holds have a tier, in the plan's order: Pinned ("Keep in memory"), Cued (a launch's starts), Head (the playing song's starts and loop points), Next (the next song). What plays at the least safe window and capture memory come before every hold. When room runs short (a cursor wants more, a recording reserves more, a higher hold comes in), holds are given back lowest tier first, newest first, and each one given back is reported (`Coordinator::given_back`), so an answer like "next song ready" that counted on it is withdrawn at once, not left standing. Holds made in one `hold_all` call are a group and go back together: half a song's starts is no use to a set list.
- **Promoting.** `promote(ids, tier)` moves holds to another tier, all or none: every one must still be held (else `Refusal::Gone`, nothing changed), and they must fit at the new tier as a new hold would. Moved holds become a group of their own. A song switch moves the next song's holds from Next to Cued once Go is accepted, then to Head once it plays, so a Head hold of the old song is given back before them. `hold_tier(id)` says where a hold is.
- **Whole-file reads.** `read_file(path, tier)` reads a whole file through the device for something other than playing (a kit's sounds, a preset, the next song's files), in steps of at most 512 KiB, one step in flight at a time and only once every playback read wanted is asked for, so a playback read never waits behind more than one step. A step also starts only while playback's cushion is longer than a stall: the cushion is the time until a playing cursor needs a unit not yet in the cache (the window when all of it is in, no limit when nothing plays: the sound thread reports rounds with no cursor, so stopped cursors are let go), and the stall is the longer of `Plan::stall_s` (0.5 s to start) and the slowest of the last 64 reads. On a stick that stalls longer than the window ahead, whole-file reads wait until playback stops (`Coordinator::cushion`, `Coordinator::stall`). Next-song reads go before background ones. Writes (a recording's audio, an autosave at the lowest tier, in the project's save order) join the same queue with P1's recording step; past three quarters of capture memory, background work stops.
- **Launch readiness.** `Coordinator::not_ready(parts)` names the sources of a launch whose part isn't in the cache yet; at the boundary, the sound thread's own answer is `Player::covers(source, from, frames)`. Moving a whole launch to the next boundary is the transport's part.
- **Admission.** Each round the coordinator adds the units the playing cursors want at the wanted window, the units held, and the units reserved for recordings (`set_reserve`), and compares them with the cache. `Admission` says whether that is covered; if not, the window planned with shrinks to what fits (never quietly: `covered` is false), and `safe` says whether that is still at least the least window (4 s). The window comes from the device's measured worst shortfall (`Plan::for_shortfall`: twice it plus one second, never under 4 s). The design's own case is a test: a stick with a 20 s stall wants 41 s ahead, about 3,550 units for 16 stereo stems, so they don't fit 128 MB (2,048 units), and 8 do.
- **Recording pressure.** The recording writer shares how full the fullest take's capture memory is through the coordinator's gate (`Gate::capture_fill`). Below half, playback reads come first (a round waits for playback's cushion, as above); from half, that take's rounds stop waiting; from three quarters, background whole-file reads stop (`STOP_BACKGROUND`) while next-song reads go on; from nine tenths the lane warns (`nearly_full`). Full, recording stops at that frame and the take is finished.
- **Still to come:** deadlines shared between a read and its decoding (with FLAC and rate conversion).

## P3: what a sound is, and the streaming ledger

Step 2 of the later phases, in `mpfree-stream` (`policy`, `ledger`), tested in `tests/policy.rs` and `tests/plan.rs`. Nothing in the app calls it yet: the sampler, the launcher and the audio tracks do as they move onto streaming.

- **What a sound is, apart from what it is doing.** A sound has a class (kit sound, keygroup sample, one-shot, stem, loop, recording), a way it is read (straight, looping, jumping, at a changing speed), the musician's rule (Automatic, Keep in memory, Prefer streaming) and a device (the stick, the card, or already in memory). What it is doing now (playing, cued, the next song, background) stays with the coordinator's hold and job tiers, so a stem doesn't change class when it is cued.
- **Memory or disk** (`place`). A sound already in memory stays there. Keep in memory always asks for memory; when there isn't room, admission says what is missing, and nothing pretends the memory is there. Prefer streaming streams. Under Automatic, kit sounds, keygroup samples, one-shots and loops load whole when they fit beside what plays (the shared memory check says), and stream when they don't; stems and recordings stream.
- **Starts sized to the refill time** (`start_frames`, `Refill`). A sound whose starts come without warning (a pad, a MIDI note, a live keygroup) keeps enough of its start in memory to play through the stick's worst refill: its worst stall (the stick test's measure), plus decoding, plus handing the audio to the sound thread. A 100 ms start doesn't survive an 800 ms stall: at 44.1 kHz that refill takes 37,926 frames. A sound read at a changing speed keeps that much at the fastest a cursor reads (4×). Stems, loops and recordings start at a known time and are fetched before it, by their deadline, so they keep none. A refill with no end can't be covered.
- **Why a workload isn't covered** (`answer`, `Need`, `Cover`), most binding first: memory short, by how much (unload something, prepare less or change the cache size), whether or not the stick was measured; then nothing measured (it plays, shown as untested, with no promise of no dropouts); then a stick too slow for the workload; then a start the stick can't refill in time, which offers Keep in memory for that sound only when the whole sound fits (otherwise the answer is the memory it lacks). A plan is measured only when it comes from the stick test (`Plan::measured`, set by `Plan::for_shortfall`).
- **The streaming ledger** (`Coordinator::ledger`). Every allocation has one owner who charges it, however many engines read it. Streaming charges its cache, its buffers and its whole-file reads (`Ledger::charged`); a sound another engine holds and lends to it (`open_memory`) is shown apart and charged to that engine. Each pool says what it allocated, what is in use, what is promised (holds and a recording's reserve, never more than the cache) and what could really be given back. The cache is made whole at start, so its empty units are room for streaming, not free memory for another engine: emptying them gives nothing back, only remaking the cache does. Capture memory belongs to the recording writer, which reports it beside this.
- **The cache for the musician's budget** (`Config::for_budget`). 64, 128 or 256 MB make that many whole 64 KiB units, at least 16; `Config::cache_bytes` says what the cache takes. The audio tracks' budget setting uses it.
- **Limits, from the sampler's worst cases.** Three limits, kept apart. Registered sounds cost only their details: past 256 (more than the biggest real kit, an auto-sampled keygroup of 61 notes with up to 4 velocity layers) a project is told it has very many, and past 4,096 a registration is refused, saying why (`policy::registry`). Open sources hold a file handle and read state: at most 256 (`MAX_SOURCES`), against the sampler's 65 at worst beside stems, takes and clips. Cursors: at most 256 (`MAX_CURSORS`, down from 512), against the sampler's 130 at worst (64 voices and an audition, each with room for a release fade while a steal starts its next) beside 16 stems, the audio tracks' lanes and the launcher's clips; a cursor past it isn't planned for and is counted (`Stats::over_limit`), never silent. The sampler's numbers are the workstation's until the device round measures them.

## P3: files at their own rate

Step 3 of the later phases, in `mpfree-stream` (`resample`, `cursor::Read`), tested in `tests/rate.rs`. Two ways over the same files:

- **Stems and clips resampled ahead** (`Coordinator::open_at_engine_rate`). A file at another rate plays from engine-rate cache units, made on the coordinator's thread (never the sound thread's) as each unit is read. Each engine frame comes from the file's frames round its exact place with a Kaiser-windowed sinc, 24 zero crossings a side, cut at 0.92 of the lower Nyquist frequency: at 44.1 kHz flat to about 18 kHz (15 kHz within 0.1 dB), and what would fold back is down more than 55 dB (a 26 kHz tone in a 96 kHz file). The kernel is symmetric, so there is no delay to make up: engine frame 0 is file frame 0, and an impulse one second into a 48 kHz file lands exactly one second in. Places are worked out in whole numbers (frame × file rate ÷ engine rate), so ten hours in they haven't drifted. A unit reads the file's frames within the kernel's reach either side of it (`Rate::span`; neighbouring units in one read share them), so each unit comes out bit for bit as one pass over the whole file would make it: the state carries across units by construction, and a seek, a loop or a unit read again changes nothing. A file already at the engine's rate isn't converted. The source's frames, holds, loop points, readiness and cursors all count in engine frames (`Coordinator::frames`), and the cache never mixes them with the file's: a file opened both ways is two sources. Sixteen 48 kHz stems play sample-aligned through starts, a seek and a loop. On the workstation one core converts a 48 kHz stereo stem at about 147 times real time, so 16 stems take about a ninth of it; on the MPC that share is several times bigger, which is why prepared copies (step 4) are the recommended way to play stems at another rate, and why the conversion's cost is one of P5's measurements (a polyphase table for common rate pairs, NEON).
- **Band-limited reads for voices** (`Cursor::set_read`). A cursor reads between frames and at other speeds through a Kaiser-windowed sinc cut at the output's Nyquist frequency, stretched with the speed up to twice (`MAX_STRETCH`), with its weights summed to 1. `Read::Sinc8` (8 crossings a side, the sampler's own clean read, the default) folds a 15 kHz tone read twice as fast back down by about 68 dB, where Hermite lets it all through; `Read::Sinc16` cuts about 88 dB for twice the work; `Read::Hermite` stays as the cheapest. At speed 1 from a whole frame every read copies the file's own samples. Its tables are made when a cursor is made, never on the sound thread.
- **The voice budget.** `mpfree-bench`'s "Stream voices" cases count what the reads cost the sound thread ([profiling.md](profiling.md#mpfree-bench)): 64 band-limited voices at 1.5 times are 18 % of a period on the workstation, against the sampler's 25 % on the MPC, so the sampler sets its read and a fixed voice limit from the device round's measurements (until then, 64 voices and 25 %, provisional). Quality never drops by itself under load.
- **Found on the way:** a unit read and waiting for a buffer was asked for again, and its second fill replaced the copy already playing while it was copied in: a dropout whenever many units were wanted at once (16 stems starting together). A waiting unit isn't asked for again now, a unit already cached isn't filled again (`Stats::duplicates`), and `tests/plan.rs` checks 16 stems read once each.

## P3: prepared copies, snapshots and readiness

Step 4 of the later phases, in `mpfree-stream` (`prepare`, `publish`, `snapshot`, `ready`), tested in `tests/prepare.rs`. These are the engine's parts; the screens that show them are the live shows thread's.

- **Prepared copies, only when needed** (`prepare::target`, `prepare::make`). Any file at the engine's rate plays as it is. Integer audio at another rate becomes 24-bit at the engine's rate with triangular dither, or 32-bit float if its resampled peaks would pass full scale (a full-scale square wave rings past it), so a copy is never clipped. Float stays float, and a set can ask for float copies throughout. Copies are made with the same resampler that plays stems ([above](#p3-files-at-their-own-rate)), a block at a time, in two passes for a 24-bit copy (the peak first).
- **Named by what made it** (`Recipe::name`): the original's content hash (SHA-256) and format, the target rate and format, the resampler's version and quality (`RESAMPLER`) and the dither (`DITHER`). A newer resampler never reuses an older copy. The dither is seeded from the name, so the same recipe makes the same file bit for bit, and a copy already there is used as it is (found by name and size, without another pass).
- **Published whole or not at all** (`publish::publish`). A copy is written to a unique `NAME.<job>.part` (made new, never over a file), synced, read back from the device past the system's cache where the file system allows (`Fs::read_fresh`, `O_DIRECT`) and checked against the hash taken as it was written, renamed into place, and its folder synced. A name already there with the same content is kept; one with other content is refused, never replaced. The tests cut a model stick after every step, every way: the published name is either absent or whole. The card's checked copies (P4) use the same step. `publish::replace` does the same for the few files MPFree keeps up to date itself (a set's snapshot), never for audio.
- **Snapshots of real versions** (`snapshot::prepare`, `Manifest`). Preparing a set hashes its files and writes a manifest naming each by its content. Published files (recordings, prepared copies, checked imports) are referred to where they are, on two promises: MPFree never rewrites a published file in place, and preparing checks each file's hash. Any other file, which MPFree might edit or replace, gets a staged copy in managed storage named by its content; a file at another rate gets its prepared copy instead. Before anything is written, preparing checks the copies fit beside the room kept for recording and saves, and says so plainly when they don't ("not enough room on the stick: the copies need about 900 MB, 700 MB free, 512 MB kept for recording and saves"). A file that changes while it is copied is refused, not staged half-new.
- **Checks.** Opening a set checks every file's size and change time (`Manifest::quick`): nothing is read. A full content check before a show hashes every file (`Manifest::full`) and remembers when it last passed; a file damaged in place with its size and time kept is only caught there. Each problem names the file in the set's own words ("Stem at 48k: missing").
- **Three readiness levels, never one green Ready** (`ready::level`). Files ready: every file present and matching its snapshot, and every start that must be in memory there ("All files ready."). Read-tested: the stick test passed reads like this set's on this stick ("Read test passed on this stick. Recording not tested."). Show-tested: a show test passed a workload covering this set, on this stick, with recording if the set records ("Show test passed on this stick, 22 Nov."). A test covers what it ran, not every set with as many stems (`Workload::covers`): the same stick and MPFree version, a cache no bigger than the set's, and no more streams, FLAC streams, files, bytes, largest file, recordings or sampler voices than it had. Files that changed since put the set back to not ready, whatever was tested.

## P4: the storage manager

Step 5 of the later phases, in `mpfree-stream` (`storage`, and the card's side of `coord`), tested in `tests/storage.rs` against a pretend handle (`testkit::PretendCard`). The real handle comes from the hardware project's storage safety layer ([above](#the-sd-card)).

- **Nothing here can switch the card on** (`storage::CardHandle`). The safety layer alone binds the controller, checks it and mounts the card read-only; when every check passes it hands over a handle: the library's folder and whether the handle still holds. This layer only accepts one. The stick is on at every start; the internal drive is never streamed from. Removing the card revokes the handle at once and for good.
- **Every insertion gets a new number** (`Storage::card_inserted`). What was found on a card (`storage::Located`) carries the insertion it was found on, and holds only while that insertion's handle does. The same card put back is a new insertion: nothing found on the first is read again, and a source opened from it stays lost until it is found and opened again.
- **Paths stay inside the library** (`Storage::locate`). Only a relative path of plain names: no `..`, no absolute path, no drive. Each step on the way is checked without following it, so a link is refused, to a file or to a folder. A sound in memory isn't streamed (`Refusal::NotStreamed`).
- **When the card is pulled.** New reads from it stop at once: the coordinator asks for nothing more for its sources (`Coordinator::open_located`). Reads already running are cancelled, and an answer that comes anyway is dropped and counted (`Stats::card_gone`), so nothing read for an old insertion is used. Audio already in memory plays until it runs out; the frames after it are silent and counted as missed, never refilled from another card. The coordinator reports each sound that lost its source once (`Coordinator::lost`); whether a stem group stops at once, finishes its bar or fades is the clip launcher's and live shows' rule. A whole-file read from the card fails as soon as the card goes, whether it had started or not (`Coordinator::read_located`). Sources on the stick play on without a gap.
- **Tests:** paths that try to leave the library (`..`, absolute, a link to a file or a folder outside), a swapped card (a new number, nothing found on the first opens), the card pulled mid-play with reads made before the pull answered after it (cached audio plays out exactly, no read of the card after the pull, the stick plays on, the sound reported once), and whole-file reads that fail when the card goes. Copies from the card are step 6.

## P4: checked copies

Step 6 of the later phases, in `mpfree-stream` (`cardcopy`, and `publish::publish_as` and `sweep_parts`), tested in `tests/cardcopy.rs`. A file on the card is copied onto the stick, which plays it, so a card pulled mid-song can't stop a stem. The card in the browser, and the screens that start and show copies, are the browser's owner's.

- **The card is read read-only** (`fsio::StdFs::read_only`): files are opened for reading only and every change is refused, as the card is mounted.
- **Each copy is published whole or not at all** (`cardcopy::copy`). It is written to a unique `NAME.<job>.part` (never over a file) and hashed as it goes: the hash of exactly what the card gave. It is synced, read back from the stick past the system's cache (`O_DIRECT`, in 4 KiB-aligned blocks) and checked against that hash, renamed into place and its folder synced. Where the file system doesn't allow a read past the cache, the read goes through it and is counted (`StdFs::fresh_fallbacks`) for the app to show. Either way it proves the stick returns the right bytes, not that its cache reached flash; that is part of qualifying a device.
- **Named by content, in one pass** (`publish::publish_as`, `copy_name`). The name, `<16 hex digits of the hash>-<name>`, is chosen once the content is written, so the card is read once. The same content already copied is used as it is. A newer version of a file on the card is a new copy beside the old one, and the old one stays while anything refers to it; clearing copies nothing refers to is the managed files' cleanup, after a save. A project refers only to the path a finished copy gives (`Checked`), so never to a file before its copy is checked.
- **A card pulled mid-copy stops it** (`Refused::CardGone`), checked before and after every block read, and the part file goes. Nothing is ever resumed: a part file a cut left is removed at start (`sweep_parts`, only `NAME.<digits>.part` names), and the next copy, from any card, starts from the beginning.
- **Room first.** A copy that wouldn't leave the room kept for recording and saves is refused before anything is written ("not enough room on the stick: the copy needs about 2 MB, 402 MB free, 512 MB kept for recording and saves"). If the stick fills anyway, the part file goes and nothing is published.
- **Behind playback and recording** (`cardcopy::Pace`, `background`). A copy asks before each 256 KiB block whether to go on. It waits while a take's capture memory is past three quarters (`STOP_BACKGROUND`, where background reads stop) or playback's cushion is short, and stops when asked. The copy's thread gets the lowest I/O priority in P5's tuning.
- **Tests:** a copy cut after every step, every way (the copy is whole or absent, a part file left is swept, and the copy then runs again whole); the card pulled mid-copy (nothing left, the old find can't copy, a new insertion starts again from the beginning); a nearly full stick (refused before writing; filling mid-copy leaves nothing); a newer version beside the old; waits, stops and the background pace; only part files swept; the read-only layer refusing every change; fresh reads matching, and an unaligned one counted.

## P6: FLAC as a compressed source

Step 7 of the later phases, in `mpfree-stream` (`flac`, and the FLAC source in `coord`), tested in `tests/flac.rs`. The decoder is our own, `mpfree-flac`, which decodes one frame at a time. A WAV unit's place in the file is worked out from its number; a FLAC unit's has to be found, so a FLAC file gets a seek index and its own kind of source.

- **The file's own seek table, only if it checks out** (`flac::Index::from_table`). Every point must land on a frame whose header passes its check (sync code and CRC-8) and starts at the point's sample, from frame 0 to the end, with no gap wider than 2 s (`MAX_GAP_S`): wider would decode too much before each jump (encoders often write a point every 10 s). Only the headers at the points are read.
- **Otherwise an index is built** in the background before the file is offered for jumping around (`Index::build`). The whole file is read once, a chunk at a time (memory stays about 1 MiB). Each frame is found as the next sync whose header passes its check and starts where the last frame ended, a point is kept about every second (`POINT_S`), and the compressed bytes are hashed (SHA-256). The saved index (`to_text`) carries that hash, the index format (`INDEX_FORMAT`) and the indexer's version (`INDEXER`); an index another version made isn't used. FLAC's own checksum isn't enough: it covers the decoded audio, so the same audio encoded with other settings has the same checksum but frames in other places.
- **Opening** (`Coordinator::open_flac`, `open_located_flac`). An ordinary open checks the index against the file's size and change time (`Index::fits`) and refuses one that doesn't fit ("its FLAC index is out of date: build it again"); preparing a set checks the full hash. A FLAC file at another rate is made at the engine's rate as a WAV is ([above](#p3-files-at-their-own-rate)). On the card, the pulled-card rules hold as for any other file.
- **Units decoded off the sound thread.** A unit's bytes run from the index point at or before its first frame to the point after its last (`Index::span`); joined reads share them. The coordinator decodes them on its own thread into the same float units the cache holds (`Index::decode`), so nothing is decoded on the sound thread and cursors play FLAC like any other source.
- **Every jump checked.** The frame a read lands on must be the one the index says (sync code, header checksum, sample number), and every frame after it must start where the last ended and pass its CRC-16. Bytes between frames that aren't a frame are stepped over to the frame that should come. A frame that isn't where the index says, or bytes that run out before the unit's last frame, mean the index is stale; a frame that fails its CRC-16 means the file is damaged there. Either way nothing from it is played: the source stops being read, what is in the cache plays out, the frames after are silent and counted, and the coordinator says so once (`Coordinator::unplayable`, `Stats::flac_failed`). A stale index costs a rescan, never wrong audio.
- **Deadlines count decoding.** Each read's time in the queue counts the decoding it brings as well as the read (`Flight::decode_s`, from the frames between the index point and the unit's end), so joined FLAC reads are kept short enough for the next deadline. Decoding's speed is measured as it goes (`Coordinator::flac_speed`), starting from a cautious 441,000 frames a second (ten times real time at 44.1 kHz) until it is. Set readiness already counts FLAC streams apart from WAV ones ([above](#p3-prepared-copies-snapshots-and-readiness)); the device sessions (P5) measure the MPC's decoding speed.
- **Tests** (marked FLAC files from `testkit::flac`, 24-bit, verbatim and constant subframes, fixed and variable blocks, with or without a seek table): an index built by walking every frame, saved and read back, its hash the file's; a file's own table used only when it checks out (refused for 10 s gaps and for a point inside a frame); 3,000 random jumps decoded exactly in fixed and variable-block files, and the edges; a stale index (the same audio encoded again) caught, never played; a damaged frame an error; FLAC played through the coordinator exactly with jumps and loops; FLAC at 48 kHz playing bit for bit as the same 24-bit WAV at 48 kHz does; a stale index that claims to fit stopping the source with nothing wrong played, and one that doesn't fit refused at the open.

## Growing sources: takes longer than memory

For the audio tracks' phase 3b (takes longer than memory), in `mpfree-stream` (`Coordinator::open_growing`, `Coordinator::grow`, `ToPlayer::Grow`), tested in `tests/grow.rs`. A take records to `NAME_ID.wav.recording`, whose header is plain RIFF with sizes `FFFFFFFF` ([recording-files.md](recording-files.md)), so the layer reads it as it is written.

- **Opened at what is committed** (`open_growing(source, path, frames)`). The source plays only the frames its writer has committed, never what the file happens to hold (a write in progress, or room set aside), and never more than the file holds.
- **Grown as the writer commits** (`grow(source, frames)`): only ever up, under the same generation. Units wholly below the old end stay cached. The unit cut short at the old end is dropped on both sides, from what waits to be filled and from reads in flight (an answer for the old length is dropped and counted as stale), and is read again whole. A frame inside the sound is never played short or silent without being counted.
- **Refused** for fewer frames than the source has, more than the file holds now (checked against its length), and for FLAC, memory and engine-rate sources: resampling near a moving end would be wrong, and takes are at the engine's rate.
- **At finish** the file is renamed in place; the open file follows it, and one last grow gives the take's whole length. The file must never be shortened or rewritten below what it committed while it is open; if that is ever needed, the source is closed and opened again.
- **Tests:** a take opened at its committed frames with half a write beyond, played to its end and never past it; grown with every frame right or silent and counted straight after, the cached units below the old end not read again and the short one read again; refusals; renamed and grown to its whole length; a fill of the short unit on its way when it grows; a read of it answered after the grow, dropped.

## What the musician is told: the Storage kinds

Phase 3 of [System status](status.md): this layer's problems are entries in the catalogue (`rust/mpfree-status/src/kinds.rs`, Storage), agreed with the status thread on 22 Nov. `mpfree_stream::status::Watch` decides when each begins, ends or happens, so every caller says the same thing about the same state, and hands each report to a callback (in the app, `ToUi::Problem`). It allocates nothing once its list has grown to the problems going at once, and does no I/O. Tested in `tests/status.rs` with a writer on the model stick, and through the status board.

| Kind | Level, shape | Fed | Why this level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stick missing | red, stays | `Watch::drive`: the data partition gone or read-only | nothing can be saved or recorded; it hides the saves, takes and room that fail because of it |
| Recording at risk | red, lasts | `Watch::take`: state At risk | a cut now loses everything since the last commit; it hides Not saved yet |
| Recording stopped | red, happens (once per take) | `Watch::take`: state Stopped | capture memory filled: the take stopped where the musician didn't stop it |
| Card removed | red, happens | `Watch::card_lost`: sources `Coordinator::lost` gave | sounds stopped mid-song (one report for the lot) |
| Stick fell behind | red, happens | `Watch::player`: the player's missed frames went up | silence was heard where audio should be |
| Recording may stop | yellow, lasts | `Watch::take`: capture memory nine tenths full while recording or at risk | nothing lost yet; the plan says the screen warns here |
| Not saved yet | yellow, lasts | `Watch::take`: unsaved past twice the interval, not at risk | the stick is slow but saving works |
| Streaming at limit | yellow, lasts | `Watch::admission`: not even the least safe read-ahead fits | MPFree says so rather than quietly read less far ahead |
| Stick nearly full | yellow, lasts | `Watch::drive`: under 1 GB, or under 10 minutes of what is armed | nothing lost yet; ends only once the room is a tenth over the line, so it doesn't come and go |
| Take damaged | yellow, until seen | `Watch::checked`: a whole-take check failed | the audio is kept for the musician to judge |
| Take recovered | note, happens | `Watch::found`: recovery finished a take (`Finished`, `CutOff`) | worth listing for the session; never colours anything |

The words name the stick, or "the stick or SD card" where streaming can be from either. Writer and coordinator counts (late rounds, the interval lengthened, stale and short reads, FLAC failures) stay on the Dev pages.

**Who feeds the watch.** The app doesn't stream or record through this layer yet, so nothing reports these kinds until the audio tracks' phase 3 connects it: each take's view every round on the recording thread, admission and the player's counters on the coordinator's tick, `lost()` as it drains them, recovery's findings and whole-take checks as they finish, and the stick's room from `statvfs` every few seconds off the screen thread (`ST_RDONLY` or a failed call is not writable; free is `f_bavail` x `f_frsize`), because a failing stick can make that call wait. Each kind's entry in the manual's "When something goes wrong" comes with the status page's phase 2.
