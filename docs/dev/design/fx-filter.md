# Filters: design

**Status:** 2026-11-17, after FX quality round 2's Filters PR and its Ladder and Res mode PR, which added the Multi filter's **Model** (Multi or **Ladder**) and **Res mode** (Soft, Hard or Clean: what holds the resonance back). The **Multi filter** (`mpfree.fx.filter`) and the **DJ filter** (`mpfree.fx.dj_filter`) are the Filter family: one effect in the picker with two modes. Their short entries are in [fx.md](fx.md); their measured targets are in [fx-quality.md](fx-quality.md) (ids F1 to F12). The research notes and the plan this page draws on are in jferments/mpfree-claude `fx/quality/` (research-filter.md, plan-2.md section 7). The EQ is built on the same state-variable filter (its design is in fx.md's EQ entry); the Auto wah, the Comb and the XY FX filter are separate engines. From 2026-11-23 this page is also the design page for the **pad filter** (each pad's and keygroup's filter in `mpfree-sampler`), which the filter gap plan brings up to the effect's standard and past it, toward what stock MPC OS 3.10.1 offers: [The pad filter](#the-pad-filter) below.

## 1. What it is for

| Job | Typical move | What matters most |
|---|---|---|
| Sweeps and builds (a low-pass opening over 8 bars, the DJ filter) | One knob or automation across most of the range, some resonance | Smooth at any speed; the level stays sensible through the sweep |
| Acid and synth-style resonance on bass lines and loops | Low-pass 12 or 24 dB, high resonance, cutoff 200 Hz to 3 kHz, moved; often drive into it | A peak that sings without blowing up the level |
| Thinning and band-limiting (radio, phone, a high-pass before a reverb) | High-pass 100 to 500 Hz, band-pass 300 Hz to 3 kHz | Accurate corners and slopes |
| Tone shaping (darker, warmer, less fizz) | A gentle low-pass at 2 to 8 kHz, shelves | A flat passband, nothing added |
| Drive and grit | Drive into a resonant low-pass | Grit without fizz |

The MPC's HP Filter, LP Filter and AIR Filter open as the Multi filter (`mpc.rs`).

## 2. Principles

**The state-variable filter.** Andrew Simper's trapezoidal ("zero-delay feedback") SVF: two integrators in a loop, solved exactly each sample, with the cutoff pre-warped, so the -3 dB point of a Butterworth low-pass is at the cutoff at any frequency below Nyquist and the filter stays stable however fast the cutoff moves (Zavalishin, *The Art of VA Filter Design*). Its band-pass state `v1` is what a resonance builds up: at the cutoff it is Q times the input, and the low-pass and high-pass outputs follow it.

**Resonance in analog filters saturates.** A linear filter's peak grows with Q without limit: at Q 40 a loud sound at the cutoff comes out 32 dB over itself, and a mix-level loop swept through it clips. In an analog filter the resonance loop runs into the limits of its transistors or op-amps, so the peak compresses as the signal grows and the filter rings up fully only on quiet material (the Moog ladder's tanh stages, Huovilainen DAFx 2004). Bounding the loop's state does the same in a digital filter. Some analog filters clip their resonance hard instead, and that is part of their sound: the Korg MS-20's diodes in its resonance loop give it a harder, buzzier scream. And sometimes no ceiling at all is what is wanted: a clean, deep ring that a quiet part can't trip over, the way MPFree's filter was before this round. So the ceiling is a choice, the **Res mode** (Jay's pick on decision 10.4).

**A switch is a jump in the output.** A filter's types share nothing in what they put out at a given moment: a low-pass and a high-pass of the same sound differ by the whole sound, so switching between them on a frame is a step as big as the signal. Crossfading the two outputs over a few milliseconds removes it.

**Drive aliases.** A saturator before the filter makes harmonics; those above Nyquist fold back as inharmonic tones. Running it at twice the rate between half-band filters, with first-order antiderivative antialiasing (ADAA), takes most of them out (the Drive PR's tools, [fx-drive.md](fx-drive.md)).

## 3. Research

The full notes are in research-filter.md. In short: musicians expect the cutoff to mean the frequency, a resonance that sings but doesn't blow up, smooth sweeps and switches, true slopes, clean drive, and on analog-styled filters self-oscillation in tune. Ableton's Auto Filter, FabFilter Volcano, Kilohearts Filter and the analog filters people know (Moog ladder, Oberheim SEM, MS-20, TB-303) were the references. Measured before this round: the cutoffs were exact (0.02 %), the passband flat, Resonance-0 sweeps clean; the resonance linear and unbounded (pink at a mix level through a swept low-pass at full Resonance came out at +2.8 dBFS), a type change clicked (64 dB over the sound) and the drive aliased at -20 dB.

## 4. Our engine

### Multi filter

```
in ─┬─ plain delay (4 frames) ─────────────────────────────┐
    └─ up 2x ─ soft curve, ADAA, flat FIR ─ down ─ (wet)    ├─ blend ─ [one-pole] ─ [plain stages] ─ last one or two stages ─ out
       the half-bands' phase + 2 frames ─ (dry, matched) ───┘
```

**The stages** (since step F4b, 23 Nov: `mpfree-filter` `multi.rs` `SlopeStages`) are the pads' slopes ([Slopes and the Peak](#slopes-and-the-peak-since-step-f4-23-nov)), set from the same plan (`multi::plan`) at the effect's 45 % top (`EFFECT_TOP`): **Slope** 6, 12, 18, 24, 36 or 48 dB an octave (1, 2, 3, 4, 6 or 8 poles). A low-pass or high-pass is Butterworth at Resonance 0 (a one-pole first for 6 and 18 dB), the resonance on the last stage (its Q times the set Q over 0.707), so Resonance 0 is a true Butterworth and the gain at the cutoff is the 12 dB's at every slope; at 6 dB the Resonance is a boost at the cutoff. A band-pass is 12, 24, 36 or 48 dB (6 and 18 play as 12). At 36 and 48 it is the pads' design: broad stages scaled to 1 at the centre, then one resonant stage, so its centre and ring are the 12 dB's. **At 24 dB it keeps its two equal resonant stages**, the effect's extra-peaky band-pass: its centre rings up twice the 12 dB's in dB (29 dB at Resonance 50 %, 64 at full) and its peak is narrower, so it whistles. Jay kept it (24 Nov) for drones, ambient and experimental sounds that want resonance pushed to extremes; the pads' 24 dB band-pass rings as their 12 dB does (F4). The two can't stack further: a 48 dB band-pass built the same way would boost about 128 dB. A notch is 12 or 24 dB (two equal stages; 6 and 18 play as 12, 36 and 48 as 24). Resonance 0 to 100 % maps to Q 0.707 to 40 exponentially. The bell and shelves have one stage at any slope. The 12 and 24 dB filters, every type, are the stages they always were, bit for bit (`the_effects_12_and_24_db_are_its_old_stages_bit_for_bit`).

**Bounded resonance** (Res mode **Soft**, the default). In the resonant stages (the last stage of a low-pass, high-pass or band-pass at any slope, both of a 24 dB band-pass's, and a 6 dB slope's boost) the band-pass state is bent as it is worked out: unchanged up to a knee, then `knee + room · u / (1 + u)` with `u` how far past the knee it is over `room`, so it approaches `knee + room` smoothly and never passes it. The low-pass state integrates the bent band-pass (`v2 = ic2 + g · v1`), so the whole loop is bounded, not just the output. At Resonance 25 % and up the knee is 0.35 and the room 0.3 (full scale 1); below 25 % both grow as 1 over (Resonance / 25 %), so a gentle filter never bends a mix-level sound; at Resonance 0 nothing bends and the stages are the plain linear SVF, bit for bit. The notch, bell and shelves don't ring up at their output and stay linear. A 6 dB slope's boost bends with its knee and room √2 lower, and Hard clips it √2 lower (`SlopeStages`' `hold`), as the pads' guard holds a bell: a boost adds its band-pass state to the sound times up to √2, where a low-pass rings at its output as that state does. The one-pole and the plain stages before the last never ring and stay linear. The result: a -40 dBFS sine at the cutoff still rings up 31 dB at full Resonance (32 dB linear), while pink at -18 dBFS RMS through a swept low-pass at full Resonance peaks at -2.0 dBFS (12 dB) and -3.1 (24 dB), from +2.8 and +1.0.

**Res mode** (Soft, Hard, Clean; Soft by default, so a project saved before it sounds as it did) picks what bounds the resonant stages' band-pass state:

- **Soft** is the bend above, at the session rate.
- **Hard** clips it instead: straight up to three quarters of a ceiling, flat from five quarters, a parabola between (the slope from 1 to 0, no corner, as a diode's edge), the ceiling 0.6 of full scale at Resonance 25 % and up and rising below as Soft's knee does (none at Resonance 0). The low-pass state integrates the clipped value, as with the bend. Because a clip adds more harmonics than the bend, the Hard filter runs at twice the rate between the same half-bands as the drive (its own stages, designed at the doubled rate, which is also what the drawing uses), so a loud sine at a full-Resonance high-pass's cutoff aliases at -82.9 dB where Soft at the session rate is at -57.5. A loud sound's ring is flatter-topped and buzzier, about as loud at its peak (swept pink at full Resonance -1.6 and -1.9 dBFS), and a quiet one rings up fully (32.0 dB), since the clip is straight up to its edge.
- **Clean** has no ceiling: the stages are the plain linear SVF (the knee is infinite), as before this round, so a loud sound at the cutoff rings up the full 32 dB and can go over full scale (swept pink at full Resonance +2.8 and +1.0 dBFS).

Soft and Clean are the same engine: a change between them moves the bend's knee and room smoothly to infinity or back (they are divided by a value that ramps between 1 and 0 over 10 ms), so it never clicks. Hard is a second engine, so a change to or from it crossfades the two over 10 ms; the one coming in starts from the other's band-pass and low-pass states, which are nearly the same at either rate (an SVF's states are those signals themselves), rather than from silence, so a long ring carries on through the change. A type or slope change on Hard crossfades as on Soft.

Chosen while building: bending the band-pass state rather than the input or the output (bending the input leaves the peak unbounded; bending the output flattens the passband of a loud sound and leaves the loop's state to grow); a rational knee rather than `tanh` (no transcendental per sample, and exactly linear below the knee, so quiet material and Resonance 0 are untouched); the knee scaled down below 25 % Resonance rather than one knee for all (a fixed knee bent loud drums through a flat low-pass).

**Found in step F2b (23 Nov), not changed yet:** the bend inside the loop (and Hard's clip, which works the same way) moves a loud, lopsided wave off centre, because the low-pass state integrates the bent band-pass. On the shared stages with the bend, as F2b's first version ran them for the pads, a full-scale 1.8 kHz sawtooth through a 2 kHz low-pass came out centred at -0.125 at Resonance 30 %, -0.105 at 60 % and -0.095 at 100 %. At the lab's -18 dBFS reference it is far smaller, which is why no target caught it. The pads use a resonance guard instead ([The pad filter](#the-pad-filter)); whether the effect takes it too, which would change the effect's Soft sound, is a choice for a later step.

**Type and slope changes** crossfade over 10 ms with a smoothstep. The old filter keeps running from its state while the new one takes over; the new one starts from the old one's state (the 12 dB filter's types share it; the last one or two stages carry theirs whatever the slope), and a second resonant stage newly in use starts from silence. A one-pole or plain stage that comes in at the front (12 to 18 dB, 24 to 48) takes up the low part of what the filter was hearing, the first stage's low-pass state (`Svf::start_from`, `OnePole::start_from`), so the stages after it hear no dip: a 50 Hz tone through a 2 kHz low-pass switched from 24 to 48 dB stays within a tenth of where the 48 dB run from the start is, against starting those stages from rest (`a_stage_that_comes_in_takes_up_the_sound`). Between slopes far apart the two filters' delays differ (a 1 kHz 48 dB low-pass lags the 18 dB at 100 Hz), so the blend moves a tone a little faster than its own slope, under 10 % more: a glide, not a click. A change in the first half of a fade fades from the same old filter; later, from the one that was coming in, so a fast run of changes never jumps.

**Drive** (0 to 24 dB) runs at twice the rate: Drive's Eco half-bands (polyphase IIR, five all-pass sections, flat to 0.19 of the doubled rate and 71 dB down from 0.31), the soft curve `x(27 + x²)/(27 + 9x²)` averaged by first-order ADAA (its antiderivative worked out in 64 bits), then the short flat FIR that makes ADAA's two-point mean flat to 20 kHz. It blends in over the first 3 dB, against a dry side taken through the half-bands' phase and the same delay, so the two add in phase. With the drive off the sound takes a plain 4-frame delay instead, and when the drive comes in or leaves the dry side moves between the plain delay and the matched one over 256 frames. So the **latency is a fixed 4 frames** (reported for compensation; it was 0), the same with the drive on or off, and the drive can be automated without the sound moving in time. The half-bands' states are flushed of values too small to hear every 64 frames on a grid counted from reset.

**At rest** (Soft or Clean, no glide, no fade, the drive off: most of the time) the delay and each stage run a block at a time with their state in registers, the same numbers as frame by frame; the clean filter costs less than before this PR (see section 8).

**Glides.** Cutoff (in the log domain), Resonance, Gain and Drive glide over about 10 ms, the coefficients worked out again each frame while they move.

**The drawing** (`MultiFilter::response_db`) is the linear filter's response (on Hard, of the stages at twice the rate), exact for quiet material; a loud sound at a high Resonance rings less than drawn, by design.

### The Ladder model

**Model** (Multi, Ladder; Multi by default) picks the filter. **Ladder** is a Moog-style 24 dB low-pass: four one-pole low-passes in a loop, the loop's input sum through a curve (on Soft `tanh`, the transistor pair at the bottom of a real ladder), solved without a delay in the loop (Zavalishin's TPT ladder: the loop is solved linearly each sample and `tanh` is taken of that solution, so it stays stable at any setting and in tune up to the top), run at twice the rate between the same half-bands as the drive.

```
in ─ up 2x ─ (drive: soft curve, ADAA, flat FIR, blended in over 3 dB) ─ tanh(in − k · out) ─ 4 one-poles ─ × makeup ─ down ─ out
                                                                          └──────────── feedback k ─────────────┘
```

- **Resonance** is the feedback `k`, 0 to 4.3 in a straight line (3.9 on Clean). At 4 the loop gains 1 at the cutoff and it sings on its own, so from about 93 % up (on Soft and Hard) the ladder self-oscillates, a sine at the cutoff (within 0.06 cents from 100 Hz to 5 kHz) whose level `tanh` holds steady (-10.8 dBFS RMS, the same within 0.01 dB across that range). Below that the peak grows as an analog ladder's does, a little under the cutoff at low Resonance.
- **The bass loss.** A ladder's passband falls as `1 / (1 + k)` as Resonance rises (14 dB at the edge of oscillation), which is part of its sound. The output makeup `1 + k / 2` gives back about two thirds, so at full Resonance the low end is 4.7 dB down instead of 14.
- **Its own saturation.** `tanh` at the loop's input bounds the resonance (swept pink at full Resonance peaks at -2.2 dBFS) and colours loud material; at a mix level it is nearly straight.
- **Res mode** on the ladder picks that curve. **Soft** is `tanh`. **Hard** is the same rounded clip as the Multi model's, its ceiling 0.5, where `tanh`'s knee is: a mix-level sound passes about as through `tanh`, the ring sings about as loud (-10.6 dBFS RMS, in tune within 0.24 cents) and swept pink peaks at -3.6 dBFS, but a loud sound clips flat where `tanh` would still be rounding. **Clean** is straight, with the feedback at full Resonance 3.9 instead of 4.3: with nothing to hold it a ladder past 4 would grow without end, so Clean rings long (about a quarter of a second at 100 Hz) but never sings, and a loud sound at its peak can go over full scale (swept pink +0.7 dBFS). A change of Res mode on the ladder blends the two curves and the feedback over 10 ms (a smoothstep; turned back during a fade it carries on from the same blend), so a singing ladder fades out or in rather than stopping.
- **Drive** goes into the ladder the same way as into the clean filter (the soft curve, at 2x with ADAA). **Type**, **Slope** and **Gain** don't apply, and the page dims them ("the ladder is a 24 dB low-pass").
- **A Model change** crossfades over 10 ms, the model coming in starting from silence (1.2 dB over the sound, against 3); turned back during a fade, it carries on from the same blend. Each of the three engines (Multi at the session rate for Soft and Clean, Multi at twice it for Hard, the ladder) has its own level that rises over 10 ms while it is the one set and falls while it isn't, so any run of Model and Res mode changes fades without a jump. The ladder's path has the clean path's 4 frames of latency.
- **Rest.** From Resonance 93 % up a self-oscillating ladder never goes quiet on its own, so the Multi filter says it never rests (`rest_frames` and `tail_frames` are `u32::MAX` there) and a chain never sleeps it mid-note; below that its tail follows the slowest poles' decay.
- **Drawn as heard:** `response_db` gives the linear ladder's response at twice the rate (each one-pole `G (1 + z^-1) / (1 + (2G - 1) z^-1)`, the loop `H^4 / (1 + k H^4)`, the makeup), within 0.002 dB of a quiet sine through it.

Chosen while building: the TPT ladder with `tanh` on the linear solution rather than Huovilainen's model with a `tanh` in every stage (that one needs its half-sample delay compensated and costs four `tanh` a sample; one at the input gives the bounded resonance and the steady self-oscillation that matter most); the makeup at two thirds rather than none (the ladder's full bass loss made a resonant ladder much quieter than the clean filter at the same settings) or full (no bass loss at all is not a ladder); Resonance in a straight line (a curve bunched the singing range into the last few percent).

### The pad filter

Each pad, and each keygroup, has one filter a hit, after the mix of the layers it plays (`mpfree-sampler` `src/dsp.rs` `VoiceFilter`, run by the hit's lead voice in `src/engine/render.rs` `finish`; one per layer before step F2c): **Low-pass** and **High-pass** at 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 or 8 poles (6 to 48 dB an octave), **Band-pass** at 2, 4, 6 or 8, **Notch** at 2 or 4 (2 and 4 poles alone before step F4), the **Peak** (a boost around the cutoff, since F4), the **Ladder** (with **Drive** up to 24 dB at twice the rate), the **Vowel** (two formants in parallel, the cutoff picking the vowel) and the **Comb** (a tuned delay and sum). Its cutoff moves by the filter envelope (`env_octaves` at full level), velocity (`vel_to_cutoff`), the pad's LFO (up to four octaves either way) and pressure, set every 16 frames (`FILTER_STEP`) and glided to across them.

**The state-variable types (since step F2b, 23 Nov)** are the Filter effect's Multi model on the shared crate (`mpfree-filter` `src/multi.rs`, `Multi`). At Resonance 0 the stages are Butterworth (Q 0.707 at 2 poles; at 4 a 0.541 and 1.307 pair, the resonance on the second, as the effect's 24 dB), so the Cutoff is the -3 dB point at either slope. Resonance is the effect's Q 0.707 to 40, evenly in octaves (`res_to_q`). A notch at 4 poles has the type's Q on both stages; the other slopes and the Peak are under [Slopes and the Peak](#slopes-and-the-peak-since-step-f4-23-nov) below. The stages stay linear, and a **resonance guard** holds a loud sound's ring back in place of the effect's bend (`Multi::guard`; the clean model's Saturated policy, built without a curve in the loop). Each resonant stage (a 2-pole type's one, the last of a steeper low-pass, high-pass or band-pass, the Peak's; both of a 24 dB band-pass before F4) keeps the peak of its band-pass state over a window of about 50 ms (four blocks of 512 frames, `GUARD_BLOCK`). Past a knee of 0.4 (`GUARD_KNEE`, 8 dB under full scale; a boost's is √2 lower, under [Slopes and the Peak](#slopes-and-the-peak-since-step-f4-23-nov)) the stage's damping `k` is multiplied by the held peak over the knee to the fourth power (`GUARD_POWER`), never past Resonance 0's Butterworth damping, so a ring settles where `ring^5 = drive × Q × knee^4`: 10 dB more drive rings 2 dB louder, and a full-scale sine on the cutoff at full Resonance rings at about full scale, where the linear filter would ring 32 dB over it. A peak over the held one is damped in its own frame, by the fourth root of how far over it is, so a loud note's first ring is held as it builds. The damping then holds still while the window holds the peak (within 3 % of it, `GUARD_SLACK`) and lets go 6 dB every 50 ms once the peak has gone (`GUARD_FALL_S`). Holding still across a note's cycles is what keeps the output centred: damping that moves inside a cycle, as a bend in the loop does sample by sample, pushes a lopsided wave off centre. A sound whose ring stays under the knee (at full Resonance, any sound under about -40 dBFS on the cutoff) hears the filter exactly as set and drawn. The notch rings up inside but not at its output, so it isn't guarded. Every 16 frames the voice sets the cutoff it has reached, and the stages' coefficients (`g`, `k` and the output mix) move there in a straight line a step a frame (`SvfCoefs::toward`, a stable filter at every step), so an envelope or an LFO moves the filter every frame. The cutoff goes up to 49 % of the rate (`MULTI_TOP`; the effect stops at 45 %), so a filter opened all the way, as a live cutoff brings one in on a pad without one, passes nearly all a sound has (3 dB down at 21.6 kHz). The voice runs both channels in one step (`Svf::step_stereo`) and flushes the states every 16 frames instead of every sample, zeroing a channel once both its states are under 1e-15 (`QUIET`, `Multi::flush_below`). The shared stage's flush, one state at a time on every sample, leaves a slow tail at a low cutoff: the band-pass state is zeroed on every sample while the low-pass one is still above the threshold, and what is left decays at about `2 g²` a sample instead of the filter's own rate (under 2e-18 at 40 Hz, but seconds to minutes to reach zero). The joint flush ends it as soon as the sound is gone.

**Chosen while building (the guard).** F2b's first version used the effect's bend, and on a pad, which hears its sound at full level, that pushed loud lopsided waves off centre: a full-scale 1.8 kHz sawtooth through a 2 kHz low-pass came out centred at -0.125 (-18 dBFS) at Resonance 0.3, and the bench's held-pad cases moved by over 1 dB. The low-pass state integrates the bent band-pass, so its average shifts by the bend's average error. A curve feeding both paths was centred but unbounded (a band-pass rang to 54 on a square). A guard that worked out the drive every 16 frames and eased the Q to a ceiling of 2 was centred but late and restless: a loud note's first ring through a 24 dB band-pass reached +22 dBFS before it acted, swept pink at full Resonance peaked at +2.0 dBFS (PF5, target -1), and it hunted under an LFO (Y1c's step noise 6.6 dB, target 3). Damping by an envelope sample by sample was quick, but it moved inside a low note's cycle and made DC again (-31 dBFS on a 110 Hz sawtooth). Damping a rise by the whole overshoot damps the ring again for what it reached before the last damping took hold, and the guard hunts; a quarter of it in the power (the fourth root of the peak) settles in a step or two. The held window with that rise meets every target (the numbers are under [Measuring quality](#7-measuring-quality)).

**The Ladder (rebuilt in step F6, 24 Nov)** is the transistor ladder with a curve in every stage, Huovilainen's model of the circuit (`mpfree-filter` `src/ladder.rs`, `LadderCore`; the sampler's `dsp::Ladder` hosts it): `y1' = ωc (c(x − k y4) − c(y1))` and `yi' = ωc (c(y(i−1)) − c(yi))`, the output `y4`, with `c` the pads' soft clip, the Padé `tanh` `v (27 + v²) / (27 + 9 v²)`, flat from ±3. So a loud sound bends at the input pair and in every stage, and squeezes the resonant peak, as in the circuit (Y1b: at Resonance 0.9 a -20 dBFS sine on the cutoff comes out 9 dB under a quiet one's gain, -3 dBFS 21 dB under). Each sample, every curve is replaced by its tangent at the voltages the trapezoidal states predict (a state runs half a step ahead of its output) and the loop is solved once as a linear one: one division for the five curves' reciprocals and one for the loop, with no branches. It always runs at twice the rate, with Drive or without, between the Drive's IIR half-bands (`LadderOs`): Drive's ADAA curve runs first at the doubled rate, and its level scale goes before the ladder (after it, the ladder heard the curve's full level and aliased 12 dB more). A one-pole high-pass at 3 Hz after it (`LADDER_DC_R`, -0.1 dB at 20 Hz, as a real ladder's output coupling) takes out the DC the solve leaves on a loud lopsided wave (a full-scale sawtooth about -40 dB without it; the exact model gives -83 at Resonance 0 and -47 at 0.5). Resonance is the feedback, 0 to 4.2 in a straight line (`LADDER_K`), the make-up `1 + k / 2` as before, and `G` and the feedback glide a step a frame (`Ladder::tick`). From 95 % Resonance (feedback 4) it sings on its own, at -8.8 dBFS (Y2b's level, the same within 0.03 dB from 110 Hz to 7 kHz), about 3 dB louder than before F6. Singing, the circuit falls flat as it gets louder: 2.2 cents at feedback 4.02, 19.6 at 4.2 and 69 at 5, the same within 0.3 cents at 250 Hz, 1 kHz and 4 kHz. So the cutoff is raised by `ladder::tuned(k)`, `2^(108.5 d / (1 + 0.572 d) / 1200)` with `d = max(k − 4, 0)` (within 0.5 cents of every measured point), unless `Ladder::authentic` is set; below 95 % Resonance the two are the same filter, sample for sample. Which the pads play is Jay's choice from F6's listening page (tuned while he chooses). 

**Chosen by measurement** (`mpfree-fxlab` `ladder_study`, against `filter_reference`'s exact render of the same model: Newton's method to convergence at 16 times the rate). The tangent at the prediction, solved once at twice the rate, is within 0.041 % of the exact harmonics (M1: a 200 Hz tone from -12 to +12 dB around full scale, feedback 0 to 3.6, cutoff 1 kHz), sings within 2.2 cents of where the exact model sings (M2), and stays finite and under 1.5 under loud noise while the cutoff jumps anywhere from 20 Hz to 20 kHz and the feedback from 0 to 6 every 1 to 64 samples (M4). A Newton step more cost 75 % more for a hundredth of that error. The secant through 0 instead of the tangent aliased less but sang up to 100 cents off at 5 kHz. At the session's own rate the tangent aliased at -20 dB near the top; at four times the rate PF7's worst row would read about -100 dB instead of -60.7, at twice the cost. Both channels as lanes of one pass cost 18 % more than one after the other. The Ladder from F2b to F5 bent only its feedback (Zavalishin's TPT ladder: the loop solved as if straight, then the feedback through the curve), so a loud sound passed its stages clean: 13 % off the circuit's harmonics at full scale and 177 % at 12 dB over, and in tune when it sang, since nothing bent its stages. Without Drive it ran at the session's rate; with Drive, two steps inside the drive's 2x (`DriveOs::run_through`, gone with F6).

**The Vowel** keeps the old state-variable stages for its formants until F9 rebuilds it, and **the Comb** is unchanged.

**Where it sits.** The filter comes before the amp envelope, pressure's gain, the kill fade, the hit's velocity, the pad's level and pan, the program's level and the movement's sides, so a Drive or a loud resonance hears the sound as it is, not where its envelope, its velocity or the pad's level put it. Before it come each layer's own gains (since F2c, `Pre`): its sound's gain and level, and the hit's key and velocity crossfades and slice gain, so the filter hears the layers mixed as they are set; and the gain from the region's fades, part of the sound. The Comb comes after all the gains, so it rings on once the sound has ended (`ring_out`). A voice with no filter, or a Comb, takes its gains in the order it always did, so it renders exactly as before (and as the plain path, `render_plain`, does).

**One filter a hit (since step F2c, 23 Nov).** The layers a hit starts mix into one filter when they can share it: those with the pad's filter (not the Comb), the same filter settings (a slice's own cutoff can differ), the same layer pan and play mode, not granular, and in one share of a split pool (a hit's voices go to the share its first filtered one took, `Voices::free_slot_near`). The first of them leads the hit (`Role::Lead`): it holds the filter, the amp envelope and every gain after the filter. The others follow it (`Role::Follower`): each renders its sound at its own gains before the filter into the lead's input (`render_chain`, 256 frames at a time, `CHAIN_CHUNK`), with its own pitch, loop, read and movement, and the lead filters the mix with its own sound (`Engine::share_filter` forms the set as the hit starts). A layer of another pan or play mode, or a granular one, keeps a filter of its own, as every layer did before; so does a hit of one layer, which renders as it did. When the lead's own sound ends first, it filters the others' on until the last of them ends (`Voice::hosting`); when the lead ends (its envelope, a cut or a cull), so does its hit. A hit goes with its lead when the pool runs out of voices too: a new voice that takes a lead's slot ends its hit at once, the lead's tail carrying the hit's sound, so the hit's other slots are free for the new hit's next layers (`Voices::end_followers`); and a follower is never the voice taken (`Engine::victim`), since its sound is inside its lead's and it has no tail of its own to leave. So a hit never plays on with only some of its layers, as it could when each layer was a voice of its own: on the bench's sixteen two-layer pads, hit 86 times a second into 24 voices, two to six pads at a time (on average, quarter-second by quarter-second) played one layer of their two (6 dB down, the layers being alike) before F2c, and none since, with 46 % fewer steals; the cases' level rose 0.1 to 0.9 dB once the pool is full ("Sampler, 20 pads" and its two parts, re-blessed). Where one layer's sound stops, the filter rings out what it held of it, where a layer filtered alone was cut off. The lead takes the tails of the voices its layers stole (each fades from the hit's start, so their sum fades as they would), and a follower's last frame, for the voice cull's choice, is its sound through its own gains. A level edit of one layer while the hit plays glides that layer alone, before the filter (`Follow::pre_to`); a layer's pan edit is heard from the next hit, since the lead's gains carry the hit's pan. The pad's LFO on the cutoff, pan or level and a random wave are the lead's for the hit; on the pitch each layer keeps its own. Not yet: a hit whose layers are all mono filtered in mono, and filtering 16 frames at a time.

**A hit's own part of its filter (since step F3, 23 Nov).** A voice works its cutoff out as one sum in octaves, made a factor once: the pad's Cutoff (or a live one, gliding), the filter envelope at its depth, pressure's octaves, the pad's LFO, and the hit's own part (`Motion::hit_fc`): velocity on the cutoff, the key, and a random. **Vel → cutoff** (`vel_to_cutoff`) now goes from -1 to 1, so a minus amount makes a soft hit brighter. **Key track** (`key_track`, -1 to 1) moves the cutoff an octave with each octave the key sounds from the sound's root at 1, half that at 0.5, and the other way below 0, as stock MPC's Keytrack (p. 228). The key is the voice's `key_cents`: where its key sounds from the sound's root in its kit's tuning (a keygroup's semitones, the tuning's offset and a 16 Levels Tune step's interval, but not the pad's or program's Tune or a bend), so the cutoff follows just intonation or quarter tones as the pitch does (PF9), and pads playing notes play their keys. A legato move takes the cutoff to the new key at once. A glide takes it there with the pitch, from where the glide starts: `motion_step` adds what is left of the glide to the key, and a new note's first step starts there too. A grain layer's cutoff moves to the new key at once while its cloud glides, and a change of tuning while a note plays moves its pitch, not its filter. Velocity also scales the filter envelope's depth (`vel_to_env`, 0 to 1: the depth times `1 - amount × (1 - velocity)`) and lengthens its attack (`vel_to_attack`, 0 to 1: up to a second more at the softest hit, `VEL_ATTACK_MS`), as MPC's velocity to the filter envelope's depth and attack (pp. 205, 228). **Random per hit** (`cutoff_random` and `res_random`, 0 to 1, as MPC's Cutoff Random and Resonance Random, pp. 308-309) moves a hit's cutoff up to 2 octaves either way (`RANDOM_OCTAVES`) and its Resonance up to half its range (`RANDOM_RES`), evenly, drawn once a hit for all its layers (`Engine::hit_random`). The draw comes from where the hit is, not from a count of hits: while the transport plays, its place in the song to a 960th of a beat and how many times the transport has jumped back since it started (`Engine::set_beat` counts a beat an eighth of a beat or more behind where the last one had got to); stopped, the engine's frame; with its kit, pad and note, mixed by SplitMix64's finish. So a song plays the same each time from its start and an export matches what played, an edit elsewhere in the song leaves a hit's draw as it was, a loop's hits draw anew each time round, and two pads or a chord's notes hit together each draw their own. **Continue** (`Mono::env_continue` on a mono pad; off is Restart, as before): a note that starts again, not a legato move, starts its amp and filter envelopes' attacks from where the pad's newest sounding note's were (`Envelope::start_from`), so a quick mono line doesn't dip between its notes (with an attack of 0 nothing changes: the envelope is at the top at once). The last note fades over the usual 64-frame cut as the new one starts, as it does on Restart. Legato already carries the envelopes on.

**Drive and Env depth** are pad settings a Q-Link, a step lock, MIDI Learn or a scene can set (`PadField::Drive` and `PadField::EnvDepth`), as Cutoff is: a playing voice follows, the depth gliding in octaves as the cutoff does (`live_step`) and the Drive at once, as an edit's is (the Ladder's drive comes in without a click). An edit of the pad's own Env depth now reaches a playing note too, gliding there. Every new setting is 0 or off by default, and there the sum is exactly the old one (`x × 1.0`, `x + 0.0`), so a kit sounds bit for bit as before: the bench's goldens and the lab's ratchets didn't move. Saves carry `key_track`, `vel_to_env`, `vel_to_attack`, `cutoff_random` and `res_random` on every pad and `env_continue` in its `mono` scope, as they carry `vel_to_cutoff`; a save from before opens with them at 0 and off. They have no controls yet: F5's screens (mockups for Jay first, under the layout spec) bring them, and until then a saved kit sets them. Not yet exact: the transport hands the engine its place once a period, so a hit in the period where a loop jumps back takes its place from the period's start (past the loop's end). It draws the same each time round, but an export that renders in other periods draws it differently.

#### Slopes and the Peak (since step F4, 23 Nov)

**The slopes.** Low-pass and high-pass come at 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 poles, band-pass at 2, 4, 6 and 8 and notch at 2 and 4 (`multi::plan`). A saved slope a type doesn't have plays as the nearest one it has below (`multi::slope`: a 5-pole low-pass is a 4-pole), and the Play tab names the one it plays. At Resonance 0 every low-pass and high-pass is a Butterworth: an odd slope starts with a one-pole (`mpfree-filter` `src/onepole.rs`: the trapezoidal integrator the state-variable stage is built from, on its own), and the 2-pole stages take the Butterworth Qs of their order (3 poles: 1.0; 4: 0.541 and 1.307, as before; 6: 0.518, 0.707 and 1.932; 8: 0.510, 0.601, 0.900 and 2.563), so the Cutoff is the -3 dB point and the shape a textbook Butterworth's at every slope (PF1, PF2).

**The resonance at every slope** multiplies the last, highest-Q stage's Q by `q / 0.707`, as the 24 dB always did, so the gain at the cutoff is the 2-pole's at every slope: -3 dB at Resonance 0, 14.5 dB at 0.5 and 32 dB at full (PF10, within 0.05 dB in the tests). The passband stays flat below the peak (at Resonance 0.5 it rises at most 0.28 dB below half the cutoff at 8 poles, 0.97 dB at 4 and 2.4 dB at 2), and the peak sits on the cutoff (9 cents under it at 4 poles and Resonance 0.5, 5 at 8, nothing at full). The steeper the slope, the higher its last stage's Q and the longer it rings: at full Resonance on a 1 kHz cutoff the ring falls 60 dB in 88, 124, 162, 240 and 319 ms at 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 poles. A 1-pole has no second pole to ring with, so its Resonance is a bell on the cutoff (gain `q / 0.707`, pole Q `q`), which brings the same peak (14.5 dB at Resonance 0.5, ringing as long as the 2-pole's) with the one-pole's 6 dB slope above it; its guard is the Peak's (below). **Chosen while building** (analog prototypes, jferments/mpfree-claude `fx/quality/filters-mpc-gap/`): spreading the resonance over every stage (each stage's Q raised by the same factor) gave the 8-pole's passband an 8 dB hump at full Resonance and pulled its peak 30 cents under the cutoff (92 at Resonance 0.5); a bell stage after the Butterworth cascade gave the same peak but a broader hump (2.7 dB below half the cutoff at Resonance 0.5, whatever the slope), changed the 24 dB's sound and cost a stage. The last stage alone keeps the 24 dB bit for bit and every slope's peak where the 2-pole's is.

**The band-pass** at 4, 6 or 8 poles is broad stages at Q 0.707, each scaled to unity at its centre, then one stage at the type's Q, so its centre and its ring are the 2-pole's at every slope and only its skirts steepen: at four times the centre, Resonance 0.5, it is -20.5 dB at 4 poles, -29.6 at 6 and -38.6 at 8, against -11.5 at 2. Before F4 the 4-pole band-pass ran both stages at the type's Q, so its centre was the square of the 2-pole's: 29 dB at Resonance 0.5 and 64 dB at full, which the guard held back on all but the quietest sounds; stacking stages that way would put an 8-pole's centre at 128 dB. Spreading the Q evenly over the stages kept the centre but made the 8-pole's peak about ten times as broad (a third of an octave at full Resonance, against a 30th) and its ring a twentieth as long, so Resonance would mean less the steeper the slope. A kit saved before F4 (`filters = 2`) with a 4-pole band-pass opens with a note to check it by ear: it rings less at high Resonance.

**The Peak** (`FilterKind::Peak`) is a bell on the cutoff (`SvfMode::Bell`, the effect's EQ stage) whose boost is the 2-pole low-pass's ring over its -3 dB point: `q / 0.707`, nothing at Resonance 0, 17.5 dB at 0.5 and 35 dB at full (`dsp::peak_boost_db`; the Play tab shows it beside the Resonance, "40 % · +14.0 dB"). Its design Q is `q / √gain`, so its pole Q is the type's Q and it rings as the 2-pole does. Away from the cutoff it leaves the sound alone (within 0.5 dB at 100 Hz and 10 kHz on a 1 kHz cutoff, PF10). A bell adds its band-pass state to the sound times `m1 = 1/0.707 - 1/q`, up to √2, where a low-pass, high-pass or band-pass rings at its output as its band-pass state does; so a bell's guard holds that state √2 lower (`GUARD_KNEE_BELL`, 0.28), and what its ring adds at the output is held as a low-pass's ring is. The damping raises the bell's `k` alone, so a loud sound's boost comes down with its ring, and the 1-pole's resonance is guarded the same way. A sound under about -43 dBFS on the cutoff hears the full boost at full Resonance (PF10 measures it at -46), and at -40 dBFS it still rings up 32.6 dB of its 35 (PF6). Since a boost passes the whole sound and adds its held ring to it, swept pink at -18 dBFS RMS peaks at -0.1 dBFS through the Peak at full Resonance (the pink's own loudest sample is -5.5 dBFS), where the low-pass's ring alone peaks at -1.4: PF5 reports the Peak without the -1 dBFS target, which is for filters that take some of the sound away. With the knee as a low-pass's, it peaked at +1.1 dBFS and the 1-pole at -0.2 (now -1.1, meeting PF5); half the low-pass's knee left the Peak at -0.3 dBFS, as the loudest moments are where the pink is loudest, and cost the quiet sound 2.4 dB of its ring, so the knee follows what the ring adds and no more.

**Cost and glides.** The odd slopes' one-pole and the band-pass's broad stages are linear and never ring, so the guard watches only the last one or two stages, and the 6 and 8-pole slopes cost no more watching than the 24 dB. A slope with stages before the last one or two runs out of line, in a function marked cold (`Multi::process_extra`: its glide step, the one-pole, the plain stages, then the last two), behind one test a frame, so the 12 and 24 dB filters' code in a voice's loop stays lean ([Cost](#8-cost)). Every stage glides to a new cutoff a step a frame as before, the one-pole's gain with them. A slope change lands at once: the stages that come in start from rest and the ones that stay keep their states, so a change while a note plays can click; the architecture's 10 ms crossfade comes with F7's two filters. The 12 and 24 dB low-pass, high-pass and notch and the 2-pole band-pass are the same stages as before, bit for bit (`the_12_and_24_db_stages_are_the_effects`).

**The drawn curve** (`dsp::svf_db`, behind the Play tab's `filter_db`) is the stages' exact response at the engine's 44.1 kHz (`multi::response_db`): what is heard, short of a loud sound's ring that the guard holds back, up to Nyquist, where the bilinear transform pulls a low-pass down and cramps a high cutoff's peak.

**Saves** carry `filters = 4` (`save.rs`, `FILTERS_VERSION`; 2 from F2b to F3, 3 from F4 to F5, 4 since F6) and save every slope and the Peak (`filter.kind = peak`). A version 2 kit with a 4-pole band-pass opens with a note to check it by ear, as its centre now peaks as the 2-pole's does (above), and a version 2 or 3 kit with a Ladder pad with a note that the Ladder was rebuilt (brighter, a loud sound's resonant peaks a little softer). A kit or project saved before F2b has no such line (version 1): it opens with its settings as they were, and with a note for each kit that has a low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, notch or Ladder pad, which may sound brighter (the Cutoff is now where the sound is 3 dB down) and more resonant (the Q curve rises faster), with a loud sound's ring held back. There is no legacy mode: the plan migrates old projects with a notice (Jay's review, 23 Nov).

What it was before F2b, from F1's baseline: each stage the effect's trapezoidal SVF but at Q 0.5 at Resonance 0 (`k = 2 - 1.95 × resonance`, Q 0.5 to 20), a 4-pole two equal stages (the second at half the resonance), retuned every 16 frames and held between, after the amp envelope and the voice's gains. So the cutoff wasn't the -3 dB point (a 4-pole low-pass at 1 kHz was 3 dB down at 435 Hz), the response sagged 3 to 9 dB from a Butterworth round the cutoff, the resonance was linear and unbounded with a lower top than the effect's, and a driven Ladder's loop ran at the base rate after the drive's 2x. The measured change is under [Measuring quality](#7-measuring-quality).

The filter gap plan (jferments/mpfree-claude `fx/quality/filters-mpc-gap/`, revised on 23 Nov after Jay's design review; step by step under [History and next](#10-history-and-next)) closes this in thirteen phases: the architecture below (F0), the measures (F1, done), the shared crate and the clean fixes (F2: Butterworth stages, a resonance policy per model, a per-frame cutoff, the filter before the amp and the driven Ladder inside the 2x), modulation through `mpfree-mod` (F3), more slopes and a Peak (F4), the first full filter UI (F5), the Ladder rebuilt (F6), two filters per pad (F7), Smooth and Scream (F8), voices (F9), Diode and Classic 12 (F10), the auto filter (F11) and device performance (F12, with a cost check in every phase).

### The shared filter system (design, step F0)

What the filter gap plan builds towards; the parts built so far are marked. Every filter, in the pads, the Filter effect and later the synths, is one crate, [`mpfree-filter`](../../../rust/mpfree-filter), in four layers (logical parts of the one crate, with plain enums rather than a plugin framework):

1. **DSP algorithms:** SVF stages (built: the effect's stage and its bounded resonance, moved from `mpfree-fx` bit for bit), Butterworth cascades, ladders, the diode solve, Sallen-Key, formant banks, comb and half-bands. They take physical values (Hz, Q, gain) and know nothing of pads, tracks or screens.
2. **Models and parameters:** what a musician picks. Each model declares its parameters (name, range, unit, curve, default), its **cutoff meaning** (the -3 dB point at neutral resonance for a Butterworth low-pass or high-pass, a nominal frequency as resonance rises, the centre of a band-pass, notch or bell, a documented reference frequency for an analog model), its **resonance policy** (neutral, musical and maximum resonance, whether it self-oscillates, its gain and limiting: Clean, Saturated or Self-oscillating in spirit, but each model its own), its **stability contract** (the range it is proven or tested stable in, what glides and what steps, behaviour at the extremes, finite-state checks and a reset to silence if a state ever goes non-finite), its solver (the cheapest that passes its stress tests: closed form, fixed-cost linearisation, a fixed few Newton steps; the [reference](#7-measuring-quality) is the yardstick) and its oversampling (linear types at the base rate; nonlinear ones 2x or 4x, by measurement; no stateful ADAA inside a feedback loop).
3. **Instances and state:** integrators, feedback, half-band state, smoothing, cached coefficients and crossfades. A configuration is shared; an instance belongs to one sounding hit, or to one channel of the Filter effect.
4. **Hosts:** the sampler (one chain per hit), the Filter effect (one chain per channel, always running) and later the synths.

**Parameter flow.** `mpfree-mod` resolves base, morph, macro, lock, touch and matrix into one value per parameter, as it does for everything ([modulation.md](modulation.md)). The host adds the voice-local sources in octaves (filter envelope, velocity, keytrack from the voice's tuned pitch `key_cents`, a random offset seeded from the project and the event, pressure and the pad LFO) and clamps the sum to the model's range; the instance smooths it every frame. Nothing else decides what a parameter means. The pad's Cutoff and Resonance are already addressable offsets in octaves that glide on playing voices (`mpfree-sampler` `src/node.rs`).

**Gain staging.** A nonlinear model is input Drive, then its core, then an output trim, so more Drive means more harmonics without much more level. The reference level is -18 dBFS RMS pink (the lab's and the Drive effect's), and Drive is in dB above it. No automatic loudness matching: fixed calibration between implementations and a model's own compensation (a ladder's bass loss) only.

**A hit's signal path.** The layers a hit plays are read and mixed with their own gain and pan; the mix passes Filter A, then Filter B, then the amp envelope, then the pad's level and pan. A hit whose layers are all mono is filtered in mono. Built in F2c for the one filter (above): the layers' gains before it, and their pan after it with the pad's, as one pan law (`pan_gains(layer pan + pad pan)`, as every voice has always panned), so only layers of the same pan share a filter; moving the layer pan before the filter would change how a panned layer sounds on a panned pad, so it waits for Jay's call. The mono path is to come. The Comb runs after the amp so it rings on after the sound ends; a self-oscillating model needs no input and stops with its hit.

**Changes while notes play.** Cutoff, Resonance, Drive and envelope depth glide on sounding hits. A change of model, slope or routing crossfades over 10 ms, old and new side by side, its cost counted in the worst case. Envelope times reach each envelope at its next stage. **Bypass** crossfades over 10 ms; a cheap model runs on while bypassed, an oversampled one stops after the fade and restarts from silence. A stolen or choked hit fades with its amp and its state is cleared before the instance plays again.

**Identity, presets and versions.** Filter A and B are stable ids whatever their order, and each parameter's address keeps its meaning across models (a model without Drive ignores it). A filter configuration can be copied, pasted and saved on its own; an instrument preset holds it. Saves carry a filter version from F2, and an older one is mapped onto the new filters with a one-time notice.

### DJ filter

One knob: below the middle a low-pass falling from 20 kHz to 80 Hz, above it a high-pass rising from 20 Hz to 8 kHz, one SVF stage with Q from 0.707 to 6.7 by Resonance. The filter fades in over the first 10 % each side of the middle, and in the middle it is off and the input passes bit for bit, its state cleared so the next turn starts clean. Perform FX runs it inside its own loop (`DjFilter::frame`). Its peak is low enough (Q 6.7) that swept pink at full Resonance peaks at -3.9 dBFS, so it has no bounded resonance; this round changed nothing in it.

**The reference for analog-style models** (`mpfree_fxlab::filter_reference`): each model the filter gap plan builds (F5) is first written as its circuit's differential equations and solved slowly and exactly: the trapezoidal rule with Newton's method run to convergence every step, at 16 times the rate (32 to check it has converged), the input brought up and the output down by the Y8 references' 120 dB filter. The real-time model, one solve a sample with its curves at twice the rate, is then measured against that render. So far it holds the linear state-variable filter (within 0.01 dB of its exact response) and the transistor ladder with its curve in every stage (Huovilainen's model, the circuit's behaviour) or on the feedback alone (the pad's Ladder today); a loud ladder's 16x and 32x renders agree to -86 dB. They differ where a ladder sings: after a click at 1 kHz, the ladder with a curve in every stage sings 20 cents flat at feedback 4.2, 70 at 5 and 100 at 6 (the circuit falls flat as it sings louder), while the feedback-only one stays within 2 cents. So a rebuilt Ladder that sounds like the circuit sings flat at high Resonance unless its cutoff is corrected for it: F6 measured the fall through its real-time ladder and corrects it (`ladder::tuned`) or, authentic, leaves it ([The Ladder](#the-pad-filter) above).

## 8. Cost

The instruction gate's cases (rustc 1.97.0, a 128-frame stereo block at 44.1 kHz): **Multi filter (LP 12)**, the clean filter at rest, 11.3k to 8.5k instructions (0.75 times: the block-at-a-time path); **Multi filter (LP 24, drive)**, 33.7k to 139.8k (4.1 times): the drive at twice the rate with ADAA, about 38k of it the ADAA's antiderivative, the rest the half-bands, the dry side's phase match and the flat FIR, as in Drive's Eco (202.6k with its tone and DC filters). The drive, filter, delay and compressor chain: 333.9k to 339.8k. **Multi filter (ladder)**, at Resonance 70 with no drive: 92.0k (the ladder at 2x and its half-bands; with drive add the ADAA's 38k). **Multi filter (LP 12, Hard)**, Resonance 70: 80.4k (the stages at twice the rate between the half-bands). With the engine and Res mode checks, (LP 12) is 8.6k and (LP 24, drive) 143.7k. The DJ filter is unchanged (13.4k).

**The pad filter** (the sampler's cases, F2b against main, both counted in the same container, since the committed baseline was counted on other machines and reads a few per cent off on cases F2b doesn't touch): sixteen held pads through a resonant 2-pole low-pass, 573k to 610k instructions a block (+6.4 %; with 16-bit sounds +5.9 %, with the LFO and pitch envelope +8.0 %); the driven Ladder +15 % (its loop now runs at twice the rate inside the drive); the Vowel +4 %; the Comb unchanged; twenty pads of a kit +3.5 %; drum kits of 24 and 64 voices +1.2 to 2.4 %; the whole-song case +1.3 %. A filtered voice costs about 18 instructions a frame more: the guard's watch on each resonant stage (a compare a frame, and the damping's rise when a loud ring passes the held peak), the glide's test and the output mix in place of the old type switch. The first version cost +48 %: the stereo step returned an array, which the compiler packed into one integer register and out again, the filter type was tested four times a frame, and the type's step was a call. Pairs instead of arrays, one test a frame for the order and two for the type took it to +12.5 % with the effect's bend on each channel, and the guard in place of the bend to +6.4 %. Filtering 16 frames at a time, with F2c's one filter per hit, is the next saving.

**One filter a hit** (F2c against F2b, both counted in this container): four pads of four layers held through a resonant low-pass (the bench's "Sampler x4 held, 4 layers each", new with F2c), 610k instructions a block with a filter a layer, 493k with one a hit (-19 %); the whole-song case, whose kit has layered pads, -2.9 %. A voice with a filter of its own pays the role test a frame and its share of the chain's bookkeeping a block: sixteen held pads +0.2 to 1.2 % (the resonant low-pass +1.0 %, the driven Ladder +0.2 %, the Comb -0.2 %), drum kits of 24 and 64 voices +0.5 to 1.2 %, twenty pads of a kit within 0.5 %. The first version cost a voice of its own 7 to 16 % more: the frame's output pair, passed into `finish` for the chain's use, was worked out before the filter and held in registers across it (about nine instructions a frame of moves and spills, found by comparing the two builds' hot loops instruction by instruction under callgrind), and a second call of the filter, out of line for the chain, lost the driven Ladder its inlining (+13.7 %). Indexing the output only where the frame adds to it, one call of the filter for every voice (the lead's input taken just before it) and the gain before the filter read once a call left the role test alone.

**A hit's own part** (F3 against F2c, both counted in this container): held pads don't move (sixteen held pads through a resonant low-pass 0.00 %, the Vowel +0.11 %, the driven Ladder +0.06 %), since the hit's octaves are one factor worked out at the hit and the envelope's scale and the resonance's random are taken where the coefficients are worked out again, past the key cache. The drum kits pay for the work at each hit (the bigger `Vary`, the hit's octaves and the envelope's start): +0.23 to +0.47 %. The whole-song case is unchanged.

**Slopes and the Peak** (F4 against F3, both counted in this container): a 12 or 24 dB voice pays one test a frame for the steeper slopes' stages, which run out of line in a function marked cold (`Multi::process_extra`): sixteen held pads through a resonant 2-pole low-pass +2.5 % (+2.3 % with 16-bit sounds, +1.8 % pitched up, +2.5 % with the LFO and pitch envelope), the driven Ladder +0.3 %, the Comb +0.2 %, the Vowel +3.5 %, four pads of four layers +1.4 %, twenty pads of a kit +1.1 %, drum kits of 24 and 64 voices +0.4 to 0.8 %, the whole-song case +0.4 %. The voice's loop is short of registers, and where the extra stages' code sits moves it: inlined, they cost the held 2-pole pads 6.0 % though those pads never run them (the loop's values spilled to memory); a `match` on a shape enum 5.3 %; F3's own two tests reused for them 4.9 %; out of line but not cold 4.5 %, and the Vowel 15.5 %, as the compiler then packed the Vowel's two formants on two channels into vectors another way, with many more shuffles (565 instructions for their lines, against 324). The Vowel now keeps its formants as four lanes side by side (`dsp::Formants`, each lane the same arithmetic as before, so it renders bit for bit as it did), which packs the same way whatever shares the loop, still about 10 instructions a frame over F3's packing; the Vowel's rebuild (F9) can take that back. The bench has no case for a 36 or 48 dB pad yet.

**The effect's slopes** (F4b against main, both counted in this container): the 12 and 24 dB filters run the stages they did, and the one-pole and plain stages a steeper slope adds run out of line in functions marked cold (`MultiFilter::clean_steep`, `hard_steep`), entered by one test a frame, so the old filters keep their code: Multi filter (LP 12) 8,596 to 8,614 instructions a block (+0.2 %), (LP 12, Hard) 80,356 to 81,005 (+0.8 %), (LP 24, drive) 143,540 to 146,914 (+2.4 %), the chain case +0.0 %, Project: fast automation (its filter's cutoff moving every frame) 572,292 to 579,563 (+1.3 %), Project: all at once +0.4 %. A moving cutoff on a 12 or 24 dB filter designs its stages as before (`set_stages`); only the other slopes build the plan. Running the stages through one path for every slope cost +19 % on the Hard case (the half-bands' code made twice, with bounds checks) and taking the 12 and 24 dB path inside the same closure +12 %; out of line, nothing measurable. The bench has no 36 or 48 dB case yet.

**The Ladder rebuilt** (F6 against main, both counted in this container): sixteen pads held through a Ladder with no Drive (the bench's "Sampler x16 held, ladder", new with F6) 860,764 to 3,126,470 instructions a block (3.6 times), and with Drive 12 dB 1,794,772 to 3,353,015 (+87 %). Against "Sampler x16 held" (617k, resting 2-pole low-passes), a held Ladder pad now costs about five plain pads (1.4 before without Drive, 2.9 with). The old Ladder bent once a sample, and without Drive ran at the session's rate (84 instructions a channel a frame); the new one bends four stages and always runs at twice the rate, about 730. A pad on another type, and the Filter effect's own Ladder (92,010 to 91,866), cost what they did. Tried: both channels as lanes of one pass (18 % more, the shuffles), a Newton step (+75 % for nothing measurable), four times the rate (twice the cost; it would meet PF7). Next saving: a voice whose two channels are the same (a mono sound, centred) could run one ladder and copy it, about half.
