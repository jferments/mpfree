# Audio tracks: recording on the song timeline

**Status:** engine crate `rust/mpfree-audiotracks`, 2026-11-12; in the pad player since 2026-11-13 (`rust/mpfree-app/src/audiotracks.rs`, see "In the pad player" below); tracks in the project's one track list since the audio tracks rethink's phase 1a (see "In the one track list"), each with its own mixer strip since phase 1b (see "Its own mixer strip"), each take a recording with a lasting id since phase 1c (see "The recordings table"), workstation only. It has not run on the MPC One.

The MPC's factory OS has audio tracks: linear recordings that play in the sequence next to the MIDI tracks. MPFree's looper (T4) records loops; this crate records in a line along the song, the way a tape machine or a DAW does, and adds what the MPC does not have: a take for every pass of a loop recording, and comping between takes.

## How it records

- **Arm, then record.** Up to 8 audio tracks (`MAX_TRACKS`). An armed track records while the transport records: Record from stopped (after the count-in), or a punch in while playing. It stops at the punch out, at Stop, or when the track is disarmed. Several tracks can record at once.
- **Input.** Each track takes the left jack, the right jack, both summed (mono takes) or both as a stereo take (`Command::SetInput`). Round 7 found the MPC One's line input records on the left jack, so `Input::Left` is the default. A track can instead record from inside the app (`Command::SetInside(track, Some(latency))`, the instrument redesign's step 9): the host puts its block in `AudioTracks::inside_input` before each period, the take is stereo and placed by that latency, and the track never plays that input through.
- **A take is one recording:** its audio and the song tick its first frame belongs to (its `anchor`). A new recording puts its region over whatever the track had there; the older audio is covered, not lost, and stays available to comp from.
- **Loop recording.** While the transport loops, each pass ends its take at the loop end and starts the next at the loop start, at the same frame. The passes share a `group` and are numbered by `pass`; the newest pass plays. A locate while recording does the same: the take before it ends where the song left off, and a new one starts where it lands.
- **Latency.** Input that arrives `latency` frames after a moment belongs to that moment (`Command::SetLatency`, for all tracks). The take's anchor is placed that much earlier, and a recording keeps writing that long after its punch out. It then writes `FADE` frames more, so its end can crossfade into what follows. Round 7 used a latency of 512 frames; the right number should be measured with a cable from out to in.
- **Monitoring.** `Monitor::Auto` (the default) plays the input on an armed track while stopped, counting in or recording, and plays the track's regions while just playing. `Monitor::In` always plays the input as well as the regions; `Monitor::Off` never plays the input. While a track records, its regions are silent (as in a DAW's loop recording, the earlier passes do not play over the new one).

## Regions, comping and edits

What a track plays is its list of regions, sorted and never overlapping. A region is a stretch of song ticks (`start..end`) that plays one take from frame `src`, at the rate it was recorded at.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `Comp { take, from, to }` | Plays the take on its track from `from` to `to`, over what was there (clipped to where the take has audio). A swipe across a take lane. |
| `Split { track, at }` | Cuts the region playing at `at` in two. |
| `Move { track, at, to }` | Moves the region playing at `at` to start at `to`, over what is there. |
| `Erase { track, from, to }` | Silences a stretch; regions across its edges are cut there. |
| `SetGain { track, at, gain }` | The region's level, 0 to 4. |
| `SetFades { track, at, fade_in, fade_out }` | Fades in frames from the region's start and to its end (each at most half the region). |
| `Undo`, `Redo` | One history of every edit and recording, for all tracks (`HISTORY`: 64 snapshots of a track's regions, one for each track a step changed). Refused while recording, and at a song edit's step (below). |
| `Retime { song, retime }` | A song time edit: every track's regions move with the stretch of song they sit in, as one step of song edit `song`. Refused while a track records or when a track would have more than 256 regions. |
| `SongUndo(song)`, `SongRedo(song)` | Take back, or bring back, song edit `song`'s steps (+ Bar pressed in a row is one song edit of several steps). Refused unless they are what Undo, or Redo, would take next. |
| `ForgetSong(song)` | The song's history no longer reaches song edit `song`: its steps go, with every step only reachable through them. No region changes. |
| `Forget(take)` | Gives a take's memory back. Refused while a region, or a step Undo or Redo could bring back, plays it (`in_use`); `ClearHistory` first. |

An edit that names a track or region that is not there, or would not fit (256 regions a track, `MAX_REGIONS`), changes nothing and is counted in `Stats::refused`.

**Song time edits** (the Arrange refactor's 3.4, `src/retime.rs`). Moving a section, + Bar and Delete bars move the audio with the song. The song's `TimeEdit::pieces()` says where each stretch of the old song goes, and a `Retime` holds them: at most four `Piece { from, end, to }` (`MAX_PIECES`), a fixed-size copy, so the command allocates nothing. `Retime::apply` cuts each region where a piece starts or ends and moves each part to its piece's new place. A part's `src` moves on by the frames from the region's start to the cut along the tempo map the region was placed on: the map of the period before (`last_map`), since the edit's own view brings the new map to the same period. A part keeps the region's fade in only at the region's start and its fade out only at its end; a cut has none. Parts of one region that meet again after the edit, in step with each other, are one region again. `retime_refused(&retime)` says first why it can't go in (`RetimeRefused::Recording(track)`, `Full(track)`), so the app can refuse the song edit before anything changes, and `busy()` says when what the tracks say now may not hold for a command sent now (an edit waiting for the period, or a full queue).

The history keeps a step's snapshots together: each carries its step's number, and a song edit's carry the song edit's number too (`Snap::song`, the app's take number). Undo and Redo take a whole step back or bring it back, every track of it, and stop at a song edit's step (`undo_is_song`, `redo_is_song`): only the song's UNDO and REDO move it, as `SongUndo` and `SongRedo`. `song_step(song, undo)` says where its steps are: next (`SongStep::Next`), under other steps (`Under`), or gone (`Gone`). A full history drops the oldest whole step, never one track of it. `ForgetSong` drops a song edit's steps once the song can't reach it: on the Undo side the newest of them and every older step, on the Redo side the first of them and every step redone after it, so the regions never go back to where they sat on a song that is gone.

**A sound brought in** (the one Sample page's step 6, Put on Audio track): `AudioTracks::place(take, cuts, map)` plays a take on its track from its anchor to its end, over what was there, cut at its frames `cuts` into a region each, as one undo step. It is a method, not a command, since the cuts are a list; it applies at once, between periods (the host calls it after `process`). It is refused (and counted) if the take is not there, records or still copies in, or the pieces don't fit.

## No clicks

Every region fades in over `FADE` (64) frames from its start and plays on `FADE` frames past its end while fading out, both equal power. Two regions that meet therefore crossfade over the same 1.5 ms. A punch in fades from the older take into the new one, a punch out back again, and a comp boundary between two passes of the same part is seamless. The demo's comp boundaries show no larger sample-to-sample jump than the music around them.

## Time

A region sits on song ticks, so it stays on its bar when the tempo before it changes. Its audio plays sample for sample from the region's start, the way a DAW plays audio that is not warped. At a tempo other than the one it was recorded at, it doesn't stretch by itself, so a region longer than a bar drifts against the bars until the take is fitted (below).

**Fitting a take to a new tempo** ([`stretch.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/stretch.rs)) keeps its pitch:

1. A take remembers the tempo it was recorded at (`TakeInfo::bpm`), and `stretch::fit_ratio(recorded, now)` says how much longer it must be.
2. Off the audio thread, the host reads the take with `read_take`, runs a `StretchJob` (the `mpfree-stretch` phase vocoder that keeps hits sharp) in steps on a worker, and loads the result under `free_take_id()` with `load_take`. The new take keeps the old one's anchor, so its frame 0 stays on the same tick.
3. Once it has copied in, `Command::Swap { old, new, ratio }` plays it wherever the old take played, with each region's `src` scaled by the ratio, as one undo step. The old take stays until it is forgotten, so Undo goes back to it.

The stretch is one ratio over the whole take. That is right for a take recorded at one tempo and played at one tempo. A test records a 441 Hz tone at 240 BPM, halves the tempo and fits the take: the take then fills its bar again, still at 441 Hz.

Positions use the transport's tempo map (`process` takes it), so a tempo map with steps or ramps plays audio in the right place: `frames_from` and `tick_after` ask the map (`TempoMap::frames_between`, `TempoMap::tick_after`), which follows a ramp's 1/16 staircase exactly as the transport plays it ([transport.md](transport.md#tempo-ramps)).

## Memory

All takes share one pool of samples, asked for once in `AudioTracks::new(rate, seconds)`: `seconds` of stereo, or twice that of mono takes. Its pages are touched there, so the audio thread never waits for the system to map one in. 300 s of stereo at 44.1 kHz is 106 MB.

- The pool is cut into equal chunks of 32,768 samples (`CHUNK`, 128 KiB; 0.74 s of mono at 44.1 kHz). A take is any number of chunks, each in any free slot, found through a small table made once ([`pool.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/pool.rs)). A recording takes one chunk at a time, so any number of tracks can start recording at any moment with no planning, and nothing is ever moved. The last chunk of a take is usually part-filled.
- A recording that runs out of memory stops there. What was written stays, with its region; the take is marked `cut_short` and counted in `Stats::full`. The track records again at the next punch in.
- `memory()` is the gauge (`used`, `capacity`, in samples).
- **Waveforms.** The pool keeps the lowest and highest sample of every block of 256 samples (`PEAK_BLOCK`) as audio is recorded or loaded. `peaks(id, from, to)` reads the table for whole blocks, so a screen column covering a second of audio costs about 170 reads instead of 44,100. It works while a take records, up to what has been written, and doesn't allocate. A chunk is whole blocks, so a block never mixes two takes. The host gives the screen waveforms (`wave`, below).

The pool is the whole limit, as a tape that ends. Each take also goes to a file of its own as it records ([Recording to files](#recording-to-files-phase-3a)), and with the stream layer's player a take can play from its file instead, its pool memory freed ([Playing from files](#playing-from-files-phase-3b)).

## Saved with the project

[`save.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/save.rs) holds the audio tracks' part of a project (`parts/audiotracks.txt`): each track's settings and regions, and each take's place, with its audio named by file. The audio is a 32-bit float WAV a take, mono or stereo as recorded.

- **Saving.** `save_into(room)` fills a `Saved::room` (made off the audio thread: every track's regions and every take, with room for a file name) with the settings, regions and takes without allocating, and gives how many tracks and takes it holds; `save_file()` does the same into a room of its own, and allocates. Both give None until every take has settled (`settled()`: no track records or has a recording's tail still coming, and no take is loading), and leave out tracks with nothing to keep. `read_take(id, from, out)` reads a take's audio a piece at a time without allocating, so the app can read across periods. `save::to_text` and `save::wav` make the files. A take never changes once recorded, so a save read slowly can't tear.
- **Opening.** Takes and tracks come back through `save::from_text` and `save::from_wav`, then:
  - `load_take(file)` puts each take back under its own id and its lasting id (`TakeFile::rec`, a new one if it has none: [The recordings table](#the-recordings-table-phase-1c)). It takes the memory at once, then copies the audio in at `LOAD_SPEED` (64 frames for each frame that plays). A loading take plays nothing until it's done.
  - `load_track(&file)` puts back the settings and regions, and forgets the undo history.
  - `take_returned()` hands the loaded audio back for the host to drop away from the audio thread.
  - A load is refused, with the take given back, if the id is in use, the audio is the wrong length or the memory is short.
- **Where a track records from inside** is a `from=` word on its line (`TrackFile::from`), the app's text, kept as given (step 9; empty for the line input).
- **Unknown words and lines are skipped,** so a newer build's text still loads. Regions must be in order and must name takes of their own track.
- **Test:** a session (three comped loop passes, a stereo punch, gains and mixes) is saved, opened in fresh tracks, and plays back the same, sample for sample.

## The audio thread

`command` and `process` never allocate, lock or panic, and give the same output for any period size (a test compares 1, 7, 128, 999 and 4096 frames). `tests/no_alloc.rs` checks a busy session with a counting allocator: recording on three tracks, punches, loop recording, locates, a tempo change, comping, every edit, undo, redo, a song time edit with its undo, redo and forgetting, forgetting takes, a full memory and stops. `tests/retime.rs` checks song time edits: every track moves as one step that only the song's undo takes back, heard in its new place; Undo and Redo stop at it; a split over it makes the song's undo wait; a recording refuses it and nothing moves; a full history drops it whole; `ForgetSong` takes the steps beyond it on both sides; and `busy` sees an edit waiting but not a setting. `src/retime.rs`'s tests cut, move, join and refuse regions for each kind of edit.

`process(block, map, input, out)` adds every track to `out`. `track_out(track, frames)` gives each track's own sound after its level, pan, mute and solo, so the host can feed each audio track into its own mixer strip instead.

## Cost

`mpfree-bench` has three cases, counted against the looper's budget (3 % of a period) until there is a line of its own:

| Case | Instructions per 128-frame block (callgrind) | Desktop mean / p99 |
|---|---|---|
| Audio tracks: 8 playing comped takes | 44,270 | 2.9-4.9 us / 8-10 us |
| Audio tracks: 6 playing comped takes, 2 recording | 44,291 | 3.0-4.4 us / 9-10 us |
| Audio tracks: 6 playing comped takes, 2 recording to files | 44,798 | |
| For comparison: Looper, 8 loops of 3 layers playing | 298,470 | |

Each case is an 8-bar song loop with every track comped so that two takes crossfade on every beat. Using round 7's calibration (the MPC is roughly 6.6 to 10 times slower), 8 tracks should cost about 1 % of a period on average and about 3 % at the 99th percentile. That is an estimate, not a device number. The worst blocks on the cloud VM vary from run to run.

## In the app: the host

[`host.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/host.rs) is what the app wires in. `AudioHost` owns the tracks (on the audio thread) and a worker thread, `mpfree-audiotracks`, that makes and drops every big buffer, so the audio loop never allocates audio memory.

- **Each period:** `period(block, map, input, out)` runs the tracks on the transport's block, then tends the save, the fit and the buffers handed back by loads.
- **Save:** `save()` waits until nothing records or loads, has the worker make room for every take, then reads the takes at `READ_SPEED` (64 frames for each frame that plays). `saving()` says how far it has got. `take_saved()` gives the result, ready for `save::to_text` and `save::wav`.
- **Open:** `load(saved)` puts the takes and tracks back. Each take copies in over the next periods. Anything that can't be put back is named in the error, and the rest loads.
- **Bring a sound in:** `import(track, at, channels, audio, cuts, bpm)` loads `audio` (interleaved, at the tracks' rate) under `free_take_id()` as a take of its own on `track`, anchored at tick `at`, in a group of its own, with `bpm` for Fit to tempo. Once it has copied in, the host places it with `place` (a region a slice) and counts it in `imports`. One at a time: `importing()` names the take while it copies, and another is refused (`ImportRefused::Busy`), as are a sound with no free take id (`NoTake`), or bigger than the memory left (`NoRoom`). A refused sound's audio goes to the worker (`drop_audio`), never freed on the audio thread.
- **Fit to tempo:** `fit(id, bpm)` reads the take, stretches it on the worker, loads the result under a free id and swaps it in as one undo step. `fitting()` names the take while it runs. `fits` and `fits_failed` count the outcomes, so a failed fit is shown, not hidden.
- **Waveforms:** `wave(id, columns)` reads a take's peak table into up to `columns` columns (at most `MAX_COLUMNS`, 4096) at `WAVE_SPEED` (1024 frames of the take for each frame that plays). `take_wave()` gives a `TakeWave`: the lowest and highest sample of each column. A column covers whole blocks of the table, so a short take gets fewer columns. The pool lives with the tracks on the audio thread, so this is how the screen sees it. For a take that is recording, ask again as it grows.
- **Clear and cancel:** `AudioTracks::clear()` puts every track back as new (no regions, unarmed, settings as at start) and forgets every take with the undo history, without allocating; it is refused while a track records, a take copies in, or a take is still being copied out to its file (`writing`). `AudioHost::clear` also waits for the copies out, then lets go of every take's file (the files stay where they are). `cancel_save()` and `cancel_wave()` stop a save or a waveform under way and drop one finished but not taken; what they had goes to the worker. `load` hands what is left of the part to the worker too, and `drop_saved` drops a part that is not going in. `settings(track)` reads a track's input, monitor, level, pan, mute and solo back. `save::write_wav` writes a take's WAV to any writer a piece at a time.
- **Tests** (`tests/host.rs`): a save made across periods opens in a fresh host and plays back sample for sample; a save asked for while recording waits for the recording to end; a fit at half the tempo fills the bar, and Undo goes back; a waveform matches the take's samples column by column, and asking again replaces a wave not yet done; a cancelled save or waveform never comes and the next one works; a sound brought in with a cut plays from its bar as two regions, across the cut too, one Undo takes both off, a second waits for the first, and one bigger than the memory is refused; `clear` is refused while a track records, then empties everything, and the saved part loads back over it under its own take ids and plays the same. `tests/no_alloc.rs` runs `clear` and `settings` under the counting allocator.

## Recording to files (phase 3a)

The audio tracks rethink's phase 3 ([plan](https://claude.ai/artifact/QTRWuX9CrPdVRdf4A5e1HX), approved with the rethink on 22 Nov) moves the takes onto [`mpfree-stream`](disk-streaming.md). Phase 3a records every take to a file of its own as it records, through the stream layer's writer and the [recording file protocol](recording-files.md); phase 3b plays them from there. The old engine's streaming (`AudioTracks::stream`, the host's disk thread `disk.rs`, its tests and bench case) is gone: `mpfree-stream` replaces all of it.

**The engine** (`AudioTracks::files(true)`, refused while anything records or is still being copied out):

- **A next recording per track.** Each armed track holds the id its next take will record under (`next_recording(track)`: a `RecordingId` and the channels its input gives), chosen as it arms and again whenever its input changes, it is cleared, or a take starts. The engine asks for that take's files at once (`Io::Arm`, through `take_io()`, a fixed queue of 1,024), and lets them go when the track disarms (`Io::Disarm`). A take starts under that id (`Io::Start`) and stays **writing** until the host has copied all of it out (`written(take)`): a take that is writing can't be forgotten, and the tracks can't be cleared. A request lost to a full queue is counted (`Stats::io_dropped`) and the take records into memory only.
- `read_take` reads a take while it records, up to what it has, so the host can copy it out as it goes.

**The host** ([`host.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/host.rs) and [`files.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/files.rs)):

- **Turning it on:** `record_to(dir, cfg)` sends the folder to the worker, which starts the writer (`mpfree_stream::writer::Recorder`) there. It doesn't allocate: the 32 copy buffers (`SPARE`, a `CHUNK` each) are made with the host. Refused, with the folder given back to try again, while anything records or writes.
- **Files at arm.** The worker arms the writer for each `Io::Arm`: `NAME_ID.wav.recording` and its checkpoint, with NAME the track's name (`set_name`, kept as a `files::Name` without allocating: characters a file name can't hold become `-`, at most 48 bytes, "Audio" when empty) and ID the take's 32 hex digits. `armed_file(track)` says whether they are made yet. **A track whose files can't be made doesn't arm** (the protocol's rule): it is disarmed and `FileEvent::ArmFailed` says why. If its take had already started, it records into memory only (`NoFile`).
- **Copying out.** Each period copies at most `COPY_SAMPLES` (8,192 samples) of the recording takes into the spare buffers, a full buffer at a time, and sends them to the worker (`Push`), which hands them to the writer. That is about 94 chunks a second at 128-frame periods, more than 8 stereo tracks record. Once a stopped take is copied out whole the worker stops its file, the writer finishes it (`NAME_ID.wav`, synced and named) and the take is `written`.
- **Take states.** The worker polls the writer every 50 ms and tells the audio thread what changed (`file(id) -> FileView`): Recording, Saving, Saved, At risk or Stopped (with why), the seconds not yet safe and whether the writer's memory is nearly full. At risk, Stopped and back to Saved are `FileEvent::State`. Every event goes to the log through `take_event()` (`FileEvent::line`, in plain words); lane chips for them wait for the layout spec, and the status monitor will count them.
- **Save.** `save()` waits until every take's file is settled (no copy out pending, none Recording or Saving). The worker then names each take (`name_save`): a take whose finished file holds all of it gets `TakeFile::path` and no audio to read, so it is **copied whole from its file**; any other is read from memory as before, and a take recorded with a file that isn't whole says so (`FromMemory`). A take recorded with a file is named for it (`NAME_ID.wav`), one opened keeps its project's name, and any other (files off, or brought in) is `take_ID.wav`.
- **Where a saved take lives.** `saved_into(samples)`, after a project save (never an autosave): each take that save named, whose file is now in the project's `samples/`, is kept from there, and its copy in the work folder goes. A project opened tells the worker each take's file (`load` sends `Known`), so the next save copies it from the project.
- **Recovery at start:** `files::recover_into(work, keep)` runs the stream layer's recovery on the work folder before anything records: a take cut off while it recorded is recovered up to its last checkpoint, one cut off while it was being finished is finished, each is checked whole, and then every take left there (cut off, or recorded and never saved in a project) is moved into `keep`, the library's Recordings, named as it was. A take whose whole check failed is kept as `.unverified.wav`, and audio that couldn't be checked is kept apart. Nothing is deleted. The lines say what was found, for the log.

**Tests:**
- `tests/files.rs`: two tracks (mono and stereo, named "Vocal" and "Keys: L/R") make their files as they arm, record two bars, and a save asked for as recording stops waits for the files and copies them whole; each file holds the take frame for frame, from the punch in. A project save takes the files over from the work folder. A track whose folder was pulled away doesn't arm and says "No such file". A take whose host goes mid-take (as when the app is killed) is recovered at the next start up to its last checkpoint, frame for frame, into Recordings, and a second start finds nothing more.
- `tests/no_alloc.rs`: recording to files never allocates in the tracks, and the host's period allocates at most 4 times in a run of three tracks recording to files (the worker's replies).
- `files.rs`'s unit test: a track name becomes a file name, without allocating.

**Cost.** The bench case "Audio tracks: 6 playing comped takes, 2 recording to files" adds the copies out: 44,798 instructions a block, against 44,291 recording into memory only.

### Measuring the disk first

Jay chose (2026-11-12) to stream takes from the stick's data partition rather than hold them all in RAM: a small RAM buffer in front of the take files, a disk thread reading ahead of the playhead and writing behind a recording, and the first second or so of every region kept in RAM so jumps between comped takes and locates play at once. The audio thread only ever touches RAM, so streaming adds no latency. The risk is the stick pausing: a pause longer than the buffer would lose audio, which is counted and shown.

How big the buffer must be depends on the stick, so `mpfree-disktest` measures it ([`src/bin/disktest.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/bin/disktest.rs)). Every I/O is 128 KiB. It runs a sequential write, a write paced at 3.1 MB/s (8 stereo tracks recording) for 60 s with fdatasync every second, reads with the file's pages dropped from the cache (sequential, then 8 paced streams), and 4 writing and 4 reading streams at once. For each it gives p50, p99 and max time per call, the calls over 50, 100, 250 and 500 ms, and how far behind schedule it fell, which is the buffer a recording or playback would need.

- **Safety (rule zero).** With no arguments it runs only on `/run/mpfree-data`, and only if that is the mount of an ext4 file system labelled `MPFREE-DATA` on partition 4 of a USB disk, with no MMC device present (it reads the superblock, read-only, for the label). It writes only inside `mpfree-disktest/` there, with files it creates itself (`O_EXCL`, `O_NOFOLLOW`): at most 448 MiB at once and at most half the free space, refusing under 64 MiB free. It deletes the folder at the end, after an error, on Ctrl-C, or when the ssh session closes, and a later run clears a folder left by a power cut (only its own file names). Free space is printed before and after.
- **On the MPC:** [`tools/mpc-one/host_disktest_mpc.py`](../../../tools/mpc-one/host_disktest_mpc.py) copies the static armv7 build to `/run/mpfree-disktest` (RAM), checks its hash and runs it, as `host_bench_mpc.py` does for the benchmark. It needs `MPFREE_ACCEPT_DISKTEST=YES`, a terminal, and Jay's go for the step. Build: `RUSTFLAGS="-C target-feature=+crt-static -C relocation-model=static" cargo build --release --target armv7-unknown-linux-gnueabihf -p mpfree-audiotracks --bin mpfree-disktest`.
- **Reading only:** `mpfree-disktest --read-only` reads files already on partition 4 and writes nothing, with many paced streams and the tail of the read times; the streaming survey runs with it. Both are step D0 and D1 of [disk streaming](disk-streaming.md#p0-the-measuring-tools).
- **On a desktop:** `mpfree-disktest --desktop-test DIR [--quick]`. Tests: `tests/disktest.rs` (a quick run leaves nothing, a folder holding other files is left alone, a leftover folder of its own is cleared, no run off the stick without the flag) and `tools/mpc-one/tests/test_host_disktest_mpc.py` (against a fake test build under BusyBox).

## Playing from files (phase 3b)

Phase 3b plays takes from their files through the [stream layer](disk-streaming.md)'s cache, so the pool only has to hold what is recording. It is built a step at a time ([plan](https://claude.ai/artifact/QTRWuX9CrPdVRdf4A5e1HX), "Build order for 3b"); streaming stays off unless the Dev switch (step 3) turns it on, and on the MPC until the stick tests have run with Jay's go.

**Step 1, the engine** ([`lib.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/lib.rs)):

- **The tracks own the player.** `stream(Some(player))` gives the tracks the stream layer's sound-thread side (`mpfree_stream::player::Player`, made and boxed off the audio thread with every buffer it will use; its coordinator runs elsewhere), and gives back the one before, to be dropped off the audio thread. Each period begins and ends the player's period around the tracks' own.
- **Where a take's audio is.** In the pool, moving, or its file. A take's stream source is its id (`MAX_TAKES` is the layer's `MAX_SOURCES`). The readers that copy out of the pool (`read_take`, `peaks`, `sample`, `take_wav`) give None for a take on its file: the host reads its file instead (step 2).
- **One read path.** A region chooses the store by its take: the pool, or `Player::read` into a run's buffer, mixed through the same gains, fades and crossfades. A frame not in the cache yet, or a take whose source isn't open at its generation with its channels and length, plays silence and is counted: `Stats::missed` for all takes, `missed(id)` a take at a time with the song tick of the latest, for the log and later the lanes and the status monitor.
- **Telling the player where it plays.** After each period, each track names the region it plays (or the first one ahead) and the next one if that starts within `CUE_S` (4 s), through cursors `2 * track` and `2 * track + 1` by the region's parity, so a region keeps its cursor as it goes from next to playing: 16 cursors in all. The place is the take's frame there and the end of the region's crossfade. Stopped, the regions at the playhead are named, so Play finds their frames read. A track recording, or the tracks turned off, name nothing.
- **The move to a file.** `Command::OnFile { take, rec, gen }` (the host, once the take's file is Saved and opened as its source) marks the take moving. It moves once its source is open at `gen` and `MOVE_S` (2 s) of every region it plays or is about to, from where it plays, is in the cache (`Player::covers`), or at once if none plays it, stopped or playing: then its pool memory goes back. So the move never plays silence. A file whose channels or length don't match the take keeps it in the pool, counted (`Stats::mismatched`), and its source is closed. `rec` guards against a take forgotten meanwhile, its id given to another.
- **Opening a take as its file.** `add_file_take(&file, gen)` puts a saved take back as its source with nothing copied in and no pool memory taken (the host opens and checks the file first). It plays once the player has the source open.
- **Closing.** A take on (or moving to) its file that is forgotten or cleared asks the host to close its source (`Io::Close`).

**Tests** (`tests/stream.rs`, with the layer's coordinator reading real files on a stick that answers each read a set number of periods late): takes move to their files half a bar into the song, on a stick a quarter of a second slow, and not one sample of the song changes, with the pool then empty; a move waits, stopped at bar 1, until the frames there have come; saved takes opened as their files play exactly as their copies in memory; frames a stick never brings play silence and every one is counted, a take at a time; a file a frame short keeps its take in memory and is closed; forgetting and clearing close the sources; and 120 generated edits (comps, splits, erases, moves, gains, fades, undo and redo, three seeds) play the same from the files as from memory, every half bar compared. `tests/no_alloc.rs` plays from files under the counting allocator: a move while the song plays, frames missed, the player taken away and given back, a clear, and a take opened as its file.

**Cost.** With no player the tracks skip all of it: the three audio tracks bench cases rose about 1 % (44,735 instructions a block for 8 playing comped takes). A bench case playing from files waits for a way to feed the cache without writing files, since the bench also runs on the MPC.

**Not in step 1:** a stick slower than the gap between two short regions makes the third region's start late, since only two regions a track are read ahead. Holding every region's start in the cache answers that (step 4, heads, below).

**Step 2, the host** ([`host.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/host.rs), [`streaming.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/streaming.rs), [`files.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/files.rs)):

- **Starting.** `AudioHost::stream(StreamConfig)` turns streaming on, once, before `record_to`, so the writer is made with the coordinator's `Gate` and waits for playback's reads. The worker makes the player and the coordinator (`streaming::start`): the player goes to the audio thread, which gives it to the tracks; the coordinator runs on a thread of its own (`mpfree-at-stream`, stepped as often as it has work and every 2 ms when it has none), its reads on another (`mpfree-at-io`). If they can't start, the takes play from memory and the log says why (`FileEvent::NoStream`).
- **A recorded take moves to its file.** Once the writer says a take's file is Saved, the worker asks the stream thread to open it as the take's source and check that it holds the take: its channels and frames at the tracks' rate, and no less audio than its header says. The answer goes straight to the audio thread on the worker's channel, which sends `Command::OnFile` if the take is still there, the same recording, and not recording; the engine then moves it as step 1 says, and its memory is free for the next recording. A file that doesn't open or doesn't match keeps the take in memory, said in the log (`FileEvent::Unopened`, kept).
- **A project opens its takes as files.** A saved take with a file in the project (`TakeFile::path`) and its lasting id opens as that file with nothing copied in: `AudioHost::load` keeps its place (`AudioTracks::reserve`, so a recording can't take the id meanwhile), drops its audio on the worker (the app need not read it), and the take goes in with `add_file_take` once its file is open and checked (`AudioHost::opening` counts those still waiting). One that won't open is left out and said (`Unopened`, not kept). The project's tracks wait until every take has come in or been left out, then go in without the regions of a take left out, so no region ever plays a take that won't come (a track that started recording meanwhile is left out and said, `TrackLeftOut`). A save waits until they are all in, and another project can't load over them (clear first). Each open carries the tracks' generation, so an answer for tracks emptied since closes its file and changes nothing.
- **Reading a take on its file.** A waveform or a fit of a take on its file reads the whole file through the coordinator (`read_file`, a background job behind playback's reads), and the bytes come back to the worker, which makes the columns the way the pool does, or stretches it. A fit or waveform that was reading a take from memory when it moved goes on from its file. A save copies a take's finished file whole, as in 3a.
- **Closing.** A take forgotten or cleared closes its source (only if the source is still at the generation it was opened at: a new take may have the id by then), and emptying the tracks (`AudioHost::clear`) closes every source and empties the cache.

**Tests** (`tests/host_stream.rs`, `tests/sequences.rs`, `tests/no_alloc.rs`): a recorded take moves to its file, its memory freed, and the song played from the files is the same sample for sample as from memory; a project opens its takes as files with no pool memory taken, plays the same, saves again naming the same files, and opens again after being emptied; a waveform and a fit of a take on its file are those from memory; a project take whose file is missing is left out and said; streaming can't start once recording to files has. The generated sequences run again with every take recorded to a file and played from it (with plenty of memory and with a few seconds only): every promise holds after every step, saves name the files, and every save opens as its files in a fresh host. The host's period allocates nothing while takes record to files, move, play, are emptied and open again as files.

**Step 3, the app** ([`mpfree-app/src/audiotracks.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/audiotracks.rs), `main.rs`, `project.rs`):

- **The Dev switch.** Dev's options (MENU on any Dev page) have **Audio tracks from their files**: Off by default, kept as `stream-audio`, used from the next start ("On (next start)" until then). At start, if it is on, `TrackHost::stream` turns streaming on before the screen's recovery hands over the work folder (`record_to`), and the log says so with the cache's size. On the MPC it stays off until the stick tests (D1 to D3) have run with Jay's go; moving it to Prefs is Jay's call after that.
- **The cache** is as big as Prefs "Audio track memory" (`StreamConfig::for_memory`: its seconds of stereo, at least 4 MiB). The pool keeps its size too, for what records. The coordinator reads as far ahead as the cache holds, up to its 8 s window.
- **Opening a project.** While takes play from their files (`Settings::audio_streams`, set at start, never saved), `project::audio_open` reads no take's sound: it checks each file's header (`streaming::check_file`, the stream thread's own check) and passes its path, so the take opens as its file. Takes recorded at another rate, and a second take of one recording, are read as before. `TrackHost::opening` covers takes waiting for their files, so a project save waits for them.
- **Dropouts.** About once a second while it happens, `TrackHost::take_file_line` gives a line for the takes on their files that played silence since the last, with the track's name and the bar of the latest: "audio tracks: the drive fell behind: 0.3 s of Vocal went silent at bar 34". Dev's Performance page shows "Audio track takes on their files" and "Audio tracks silent, the drive late" (seconds since the start) while streaming is on. The lanes and the status monitor take these over when they can show them.

**Tests:** in `mpfree-app`, a project's take opens as its 30-second file with no memory taken, a jump the cache can't have yet plays silence that the Performance numbers count, and the log names the track and bar within a second; the Dev option is off by default, kept and used from the next start; `audio_open` with streaming reads no sound, refuses a file of another length by its header, and still reads takes at another rate. In `mpfree-audiotracks`, a project's tracks wait for its takes' files, and one emptied while they open lets nothing in.

**Step 4, region and loop starts** ([`host.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/host.rs) `tend_heads`, [`streaming.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-audiotracks/src/streaming.rs) `hold_heads`): every region of a take on its file has its start held in the cache, and so has each track's frame at the loop's start, so a jump, the loop going round, a region after a gap, or the third of three short regions never waits on the drive.

- **What is held.** From each region's first frame of its take, twice the longest stall the plan expects of the drive (`Plan::stall_s`, 0.5 s, so 1 s; never under 0.25 s), or to the take's end. With a loop set, the same from the frame each track's region plays at the loop's start (along the tempo map), unless a region starts there anyway. The host follows the loop from the transport's blocks (`Mark::LoopSet`), so the app passes nothing.
- **When.** About every 93 ms (32 periods of 128 frames) the host fingerprints the regions of takes on their files, the loop's start frames and which four bars the playhead is in, without allocating. When that changes, it fills a list made once at start (`MAX_HEADS`, every region of every track and a loop start each) and sends it to the stream thread: the starts ahead of the playhead first, nearest first (the loop's start by where the loop ends, as that is when it plays), then the ones behind it, most recent first.
- **On the stream thread** the old holds are let go and the new ones taken, 16 to a hold (`Coordinator::hold_all`, tier Head), in that order, until one doesn't fit beside what plays (at the least window) and what is reserved. The answer says how many are held and brings the list back.
- **What plays comes first.** When playing needs the room (more regions at once, the window growing), the coordinator gives back the last holds first; the stream thread tells the host how many are left.
- **Too small a cache.** If the cache can't hold every start, the soonest are held, the log says so ("audio tracks: the cache holds the starts of 16 of the 24 regions playing from the drive; the others may start late (a bigger Audio track memory helps)", `FileEvent::HeadsShort`) once for each number of regions, and again only if fewer still are held, and the host asks again every 2.7 s or so in case room came back. Dev's Performance page shows "Audio track region starts in the cache" (held of wanted) while streaming is on.
- **Cost.** The audio thread sorts at most a few thousand small entries when the regions change, and nothing else; each hold costs the stream thread a pass over the held units, so thousands of regions cost it a few milliseconds per change, off the audio thread.

**Tests** (`tests/host_stream.rs`): a 24-bar take cut in four has its four starts in the cache and not the middle of a region; playing, a jump into the middle of a region plays silence for a moment but a jump to a region's start doesn't; moving a region over the next moves the starts held. With a loop set inside a region, the frames at its start are held too, so going round never plays a frame late (without them it does), and clearing the loop lets them go. With a cache of 24 units and 24 regions, the first 16 starts from the playhead are held and the last 8 not, said once, and asking again a few seconds on says nothing more. The app's Performance numbers show the start held.

## In the pad player

[`rust/mpfree-app/src/audiotracks.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/audiotracks.rs) holds `TrackHost`, which owns the `AudioHost` in the app's audio loop.

- **Each period**, right after the looper (which reads the line input), `TrackHost::period` runs the tracks on the time engine's block and tempo map (`TimeHost::block`, `TimeHost::tempo_map`) with the looper's copy of the line input, and `TrackHost::play_into` adds each track's sound to its own channel (phase 1b, below). It sends `SetActive` as song mode turns on and off (`TimeHost::song_on`), so the tracks play and record in song mode only (layout spec 4c); monitoring goes on either way. REC records an armed track as the transport records.
- **Latency:** `TrackHost::follow_latency` sends the looper's line input latency (`Settings::loop_latency`, estimated from the audio buffer until set by hand) to the tracks as `SetLatency` whenever it changes, every period from the loop. Layout spec 4c asks for one line input latency shared with the looper; this is it. It is Prefs "Line input latency" (after Line input, before System info) and the Looper's options' "Latency to make up for": one `Item::LoopLatency`, named for where it shows.
- **Memory:** Prefs "Audio track memory" (layout spec 4c; `audiotracks::AudioMemory`, kept as `audio-memory` in seconds): 30 s, 1, 2, 5 or 10 min of stereo, 1 min (about 22 MB) by default, set aside at start, so a new size applies from the next start. Takes also stay in RAM while they play; each goes to a file as it records (phase 3a, below). With Dev's "Audio tracks from their files" on (phase 3b step 3, above), takes play from their files instead, through a cache as big again.
- **The Song view:** `TrackHost::fill` puts the lanes (`AudioLane`, built with `song::audio_blocks`), the waveforms, `audio_free` and `audio_counts` (refused, dropped, cut short, fits failed: shown while not zero) into the `TimeState` the app sends. `TrackHost::take_changed` checks a fingerprint of the lanes every 8 periods without allocating, so the state goes out again when a region, an arm, a recording or a counter changes.
- **Level bars** (layout spec 4c: 3 px along the header's bottom edge): each period `TrackHost::measure` takes each track's peak from `AudioTracks::track_out` (after its level, pan, mute and solo) and, while the track is armed, from the line input it records (its input's side or both), as a bar from -48 dB to 0 dB that falls back over 1.5 s. Every 50 ms while Arrange shows, the loop asks `TrackHost::take_levels`, which gives the eight bars only when one has moved by 1 % since, and sends them as `ToUi::AudioLevels([f32; 8])`: no box, no whole `TimeState` (too costly a few times a second), and not counted as an edit. The screen puts them in its copy of the lanes (`AudioLane::level`) and redraws the page. `TrackHost::fill` puts the bars in each full state too.
- **Waveforms** are read a take at a time with `AudioHost::wave` (one column per block of the peak table, at most 4096); a recording take is read again about every quarter second as it grows. Replaced waves go back to the host's worker to be freed (`AudioHost::drop_wave`), so the audio thread never frees.
- **A sound from the Sample page** (its Slice tool's Put on Audio track, the one Sample page's step 6; as the MPC's Audio Track from Samples): the page has the sound the slices span copied out at the app's rate on the sampler's job service (`SamplePage::send_to_audio`, converting a sound at another rate, after a memory check; never on the screen) and sends it once made with its cuts and tempo as `FromUi::SampleToAudio(Box<audiotracks::Sound>)`. `TrackHost::bring` puts it on the first empty audio track (no region, not armed), else the first with nothing from the bar on, from the start of the bar the playhead is in, and sends `AudioHost::import`; it waits while a track records. With no audio track there (`TrackHost::bring_track`), the app first adds one at the end of the track list (`desk.rs`, `TimeAction::AddAudioTrack`), not armed. Where it went, or why not (another still going in, no track free from that bar, the memory: Prefs > Audio track memory), is logged and said in place of the hint (`ToUi::Tell`); with song mode off it adds that the track plays in song mode. With every engine track bound and none empty or free from that bar, nothing goes on. `Sound`'s `Debug` names it without its samples, since actions are logged by name.
- **Touches** (`AudioAction`): Arm (with a note when the line input is off), Split at the playhead, Erase (the picked region), Undo, Redo (SHIFT + Undo on the audio row: the screen turns `Undo` into `Redo` while SHIFT is held, layout spec 4c), and a region's drag, `Move { region, to, fine }`: the host snaps `to` to the nearest sixteenth unless `fine` (SHIFT, set by the screen), refuses it while the track records, and sends `Command::Move`; once the engine has the region at its new start, `TrackHost::take_repick` gives its new index and the loop picks it again. Each answer is a log line. A pick stays with the time engine.
- **Song time edits** (the Arrange refactor's 3.4): `TrackHost` is the time engine's `SongAudio` (`time/control.rs`). Before the song changes, `TimeControl::act_with_audio` asks it (`refused`): a project's tracks still going in, `busy`, a track recording ("Vocal is recording (stop it first)") or no room refuses the song edit, and nothing changes anywhere. After it, the host sends `Command::Retime` numbered by the song edit's take, which applies at the start of the next period, the one the song's edit starts in. UNDO and REDO of that take send `SongUndo` and `SongRedo` with it. The audio row's Undo and Redo stop at its step and say that UNDO, or SHIFT + UNDO, moves it with the song; when the song's history no longer reaches it (`TimeHost::song_reaches`), they send `ForgetSong` and say there is nothing to take back (`TrackHost::forget_song_step`). See [arrange.md](arrange.md#in-the-pad-player-timehost).
- **Take lanes and comping** (layout spec 4c): `Takes(track)` (the panel's Takes or the region row's) opens the track's take lanes, or closes them; it closes the panel, which would sit where they go, and is refused while the track has no take. The host keeps which track's show and sends it as `TimeState::audio_takes`, and fills every lane's `AudioLane::takes` (`song::take_lanes`: newest first, named "take N" oldest first as the regions' tags are, each with its stretch along the tempo map and where the track's regions play it). A swipe across a take lane sends `Comp { take, from, to, fine }`: the host snaps both ends to the nearest sixteenth (`GRID`, at least one step) unless `fine` (SHIFT, set by the screen), then sends `Command::Comp`. A tap sends `UseTake(take)`, which the host sends as `Comp` over all ticks; the engine keeps it to where the take has audio. Both are refused while the take's track records. Undo takes each comp back, as the engine's history holds them.
- **The track panel** (layout spec 4c): a tap on a header's name or chip sends `AudioAction::Panel(track)`, which opens that track's panel or closes it if it is open; `ClosePanel` closes it. The host keeps which panel is open and sends it as `TimeState::audio_panel`; a track with its panel open shows its lane even when empty. The panel (drawn by `SongLanes` in `mpfree-time-ui/src/song.rs`, under the track's lane if it fits, else over it) has Arm, Mute, Solo, Takes, the input (`song::INPUTS`: L, R, L+R, ST), the monitor mode (`song::MONITORS`: Auto, In, Off), the level and pan with a step either way (`Turn` with no region, the strip's since phase 1b), and "Its strip in Mix ›" (`AudioAction::Strip`). The host sends `SetInput` (refused while the track records), `SetMonitor`, `Mute` and `Solo`, keeps what it sent, and carries it back in `AudioLane::input`, `monitor`, `mute` and `solo`.
- **Recording from inside the mix** (the instrument redesign's step 9, [instruments.md](instruments.md#step-9-recording-points-for-audio)): the panel's **Mix** (`AudioAction::Mix`), its From steps and its Effects (`MixEffects`) make a track record an instrument's channel, Drums, Synth, Loop, a submix or the master (`MixHost::inside_sources`, before or after its effects, before its fader either way). `TrackHost::feed_inside` copies each such track's capture point (`MixHost::audio_take`, slots `AUDIO_CAPTURE`..) into the engine before the tracks run, with the latency of the capture and one period. The lane carries it back as `AudioLane::mix`; the project keeps it as the track's `from=` word.
- **Q-Links with a region picked or a panel open** (layout spec 4c): the screen shows the cards from `audiotracks::audio_knobs` (Q1 the track's level, Q2 its pan, Q3 the region's gain, Q4 its fades) and sends each turn as `AudioAction::Turn { track, region, .. }`. The panel's track wins over a picked region's; Q3 and Q4 turn a region only when one of that track's is picked, and say "pick one" otherwise. Q1 and Q2 move the track's mixer strip (phase 1b, below); Q3 and Q4 become `SetGain` / `SetFades` on the region (read back through `AudioBlock::gain` and the fades). Steps: 0.5 dB (0.1 with SHIFT) for level (-60 dB is off, +6 dB the top) and gain (to +12 dB), 2 (1) of the strip's 100 for pan, 5 ms (1 ms) for both fades together. Gain and Fades in the bottom row point at Q3 and Q4. A turn writes no log line.
- **Projects** (part `audiotracks`; the format is in [browser.md](browser.md#the-parts)). A save asks `AudioHost::save` with the looper's save; the audio loop holds whichever finishes first and collects the parts once both have (`project::Collected::audio`). The screen keeps the name the host gave each take (`project::audio_part`, a `TakeAudio` each): a take with a finished file of its own is copied into the project's `samples/` whole (`NewSample::file`), any other is written there from its samples, and a name `samples/` already has is not written again. Once the save has finished (`ProjectSaved`, not an autosave), the screen sends `FromUi::AudioSavedInto` with the project's `samples/` (`Library::samples_dir`) and `TrackHost::saved_into` passes it on, so the work folder's copies go. Opening any project, or New, calls `TrackHost::clear` (which empties the tracks with `AudioHost::clear`: a fit still under way is the old project's, and whatever it still sends is dropped as it comes, [Project safety](project-safety.md#late-results-never-reach-the-wrong-project-03-as-built)): the save under way stops, every armed track is disarmed (a recording ends), and once nothing records the tracks are emptied, the waveforms and the panel, take lanes and "+ Audio" lanes go, and this side's copy of each track's settings is read back from the tracks (`AudioTracks::settings`). The project's part, read on the screen's `audio-open` thread (`project::audio_open`), comes as `FromUi::OpenAudio` and goes in with `TrackHost::open` once the tracks are empty: `AudioHost::load`, then the settings read back, the saved tracks' lanes shown, and a log line ("project: 2 audio tracks and 3 takes in"; a take that didn't fit the memory is named, and `TrackHost::take_left_out` tells the screen, `ToUi::LeftOut`, so the project isn't saved over, as for a take whose file `audio_open` couldn't read: [Project safety](project-safety.md#a-project-that-opened-with-things-left-out-is-never-saved-over-as-built)). `TrackHost::opening` holds a save off until the takes have copied in. A project with no part (saved before it had one) has no audio tracks: they are emptied, as for New project, and a part this build can't read stops the open before anything changes ([Project safety](project-safety.md)).
- **Recording to files** (phase 3a): at start, once the library is open, the screen runs `files::recover_into` on the audio tracks' work folder (`Library::work_dir("audiotracks")`, `library/.work/audiotracks/`) on a thread of its own (`audio-recover`, `Player::recover_audio_files`), into the library's Recordings; it says the lines, tells the musician when takes were recovered, then sends `FromUi::AudioFiles` with the folder. `TrackHost::record_to` hands it to the host, again each period while a track records. Without a work folder the takes record into memory only, and the log says why. `TrackHost::sync` keeps each engine track's file name up to date with its track's name ("Audio N" when it has none, as its lane says), and `TrackHost::take_file_line` turns the host's `FileEvent`s into log lines that name the track. Opening a project reads each take's file where it is (`project::audio_open` with `Library::sample_path`) and keeps that path, so the next save copies it from there.
- **Tests:** `audiotracks.rs` records a tone in song mode onto an armed track, checks the lane, the red block, the waveform and the playback, and that the tracks are silent out of song mode; Split, Erase and Undo, and each refusal's reason; the panel's monitor modes, mute, solo and input by what the tracks play; two takes over a bar, the take lanes, a snapped swipe, a free one, Undo and a tap, by the regions the engine then has; steady play with the waveform read neither allocates nor frees; a project opening while a take records ends it, empties the tracks and puts the saved part back (regions, settings, lanes, waveform, playback), and New empties them; the level bars follow the armed input and the playback, fall back, and are sent only when they move; SHIFT + Undo redoes an erase, once; a sound from the Sample page goes on the first empty track from the playhead's bar a region a slice, the next waits for it, then takes the next track, with none empty the first free from that bar, Undo takes it off, and one bigger than the memory says where to get more. `project.rs` names takes by their audio (one file for equal takes, two for the same samples as mono and stereo), reads them back, and leaves out a take whose file is missing or another length with its regions. `gui.rs` saves a project with an audio track and opens it again, the take coming back from its WAV. `song.rs` checks the panel's place and that each control reports itself, and `view.rs` that they ask for the open panel's track. `rt_safety.rs` runs the tracks (one armed) in the sound path under the counting allocator.

## In the one track list (phase 1a)

The audio tracks rethink ([plan](https://claude.ai/artifact/2rrcXW5CTeSmEuMA6EWwu7), approved 22 Nov) makes an audio track a track like any other, in the project's one track list, instead of the 8 audio tracks under the song. Phase 1a is the model, the format, the engine's binding and the screens (layout spec 4c, 3a and 4f, approved as drawn 22 Nov 07:28Z); the own mixer channel is phase 1b.

- **The model** (`mpfree-seq`): `TrackKind::Audio`, made with `Track::new_audio(name, dest)`. `Track::is_audio()` says it; `Track::plays_notes()` is false for an audio track and a loop track, and the places that send notes, arm for notes, edit steps or take clips ask it. `Project::check` refuses clips on an audio track (`ModelError::AudioTrack`), and the arrangement refuses placements on one, and moving one onto one. An audio track keeps its lasting id, name, mute and solo like any track; its regions and takes stay in `mpfree-audiotracks`.
- **Adding one:** `TimeAction::AddAudioTrack` adds "Audio N" (the first N not taken) at the end of the track list, as one undo step, and refuses it with every engine track bound ("8 audio tracks is the most this MPFree plays") or the list full. Undo takes it away, Redo brings it back. "+ Track" (the Clips grid's title line and the Song view's last lane) opens a chooser, Note track, Audio track, Loop track (`TimePage::chooser`, the page's own state; `song::ADD_KINDS`, `widgets::ADD_KIND`); SHIFT + "+ Track" still adds a Loop track at once. An audio track added from the chooser is armed once it has an engine track (`TrackHost::arm_next`, within `ARM_WINDOW` periods), and the page brings the new track into view (its grid page; the Song view's lanes scrolled to the end, `first_lane = usize::MAX`).
- **The screens** (layout spec 4c, 4f): the Song view draws a lane a track in track order, an audio track's lane in its place (`AudioLane::row`, filled by `TrackHost::fill`), its take lanes under it, then "+ Track"; there is no audio block, divider or "+ Audio". The Clips grid shows an audio track as a column with a wave mark and its input under the name (`TrackInfo::audio`, `GridTrack::audio`; the input chip filled by `gui::fill_sounds`), its cells dashed and empty; a tap on one says to record it in the Song view. TRACK MUTE mutes it as any track.
- **The engine stays as it is.** `AudioTracks` keeps 8 fixed engine tracks (`MAX_TRACKS`). `audiotracks::Slots` maps each engine track to the lasting id of the project track it plays (`TrackId::NONE` for a free one). `TrackHost::sync(project, silenced, now)` runs every period from the loop: every 8 periods (`CHECK_EVERY`), or at once with `now` after an add, Undo, Redo, mute or solo, it finds each audio track's place in the list, gives an audio track with no engine track the first free empty one (its settings as new), and frees an engine track whose project track is gone only once it is empty, so Undo of a removal finds its regions where they were. It never allocates.
- **Mute and solo are the track's.** The panel's Mute and Solo (`AudioAction::Mute`, `Solo`) are turned into the time engine's track mute and solo (`TimeHost::mute_or_solo`), so a solo on a note track silences an audio track and the other way round. Each period `sync` sends `Command::Mute(slot, quiet)` when an engine track's silence changes: quiet while its track is muted, left out by a solo anywhere in the project (`TimeHost::silenced`, one bit a track), or gone. The engine's own solo is no longer used; `AudioLane::mute` and `solo` are read from the project.
- **Saving:** `TrackHost::take_saved(project)` writes each engine track's `id=` and leaves out an engine track whose project track is gone. A track line holds only the engine's own settings (input, monitor, what it records from inside): mute and solo are in the `clips` part, level and pan in the `mixer` part. The `clips` part is `mpfree-clips 3` while any track is audio, with an `audio` line in that track's block, so an older build refuses it; the audio part is `mpfree-audiotracks 2`; `project.txt` says `needs = audio-tracks` ([Project safety](project-safety.md)). Both formats are in [browser.md](browser.md#the-parts).
- **Opening** (`project::audio_slots`, run in `project::opening` before anything changes): the part's lines name their tracks by id; a line naming a track the project hasn't got, or a note track, stops the open. An audio track the part doesn't name gets the first free engine track; more audio tracks than 8 stops the open. A `mpfree-audiotracks 1` part, saved before audio tracks were tracks, stops the open too, saying so (`save::OLDER`: "its audio tracks were saved by an older mpfree, before audio tracks were tracks; this mpfree doesn't open them"). Jay, 22 Nov: mpfree is early and has no serious projects yet, so correctness and clean code come before opening old files; there is no conversion. The loop binds the table (`TrackHost::bind`) just before it empties the tracks for the new project.
- **Arming** stays with the audio tracks for now (their own arm dot); arming an audio track from the track list is refused with where to arm it. One arm for every kind of track is phase 3.
- **The lines name the track as the musician named it** ("Vocal: region split", "Vocal armed: REC records it in song mode"), not by its engine track: `sync` keeps each engine track's project track name (`TrackHost::names`, room for `NAME_ROOM` bytes made at start, so it never allocates; a longer name is cut short), and `TrackHost::who` gives it, or "Audio N" for a track with no name. A track UNDO took away keeps its name for the lines about its regions.
- **Tests:** `mpfree-seq` reads an audio track back in a part of its own format and refuses clips and placements on one; `mpfree-audiotracks` reads and writes `id=`, refuses a repeated id and refuses version 1 (`a_part_from_before_audio_tracks_were_tracks_is_refused`); `project::tests::audio_tracks_find_their_tracks_by_id_and_an_older_part_is_refused` (ids, free engine tracks, refusals) and `a_project_with_an_audio_track_says_it_needs_audio_tracks`; `audiotracks.rs` adds a track, binds and frees engine tracks across Undo, and checks that track mute, a solo on another track and a removed track silence the right engine track; `split_erase_and_undo_edit_the_region_and_refusals_say_why` renames the track and its line follows.

## Its own mixer strip (phase 1b)

Decision 1 of the plan: the mixer owns an audio track's level and pan; its mute and solo are the track's.

- **Routing.** Each period, after `TrackHost::period`, `TrackHost::play_into(&mut MixHost)` adds each bound engine track's `AudioTracks::track_out` (interleaved stereo) into `mixer.track_input(mix.track_channel(place))`, the channel any track has (`mixing::track_channel`; an audio track is never track 1, so never on Drums). It sets `MixHost::audio_tracks` (a bit a project track), which `channels_in_use` counts, so the strip shows and stems and throws list it. The shared Audio strip (`mixing::AUDIO`) gets no sound; `MixHost::period` takes only the synth and the loop. The engine tracks stay at level 1 and pan 0 (`AudioTracks::clear` sets them so, a load leaves them, and `TrackHost::action` refuses knobs 0 and 1), so nothing counts twice.
- **Level and pan.** The panel's steps and Q1, Q2 (`AudioAction::Turn` knob 0 or 1) are caught in `main.rs`'s `time_action` and become `MixAction::Set` on the strip's `VOLUME` (dB, 0.5 or 0.1 a step, from -60 dB, which is off, to +6) and `PAN` (-100 to 100, 2 or 1 a step, snapping to centre). They step from `MixHost::wanted` (`Mixer::wanted`: the last value queued since the last block), so turns between two periods add up. Each period `play_into` mirrors the strip into `TrackHost::volume` and `pan` (0 to 2 and -1 to 1), which the lane, the panel and the cards show (`audio_knobs` labels Q1 and Q2 "ITS STRIP").
- **"Its strip in Mix ›"** (`song::PANEL_STRIP`, `AudioAction::Strip(track)`): `time_action` picks the strip (`MixAction::Select`), opens the Mix tab (`App::open_tab`) and its Tracks view; the mixer pages to the picked strip.
- **The strip on the Mix tab.** `TrackHost::dress_strips` fills the view's strips of the audio tracks after `poll_state`: the track's name and colour (`StripView::color`), the input as the screen names it (`StripView::audio`: "In L", "Mix"), drawn right of the name with a wave mark, mute and solo from the project, `audible` from `silenced`, and no freeze. M and S tapped there (`MixAction::Set` of `MUTE` or `SOLO` on an audio track's channel) are caught in `desk.rs` (`audio_track_on`, `audio_strip_switch`) and become `TimeHost::mute_or_solo`, so a solo is project-wide.
- **The old Audio strip** gets no sound. It shows, named "Audio (old)" (`mixing::OLD_AUDIO`), only while it holds settings or effects a project an older mpfree saved gave it (`MixHost::old_audio`), so they aren't hidden; stems and throws leave it out otherwise. Nothing moves them anywhere.
- **The looper's pads, wet** (`MixHost::pads_wet`) take each audio track's channel out of the master's input, as they take out the Loop track and the line input: `MixHost::point_audio_out` points capture `AUDIO_OUT_CAPTURE + slot` at the channel after its fader (only on a change), and one going to a submix is refused with "an audio track goes to a submix".
- **Recording from inside** (`MixHost::inside_sources`) never offers an audio track's own channel (it is neither an instrument's nor a kit's).
- **Stems by Track** (`export::track_stems`): `TrackHost::heard` lists each bound audio track with a region that is heard (not silenced), with its strip and name, in track order. Each is a first-pass stem from its own strip, among the other tracks in track order; there is no audio replay any more (`Only::AudioTrack` is gone, and `AudioTracks::set_only` is unused by the app).
- **Saving and opening.** The audio part holds no level, pan, mute or solo: the strips (the `mixer` part) hold level and pan, the `clips` part mute and solo. A part an older mpfree saved, with them, is refused as it opens (phase 1a).
- **Tests:** `audiotracks::tests::each_audio_track_plays_into_its_own_mixer_channel` (only its own channel, in use, the strip mirrored, `heard`, a mute leaves it out); `main.rs`'s `an_audio_track_s_level_pan_and_strip_button_are_its_mixer_strip_s` (two turns add up, pan, the strip button opens the Mix tab with the strip picked); `desk.rs`'s `an_audio_track_s_strip_m_and_s_are_the_track_s`; `mixing.rs`'s `audio_tracks_show_their_own_channels_and_the_old_strip_only_with_settings` and `the_pads_wet_leave_out_each_audio_track_s_own_channel`; `export.rs`'s stems by track with audio tracks in their places; `mpfree-fx`'s `wanted_is_the_last_value_queued_else_the_value_now`; `song.rs` and `view.rs` the panel's strip button.

## The recordings table (phase 1c)

The plan keeps recordings, clips, tracks and players apart: a recording is sound recorded or brought in, and the project keeps a table of them, each with a lasting id that is saved and never reused. Phase 1c gives the audio tracks' takes those ids.

- **`RecordingId`** (`mpfree-audiotracks`): a 128-bit number, 0 for none, written as its 32 hex digits (`Display`, `RecordingId::parse`), the same number the recording files of [recording-files.md](recording-files.md) carry in their names (`NAME_ID.wav`). Every take has one (`TakeInfo::rec`): given as it starts, whether recorded (`punch_in`, on the audio thread), brought in from the Sample page (`AudioHost::import`) or a stretched copy (`stretch`), and kept from its save when it loads (`load_take`, `add_take`). A take loaded with no id, or with one another take already has, gets a new one, so two takes never share one.
- **Never reused.** `AudioTracks` makes ids with a key drawn at random when it is made (`RandomState` and the clock) and a count put through SplitMix64's one-to-one finish, without allocating: no id repeats in a run, runs don't meet but by a chance of about one in 2^128, and a forgotten take's id is never given again.
- **The take's `u16`** stays its place in the engine. Regions and the undo history's snaps name that place, not the lasting id, so they cost nothing more; a place that a region or an undo step names is never given to another take (`in_use`, which refuses `Forget`), so undo steps hold their recordings. `AudioTracks::take_of(rec)` finds a recording's place, for the clips and the streaming layer that will name recordings by id.
- **Saved** as `rec=` on each take line (`TakeFile::rec`), part of `mpfree-audiotracks 2` (phase 1's format). Every take line has one: `save::from_text` refuses a line without it, and an id that isn't 32 hex digits, is 0, or is there twice.
- **Files.** Every recording is the project's own, its WAV in the project's `samples/`, at the part's rate. Since phase 3a a take recorded with files on is named for its track and its id (`NAME_ID.wav`, made at arm), and any other for its id (`take_ID.wav`); a project saved by phase 1 keeps its content-named files (two takes with the same audio share one). Recordings from outside the project (who owns a file), files at their own rates and reading takes through the streaming layer's sources, keyed by these ids, are phase 3b and later.
- **A stretched copy** (Fit to tempo) is a recording of its own for now; the plan's cache of derived copies, filed under the recording they came from, is the tempo phase's.
- **Tests:** `mpfree-audiotracks`'s `tests/save.rs` `each_recording_keeps_its_lasting_id` (ids given and distinct, `take_of`, saved and opened twice to the same text, a part without ids refused, a forgotten take's id never given again, a repeated id replaced), `save.rs`'s `each_recording_keeps_its_lasting_id` (the text, refusals) and `recording_ids_never_repeat`; `project.rs`'s audio part test (ids through `audio_part` and `audio_open`); `audiotracks.rs`'s `a_project_opening_empties_the_tracks_and_puts_its_part_in` and `gui.rs`'s `a_project_saves_opens_again_and_new_starts_empty` (the id through a save and an open).

## Joining the rest of MPFree

- **The arrangement.** Audio tracks keep their own regions on song ticks, next to the arrangement's MIDI placements (PR #160), so the two are independent. The Song view can draw each audio track as one more lane, with its take lanes under it for comping. `SetActive(false)` silences the tracks when the song is not playing (clip launch mode), if that is what the time engine wants.
- **Live to song (PR #164)** records the launch grid into the arrangement. Recording audio at the same time is just an armed audio track while the transport records.
- **The line input** comes from the pad player's capture (`alsa.rs`), as for the looper. A mixer capture point (`Mixer::set_capture`) lets a track record an instrument, a submix or the master instead (step 9, built 20 Nov).
- **The screen.** `regions`, `takes`, `take`, `peaks` (waveform), `recording`, `armed`, `history`, `memory` and `stats` are what a page needs. Where audio tracks go in the layout is the app thread's call, and needs Jay's approval.
- **Export.** `take_wav(id)` writes a take as a 32-bit float WAV for the store (it allocates, so not on the audio thread).
