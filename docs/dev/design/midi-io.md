# MIDI I/O, sync, MIDI Learn and CV/gate (`rust/mpfree-midi`, `rust/mpfree-midi-ui`)

The plan for MPFree's external MIDI and CV, and the contract of the host-first crate `mpfree-midi`. Scope:

- MIDI in from the 5-pin MIDI In jack and from USB keyboards on the USB-A socket;
- MIDI tracks out of the MIDI Out jack;
- clock sync in and out;
- MIDI Learn;
- the CV/Gate outputs.

The MPC's own pads, knobs and buttons are not part of this. They come through the controller protocol (`mpfree-controller`) and the app's mapper.

The owner-facing design and architecture doc, with the research below in full and the open decisions, is [MPFree MIDI, sync and CV/gate: design and architecture](https://claude.ai/artifact/RxdZ76ve5hE5VptdkU3JMo); the proposed screens are drawn in [MPFree MIDI screens](https://claude.ai/artifact/5ygP92smCQVhu31qq2kfYZ). This page stays the source of truth for the hardware facts and the crate.

Device safety (rule zero) applies to every step below that touches the MPC. Each such step is labelled read-only or state-changing, and waits for the owner's go.

### The read-only check that settles the DIN cable and the CV path

`MPFREE_ACCEPT_USBMON=YES MPFREE_CAPTURE_PHASES=midi-cv bash tools/mpc-one/collect-over-ssh.sh --midi-capture`. It is **read-only on the MPC** (the opt-in passive usbmon reader, see [audio-midi-diagnostics](../guides/audio-midi-diagnostics.md)) and runs on stock 3.6 with Akai's app.

The owner sets up a MIDI track (output MIDI Out) and a CV track, without saving anything. The tool then walks through six phases:

1. idle;
2. notes into MIDI In;
3. pads on the MIDI track;
4. pads on the CV track;
5. one held CV note;
6. one CV slider.

The report counts messages per cable against idle, which shows:

- which cable carries DIN In and Out under Akai's app;
- whether CV moves anything on USB, and in what form.

If the CV phases show no USB traffic, the CV is driven some other way. The next read-only step is then the device tree (`offline-dtb.py show` on the owner's saved tree) for a DAC or SPI node.

**First result (2026-11-12):** MIDI In is cable 2 in, and the CV jacks are driven by CCs on cable 1, channel 16. Nothing was seen for MIDI Out. Details are in [controller-protocol](../hardware/controller-protocol.md#the-rear-jacks-under-akais-app-2026-11-12).

**Second round (2026-11-12):** `MPFREE_CAPTURE_PHASES=cv-scale`, read-only in the same way. The keyboard on MIDI In played known notes (C in three octaves, then four semitones) into the CV track, and the capture saw those note numbers on cable 2, so no pad layout was needed. Result: a CV track's pitch is `floor((note − 36) × 16383 / 60)`, and Akai's CV ports 3 and 4 are the next CC pairs (CC 4 to 7). A MIDI track again sent nothing on USB. Details are in [controller-protocol](../hardware/controller-protocol.md#second-round-the-cv-scale-and-the-next-outputs-2026-11-12), and the bytes are in `cv_wire`, below.

**Third round (2026-11-12):** `MPFREE_CAPTURE_PHASES=midi-out`, read-only in the same way. In rounds 1 and 2 the MIDI track had only its MIDI Output Ch set; the owner found that its MIDI Out routing also had to be set (to "MPC", 1), and a synth on the jack played. Result: the MIDI Out jack is **cable 2 out**. Notes leave on the track's channel 5 to 8 ms after they arrive, note-offs as `80 nn 00`, pad pressure as poly pressure. PLAY and STOP sent no clock. Details are in [controller-protocol](../hardware/controller-protocol.md#third-round-the-midi-out-jack-2026-11-12).

M0 is done: both 5-pin jacks are cable 2 of `midiC0D0`, and the CV jacks are CC pairs on cable 1. Clock out from Akai's app was not seen (Sync may have been off); it is not needed for MPFree's own clock out, which M4 writes to cable 2 itself. The CV volts per count need a meter on a jack while MPFree sends a known value, which is state-changing and belongs to M6.

**No MIDI port and no CV output is opened by MPFree until the owner has seen these results.**

## The crate: `mpfree-midi`

Bytes and volts only. It opens nothing, it has no unsafe code, and every part that can run on the audio thread or the MIDI reader thread is free of allocation after `new`, which `tests/no_alloc.rs` checks.

| Module | What it does |
|---|---|
| `port` | External ports. `DIN` is always id 0. A USB device gets its old id back when it is plugged in again, by name, so its track inputs, Learn mappings and clock settings follow it. There are 16 ports at most (`MAX_PORTS`): the 5-pin jacks and up to 15 USB ports, one for each MIDI port of a USB device |
| `msg` | Channel messages as values, read from `mpfree_controller::midi::Parser` output. A note-on with velocity 0 is a note-off |
| `input` | `InputRouter`: every byte of every external port in, then track messages, `Seen` messages (for Learn, the monitor and the port lights) and clock bytes out. **Selected** mode (the default, as on Akai's MPC): any port and channel plays the track on screen. **Per-track** mode: each track has its own port, channel and transpose. **Routes** (`set_routes`, up to `MAX_ROUTES` = 32): a port and a channel (or all) play one track of up to 128, as `In::Routed`, whichever track is selected; a message a route takes goes only to its routes, never to the selected track as well (see [Routes](#routes-keyboards-on-several-tracks)). A note-off always goes where its note-on went, even if the selection or the routes changed while the key was held. Unplugging a port ends its notes. Only the chosen clock source reaches the clock. Each port can ignore clock, program, aftertouch (key and channel pressure) and All notes off (`set_ignore`, `setup::IgnoreSet`): an ignored message is still `Seen` (for the monitor), then `Ignored`, and is never routed, learned or clocked. All sound off, All notes off and the omni and mono/poly messages (CC 120, 123 to 127) that aren't ignored let go of the keys the port holds on that channel |
| `output` | `OutputRouter`: each MIDI track has a port and channel. A route change, stop or panic ends exactly the notes that are on, never 2048 blind note-offs. `OutQueue` holds one port's period, in frame order, with clock bytes first among messages at the same frame, and writes the bytes, with running status if asked. `DinPacer` models the 31250 bit/s wire: a note takes 0.96 ms and a 16-note chord about 15 ms. It reports how late each message and each clock pulse leaves |
| `learn` | `LearnMap`: arm a parameter (`mpfree_param::ParamAddr`), move a control, and it is learned. CC, note, pitch bend and channel pressure can be learned. Modes are absolute, relative (two's complement, offset 64, sign bit), toggle and momentary. Range and inversion are per mapping, and so is takeover: jump, or pickup, which waits until the control passes the value. `takes` says whether a message is Learn's (an armed press or move, a mapped control), so the app doesn't also play it; a key's release while armed is not, so a held note still ends. `observe` gives a parameter's value set elsewhere, for pickup, encoders and toggles. `Saved` is a mapping by device name, as a project keeps it (`MidiSetup::learn`, `learn = …` lines) |
| `cv` | `CvEngine`: 8 outputs. Each one is pitch (1 V/oct or Hz/V; last, low or high note priority; bend range), gate (with an optional retrigger gap), trigger (any note or one note, for drum modules), velocity, pressure (the track's last channel or poly pressure, 18 Nov 2026), a CC, an LFO or an envelope (`cv_mod`), clock (any PPQN), run, reset, or fixed (the CV track's sliders). Pitch holds after the gate falls, so release tails stay in tune. Calibration (gain and offset) is per output. It emits a `CvChange` only when an output's voltage changes. **No range is assumed**: the hardware layer will clamp once the range is measured |
| `cv_mod` | CV outputs that move on their own (18 Nov 2026, plan item 3.9). `Lfo`: a `Shape` (Sine, Triangle, Ramp up and down, Square, Random, Drift; Random and Drift take a level per cycle from a hash of the cycle's number, so the same bars give the same levels), a `Rate` (`Hz`, free; `Beats`, quarter notes a cycle at the song's tempo), Low and High volts, a track whose notes restart it, and `Notes` (a 12-bit pitch-class mask and a root: the voltage snaps to the nearest such note at 1 V/oct, 0 V = C). An `Lfo` on `Beats` with no restart track sits on the song: its position is the song's beat over the cycle while the song plays (`CvEngine::song`, one call per `Span`), and keeps the tempo's pace while it is stopped. `Env`: an ADSR of a track's keys, attack a straight line, decay and release exponential (4.6 time constants, 99% there at their time), a new note restarting the attack from where it is, the top optionally the note's velocity. The engine works them out every `MOD_STEP_MS` (4 ms) on a grid of frames from its start, and at each note that moves one (kept per period in `ModEvent`s, sorted by frame at `end_period`, so the clock's calls for later frames may come first), so the voltages are the same for any period size; a turned setting keeps an LFO's place and an envelope's stage. Nothing allocates |
| `mpe` | Expression in (18 Nov 2026, plan item 3.7). `MpeIn`, one per port in `MusicIn`, reads the channel messages a track or route takes (after its blocks, so the notes match what plays) and says what each does to the notes held (`Expr`): any keyboard's pitch bend is `Bend` (-1 to 1, every note) and its channel pressure presses every note held on that channel. With a `Zone` (`Lower(n)`: master channel 1, members 2 up; `Upper(n)`: master 16, members 15 down), a member channel's bend is that note's `Slide` (semitones, 48 across the range unless RPN 0 on a member says another), its pressure that note's and its CC 74 that note's `Tone` (0 to 1); the master's bend and pressure move every note. A member's values from before its note are said just after the note (`msg` returns `Took::No` for notes, and the app calls it after `key_of`), and a channel's values reset when its last note ends. The zone is the port's profile's `mpe` line, or the controller's own RPN 6 (`Took::Zone`, logged), which takes over until the profile changes. The app turns `Expr` into `Key::Bend`, `Key::Pressure` and `Key::Expr`, which reach the armed or routed track's instrument (`Instruments::bend`, `note_expression`, `note_pressure`) or the synth (`Synth::bend`, `expression_note`) as `NodeEvent::Bend` and `NodeEvent::Expression`. A per-note expression goes only to a note the instrument holds, so a note the MIDI effects moved, or the arp played, keeps nothing stale for a later note. Unplugging a port puts a bent wheel back. A track on MIDI out sends them to its gear instead (`expr_out`), and its CV outputs take them as well (the app's `CvOut::expression`); recording doesn't take them yet. Nothing allocates |
| `expr_out` | Expression out (18 Nov 2026, plan item 3.7's converters, as the Deluge's poly-to-mono expression). `ExprOut`, one per track in the app's `MidiOut`, takes the keys a track's gear plays (`In`: `Down`, `Up`, `Wheel`, `Slide`, `Tone`, `Pressure`; the app's `Key::expr_out`) and makes the one bend, pressure and CC 74 gear without MPE has: the bend is the wheel plus the newest key's slide over the gear's range (`DEFAULT_BEND_SEMIS` = 2, or the port's profile's `bend` line), the pressure the hardest held key's as channel pressure, the tone the newest key's as CC 74 (`TONE_CC`). Each goes out on change only, on channel 0 for the router to put on the track's. It keeps `HELD` = 16 keys, oldest let go of first. `release` (a stop, a panic, a new route, another track armed) sends the bend and pressure back to rest if they weren't; `reset` forgets them unsent. `MidiOut::expression` sends a key's pressure as poly pressure on its note instead when the profile says `pressure = poly`. The app's `CvOut` keeps its own `ExprOut` per track and feeds its messages to `CvEngine::track_msg`: a Pitch output's bend over its own `bend_semis` (the track's first Pitch output's range scales the slide), a Pressure output from the channel pressure, a CC 74 output from the tone; a routed key's pressure reaches CV only this way, as the hardest key's. The armed track's keys reach it before the arp (their notes as the keys came); a routed key's going down reaches it before its note-on, so the bend leads the note, and its going up after its note-off. Nothing allocates |
| `cv_wire` | The bytes Akai's app sends to set the jacks, as captured: the CC pair of each output on cable 1, channel 16 (`ccs`, `encode`, `decode`), Akai's pitch for a note (`akai_pitch_counts`), and counts from volts with the full-scale voltage as a parameter (`counts`), since it is not measured. Its tests hold every value the two captures saw. It sends nothing |
| `profile` | Device profiles (plan item 3.3, 17 Nov 2026): what a synth or controller calls its CCs, NRPNs and programs. `Profile` is read from text (`mpfree-profile 1`, then `name`, `match`, `cc.N`, `nrpn.MSB.LSB`, `program.N` as `key = value`; a line it can't read is a warning, a file with no usable name is refused) and written back by `to_text`. `Profiles` holds the built-in ones (`general_midi`: the GM controllers and 128 programs) and the musician's, no two with one name, at most `MAX_PROFILES`; `Profiles::of` gives a port's from its `setup::ProfilePick` (Auto: the first whose `match` is the name the port calls itself). `cc_name(profile, cc)` names a CC by the profile, else by `common_cc_name`, the controllers most gear agrees on |
| `smf` | Standard MIDI Files for the export ([Export plan](https://claude.ai/artifact/CjGGYj3e9wEc4AX4MgCNqz)) and for MIDI file import (`read`: see [MIDI file import](#midi-file-import)). `NoteLog` takes the notes the player sends during the export's pass at their ticks, on the sound thread: room set before the pass, no allocation, the overflow counted. `NoteLog::smf` makes a type 1 file: a conductor track (the song's name, its tempo map and meter) and a track per MPFree track that played, named after it, at 960 ticks a quarter (the transport's own, so no tick moves). At one tick, note-offs come before note-ons; a note still on at the end gets its note-off there; every event has its status byte |
| `monitor` | The MIDI monitor's model, in two halves so the port side never waits for the screen. `Feed` lives where the bytes are: each port's bytes in and out (`Dir::In`, `Dir::Out`) become `Seen` values (time, port, way, `What`) in a fixed queue of 512, with no allocation or lock. A channel message keeps its bytes as they came (a note-on at velocity 0 stays one; running status gets its status back). Clock, active sensing and time code are counted, not listed: one line when each starts arriving and one when it stops (1 s without), and the clock's tempo, measured over at least a beat and half a second. A SysEx is one line with its whole length and its maker. `Monitor` is what the screen shows: the newest 1000, numbered from the start so a number keeps naming the same message, when each port's channels last had something each way (the lights, lit for 0.25 s), the clock tempos and the port table. `Feed::drain_into` moves the queue over while the app holds the I/O model's lock; more than 512 between two drains are counted as lost. `Filter` picks kinds (notes, CCs, programs, bend and pressure, clock, system, and debug mode's five), ways, a port and a channel. Each `Seen` also says where its message went (`To`: the armed track and the kit pad its note plays, `Routed` a track by its route, `Learned`/`Took` a parameter, `LearnOnly`, `Follows`/`NotFollowed`, `Nothing`, `Ignored`, and going out `SentBy(track)` or `Clock`), set by the code that routed it with `Feed::to` after each `Feed::bytes` (`Feed::listed` says what that call listed), or by `Feed::sent` for a message out. **Debug mode** (`Feed::debug`): two sources that aren't ports, `MPC` (the controller's pads, buttons, Q-Links and wheel as decoded, and the lights, pad colours, keep-alive and CV values written: `Mpc`) and `SYSTEM` (MPFree's events: `Event`), each listed only while it is on. `Monitor::set_epoch` ties the screen's clock to the app's, so the lights go out on the clock the lines were timed by. **Now** (`monitor::now`, 16 Nov 2026): `Monitor::push` also keeps a `Held` for each port, way and channel (16 streams × 16 channels, made once): each key's velocity, key pressure and when it went down (and the track a note went out by), each CC's value and when it moved, bend, channel pressure and program. All notes off, All sound off and the omni and mono/poly messages let every key go; Reset all controllers resets bend and pressure. `Feed` times each clock pulse as it is read (`Pulses`: the gap and how much it wanders, one second's worst gap for the last 60 s, and pulses late or lost, a gap past 1.5 pulses counting the pulses it missed); `drain_into` copies it to the monitor while the clock runs. `Monitor::still_on` lists the notes still on going out (oldest first) and `out_notes_up` forgets them once their note-offs are sent |

MIDI clock bytes are `mpfree-midi-clock`'s ([midi-clock.md](midi-clock.md)). `InputRouter` feeds `ClockIn` from the clock source. `ClockOut`'s messages go into an `OutQueue` as `Timed::realtime` or `Timed::raw`. How the transport follows another device's clock is the time engine's call.

## Defaults taken (the owner may change them)

1. Input goes to the selected track from any port and channel, as on Akai's MPC. Per-track listening is a mode.
2. MIDI Learn listens to external ports only. The Q-Links keep their own learn (`mpfree-mod`'s bindings).
3. A control moves one parameter: learning it again replaces the old mapping.
4. Learned CCs start as absolute with jump. Learned keys start as momentary.
5. Clock out is off and the clock source is none (internal) until set in the project's I/O view.
6. Running status on the DIN wire is up to the MPC's controller: MPFree hands it whole messages over USB (USB-MIDI packets carry no running status), so `DinPacer` estimates the wire time without it.

## A rule for the MIDI screens (the owner, 2026-11-12)

Akai's MIDI track has two settings with nearly the same name on the same screen, "MIDI Output Ch" and a "MIDI Out" routing, and both have to be set before a note leaves the jack. MPFree does not do that. Each track has **one** MIDI output setting, port and channel together, and no two settings on a screen share a name or look alike while doing different things. If a track would send nothing (no port, or a port that is unplugged), the field says so in place.

| MIDI Learn without ranges | a slider's top gives +6 dB, no mappings outside a project, mappings break when tracks move ([MPC-Forums](https://www.mpc-forums.com/viewtopic.php?p=1898047)) | a range and takeover per mapping with musical defaults; global and project mappings; a mapping follows its sound, not a track position |
| Learn that fails silently, and MIDI that breaks between updates | [MPC-Forums](https://www.mpc-forums.com/viewtopic.php?p=1890369) | Learn always shows what it caught and where it went; every routing case is a host test in CI; a MIDI log on the Dev tab |
| Two look-alike settings, both needed | the owner's own run (third capture above) | one output setting per track (see the rule above) |

The CV range is set by Akai's hardware (5 V at most, if the forum report holds). MPFree can show it, calibrate each output and offer Hz/V, but cannot raise it.

What musicians praise in other sequencers: clock they can trust as leader and follower, measured per port ([Elektronauts](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/clock-sync-latency-and-jitter/256632?page=2)); many ports with each lane routed freely, automation lanes to CC, pitch bend, aftertouch or CV, MIDI effects per track and MPE input ([Perfect Circuit, Hapax](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/signal/squarp-hapax-review)); CV outputs that can be LFOs or envelopes ([Perfect Circuit, OXI One](https://perfectcircuit.com/signal/oxi-one-sequencer-review)); USB host for several devices, CC lanes and microtuning through pitch bend ([Elektronauts](https://stage2.elektronauts.com/t/how-would-your-dream-sequencer-look-like/138255)).

## M2: keyboards in

Built 2026-11-12 in `rust/mpfree-app/src/midi_in.rs` and the app's `main.rs`. Read only: nothing is written to a MIDI port.

- **Cable 2 is music, not MPC controls.** Before M2 the app decoded cable 2's bytes with `mpc_one::decode`, as if they came from the MPC's own pads and buttons, so a keyboard on channel 1 pressed MPC buttons and one on channel 10 hit pads, and each clock tick printed a log line. Now `Ports::poll` hands cable 2's raw bytes to `MusicIn`, which parses them with `mpfree_midi::input::InputRouter` in Selected mode (any port, any channel).
- **USB keyboards.** A thread named `usb-midi-scan` lists the ALSA cards every 2 s, skips the MPC's own audio and controller cards, and opens every rawmidi input of each new card for reading (up to 8 a card). Each MIDI port of a device is a port of its own (`midi_in::usb_ports`): a device with one keeps the card's name, and one with several names each by its ALSA subname, or "card N" where that is empty or repeats. Outputs are counted from `/proc/asound/cardN/midi0`, so a port with only an output is listed too; the scanner never opens an output itself, it hands MIDI out a way to open it (`usb_output`, see [M3](#m3-tracks-out)). When a card goes from the list, its ports go (`Scan::Gone`), their held keys are let go and MIDI out forgets them (`MidiOut::unplugged`).
- **Where keys go.** With a synth on the pads (`pad_layout` set), keys go by note to the armed track's arp engine through `App::key_down` and `App::key_up`, and share hold counts with the pads, so a note held on both ends when both let go. With a kit, keys whose note is one of `mpc_one::PAD_NOTES` become pad hits; other keys play nothing, and the first such key is logged once with the pad notes. Poly pressure reaches the synth.
- **The log.** Each channel message gets one line naming the port. Clock (F8) and active sensing (FE) are counted, not listed: the log says when clock starts and stops arriving. Start, continue, stop and reset are listed. SysEx is logged by length and ignored. The run's counts are printed at exit.

Not yet: CCs and pitch bend do nothing until learned (see [M5: MIDI Learn](#m5-midi-learn)), and program change waits for the track settings; clock-follow waits for M4; keys that don't land on a pad aren't recorded. Not yet tried on the MPC: the read-only device round comes with the app thread's next live test.

## Routes: keyboards on several tracks

Built 2026-11-17 (phase 2, PR 3 of the MIDI powerhouse plan; the owner chose board 5's Routing section on 17 Nov 02:27Z). Read only on the ports: a route plays inside MPFree, or out of MIDI out and CV where its track already plays.

- **The setting.** `setup::TrackRoute { port, channel: Option<Channel>, track }`, the project's (`MidiSetup::routes`, at most `MAX_TRACK_ROUTES`), saved as `route = track 1 < MIDI · ch 10` or `route = track 3 < Keystep 37 · all channels`. The same route twice is refused when read; two routes from one port and channel to two tracks play both (layering). A route names its port by name, so one for a device that isn't plugged in waits for it.
- **The router.** `InputRouter::set_routes` takes them (`input::Route`: a `PortFilter`, a `ChanFilter` and a track, 0 to 127). For a channel message, the routes come first: each route that takes the port and channel gets it as `In::Routed { track, msg, frame }`, and if any did, the selected track doesn't (no doubled notes, which is what the owner disliked on Akai's MPC). The held-key masks are `u64`: 16 bits for the selected-mode tracks, then one per route slot (`ROUTE_BIT`). Changing the routes lets go of the keys held through a slot that changed, on the track they played (`set_routes` returns how many it left out).
- **Input and Learn.** A port's Input applies to the selected track only: a **Learn only** port still plays its routes. Learn takes learned controls first, routed or not.
- **The app** (`midi_in.rs`, `main.rs`). `MusicIn::sync` hands the project's routes to the router, with port names looked up in the port table. Routed keys collect in `MusicIn::routed` (256 at most a period) and the loop takes them with `MusicIn::take_routed`: keys for the armed track join its keys (so they go through the arp and record), others go to `play_routed`, which plays them at once where that track's clips play: `CvOut::send` and `MidiOut::send` by note (`NoteIs::Note`), its own instrument (`instruments.rs`), or the pads' sound (the synth by note, or the kit pad `mpc_one::pad_index` names), as the track's clips do; one kit at a time until the sampler's kit per track. No arp and no recording for a track that isn't armed (multi-arm is the sequencer's). `RoutedHeld` remembers, per track and note, whether a key went to the armed track or straight to `play_routed`, so its note-off follows even if the armed track changed in between. The monitor marks routed keys `To::Routed` ("Drums, by its route"). `rt_safety` checks `play_routed` makes no allocation.
- **The Routing section** (`rust/mpfree-midi-ui/src/routing.rs`): see [the I/O view's sections](#a-projects-settings-and-the-io-views-sections).

Blocks on a route (board 5, built 18 Nov): `mpfree_midi::blocks` holds Filter, Channel, Split, Transpose and Velocity, up to `MAX_BLOCKS` (4) a route, and `Blocks::run` says where a message ends: `Through::Out` (it leaves, shaped), `Dropped` (a block took it and dropped it) or `Free` (a note outside a Split: the route doesn't take it). `Route::blocks`, `Thru::blocks` and `InputRouter::set_port_blocks` (a port's way to the tracks, its Armed track square) apply them in `InputRouter::route`: a route or thru whose Split frees a note doesn't set its bit, so the note goes on to another route or the armed track, while a Dropped one does (nothing else plays it). A note-off and poly pressure go through the same blocks as their note-on; a route or thru whose blocks change in more than velocity (`Blocks::same_notes`) lets its held keys go first, through its old blocks, and a track keeps the note it was sent (`sent_note`), so a port's blocks change without letting go. `In::Routed` and `In::Thru` carry `via`, the route's place. `MusicIn` hands Learn and MIDI Follow the message as it came (`In::Seen`), before blocks, and still offers it to them when no block let it through. Saved as `route.blocks`, `thru.blocks` (the line after the route) and `port.N.blocks`, all the song's. The pads under an output say whether the armed track plays its kit there (drum pads out). The tracks the section offers are `Live::tracks` (16 in the app), though the engine takes any of 128.

## Thru: a port straight to an output

Built 2026-11-17 (phase 2 of the MIDI powerhouse plan, its own PR after routes; board 5's squares from a port to a MIDI out port). **State-changing on the gear**: what comes in on a port leaves another, through MIDI out's writers, in RAM only, never SysEx or clock, and only while Prefs' **Send MIDI and CV out** is on.

- **The setting.** `setup::ThruRoute { from, channel: Option<Channel>, to }`, the project's (`MidiSetup::thru`, at most `MAX_THRU_ROUTES` = 16), saved as `thru = MIDI < Keystep 37 · ch 10` (output, then input, as routes write their track first). Read once refused: the same thru twice, and a USB device to itself (`ThruRoute::check`, `thru_allowed`: its own messages would go back to it; the 5-pin jacks are two pieces of gear, so MIDI to MIDI is allowed). It replaces the per-port `PortSetup::thru` switch, which was the rig's and did nothing: an old `port.N.thru` line is read and dropped. Board 5 and the plan's rule (routes, MIDI effects and project mappings stay with the song) put thru with the song.
- **The router.** `InputRouter::set_thru` takes `input::Thru { port, channel, to: PortId }` (the outputs share the inputs' port numbers). A channel message on a thru's port and channel goes out as `In::Thru { from, to, msg, frame }`, as it came (no transpose), before the routes; like a route, a thru takes its channel from the selected track (no doubled notes), and a route on the same channel still plays (layering). Clock and Song Position never reach it, nor SysEx (the parser doesn't make channel messages of them), nor what the port ignores. Its held keys have a bit each in the `u64` masks after the routes' (`THRU_BIT`), so a note-off follows its note-on, a thru changed or taken away lets go of its keys at the output they went to, and an unplugged input ends them there.
- **The app.** `MusicIn::sync` hands the project's thru to the router, by port name (an input with `has_in`, an output with `has_out`; either missing and it waits). `MusicIn::bytes` keeps what goes thru in `MusicIn::thru` (`Passed`, 256 a period at most, the rest dropped and counted so the sound loop never grows it); Learn takes a learned control first, and a message Learn took reaches nothing else, however many routes or thru name its channel. The loop hands them to `MidiOut::thru` after the routed keys, at offset 0 of the period, so they leave when the armed track's live notes would (the sound's lead less the gear's delay). `MidiOut::thru` refuses, and counts, while sending is off or the output has no writer.
- **Notes left on.** `OutputRouter::thru` counts thru's notes per output port, channel and note (with the input each came from), so a panic (`all_off`: STOP while stopped, the Monitor's Send note-offs) and the port page's All notes off (`port_off`) end exactly those, and a note-off for a note it never sent goes nowhere. A per-port count lets a panic skip ports with none.
- **The monitor.** In: `To::Thru { to }` ("thru to MIDI out", or "thru: not sent, MIDI out is off" while `Built::notes_out` is off). Out: `Timed::thru(from)` marks what thru sent (and the panic's note-offs for it), and the loop gives the monitor `To::ThruFrom(from)` ("thru from Keystep 37 in").
- **The screens.** The Routing section's square from a port to a MIDI out port, its card (a channel stepper per thru, + Route, Remove route, Q-Link 1), "—" for a USB device's own output; the MIDI section's Thru field (Off, or the one output every channel goes to; anything more is set on Routing and the row says so); the port's page lists thru with its routes and, on the output's page, "Thru from …".
- **Tests.** `thru_sends_its_channel_out_as_it_came_and_the_selected_track_gets_the_rest`, `a_thru_changed_while_a_key_is_held_ends_the_note_where_it_went` and `thru_notes_out_end_on_a_panic_and_say_where_they_came_from` (mpfree-midi `routing`); `thru_reads_back_belongs_to_the_song_and_an_old_port_switch_is_dropped` (setup); the no-allocation test with a thru set; `thru_sends_a_channel_on_as_it_came_and_learn_keeps_what_it_learned` (the app's `MusicIn`); `rt_safety::thru_passes_a_keyboards_channel_out_as_it_came` (bytes on the wire, a panic, no allocation); the Routing, MIDI section and port page tests; the golden `mixer-io-routing` has a thru.
- **Not run on the MPC.** It sends through the same writers as M3's tracks, which haven't been tried on the unit yet; the device round for MIDI out covers both.

## MIDI file import

Built 2026-11-17 (phase 2 of the MIDI powerhouse plan, PR 6; the MPC import's scope, narrowed by the owner that day, brings the MPC's patterns in this way, through its *Track as MIDI* and *All Tracks* exports). **Read-only** on the gear: it reads a file and changes the project in RAM; nothing is sent.

- **The reader** (`mpfree-midi::smf::read`). Bytes in, an `Smf` out (the same type the export writes) at 960 ticks a quarter, whatever the file's timing: ticks a quarter are scaled and rounded to the nearest (exact for 96, 192, 480 and 960), SMPTE frame timing is read as clock time at 120 BPM (`FRAMES_BPM`). Type 0, 1 and 2. Running status, note-on at velocity 0 as a note-off, SysEx and metas skipped and counted (a track's first name, tempos, meters and the end of track are read), chunks of other kinds skipped, a header longer than 6 bytes allowed. A type 1 file's first track names the song when it plays nothing (a conductor track). Bounded: 4 MiB (`MAX_FILE_BYTES`), 256 tracks (the rest counted), a million channel events. A file cut short is read up to the cut and says so (`Reading::cut`); a file it can't read is an `SmfError` that says where and why (a data byte with no status, a system message, a number longer than 4 bytes). It never panics: a test feeds it every prefix of a file, every byte changed and random tracks.
- **The parts** (the app's `midi_file::import`, on the screen's side in its own thread, `read_in_thread`). Each file track's notes paired with their note-offs, first in, first out, per channel and note; a note never ended ends at the file's end (`unended`). A track with notes on one channel is one `Part`; one with several (a type 0 file's one track) is a part per channel ("Groove ch 10"). At most 16 parts (`MAX_PARTS`). Every clip is the file's length in whole bars of its first meter (4/4 without one), at least a clip's shortest and at most its longest (256 bars of 4/4); notes past it and past a clip's 8192 are counted. Controllers, bend, pressure and programs are counted, not kept (lanes are the sequencer's).
- **Onto the tracks** (`midi_file::put`, in the sound loop: `main.rs` and the smoke session, as `FromUi::MidiFile`). Part `i` goes on the armed track plus `i` (track 1 if none is armed), as a clip in that track's first empty slot. Clips hold pads (the sequencer's design: a step's note is a pad of the pad layout, and S1 to S6 keep it so), so each note becomes the pad that plays that note alone on its track, as the track's clips play (`midi_file::pad_on_track`): a track's own instrument through its pad map (`Instruments::pad_of_note`), else the synth's pad layout if the track plays the synth, else its kit by the MPC's pad notes (`midi_out::kit_notes`: C#2 is pad 1, C2 pad 2), so an MPC drum export lands on the pads it was played on. A note no pad plays is left out and counted; a part with none left makes no clip. `TimeHost::put_clips` puts every clip in as one capture take (`Takes::capture_clips`: all checked, then all in, or none), so one UNDO on the Clips page takes the whole file away; none is launched. The tempo is said, not set.
- **BROWSE.** `.mid`, `.midi` and `.smf` are `Kind::Midi` ("MIDI file", a 5-pin socket icon), with Load, Rename, Copy, Cut and Delete; Load sends `BrowseAction::LoadMidi { loc, path }`. The line the loop says (and shows in place of the hint) names the clips, where they went, the file's tempo and what was left out: "MIDI file Beat.mid: 2 clips with 48 notes, from slot 1 of Track 1 (UNDO takes them away); its tempo 92 BPM; left out: 3 notes no pad plays".
- **Tests.** The reader's in `smf` (the export's file read back the same, a type 0 file at 96 with running status, rounding and frames, a conductor track and unknown chunks, errors that say why, a file cut short, no panics); `midi_file`'s (an MPC export, a type 0 file by channel, a note with no note-off, notes on the pads that play them, the armed track on as one take); `takes::clips_captured_together_are_one_take_or_none_go_in`; `time::clips_for_several_tracks_go_in_as_one_take_or_not_at_all`; the browser's `a_midi_file_loads_as_clips`; the smoke workflow `a_midi_file_from_browse_lands_on_its_pads_and_undo_takes_it` (BROWSE, Patterns, the row, UNDO).
- **Not yet.** Notes past what 16 pads reach (a melody wider than the pad layout); a clip that held MIDI notes would be its own design decision for the owner. Lanes for controllers and bend come with the sequencer's lanes. The file's tempo could become the project's on a second press.

## M3: tracks out

Built 2026-11-12 in `rust/mpfree-app/src/midi_out.rs`, on the time engine's `Pads::out` hook (PR #232); USB devices as outputs 17 Nov 2026 (the MIDI powerhouse thread). Host-tested only. **State-changing on the MPC and on the gear**: note bytes leave the MIDI Out jack (cable 2) and a USB device's own output, in RAM only, never SysEx. So the app opens the port only while Prefs' **Send MIDI and CV out** (off until the musician turns it on, kept across restarts; the owner's pick, 16 Nov 2026), and the device round with the owner is still to come. Turning it off ends the notes on, stops the clock and sets the writer aside without the sound loop waiting (`MidiOut::switch_off`, `port_writer::Closing`): it is let go once its last messages have left, and turned on again before then it is taken back (`reopen`), since the MIDI Port may refuse a second open while the first file is still held. Opening runs on the sound loop, once per switch.

- **Which tracks.** A track whose `TrackOut` names a port with an output that is sending (the 5-pin jacks, `DIN_NAME`, or a USB device by its port name) plays there and not on the pads. The pads still flash for its notes. Otherwise it plays on the pads and its row says why (`PortLive::sending`, set by `MidiOut::apply` each generation): "sends nothing: MIDI out is off", "… is not plugged in", "… has no output" or "its output didn't open". With MIDI out off (the Prefs switch off, or the jacks failed to open), the MIDI section's banner says "MIDI out is off".
- **USB devices.** `MidiOut` keeps a slot per port (`PortOut`, by `PortId`, `DIN` is 0), each with its own queue, writer, clock, delay and test note. When the scanner finds a port with an output it hands `MidiOut::plugged` an `Opener` (`usb_output`: `alsa::open_rawmidi` on that card and subdevice, refused if ALSA opened another one). While sending is on, the port's writer runs it in its own `midi-out-usb` thread (`Writer::start_opening`), so the sound loop never waits on a device; a device that won't open is logged ("MIDI out to Digitone: could not open its output: …") and let go, and its tracks stay on the pads. Turning the Prefs switch on opens every plugged-in port (`switch_on`); off closes them all, as for the jacks. Unplugged (`MidiOut::unplugged`), a device can't be sent its note-offs, so its notes and queued messages are forgotten, its writer is let go and the monitor says "MIDI out to … stopped". USB-MIDI has no 31,250 baud wire, so USB ports are not paced by `DinPacer`.
- **The hook.** The clips' events reach `SeqPads::out` first (`Pads::out`, #232). The arps' notes (the pads played live, a latched arp) reach `SeqPads::live`. For a track on a port, `live` sends the note with the pads' velocity curve, shows its pad and returns the pad to record, as it does for the pads. A panic's `AllOff` ends MIDI Out's notes and still stops the pads. Locks and lanes stay with the mixer.
- **Which notes.** A kit pad goes out as the MPC's own note for that pad (`mpc_one::PAD_NOTES`, pad 1 = C#2, note 37), as Akai's MPC sends it. A synth pad sends the notes the synth would play for it (`Synth::notes`, a chord too). An arp note with a synth pad layout is already a MIDI note. A pad's note-off and pressure go to the notes its note-on sent, even if the layout changed in between.
- **Drum pads out** (plan item 2.3; Jay's card 17 Nov 23:28Z, "Gear's profile now, Pads tab later"). A kit track's pads reach `MidiOut::send` as `NoteIs::Kit`, the event's note being the pad (0 is A1, up to `KIT_PADS` = 128). `Chords::messages_on` asks the track's port for what its device profile has that pad play (`PortOut::pads`, filled in place from `Profile::pad` when the setup's generation moves; a `PadOut` is a note and an optional channel), else the pad's own note, `mpfree_midi::profile::KIT_NOTES`: bank A the MPC's `PAD_NOTES`, banks B to H the other 112 notes in turn from 35 up, then from 0, so every pad has a note no other pad has. A pad's chord remembers the channel it started on, and the router counts sounding notes per track, channel and note (`OutputRouter::send_on`, `track_on` to skip quiet tracks), so its note-off, pressure, a route change, a port's All notes off and a panic all end it where it started, whatever the profile says by then. CV and an export's tap read a kit's pads by `KIT_NOTES` (no port, no profile). The per-pad setting on screen waits for a mockup (the Play, Pads and Sequencer redesign proposes a "MIDI out" pad type).
- **Bank and program** go out (CC 0, CC 32, then program change) when a track's output is set or changed, and again for every routed track when the song starts, ahead of that period's notes.
- **Ending notes.** `OutputRouter` counts the notes that are on per track. A changed channel or port ends them where they started; a panic and closing the ports (`MidiOut::close`, at exit) send note-offs only for the notes that are on, and a port's own All notes off only those on that port (`OutputRouter::port_off`). If the app is killed or the MPC loses power, notes still on stay on in the other instrument: its own all-notes-off, or switching it off and on, ends them.
- **Timing.** The sound loop never writes to the port. `MidiOut::period_end` hands each message, in frame order, to the `midi-out` thread (`crate::port_writer`, shared with CV out) over a bounded queue (`WIRE_QUEUE`, 4096 messages, over a second of a full DIN wire), due at the loop's start time plus `MIDI_OUT_LEAD` plus its offset. `MIDI_OUT_LEAD` is the sound card's buffer, 3 periods of 128 frames at 44.1 kHz (8.7 ms), so the jack lines up with the sound. The thread sleeps until each message is due and writes it to the non-blocking port, waiting up to 500 ms for room before it drops and counts it. A message that finds the queue full is dropped and counted. A write error stops the thread; the loop logs it and lets the port go, and its tracks play on the pads again.
- **The gear's delay** (a port's page, below): each message leaves the port's delay before its frame is heard, but never before its period was made (`leave_at`), so at most `MIDI_OUT_LEAD` early; the page shows that limit (`Live::early_ms`). Each port has its own. The sequencer's look-ahead, when it comes, only needs to hand messages over earlier; it must not take the delay off again.
- **A port's clock, divided and nudged** (its page's Divide and Nudge). `PortClock` counts the gear's ticks as the transport's over `clock_divide` (`PortClock::tick`, `div_euclid`), so its pulses come every `divide` of the transport's, Always's free pulses at that rate, and Start's, a locate's and a loop's Song Position in the gear's sixteenths. A divide changed while it runs sends Stop, then the clock joins again at the next sixteenth (`restart`). The nudge moves MPFree's own clock bytes (`Timed::is_clock`: F8, FA, FB, FC and F2 that no track, page or thru sent) `clock_nudge_tenths` later or earlier than the frame they were made on, before the gear's delay; earlier stops where `leave_at` does. The period's queue is put in that order (`OutQueue::sort_by_due`), and the writer keeps a nudged clock in a lane of its own (`Wire::nudged`, `port_writer::Lanes`): of the two lanes' first messages the one due first leaves (the one sent first on a tie), and each lane keeps the order it was sent in. So a pulse nudged later never holds up the notes of the periods after it, one nudged earlier doesn't wait behind notes due after it, and the order within a lane never follows times that a late period made (a note's end never passes its start). Without a nudge every message stays in the one lane, as before.
- **The clock as it left** (`PortLive::clock_measured`): the writer measures the gaps between the pulses it writes (`ClockGaps`, the last 24, a beat's), their mean and half the spread between the longest and shortest, from 4 gaps on; a second without a pulse forgets them (`Writer::clock_measured`). `MidiOut::sync` copies it, to a tenth of a millisecond, to the port's page: "Pulses leave every 20.8 ms, ±0.3 ms."
- **The port page's sends** (`asks.port_send`, answered in `Live::port_said`): a test note (C4, velocity 100, half a second, on the first track's channel on the port, else 1) and All notes off (`port_off` for MPFree's own notes on that port, then CC 123 on all 16 channels). Any port with an output that is sending; greyed otherwise, with the reason. They are marked `Timed::from_page`, so the monitor says "sent from its page".
- **The log.** The Dev tab's MIDI page lists the first 64 messages sent ("+12.345 s MIDI Out: 99 25 64 = note on, ch 10, C#2, velocity 100"), then counts. At exit: messages written and dropped, how late the latest one left after its frame, and `DinPacer`'s estimate of the wire's own delay.
- **Sound path.** `sync`, `send` and `period_end` never allocate, wait or write: `rt_safety` plays a recorded clip on MIDI Out for 4,000 periods under the counting allocator.
- **The sampler's auto-sample of outside gear** (17 Nov 2026, asked for by the sampler thread). The Sample tab plays a synth on MIDI Out one note at a time and records each note from the line input. `MidiOut::gear_out` hands it a `sample_page::GearOut` (the name *MIDI Out (5-pin)* and a `send` for any thread) while the jacks are open and sending is on; `gear_changed`, called once a period, says when that comes or goes, for `SamplePage::set_gear`. `send` takes only a note on or off of three bytes, or no bytes for the end of the job (or its cancel), and refuses anything else, so never SysEx or clock. It never waits: messages go through a bounded queue (`GEAR_QUEUE`, 64) that `period_end` drains at the start of each period, and a message that finds it full is dropped and counted. The notes go out of the jacks at once, with no song timing, marked `Timed::from_sampler` so the monitor says "sent by the Sample tab's auto-sample". `MidiOut` keeps the notes the job has on in a bitmask and ends them when the job ends, at a panic (`all_off`), when sending is switched off, and when the jacks' writer stops. The exit log counts them ("Auto-sample: its notes to the gear on MIDI Out: …"). The sound loop hands it to the screen (`ToUi::Gear`) each time `gear_changed` says it came or went, and the Sample tab maps the job's All Notes Off to the end of the job (no bytes) and sends that again once the job has landed or is dropped ([sampler architecture](sampler-architecture.md), Auto-sample). Every open output, a USB synth too, can follow through the same queue.

Not yet: CCs, pitch bend and pressure lanes from clips, and naming a port on its page (it needs an on-screen keyboard in the MIDI section). The device round, with its exact steps and undo, goes to the owner first.

## M4: clock out

Built 2026-11-12 in `MidiOut::clock` (`rust/mpfree-app/src/midi_out.rs`), on the same Prefs switch as M3, so it is state-changing in the same way and waits for the same device round.

- **The setting.** Each port's "Clock out" in the I/O view's Sync section (and on its page): Off (the default), While playing, or Always. Each port runs its own `ClockOut` (`PortClock`) only while its writer is open, so a USB device plugged in mid-song starts at the next Start or locate; a port whose output isn't sending says why on its row.
- **From the transport.** Once a period, after `TimeHost::advance`, the app hands `TimeHost::block()` to `MidiOut::clock`, which drives `mpfree_midi_clock::ClockOut` in time order (a marker on a span's first frame comes before that span):
  - `Mark::Started { tick }`: Start (FA) at tick 0, else Song Position (F2) and Continue (FB) with pulses from the next sixteenth.
  - Playing and recording spans: a pulse (F8) on every 40-tick line (`Span::lines(PULSE_TICKS)`, 24 a quarter note), so pulses follow every tempo change.
  - `Mark::Located` and `Mark::Looped`: Stop, Song Position, Continue. `Mark::Rewound` (bar 1 while stopped): Song Position 0. `Mark::Stopped`: Stop (FC).
  - A count-in sends nothing. Its spans roll, but Start comes with `Started`, when playback begins.
  - Clock set while playing (or the port opened while playing) joins at the next sixteenth with Song Position and Continue.
  - Set to Off while playing: Stop. Closing the port (at exit) sends Stop too.
- **Always** also sends pulses while stopped, at the transport's tempo, from the moment it stops.
- **Order.** The clock is queued before the period's notes, and the queue is sorted by frame only (`OutQueue::sort_by_frame`), keeping the order pushed: Song Position must come before Continue, which `OutQueue::sort` (real-time bytes first) would swap.
- **Tests.** Start and 48 pulses a second at 120 BPM while playing, nothing while stopped; Always; a locate; a count-in; turned on and off while playing; and `rt_safety` plays a clip on MIDI Out with clock on for 4,000 periods with no allocation.

## M4: clock in

Built 2026-11-12 in `MusicIn` (`rust/mpfree-app/src/midi_in.rs`) and the app's loop, on the time engine thread's follower (`rust/mpfree-app/src/time/follow.rs`, described in [midi-clock.md](midi-clock.md)). It only reads, from the ports M2 already reads, so it needs no round of its own.

- **The setting.** The I/O view's Sync section: "Tempo from" MPFree or a port (by name, so a USB device plugged in later is found), and "Start and stop" and "Position" (both on by default). `MusicIn::sync` turns them into `time::Follow` on each generation of the model, and sets `Built::clock_in`, so the Sync section's rows no longer say following isn't built.
- **The leader's bytes.** `MusicIn::bytes` keeps the followed port's clock (F8), Start, Continue, Stop and Song Position with its two data bytes, in a 64-byte buffer for the period; anything else is left out, and a status byte that cuts a Song Position short is kept so the follower drops it too. Past 64 bytes in a period (more than any clock sends) bytes are counted at exit.
- **Each period**, before `TimeHost::advance`: `MusicIn::leader_clock` hands the options and the bytes to `TimeHost::follow` and `TimeHost::clock_in`, at `TimeHost::next_frame()` (read at the period's top, so up to a period late, about half a period on average). `follow_tempo` with the app's tempo is ignored while the clock arrives.
- **The tempo.** After `advance`, `TimeHost::followed_bpm()` becomes the app's tempo (`App::set_tempo`, rounded to 0.1 BPM, without its log line) when it has moved more than 0.08 BPM from it, so the tempo chip, Note Repeat, the arps and tempo-synced effects follow the leader without flicker. While `followed_bpm()` is Some, the loop sends `ToUi::ClockFrom(Some(name))`, and `None` when it ends, only when it changes: `MusicIn::leader_name` gives "MIDI In" for the 5-pin jack and a USB device's own name, and the tempo chip reads "120.0 from MIDI In" (layout spec, section 4d). When the clock stops arriving the follower lets go and the transport keeps the last tempo.
- **The log.** "clock arriving …; MPFree follows it" (or "not followed: the tempo isn't from this port") once per port; the time engine's status line counts jumps to the leader's position and lost clocks.
- **Sound path.** No allocation: the bytes go through a fixed buffer, and `MusicIn::bytes` borrows the port's name for its log lines instead of building one.
- **Tests.** `the_followed_port_hands_its_clock_on` (what is kept, from which port, with which options) and `a_clock_on_the_jacks_sets_the_tempo_and_starts_the_song` (a 100 BPM clock with Start through `MusicIn` and the follower, as the loop runs it: 100 BPM within 0.1, and playing). The follower's own timing tests are the time engine's.
- **Not yet.** Following from USB-B (a computer).

## M5: MIDI Learn

Built 2026-11-12. Host-tested only. On the MPC it only reads (the same inputs as M2), so it needs no round of its own.

- **Arming.** The chain editor's existing Learn button (mod thread code, `mpfree-chain-ui`) arms both learns: `ToUi::ChainLearn(addr)` also calls `MusicIn::arm(addr)`, and its armed text now reads "Turn a Q-Link or a MIDI control". A Q-Link turned first ends it as before (`FromUi::ChainLearned` disarms MIDI Learn); a MIDI control taken first sends `ToUi::ChainLearnDone`, which ends the editor's learn (`ChainPage::cancel_learn`). Back or closing the editor disarms it (`Panels::learning`).
- **SHIFT + a touch on a value** (17 Nov 2026, PR 4b of the MIDI powerhouse plan; layout spec 4d). With SHIFT held, a finger that lands on a value sends `FromUi::LearnValue` instead of touching it (`Player::learnable_at`: the smallest target under the finger, as the outline picks), and its moves and lift do nothing (`Player::learn_finger`). The values: a strip's fader, pan and sends on the mixer's Faders look (`mpfree_mixer_ui::param_of`, the strip and the parameter id, `Owner::Strip`), an effect's knob, fader, slider or drawbar on the chain editor and a macro's knob on the macros panel (`mpfree_chain_ui::param_of`), and a knob, fader, slider or drawbar on the Synth tab (`SynthPanel::param_of`, the parameter id; the desk makes it `Owner::Inst` of the selected track). Anything else keeps its SHIFT + touch job. The desk arms Learn with `MusicIn::arm_from(addr, ArmedBy::Touch)`, or disarms it when the same value comes again. Armed this way it waits until a control takes it (`MusicIn::waits`): the app tells the screen what it waits for (`ToUi::Learning`, the hint line's "LEARNING Volume (Bass): move a knob, a key or a pedal on any controller"), and when a control takes it, says what the control moves (`ToUi::Tell`). SHIFT's hint line on the mixer, the chain editor, the macros panel and the Synth tab starts "touch a value: MIDI Learn".
- **Remote control** (17 Nov 2026, PR 4c of the MIDI powerhouse plan, board 8's "PLAY and STOP" and "Tempo" rows). Learn's targets stay `ParamAddr`s: a new owner, `Owner::Transport` (`transport.N` in text), has the ids in `mpfree_param::transport`: `PLAYING` (PLAY and STOP on one control, a switch), the presses `PLAY`, `STOP`, `PLAY_START`, `REC`, `OVERDUB`, `TAP_TEMPO`, and `TEMPO`. Locks, macros and the mod matrix never target it. The app's `remote.rs` turns a learned control's 0 to 1 into what it does (`Remote::write`): a press when the value goes past the middle and a let-go when it comes back under (each press id keeps whether it is held, so a knob turned past the middle presses once); `PLAYING` on taps PLAY while stopped, off taps STOP while rolling; `TEMPO` is 30 to 300 BPM (`remote::TEMPO`, set to a tenth). A press goes through `App::remote_press`, which looks up the button's number (`ButtonMap`, so REC, OVERDUB, PLAY START and TAP TEMPO are wherever Map controls found them) and calls `App::button` as the MPC's own button would, on the page showing; never while Map controls listens. The tempo goes through `App::set_tempo` without its log line, as a followed clock's does; while the project's tempo follows a port (`MusicIn::leader_name`) it does nothing, and the log says so once. `learn_describe` reads `PLAYING` (rolling) and `TEMPO` (`App::bpm`) from `remote::Now`, so a button's next press flips from where the transport stands and a picked-up fader waits for the tempo; the presses have no value to read. Arming: the Learn section's **Transport and tempo** card (`Asks::learn_target`, armed as `ArmedBy::Io`), or SHIFT + a touch on Play's tempo (`Player::learnable_at`). The transport's buttons on screen keep their SHIFT + touch: the layout spec gives SHIFT + a touch to values only. Track mutes and solos are the mixer's own parameters (`Owner::Strip`, `mx::MUTE`, `mx::SOLO`), learned by SHIFT + a touch on a strip's M or S (`mpfree_mixer_ui::param_of`).
- **Defaults for remote control.** A key learned on a switch you flip (`midi_in::key_toggles`: PLAY and STOP, a strip's mute or solo) becomes a toggle as it is learned, before its release arrives (`LearnMap::set_mode`), so learning it doesn't stop the transport or unmute the track on the key's release. A new toggle starts from where its parameter stands (`LearnMap::add` and `set_mode` read the last value seen). A control learned on `Owner::Transport` is kept in the rig (every project); everything else starts in the song.
- **Which ports learn.** A port whose input is "Learn only" or "Both" (the default). "Plays tracks" never learns, and its learned controls do nothing; the I/O view says so on the row.
- **What Learn takes doesn't play.** `MusicIn::bytes` hands each track message to `LearnMap::takes` first: an armed press or move, and every message of a learned control (a learned key's release too), go to Learn and not to the armed track. A key's release while armed still reaches the track, so a note held while arming ends.
- **Defaults.** A key learns as momentary ("Button: while held"), anything else as absolute ("Knob or fader"), over the whole range with jump takeover. **Takeover** (`learn::Takeover`): Jump; Pickup, which waits until the control passes the value; Scale (17 Nov 2026), which moves the value at once by the same share of the way to the end the control moves towards, until the two meet (as the Deluge's MIDI Follow does; the first move after a disagreement only says where the control is). Learning a control again (same port, channel and source) replaces its old mapping (`MidiSetup::learned`); a project keeps at most `MAX_LEARNED` (256), and one past that is counted at exit.
- **The project's or the rig's** (17 Nov 2026). `MidiSetup::learn` is the song's; `MidiSetup::rig_learn` is kept for every project with the rig (`learn-rig = ...` lines in the rig's text, `files/midi/rig.txt`; `project()` leaves them out, `set_rig` takes them). `MidiSetup::in_use` is the song's, then the rig's whose control the song doesn't use: the song's own mapping wins. `all_learned` lists both (the song's first) with `Kept`, and the Learn section and `Live::learn_names` count in that order (`learned_at`, `forget_at`, `keep_at`). A learned control is pushed to `MidiSetup::learn` at the next `sync` (by device name, so it finds the device again under another port number), which bumps the model's generation; `sync` rebuilds the `LearnMap` from the setup on every generation change, so the I/O view's "Works as" and Forget take effect at once. Learned controls are saved with the project, in its `midi` part (see the status below).
- **Setting the value.** A learned control's 0 to 1 becomes the parameter's own value with `ParamInfo::from_unit` (a macro takes the 0 to 1 as it is), set with `Panels::set_param`, which is how a chain editor knob sets it (the mod engine's `hand` first, else the mixer) and refreshes the open panel. A synth's parameter (`Owner::Inst`, 17 Nov 2026) is set as the Synth tab's knob sets it (`learned_synth`): the track's own instrument while it holds one (`Instruments::param`), else the synth on the pads (`Synth::set`); the Synth tab is sent its values (`ToUi::SynthValues`) at most every 50 ms. A learned synth knob's moves aren't recorded into a clip. A parameter the app can't set from a control is counted and logged once.
- **Where the value stands.** Every 50 ms `MusicIn::observe_learned` reads each learned parameter (`learn_describe`: `modmix::describe`, or a synth's parameter as the Synth tab shows it) and, when something other than the control moved it, tells `LearnMap::observe`, so an endless encoder steps on from a value set on the screen and a button's next press flips from it. For 250 ms after a learned control set a parameter, and while the parameter holds what the control set (as the parameter rounds it, so a stepped parameter keeps an encoder's part steps), it is left alone.
- **On screen: the Learn section** (`learn_section.rs`, board 8 of the MIDI mockups, 17 Nov 2026; before, a "MIDI Learn" part at the end of the MIDI section). A table of every learned control: Control (the device and "CC 74", "note C2 ch 10"), Moves (`Live::learn_names`, which the app fills from `learn_label` on each generation: `modpanels::label`, or "Poly cutoff (Track 2)" for a synth's knob), Now (a bar from `Live::learn_live`, which the app updates in place four times a second from `learn_describe`) and Kept in (RIG or PROJECT). A row that does nothing says why (unplugged, its port only plays tracks, the song's own mapping wins). The card under it, for the picked row: Learn again, Remove, Range (each end stepped by 1 %, a tenth with SHIFT on the Q-Link, shown in the parameter's units from `LearnLive::lo_text`/`hi_text`, which the app writes with `mpfree_mod::text::value_text`), Works as (the six modes), Takeover (Jump, Pick up, Scale; for Knob or fader only) and Kept in (Rig, Project: `keep_at`). The wheel picks a row, - and + step Works as, Q-Links 1 to 4: low, high, Works as, Takeover. **Transport and tempo**, a button by the heading, swaps the card for eight buttons (`pick_rects`, `transport::ALL`'s order) with Back; while nothing is learned the card shows them on its own. A tap asks `Asks::learn_target`, and the button stays lit while Learn waits; the Q-Links and - and + do nothing while the card shows.
- **Learn again and Cancel.** The section asks the app (`Asks::learn_again` with the row's place in `all_learned`, `Asks::cancel_learn`). `MusicIn::sync` arms Learn on that control's parameter and remembers it (`MusicIn::relearn`); the next control learned for that parameter takes its place in its list, with its range, takeover and, when it is the same kind of control (a key or not), its mode. Learn armed this way stays armed until a control takes it or Cancel (`MusicIn::waits`, `ArmedBy::Io`); the chain editor's ends with the editor, as before. While Learn is armed the app names the parameter in `Live::learning` and the section shows a LEARNING banner with Cancel.
- **The log.** "+1.234 s Keystep 37: MIDI Learn: CC 74 · ch 1 · Keystep 37 takes t1.fx3.2" on the Dev tab's MIDI page.
- **Sound path.** Learn runs in the sound loop with the rest of `MusicIn`: no waiting (the model is only try_locked), and no allocation once a control's parameter has been seen (`learn_sets` has room for every learned control from the start).
- **Not yet.** SHIFT + a touch on the Play page's other values, the Pads and Sample tabs, the arpeggiator and a plugin's own page (a skin); recording a learned synth knob's moves; launching clips and scenes from outside (the sequencer thread offers those targets; they can join `Owner::Transport` or an owner of their own); MIDI Follow's values sent back to the controller (board 8's Send values back).

## M6: CV out

Built 2026-11-12 in `rust/mpfree-app/src/cv_out.rs`. Host-tested only. **State-changing on the MPC**: control changes on the controller's cable 1 ("Private"), channel 16, the CC pairs Akai's app was seen sending (`mpfree_midi::cv_wire`), in RAM only, never SysEx. So the app opens CV out only while Prefs' **Send MIDI and CV out** is on, and the device round with a meter is still to come. Turning it off sets CV 1 to 4 to 0 V (`CvOut::switch_off`); the writer is kept aside until those values have left, and the lights go through it until then, so a light still never lands inside a CV pair.

- **Which outputs.** Only CV 1 to 4 (`CHECKED_OUTS`, CC 0 to 7), whose pairs were seen in the owner's captures. CV 5 to 8 would be CC 8 to 15 by the pattern, but nobody has seen them, so they are never sent: the engine still follows them, and their rows say "sends nothing yet: not checked on the MPC". `Built::cv_outs` tells the CV section how many outputs this run drives (0 with CV out off, and every row with a job says "sends nothing: CV out is off").
- **Volts.** A change's volts become a 14-bit value with `cv_wire::counts`, taking full scale as 5 V (`MAX_VOLTS`). That is INFERENCE from Akai's own pitch values (60 semitones across the 14 bits): no meter has been on a jack. MPFree rounds where Akai's app truncates, so a note can be one count higher (C3: Akai 3276, MPFree 3277; a third of a millivolt if full scale is 5 V). Per-output tuning (`Calibration`) is in the engine but has no screen yet: it is a rig fact for Prefs, after the meter round.
- **What drives it.** The same hooks as MIDI Out, but CV only follows: a track's events still play wherever the track plays. `SeqPads::out` hands every clip event to `CvOut::send` before MIDI Out sees it; `SeqPads::live` hands every arp note on (with the pads' velocity curve). Notes are read as MIDI Out reads them (`Chords`, shared): a kit pad is the MPC's own pad note, a synth pad its layout's notes. A panic lets every gate fall. `CvOut::clock` takes the transport's period: Started (from tick 0: a reset pulse), Stopped, and each clock output's lines (`Span::lines(PPQ / ppqn)`) while playing or recording, never in a count-in. Opened while the song plays, run joins at once.
- **LFOs and envelopes** (18 Nov 2026). `CvOut::clock` gives the engine each span's song position (`Span::tick_at` over `PPQ`), tempo and whether it rolls, for LFOs that follow the tempo. A moving output changes every 4 ms (176 frames at 44.1 kHz), so CV 1 to 4 can send up to 1000 changes a second together, 2000 control changes on the Private port, against the few a note makes. That rate is a choice, not measured on the controller: the device round watches whether the lights and pads keep up while four LFOs run. `period_end` skips a change whose 14-bit value is the one the output already has (`CvOut::last`), so a slow LFO, a held sustain or a snapped LFO between notes sends nothing.
- **Gates that touch.** A gate that falls and rises on the same frame (one note ending as the next starts, which is how a sequence's notes meet) stays low for `GATE_GAP_MS` (2 ms) before it rises, so the module's envelope starts again. The pitch moves at once. 2 ms is a choice, longer than USB's 1 ms frames, not yet tried on a module.
- **Timing.** As MIDI Out: `CvOut::period_end` sorts the period's changes by frame (keeping their order within a frame), sends only the last change of each output on any one frame, and hands each change's two control changes, as one 6-byte write, to the `cv-out` thread (`crate::port_writer`, shared with MIDI Out), due at the loop's start plus `MIDI_OUT_LEAD` plus its offset. A 5 ms trigger's fall leaves about 5 ms after its rise. Past 120 changes in a period, each output's last value is sent and the period is counted.
- **The lights.** The app's button lights are 3-byte control changes on the same Private port, written by the sound loop. While CV out is open they go through its writer (`CvOut::light`) instead, so a light's bytes can never land inside a CV pair if the kernel's buffer takes only part of a write.
- **Open and close.** The first period after it opens, CV 1 to 4 go to their job's voltage (0 V with no job). Closing (at exit) sets all four to 0 V, so no gate is left up. If the app is killed or the MPC loses power, the jacks keep their last value until the controller resets.
- **The log.** The Dev tab's MIDI page lists the first 64 writes ("+12.345 s CV out: BF 00 19 BF 01 4D = CV 1 = 3277 (1.000 V if full scale is 5 V)"), then counts. At exit: writes, drops, and how late the latest one left.
- **Sound path.** `sync`, `send`, `clock` and `period_end` never allocate, wait or write: `rt_safety` plays a recorded clip into pitch, gate, a clock and run for 4,000 periods under the counting allocator.

The device round (the owner's go first, with exact steps and undo): with nothing plugged into the jacks, turn on Prefs' **Send MIDI and CV out** with CV 1 set to Fixed at known volts, read each jack's tip and ring with a multimeter to learn which output is which and the volts at full scale; then a pitch and gate on a module. Until then, CV 5 to 8 and tuning wait.

## A project's settings and the I/O view's sections

Built 2026-11-12, and on screen since the app thread's #252 (the I/O icon; `ToUi::IoModel` hands the sections the model the sound loop shares):

- **`mpfree_midi::setup::MidiSetup`**: a project's MIDI, sync and CV settings. Ports are named, not numbered (`DIN_NAME`, "MIDI", for the 5-pin jacks), so a project names "Digitakt" and the app finds it when it is plugged in. Each track has one output setting, `TrackOut` (port and channel together, with program and bank). The project's routes (`TrackRoute`) and thru (`ThruRoute`, none by default) name their ports. The tempo is MPFree's own or a port's clock, with follow start/stop and follow position. Each of the 8 CV outputs has a `CvAssign`. The text is `key = value` lines after `mpfree-midi 1`, only what differs from the defaults; a line that can't be read is skipped and named in a warning, never dropped silently.
- **`rust/mpfree-midi-ui`**: the MIDI, Routing, Learn, Controller, Sync, CV and Monitor sections of the mixer's I/O view, through `mpfree_mixer_ui::IoSection`. They share an `IoModel`: the setup, `Live` facts the app fills in (ports plugged in and when each last received something, track names, clock arriving, CV volts) and `Built`, which says which parts of MIDI this build has. A setting whose part isn't working in this run is kept and says so on its own row ("sends nothing: MIDI out is off", "thru sends nothing: MIDI out is off", "sends nothing: no output to Keystep 37 yet"), as does one for a device that isn't plugged in, following the owner's rule. A tap on a setting changes it (a choice steps, "Plays on" opens a picker with the port and the channel on one page); the wheel selects a row, - and + step the selected setting, and the Q-Links take the row's settings. `MidiPart` is the project's `midi` part (`parts/midi.txt`); lines it couldn't read are counted on the MIDI section and named in the log.
- **The Monitor section** (13 Nov 2026, the missing MPC features thread) sets nothing: it shows `IoModel::monitor`. Chips pick what is listed (Notes, CC, Program, Bend & pressure, Clock, SysEx) and which way (In, Out); Hold stops the list and Clear empties it (where the section is narrow, beside the mixer's master strip, the ways, Hold and Clear take a second line). Under them, a row per port (the 5-pin jacks always, other ports while plugged in) with a light per channel, in on the upper half and out on the lower, and the tempo of a clock in or out. A tap on a light lists only that port, way and channel, a tap on a port's name only that port and way, and the same tap again lists everything. The list is newest first: time on the app's clock, port and way, channel, the message in words (`monitor_words`: notes named as in the app, 48 is C3; common CCs and SysEx makers named) and its bytes. The wheel and page arrows scroll back, and the list holds while scrolled back; at the top it follows again. `Built::monitor` says whether the app feeds it; until it does, a banner says nothing will be listed. A **Went to** column says where each line's message went, coloured by what took it (a track: text; Learn: the second accent; the clock: good; nothing: warn; sent by: dim); a track is named from `Live::tracks`, a learned parameter from `Live::learn_names` by its place in `setup.learn`, else by its address. Below 1000 px wide the fixed columns narrow. **Save log** (`log::saved`) words the list as it shows into a text file with a heading and columns, oldest first, and asks the app for it (`IoModel::asks.save_log`); the app's `mpfree-midilog` thread (below) has the store keep it and answers in `Live::log_saved`, which the section's next tick says on the hint line. It is greyed without `Live::saves_logs`. **Now** (List | Now, not in debug mode) draws `now.rs`'s cards for one stream and channel (the newest to have sent something until a port chip or the channel arrows pick one): a three-octave keyboard (C3 up, moved to show every key held) with the notes, velocities, key pressure, sustain and where they went; the controls that moved, newest first, with a bar each (purple for a learned one, with what it moves), bank and program; the clock's tempo, pulse gap and spread, worst gap and pulses late, with one bar per second; bend and channel pressure; and the notes still on going out of every port, stuck from 30 s, with **Send note-offs to MIDI out** (`asks.note_offs`, greyed while MIDI out is off or none is on). Hold freezes the Now view's data. **Debug mode** is `DevMonitor`, the same section as a page widget for the Dev tab's MIDI page (`debug_page::MONITOR`): its chips pick sources (Ports, MPC controls, Lights & colours, CV out, System, Clock, Keep-alive), and it draws again whenever what it shows changes (`Widget::animating` compares the section's live hash with the one painted).
- **The app's part** (built 16 Nov 2026, the MIDI powerhouse thread). `MusicIn` keeps the `Feed` (`MusicIn::feed`) and feeds it a byte at a time as it routes them, so each message's `To` comes from what took it: Learn's mapping (`LearnMap::target_of`) or the armed control, the armed track (`MusicIn::set_armed(track, by_note)` once a period; with a kit, `mpc_one::pad_index` names the pad), a Learn only port, the clock follower (as the project's Follow says for Start, Stop and Song Position), or nothing. `MusicIn::sync` drains it into the model, sets the ports when they change, `set_epoch` and `live.built.monitor`. MIDI out: `Timed` carries its track (`Timed::by`, set by `OutputRouter`), and `MidiOut::sent` gives the last period's messages the writer took with when each is due, which the loop lists with `Feed::sent` (`SentBy` or `Clock`). In debug mode (Prefs › Show developer tools) the loop also lists each decoded controller event, what `Ports::send` wrote (`akai::monitor_out` names lights, pad colours and the ping; lights that go through CV out's writer are tapped too), `CvOut::sent`'s values, and events: a USB device plugged in or gone (with the held notes let go, `MusicIn::unplugged`'s count), MIDI out stopping (`MidiOut::tick` returns it) and messages dropped with its writer's queue full. `MusicIn::sync` also takes the Monitor's asks: `note_offs` makes the loop call `MidiOut::all_off(0)` (the notes the router left on, as STOP's panic does; nothing while MIDI out is off) and forgets them in the monitor. **`midi_log.rs`** (16 Nov 2026) is the `mpfree-midilog` thread, four times a second: it takes `asks.save_log`, names the file so it replaces none (`log::file_name` against the store's list of `files/midi-logs/`; nothing past the list's 256), has the store write it, waits for the store and answers (also as a `midi log:` line in the run's log, which the `midi-now` device check reads). While the developer tools are on it appends each new monitor line (`log::console_line`: nine tab-separated fields) to `/run/mpfree-app/midi.log` in RAM, set aside as `midi.log.1` at 1 MiB with a last line `@@ROTATED` (and the same for a file an earlier start left); it never makes the folder, and stops writing it at the first error. The workstation's `tools/mpc-one/midi_console.py` (`collect-over-ssh.sh --midi-console`) follows that file over SSH, read-only, and prints it in colour with filters.
- **A port's page** (`port_page.rs`, board 6 of the MIDI mockups, 16 Nov 2026). A port's row has a Page field (**Open ›**); it, the row's name or + on it opens the page over the MIDI section (`Form::page`, `form::Page`), and ‹ MIDI or Back closes it. Its cards: This port (what it is, `PortLive::about`, its Name and Input), Ignore coming in (four chips), What it does (read only: its routes, where the rest of its notes go, Learn, the tempo, the tracks that play on it, MPFree's clock; the Routing section changes them), Now (the newest message in and out), Clock to this port (the Sync section's clock out: one setting, two places, never on one screen; with **Divide** and **Nudge** on one line where the card is wide enough, else one under the other, and a line with the clock as it left: `PortLive::clock_measured`, below), This gear's delay (− value +, what MIDI out does with it) and Send to it (**SENDS MIDI**: test note, All notes off; greyed, with the reason, while MIDI out is off or the port isn't plugged in, has no output or didn't open). The wheel picks a control, - and + step it, the Q-Links take Input, Clock, the delay and Divide. Input, the ignored kinds, the delay and the nudge are the rig's; clock out and its divide are the project's.
- **Port names** (17 Nov 2026, plan item 2.7). A USB device can be given a name of its own: `PortSetup::shown` (the rig's, `port.N.shown = Juno`, at most `MAX_SHOWN`, 24 characters, so it fits Routing's columns). The setup, the routes, thru, tracks and Learn keep naming the port by its own name, so a name given changes nothing that plays; only what the screens say goes through `MidiSetup::shown`. `MidiSetup::set_shown` trims it, takes it off when it is empty or the port's own, and refuses one longer than 24 characters or one another port has (its own name or a given one, the 5-pin jacks' `DIN_NAME` and `DIN_SHOWN` included, whatever the capitals). On the port page, Name (a USB device only: the jacks keep the panel's MIDI In and MIDI Out) asks the app (`IoModel::asks.name_port`); the app opens the shared text keyboard (`mpfree_ui::textpad`, `TypeTo::PortName` in gui.rs) on the name showing, and on Enter calls `set_shown` with the ports plugged in, bumps `gen` (so `midi_saved::Saver` keeps the rig) and says *Keystep 37 is called Juno now*; a refusal stays on the keyboard in red. The Monitor names ports through `Monitor::set_shown` (`MusicIn::shown_names` hands it the renamed ports when the setup or the ports change) and compares by `Monitor::own_name`; the tempo chip (`MusicIn::leader_name`) and what Learn says it took use the given name, the log the device's own.
- **Device profiles** (17 Nov 2026, plan item 3.3, board 6's Profile). `PortSetup::profile` is a `ProfilePick` (the rig's: `port.N.profile = auto | none | <name>`, Auto by default and not written). `IoModel::profiles` holds the profiles; the app's `midi_saved::load_profiles` adds the musician's from `files/midi-profiles/*.txt` at start, read only, logging what it skipped and what it found. `IoModel::profile(name)` and `profile_of(port id)` give the profile a port uses. The Monitor's lines (`monitor::words_named`, through `line_words` and the saved log), the Now view's controls and program, the port page's Now card and the Learn section's controls name CCs and programs by it, else by the common names. The port page's This port card has a Profile row under Input (a tap steps Auto, None, each profile; - and + stop at the ends), with a line saying how many controls and programs it names, or that the profile picked isn't there. So that What it does keeps room for five lines, the Ignore coming in chips sit in one row on a page wide enough for them (`CHIP_MIN_W`, as on the MPC's screen), their note in the card's heading. Profiles change no MIDI. A profile's `knobs` line (up to `MAX_KNOBS`, 16, CCs 0 to 119, none twice) lists a controller's knobs for MIDI Follow. Still to come: NRPN names in the Monitor once NRPNs are read as one message, and profile names in the sequencer's CC lanes.
- **MIDI Follow** (17 Nov 2026, plan item 3.5, board 8's right card). `MidiSetup::follow` is a `FollowSetup` (the rig's: `midi-follow = on`, `midi-follow.port = Keystep 37`, `midi-follow.knobs = 74 71 76 77`; `project()` leaves it out, `set_rig` takes it): on or off, the controller by its own name, and its own knobs' CCs in Q-Link order (at most `FOLLOW_KNOBS`, 4); with none, `knobs_of` takes its profile's `knobs`. In `MusicIn` a `Knobs` holds it on this run's port numbers, set again on each generation (the port found by name with an input, and each knob's last value forgotten). In `bytes`, after Learn (a learned control wins), a CC from the controller on one of the knobs is Follow's: it plays nothing, goes nowhere else, and the Monitor says *MIDI Follow: Q-Link 2* (`To::QLink`). Its change from the knob's last value is a Q-Link turn of as many detents (the first value only says where the knob is), kept in fixed room (`KNOB_TURNS`, 64) until the main loop takes them (`MusicIn::take_knob_turns`) and pushes each as the MPC's own `ControlEvent::QLinkTurn` on the music cable, so it turns whatever the Q-Links turn on the page showing. **Learn knobs** (`Asks::follow_learn`: `Some(true)` listens from none, `Some(false)` stops): while it listens each new CC from the controller is the next knob and plays nothing, the knobs found replace `follow.knobs` (a generation, so the rig is saved), it stops by itself at four, and `Live::follow_learning` says how many it has found. On screen, the Learn section's MIDI Follow card (`paint_follow`, `follow_rects`): Learn knobs (Done while it listens), Controller (− and + step `follow_ports`: the ports plugged in with an input, then the one picked if it isn't; another controller clears the knobs), Follow (Off, On; On picks the first port if none is), Knobs (the CCs, the profile's marked *(profile)*, with Clear for the controller's own) and what it does or why it waits (`follow_says`). It sits beside the picked row's card when the section is wide enough for both (`ROW_CARD_W` + `FOLLOW_W`, as on the MPC's screen); narrower, the heading's MIDI Follow button swaps it in. **Send values back** (18 Nov 2026, plan item 3.5; board 8's row): `FollowSetup::send_back` (the rig's, `midi-follow.send-back = on`, off by default). The screen hands the Q-Link column's four values to the main loop when they change (`Player::qlink_values_changed`, sent by `send_actions` as `FromUi::QLinkValues`: 0 to 127 from each card's `pos`, None for a blank card; the Looper tab's own column while it shows), and `MusicIn::set_qlink_values` keeps them. Each period `MusicIn::send_back` sends each knob whose value differs from the one it last sent as its CC on the channel it last came in on, to the controller's output (the port of that name with an output), through `MidiOut::send_back` (tagged `SENT_BACK`, so the Monitor says *MIDI Follow: a value sent back*; nothing while MIDI out is off, and it tries again). Not to a knob that turned in the last `HOLD_BACK` (0.3 s): its own turns moved the value, and a motor fader would fight the finger. A knob's next value counts from the one sent, since its lights or fader show it now, so a plain pot would jump: the manual says to turn it on only for a controller with lights or motor faders. A change of the controller, its output or its knobs sends every value again. The card shows **Send values back** on the Follow line where it is wide enough (the MPC's screen swaps it in at the card's width), else as **Send back** at its foot. Still to come: learned controls' values sent back.
- **Controller mode** (18 Nov 2026, plan item 3.10; the owner's card, 01:21Z: "A Controller switch in I/O"). `MidiSetup::controller` is a `ControllerSetup`, the rig's: `controller.port = MIDI`, `controller.channel = 3`, `controller.pads = from 36` (else Kit), `controller.pressure = poly` (off, poly, channel) and `controller.qlinks =` 16 CCs (0 to 119, CCs 16 to 31 by default); `on` is never written, so every start has it off, and `project()` resets it while `set_rig` takes it. `ControllerSetup::pad_out(pad, profile)` is what a pad sends: Kit is the port profile's pad line, else `kit_note` (as a kit's track plays a drum machine); `From(n)` counts up from `n` across the 128 pads and stops past 127. The I/O view's **Controller** section (`controller.rs`, a `Form` after Learn) sets it: Controller mode, Sends to (the ports with an output, then the one named if it isn't plugged in; the channel), Pads send (Kit notes or One by one, From), Pad pressure, and a row per bank of four Q-Links, each field the CC's name (`profile::cc_name`, the port's profile first) or its number. `MusicIn::sync` turns it into a `controller_out::Plan` at each generation (the port found by name with an output, its profile, its shown name) for the main loop (`MusicIn::take_controller`). There `controller_out::Controller` takes the pads (the MPC's and the screen's), their pressure, the Q-Links (turns and drags), Q-LINK and PAD BANK while it is on, before STEP SEQ, the mutes and the kit. It keeps the note each pad sent (`held`), so a pad's release ends that note whatever changed since, and `set` ends every note it holds (and puts channel pressure back to 0) when it turns off or its port or channel changes. Poly pressure goes on the pad's note; channel pressure is the hardest pad held, sent when it changes. A Q-Link's value is absolute, 0 to 127, from 0, one step a detent, kept until controller mode goes off or moves. It sends through `MidiOut::controller`, which hands `OutputRouter::controller` the message as a thru tagged `CONTROLLER` (`THRU | 0x7C`), so the port's writer, its gear's delay and the Monitor take it as any message out; the Monitor says *sent by controller mode* (`To::Controller`). While MIDI out is off, or the port has no output open, nothing goes, and the run's closing lines count what wasn't sent (`controller_unsent`). The screen gets `ToUi::Controller(Option<ControllerShow>)` when it changes: a warn chip in the kit chip's place (the port, the channel and the pad bank, or why nothing goes; a tap is `FromUi::ControllerOff`, which clears `on` in the model and bumps `gen`), the Q-Link column's cards on every page (name, CC, last value), the hint line's Q-Link part (`controller_qlinks_hint`) and Play's own hint. The pads light `CONTROLLER_PAD` blue, bright while held, and the PAD BANK lights show the bank. Still to come: USB-B to a computer (rule zero review), and values sent back to the gear's knobs.
- **The Routing section** (`routing.rs`, board 5 of the MIDI mockups, 17 Nov 2026; its blocks 18 Nov). A grid (`grid`): rows are the ports with an input (from `midi::ports`, plugged in or named by the project), The pads and `Live::tracks`; columns are Armed track, Tracks by channel, Learn, each port with an output (as many as fit; the rest are counted under the grid) and CV out. Each square (`square`) is a `Kind`: Set (two lines, green bar for playing in MPFree, blue for sending out, the waiting colour when its device isn't plugged in or its part isn't on), Empty (+) or None (—), with a badge counting its routes' blocks. A port's Armed track and Learn squares are its `PortInput` (the rig's; turning both off is refused with a note), Tracks by channel its `TrackRoute`s, its squares under each port with an output its `ThruRoute`s to it (None for a USB device's own output, `thru_allowed`), a track's square under a port its `TrackOut`, and its CV out square the CV outputs that follow it. A tap on an empty square sets it and picks it; on a None square the hint line says why (`why_not_here`). The card under the grid shows the picked square: On, Off or + Route, Remove route, and up to three route lines with channel and track steppers, or for thru a channel stepper each (the same route twice is refused, with a note; a thru starts on all channels). The wheel moves across the squares, Page by four rows, - and + set or clear, and the Q-Links turn the picked route's channel and track (or a track output's channel). Blocks (`Whose`: a port's on its Armed track card, a route's or thru's after its line's Blocks ›): tiles `TILE_H` tall with a › between two (a tap swaps them), + Block (its five kinds, then Cancel), the picked tile's × (or -), and the Q-Links turning the picked block (`block_knobs`, `turn_block`; a Filter's four are notes, bend and pressure, controls, program). The card's last line (`last_through`) is the newest monitor message on the route's port (and channel) that went its way or nowhere, run through the blocks again to say what it became. Every change bumps `IoModel::gen`, so `MusicIn::sync` applies it.

## What's left

At the thread's handoff (2026-11-12), M1 to M6 are built on the host, and nothing has been tried on an MPC since the M0 captures.

- **Project and rig, kept between starts (app's `midi_saved.rs`).** The owner (13 Nov 2026) asked for project settings apart from system-wide ones. `MidiSetup::project` is the song's share, saved as the project's `midi` part by `MidiPart`: each track's Plays on, the routes and thru, Tempo from and following, each port's clock out, the CV jobs and learned controls. `MidiSetup::rig` is the gear's share, kept for every project: each port's input, ignored kinds, delay and the name given to it, and the controls learned for every project (CV tuning is for Prefs). Both texts have the part's form; `set_project` and `set_rig` join them. The store keeps the rig as `files/midi/rig.txt`. It is read once at start (lines it can't read are logged as `midi: setting not read: …` and counted on the MIDI section), and `midi_saved::Saver` hands the store its text when it changed, a few times a second; it only `try_lock`s the model, so the sound loop never waits. The project part is saved and opened with the project (gui.rs, through `MidiPart`), so a fresh start has the default song settings until a project opens. Opening a project replaces only the project part; New project resets it to defaults and keeps the rig.
- **Device rounds, each with the owner's own go.**
  - M2, read-only: keyboards in. MIDI Learn and clock in only read, so they ride on it.
  - M3 and M4: notes and clock out of the MIDI Out jack (cable 2), with Prefs' Send MIDI and CV out on, and the port page's test note and All notes off.
  - USB out: the same with a USB synth on the USB-A socket (a track, the test note, clock, unplugging it while a note is held).
  - M6: a meter on the CV jacks, with the same switch on, to learn which jack is which output and the volts at full scale.
  - CV LFOs and envelopes: four LFOs on CV 1 to 4 at once while the pads and lights are played, to check the controller keeps up with a change every 4 ms on each (else `MOD_STEP_MS` grows), then an envelope opening a module's VCA.
  - Expression in, read-only: a keyboard's pitch wheel and channel pressure on the synth, and if an MPE controller is at hand, a slide on one finger of a chord bending only that note.
  - MIDI Follow's Send values back, with Send MIDI and CV out on: a controller with lit encoders or motor faders following the Q-Links' values as pages change and as the MPC's Q-Links turn, with no fight while a knob is turned.
  - Expression out, with Send MIDI and CV out on: a keyboard's wheel and channel pressure on a synth on MIDI OUT through a track's Plays on, its bend range set by a profile's `bend` line, and if an MPE controller is at hand, a slide on the last note played bending the synth and its Y axis on CC 74; then the wheel bending a CV pitch output and the keys' pressure on a Pressure output, read on the meter.
  - Controller mode, with Send MIDI and CV out on: the pads (kit notes, then one by one), poly and channel pressure and the Q-Links playing a synth on MIDI OUT and one on USB, PAD BANK and Q-LINK stepping their banks, and the chip turning it off with a pad held (its note ends).
- **CV 5 to 8.** First the read-only `cv-ports` capture (`MPFREE_CAPTURE_PHASES=cv-ports`, [the diagnostics guide](../guides/audio-midi-diagnostics.md)): Akai's app drives a CV track on ports 5 and 6, then 7 and 8, and the report names the control changes each port got. If they are CC 8 to 15, as the pattern says, `CHECKED_OUTS` becomes 8 and the meter round covers all eight outputs.
- **After the meter round.** The measured full scale in `cv_wire::counts`, which jack is which output, and a tuning page for each output.

  - MIDI Learn by SHIFT + a touch on the Play page, the Pads and Sample tabs, the arpeggiator and plugin skins.
