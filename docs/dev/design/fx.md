# FX: the effects, the insert chain and the mixer

**Status:** 2026-11-14 (MPC coverage: fifteen effects, XY FX and its pad page, the category browser picker and the MPC name table; the rest as of 2026-11-12). The code is [`rust/mpfree-fx`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx), built on the shared [`mpfree-node`](../../../rust/mpfree-node) interface ([synths.md](synths.md) describes it). Every sound in the app plays through the mixer (round 8), and the mixer screen, its I/O view, the chain editor and the Sample tab's FX are in the app. The effects' costs on the MPC are still estimates beyond round 7's bench: the next device round measures Eco, Character, the wash, frozen tracks and the mixer on two cores. The mixer's MPC parity review (what the MPC 3.6 mixer and routing can do, and how MPFree covers each part) is in the doc "MPC mixer and routing, and the MPFree mixer". The full design (what makes FX sound great or cheap, what users ask for, other devices, libraries, the architecture, the roadmap and the decisions for Jay) is in the design doc "MPFree FX and filters design"; the skill tree and the mockups are in mpfree-claude (`skill-tree/fx-skill-tree.html`, `fx/mockups/`).

## Clipping

Deliberate clipping is a feature; clipping nobody asked for is not. No effect clamps a signal unless that is its job (Drive, Crush, the Delay's feedback saturation, Master safety), and nothing fails, stops or mutes because a level is high. Every output is meant to end in **Master safety**, which keeps the converter from clipping unless the user picks its Raw mode, and counts what it caught.

## The effects

Every effect is a `Node`, so it takes sample-timed parameter events (automation, p-locks, macros), describes its parameters for a generic GUI page, and keeps the node rules: no allocation, lock or panic in `process`, and the same output, sample for sample, for any block size.

- **Multi filter:** zero-delay-feedback state-variable filter after Andrew Simper (Cytomic): low-pass, high-pass, band-pass and notch at 12 or 24 dB/octave, a bell and two shelves, resonance up to a long ring, and optional drive into a soft saturator before it. Cutoff sweeps glide in the log domain and never zip or blow up.
- **DJ filter:** one knob, low-pass to the left (20 kHz down to 80 Hz), high-pass to the right (20 Hz up to 8 kHz), and bit-for-bit off in the middle.
- **Drive:** Soft (tanh), Hard, Tube (biased), Foldback, Fuzz and Rectify, run inside 2x (Eco) or 4x (HQ) oversampling, then a DC blocker, a tone low-pass, mix and output. Auto-gain keeps the level where it was, so more drive means more grit, not just louder. HQ resamples with linear-phase FIR half-bands. Eco (since 2026-11-13, Jay's call) uses polyphase IIR half-bands (5 all-pass sections, flat to 18 kHz, at least 71 dB down from 30 kHz): the worst alias of a 5 kHz sine through tanh at +18 dB is the same -59 dB as with the 95-tap FIRs, because at 2x it comes from harmonics folding inside the 2x band, and the effect costs 30 % less on the MPC's ARM. The IIR pair with a straight line between is exactly one all-pass, so Eco's dry side runs through that all-pass (`IirPhase`) and any Mix adds wet and dry in phase, as flat as HQ at every frequency (`drive_eco_blends_with_the_dry_side_at_any_mix`). 55 frames of latency in both qualities, low frequencies on time in both, so switching never moves the sound (Eco's impulse, not symmetric, peaks a frame later). The tanh curves use a rational fit (within 2e-7 of tanh) instead of the C library's tanh, which is slow on the MPC's ARM.
- **Comb:** a tuned feedback comb, so the input rings at a pitch and its harmonics like a string or a metal tube: Pitch (20 Hz to 5 kHz), Feedback (-99 to 99 %; negative keeps only the odd harmonics, an octave lower and hollow), Damping (a low-pass in the loop, 500 Hz to 20 kHz), Spread (the right side tuned up to 100 cents higher, for width), Mix, and Key follow: notes sent to it play the pitch, gliding over Glide. The fractional part of the loop's delay is a first-order all-pass (Thiran), which loses no top end however many times the sound goes round, and the damping's own delay at the pitch comes off the line, so it rings within 5 cents of the pitch from 55 Hz to 4 kHz. The input is scaled by 1 - |feedback|, so the peaks stay near unity gain. `Comb::response_db` gives its curve for drawing.
- **Crush:** bit depth (1 to 16) and sample-rate reduction (200 Hz to 48 kHz) with optional TPDF dither. Aliasing on purpose.
- **Delay:** free (1 ms to 4 s) or synced to the tempo (1/32 to 1 bar, dotted and triplet), ping-pong, and a feedback path through a high-pass, a tone low-pass and a soft saturator, so repeats darken and roughen like tape. Feedback goes to 110 %: the saturator keeps self-oscillation bounded. Time changes glide (tape-style pitch bend, no clicks). Freeze holds the buffer as a loop.
- **EQ:** four parametric bands, each a bell, a low shelf or a high shelf (frequency, ±18 dB, Q), low and high cuts at 12 or 24 dB per octave (Butterworth), and an output gain, on the same state-variable filters as the Multi filter. At its defaults it is the input, bit for bit, and a band at 0 dB or a cut that is off rests and costs nothing. A shelf's Q stops at 2 (a 5 dB bump past the corner at full gain; at Q 18 it would be 24 dB). A slope change crossfades over about 20 ms; a band type change eases the band's gain to 0 dB, where every type passes the input exactly, swaps and eases back, so neither clicks. `Eq::response_db` gives the curve the settings are heading to, exact to the filters (a sine measures within 0.05 dB of it), for drawing on an EQ page.
- **Chorus, Flanger, Phaser:** share an LFO that runs free (in Hz) or synced to the tempo (1/16 to 8 bars a cycle) and restarts in time with the bar when the transport starts; Width moves the right side's LFO a quarter cycle on at 100 %, and 0 % keeps a mono input mono. The LFO is a polynomial sine and the delay reads are cubic, so a swept delay stays bright. **Chorus:** up to four voices at fixed spread phases (adding a voice never moves the others, and voice gains crossfade), 2 to 40 ms, and a Tone low-pass on the wet signal. **Flanger:** 0.1 to 10 ms swept up to two octaves each way (so the comb moves evenly), feedback from -95 % (hollow) to +95 % (metallic). **Phaser:** 2, 4, 6, 8 or 12 trapezoidal first-order all-pass stages swept up to two and a half octaves around a centre, with feedback; a stage-count change crossfades between taps, and unused stages rest.
- **Lo-fi:** worn tape and dusty vinyl under one **Amount** knob, so it can be a whisper of age or a broken machine. Tape: wow and flutter (a delay swept by two pairs of sines at unrelated rates, up to 0.4 % and 0.15 % of pitch) and saturation (soft, slightly asymmetric, up to 18 dB of drive). Vinyl: crackle (random clicks and the odd duller pop) and hiss, which ride the music: they rise with the input and fade over about a second after it stops, so a stopped track is silent and a bypassed slot rests. Tone: low cut, high cut, width. Amount 0 takes everything to nothing and opens the cuts to 20 Hz and 20 kHz. The tape delay costs a fixed 2 ms of latency, reported for compensation, and the dry path is delayed to match, so Mix never combs. Presets: Worn tape, Dusty vinyl, Cassette, Old radio.
- **Compressor:** feed-forward, soft knee, gain computed and smoothed in decibels (Giannoulis, Massberg and Reiss, JAES 2012), stereo-linked, makeup, parallel mix, a sidechain input for ducking, and a gain-reduction meter. Its decibel conversions use fast log2 and exp2 fits (within 0.0001 dB) instead of `log10` and `powf`, which were most of its cost on the MPC's ARM. The detector hears the strip (or the key) through **SC filter**, a 12 dB per octave high-pass from 20 to 500 Hz that is off, and not run, at 20 Hz, so a kick's low end doesn't pump a mix bus; **Detect** reads the Peak or the RMS level (over 10 ms, linked as the louder side). RMS is calibrated 3 dB up, so a steady sine reads the same in both and switching changes how hard short hits push it, not where the threshold sits (a 1 ms click every 50 ms: Peak reduces at least 4 dB more). At their defaults (off, Peak) it is the compressor it was, bit for bit (`fx_render` output byte-identical), at the same cost: each detector setting has its own loop (23k ARMv7 instructions a block; 32k with RMS and the filter). Gentle glue now uses RMS with the filter at 90 Hz and Vocal level RMS.
- **Ducker** ([`ducker.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/ducker.rs), `mpfree.fx.ducker`): a volume envelope, the "pump" without a compressor's level dependence. Source **Sidechain** triggers when the strip's key (its sidechain input) goes over Threshold, with 3 dB of hysteresis and a peak follower that falls 60 dB in 50 ms; the duck holds while the key stays over. Source **Tempo** triggers on every 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2 or bar: from the song position while the transport plays, and from the absolute frame and the tempo while stopped, so it pumps the same however the stream is cut into blocks. The envelope: a straight attack (0.1 to 50 ms), a hold (0 to 500 ms), then a release (10 ms to 2 s) shaped from straight (Shape 0) to `1 - t^4` (Shape 100, stays down and snaps back), blended between whole powers so there is no `powf`. A trigger during the release attacks from where the envelope is, so nothing jumps. Depth 0 to 48 dB, Mix, and a Gain meter. `Ducker::curve` gives one cycle for drawing.
- **Transient shaper** ([`transient.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/transient.rs), `mpfree.fx.transient`): the stereo-linked peak is held for 25 ms, so a steady tone above 40 Hz reads as a steady level, then two pairs of dB followers: Attack is a fast follower minus one that rises over Speed (5 to 50 ms), Sustain a slow-falling follower minus a fast-falling one. The detector's floor is -60 dBFS, so a hit out of silence opens a gap of its level above -60 dB. Each gap times its amount (-100 to +100 %) times 0.6 is the gain, limited to ±15 dB, plus Output. Level-independent: the same setting works on a quiet and a loud hit, and a steady tone passes at 0 dB. At Attack and Sustain 0 the output is the input, bit for bit.
- **Pitch shifter** ([`pitch.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/pitch.rs), `mpfree.fx.pitch`): the classic two-head delay-line shifter. Two read heads sweep a 20 to 120 ms window at `1 - ratio` a frame, half a window apart, with raised-sine windows (Bhaskara's rational sine, normalised so the pair always sums to one); linear interpolation in the line. Pitch ±24 semitones, Fine ±100 cents, Spread (up to 50 cents, the left side down and the right up, for a doubler), Feedback (up to 90 %, through the soft saturator, for stairs) and Mix. Ratios come from `fast_exp2` only while pitch glides. No pitch detection, so it never glitches on chords or noise; for a voice, Pitch correct and the Harmonizer (below) follow the pitch sung.
- **Pitch correct** and **Harmonizer** ([`vocal.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/vocal.rs), `mpfree.fx.pitch_correct` and `mpfree.fx.harmonizer`, category Pitch and vocal): for one voice (singing, a solo instrument). Both hear the pitch with **YIN** (de Cheveigné and Kawahara, JASA 2002): the input's middle, low-passed (two SVF stages at a fifth of the tracker's rate) and taken down to 6, 8 or 12 kHz for the Low, Mid and High **Range** (60-600, 90-900 and 150-1500 Hz), every 5 ms: the difference function over the longest period, its cumulative-mean normal form, the first dip under 0.15 (else the deepest under 0.25; a local minimum counts by the bottom of the parabola through it, so a short period that falls between two lags of the coarse grid is still found), a parabola through it. The search stops as soon as the first dip turns up, so a voice in the middle of the range costs about half the full search. Under -50 dBFS, or with no dip deep enough, it is unvoiced, after two looks (10 ms) of grace. The shifting is **PSOLA** (pitch-synchronous overlap-add): pitch marks one period apart on the waveform's peaks (the highest peak of the low-passed middle from 3/4 to 5/4 of a period after the last; every 5 ms while unvoiced), and a voice lays a grain from the mark nearest its target time every local period divided by its ratio. A grain rises over the gap back to the mark before and falls over the gap on to the mark after (a smoothstep and one minus it, so neighbours always sum to exactly one), and where faster grains overlap more the sum of the windows divides the output back down. Grains keep their content, so the pitch moves and the formants stay (`the_vowel_stays_where_it_was`), with no comb or chorus at the grain rate. At ratio 1 every grain lands on the next mark and the voice is the input, sample for sample, one latency late (within 1e-5 on a voice, a breath and a glide); while the ratio sits at 1 a grain that is off its mark eases back by half a percent of a period per grain. The **latency** is two and a half of the Range's longest periods (Low 2002 frames, Mid 1336, High 802 at 48 kHz: 42, 28 and 17 ms), so a grain never reads ahead of the input; the mixer compensates it like any insert's. Positions are kept as 32-bit offsets from the newest frame, so hours in nothing drifts. Pitch correct (**Key**, **Scale**: Chromatic, Major, Minor, Harmonic minor, Major and Minor pentatonic, Blues; **Speed** 0-200 ms, 0 snaps; **Amount**; Range; Mix; meters **Note** and **Correction** in cents) moves the voice by Amount of the way to the nearest note of the scale, gliding over Speed; it shifts the stereo input as it is. The Harmonizer (Key, Scale, two voices each an **Interval** (Off, octave, 6th, 5th, 4th, 3rd down; unison; 3rd to octave up), **Level** and **Pan**, **Glide**, **Dry**, Range, meter Note) takes each interval in scale steps in a seven-note scale from the scale note nearest the one sung, so a 3rd up is major or minor as the key has it, and in the other scales the interval's size taken to the nearest scale note; the voices keep the singer's own intonation and vibrato, sit on an equal-power parabolic pan, and fade out in 20 ms while unvoiced. Five presets each (Natural, Hard tune, A minor snap, Pentatonic glide, Low voice; Third above, Close harmony, Octave stack, Wide fifths, A minor thirds). Cost on the MPC's ARM: Pitch correct 74k instructions a block (77k on Low), the Harmonizer 97k, measured on noise, the tracker's worst case. Getting there (from 228k and 328k): no float to 64-bit conversion, `floor` or `ceil` per frame (library calls on ARMv7; offsets in 32 bits, one fraction per grain), a rate per range, the early stop, and the per-frame paths kept to a compare, with grains and looks out of line.
- **Tuner** ([`tuner.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/tuner.rs), `mpfree.fx.tuner`, category Utility): the note a single sound is on and how many cents off, for a guitar, a bass, a voice or a synth. It passes the sound through untouched (latency 0) or, with **Mute**, silences it to tune quietly; **Reference** sets A4 from 415 to 466 Hz; meters **Note**, **Cents** and **Frequency**. Every 20 ms the vocal effects' YIN tracker finds the period over one wide band, 27 to 1600 Hz (a five-string bass's low B to the top of a voice), at about 6 kHz; then the difference function runs again at the full rate on a 20 ms window, first around that period (the dip, by a parabola) and then around 8, 64 and so on whole periods, up to as many as fit in 45 ms, each dip shared out among the periods it spans, eight times more each step so the error stays inside the three lags searched either side. That reads a period to a few thousandths of a frame: 0.01 cent or better from 31 Hz to 1.5 kHz in the tests. The reading glides (about 50 ms) while the note holds, jumps on a move of more than 30 cents, and holds for 300 ms after the sound stops. 45k instructions a block on the MPC's ARM (on noise; a note stops the coarse search early but adds the refining). The page draws a needle across ±50 cents, green within 3, amber within 15, red past that.
- **Cloud reverb:** a Rust port of the MIT [Cloud Seed](https://github.com/GhostNoteAudio/CloudSeedCore) reverb core by Ghost Note Audio (the notice is in [`LICENSE-cloudseed.txt`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/LICENSE-cloudseed.txt); MPFree is not made or endorsed by Ghost Note Audio). Cloud Seed's path: input cross-mix, low and high cut, pre-delay, a multi-tap early pattern, a chain of modulated all-pass diffusers, then up to 12 modulated feedback lines, each with its own diffuser, shelves and damping in the loop, every length and rate from a seeded random series that differs per side, so the tails are decorrelated. Its 45 parameters become 15: a **Character** (Plate, Room, Hall, Cloud, Echoes) picks the hidden structure, and the controls are Size, Decay (50 ms to 60 s), Pre-delay, Diffusion, Modulation, Mod rate, Damping, Low cut, High cut, Width, Early, Mix, Freeze and **Quality**. Quality has three settings. Light runs half the lines (at least 4), half the stages in each line's diffuser, half the early diffuser and half the early taps, with the same seeds, lengths, EQ and decay: the same size, colour, level (within 1.5 dB) and decay time (within 6 %), a little less dense, for about 45 % of Full's cost. **Eco is the default** (Jay's pick, so one reverb fits the MPC's effects budget): the Light structure at half the node's rate, between polyphase IIR half-band filters (six first-order all-passes each, flat to 0.19 of the node's rate and 85 dB down from 0.31, [`oversample.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/oversample.rs)). They take about six multiplies a pair each way where the 63-tap FIRs they replace took about 33; their phase is not linear, which a reverb's wet signal does not mind. Mix stays at the node's rate, so the dry signal is untouched; the wet signal is about 7 frames (0.15 ms) late. At half rate a cubic or linear read of a fraction would dull the top of the band on every pass round the loop, so Eco's stages and early taps read fractions with a first-order all-pass (Thiran), which loses nothing, and its damping and input high cut match the full-rate filters' loss at a quarter of the network rate. Eco keeps Light's colour within 2 dB from 1 to 10 kHz in every character (at 48 kHz) and has nothing left above about 14 kHz (13 kHz at 44.1 kHz), for about 30 % of Full's instructions (Echoes 65 %) and a sixth of its cache misses. Full is there when the top end matters and the budget allows. Five factory presets (Dark plate, Small room, Big hall, Endless cloud, Echo chamber). What changed in the port: feedback per frame (so the output does not depend on the block size; Cloud Seed feeds back one buffer late), deterministic modulator phases, cubic reads and whole-frame base delays (so Modulation 0 is lossless and Freeze holds without dulling), the diffusers counted in the loop length (Decay is the time to fall 60 dB: within 6 % at the default diffusion at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz; at Diffusion 100 the tail runs up to about 30 % long), an O(1) reset, and every control glides. A Character or Quality change dips the wet signal for 30 ms while the structure swaps, and the lines and stages that start running again start from silence. A change to or from Eco also waits for the resamplers to empty (about 5 ms) and restarts the whole tail, on the same frame in any block size. Freeze mutes the input and holds the tail; the tail estimate is endless while frozen, so a bypassed frozen reverb keeps ringing.
- **Master safety:** trim, a 5 Hz DC blocker, then Safe limiter (default), Soft clip or Raw. The Safe limiter looks 1.5 ms ahead; its gain is the smallest any frame in the window needs, released and averaged over the window, so it is always down before the peak arrives and the output never goes over the ceiling (-0.3 dBFS by default). Every mode counts overs and frames that leave above 0 dBFS. The latency is the same in every mode.
- **Character** ([`character.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/character.rs), node id `mpfree.fx.character`): the colour of analog gear as four small, cheap processors in the spirit of Airwindows' single-purpose plug-ins (original code, not a port). **Console** is a channel's rounded warmth: a sine-shaped curve (an odd quintic with unity slope at 0 and a flat top, so it never folds back), odd harmonics only. **Tape** is soft saturation, a head bump at 75 Hz (up to +3 dB) and a little top-end loss (a corner from 22 kHz down to 11 kHz). **Iron** is a transformer's low end: everything under 120 Hz is driven twice as hard as the rest, with an even harmonic that grows as it is driven, then a 10 Hz DC blocker. **Soften** is a smooth slew limit: loud, harsh highs lose their sharpest steps (a full-scale sine at 2.5 kHz up at Amount 100) while quiet highs and loud lows pass untouched. Controls: Mode, Amount (up to 12 dB of drive into the curves), Output and Mix. Quiet signals come out at the level they went in, in every mode, so Amount changes the colour and how hard the peaks are held, not the level of quiet parts. No oversampling (the curves are gentle) and no latency; a change of mode crossfades over 10 ms; the coefficients that follow Amount are worked out every 16 frames while it glides, on a grid counted from the stream's start, so the output is the same in any block size. Cost on the MPC estimate (cachegrind and the round 7 fit, at Amount 70): Console 0.7 %, Soften 0.8 %, Iron 0.9 %, Tape 1.1 % of a period, against Drive's 4.6 %. Presets: Console warmth, Hot console, Tape glue, Pushed tape, Iron bass, Soft highs. Its page draws the curve (or, for Soften, says it holds steps rather than levels) and a sine through it.
- **Factory presets** ([`presets.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/presets.rs), `mpfree_fx::presets(id)`): EQ (Kick weight, Snare crack, Vocal clarity, Thin for layering, Warm and dark, Telephone), Multi filter (Warm low-pass, Acid squelch, Thin high-pass, Radio band, Hollow notch, Sub lift), Drive (Warm tape, Tube glow, Hard clip, Fuzz box, Foldback grit, Octave up), Character (above), Comb (Metal ring, Plucked string, Hollow tube, Robot voice, Wide shimmer), Crush (12-bit classic, Soft grit, Chip, Phone line, Destroyed), Delay (Slapback, Dotted ping-pong, Triplet bounce, Quarter repeats, Dub echo, Tape runaway), Chorus (Subtle widen, Lush ensemble, Dimension, Fast wobble, Bar swirl), Flanger (Gentle flange, Jet sweep, Metallic, Hollow flange, Two-bar sweep), Phaser (Slow swirl, Deep phase, Funk wobble, Liquid, Quarter pulse) and Compressor (Gentle glue, Drum punch, Vocal level, Smash, Sidechain pump), with the reverb's and Lo-fi's. A preset sets the sound, never Mix or Quality, so Smash under a lower Mix is parallel compression. `tests/presets.rs` checks that every value names a parameter once and is in range, every preset changes something, and every preset plays finite and audible on bursts of noise with no peak over +12 dBFS.

- **The registry** ([`registry.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/registry.rs)): `mpfree_fx::EFFECTS` is every effect in one table: node id, name, a line about it, its `Category` (Dynamics, EQ and filter, Drive and colour, Modulation, Delay and reverb, Pitch and vocal, Performance, Plugins, Utility), a factory and its presets, in the picker's order. `registry::make(id)` builds one (project files and the app's builder thread go through it, via `mpfree_fx_ui::make`), `registry::offered(master)` is what a strip's picker lists (Master safety only on the master), and `presets(id)` reads the table. A new effect joins this table and nothing else; `mpfree-fx-ui` may name its pages and drawings, and without them it gets a page from its parameter groups. Hosted plugins are not in the table: the plugin rack lists them under `Category::Plugins`, and the app's builder makes `clap:` ids.

## Perform FX

`Perform` ([`perform.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/perform.rs), node id `mpfree.fx.perform`) is 16 pads of effects played live, one per hardware pad: hold a pad and its effect starts on the next grid line (Punch in on: Off, 1/32 to 1 bar, 1/16 by default); let go and the sound snaps back over the Fade (4 ms by default), while an echo rings out. A pad is a note: `NoteOn` 0 to 15 holds a pad, `NoteOff` lets it go, `AllOff` lets them all go; latching is the caller's (it does not send the `NoteOff`). Each pad has an Effect, a Length (1/64 to 4 bars, with triplets and dotted values) and an Amount, plus a read-only State (off, waiting for the grid, on, fading out) for the pad lights. Entry waits for the grid only while the transport plays; when it is stopped, a pad starts at once.

- **Time effects** take over the sound, one at a time: the pad held last wins, and letting it go goes back to the one held before it (which starts again from that moment) or to the live sound. **Stutter** loops the Length that comes next (the first pass is the live sound); **Beat repeat** loops the Length that just played; **Reverse** plays that Length backward, again and again; **Half speed** plays each Length at half speed (an octave down) and catches up at its end; **Tape stop** slows to a stop over the Length (speed falls in a straight line) and **Vinyl brake** brakes harder at the start, both fading out over the last tenth and silent while held after; **Pitch drop** glides down by up to two octaves over the Length (Amount 50 % = 12 semitones) with a two-grain shifter, keeping the time. Amount is the mix for all but Pitch drop. Loops fade into the frames before their start (after their end, for Reverse) at each wrap, so they loop without a click and keep their attack.
- **Tone effects** stack on top, in pad order: **Trance gate** (on for the first half of each Length, 2 ms edges, Amount = depth), **LP sweep** and **HP sweep** (the cutoff glides from 20 kHz down, or 20 Hz up, by Amount x 10 octaves over the Length, then holds), **Crush** (16 down to 4 bits and a hold of 1 to 8 frames) and **Noise riser** (band-passed noise rising from 300 Hz and swelling over the Length; Amount = level). They fade in and out over the Fade.
- **Echo throw** sends what you hear into a ping-pong echo of the Length (Amount = feedback, up to 95 %) while the pad is held; the repeats ring out after it is let go. It is the Delay node inside, run only while it rings (it goes to sleep, on a 32-frame grid, once its tail has died).
- **Wash throw** sends what you hear into a long, dark reverb while the pad is held (Amount = decay: about 1 second at 0 %, 2 at 70 %, 5 at 100 %; Length is not used); the tail rings out after it is let go. It is a small reverb of its own (four damped combs and two all-passes a side, Freeverb's line lengths, fed with the mono sum), not the Cloud reverb, so a throw costs about 1.5 % of a period on the MPC estimate rather than Cloud Eco's 10; it is awake only while fed or ringing and sleeps once its output has stayed under -100 dB for its longest comb. The Play page's Reverb wash is pad 16.
- **Mix** (id 85, 0 to 100 %, 100 by default) is how much of all of it is heard, tails included: 0 is the input exactly, 100 the effects alone (bit for bit as before the Mix existed). It glides over 20 ms. The Play page's FX mix knob turns it.
- **The DJ filter and the echo knob** sit at the end of the path, for the XY pad and the DJ knob: DJ filter (-100 low-pass to 100 high-pass, off in the middle; the same filter as the DJ filter effect), then Echo (0 to 100 %: how much of the sound goes into the same echo the throws use, with its feedback rising from 25 to 85 % as it goes up) and Echo time (1/8 dotted by default). A held throw sets the echo's time and feedback; otherwise the Echo knob does while it is up, and a ringing tail keeps its time. `XyPad` ([`xy.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/xy.rs)) is the pad's logic for the control thread: a finger plays two parameters (`XyPad::perform()`: the DJ filter across, resting in the middle, and the echo up, resting at the bottom); when it lifts, the pad glides back (60 ms to cover 63 % of the way, there in under 300 ms) unless Hold is on, and the values go to the node as parameter events. The DJ knob is the DJ filter on a Q-Link.
- Each slot's parameters (Effect, Length, Amount, Hold) are one group, "Slot 1" to "Slot 16", which is a page in the chain editor. They are not called "Pad": on a kit pad's own channel (the Sample tab's FX) "Pad 5" already names the pad the chain belongs to.
- The default bank follows the Perform FX mockup and the Play page's throws: Stutter 1/16, Beat repeat 1/8, Tape stop 1/4, Vinyl brake 1/2, Reverse 1/4, Half speed 1/4, Pitch drop 1/2, Trance gate 1/16, HP sweep 1 bar, Echo throw 1/8 dotted, LP sweep 1 bar, Crush, Noise riser 4 bars, Stutter 1/32 (a roll), Beat repeat 1/4 and Wash throw (decay 70 %).
- Lengths are fixed from the tempo when a pad starts, and every effect counts frames from its start (no 64-bit division per frame, which is a library call on the MPC's 32-bit ARM), so the output is the same, sample for sample, at any block size. Loops are copied out of the input's ring one frame per frame as they play (no copy burst on a press), into one of two loop buffers, so a loop holds its slice however long the pad is held. The longest loop is 4 seconds (2 bars at 120 BPM; longer lengths are cut to it), the longest stop 8 seconds.
- Memory at 48 kHz: 9.0 MB (an 11-second input ring, two 4-second loop buffers, the echo's lines and the wash's). Cost, from instruction counts under cachegrind and the round 7 fit to the MPC (per 128-frame block at 44.1 kHz): nothing held 0.3 % of a period; one time or tone effect 0.7 to 1 % (Half speed 1.3 %, Pitch drop 2 %); an echo throw 3 % while it rings; a wash throw about 1.5 % while it rings (scaled from `mpfree-bench --filter perform`, 3.7 µs a block on the build host against the stack's 16.7); the worst stack (Pitch drop with the gate, both sweeps, crush, the riser and an echo) 6.3 %. Perform FX runs only while pads are held or the echo rings, so this is a moment in a song, not a constant; `mpfree-bench --filter perform` has three cases.

## MPC coverage effects

Sixteen effects (14 Nov 2026) bring the MPC's effect list within reach: fifteen in new files of `mpfree-fx` and XY FX (below). Each follows a method from the literature or the best plug-ins, cut down to fit the MPC's ARM core; the research, sources and the picker decision are in the doc "MPFree effects: research and organisation". Shared parts live in [`kit.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/kit.rs): the parameter builder, a tempo-aware LFO (free in Hz or synced from 1/32 to 4 bars with triplets and dotted values, six shapes, restarting with the bar when the transport starts), a polynomial sine, a power-of-two delay line with cubic reads (and a precomputed `Frac` for reads whose position holds still), a Simper state-variable stage, a Linkwitz-Riley crossover and its matching all-pass, and a peak follower. Nothing calls the C library per frame, every control glides, and every one has no latency unless it says so.

- **Tremolo** ([`motion.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/motion.rs), `mpfree.fx.tremolo`): volume moved by the LFO (Rate or Sync, Shape, Depth, Width moves the sides apart). The square's edges are smoothed so the chop never clicks. **Harmonic** mode splits at 700 Hz (a 12 dB low-pass and what it leaves, summing back to the input) and moves the lows and highs in opposite phase, the early-1960s amp swirl. Presets: Amp wobble, Eighth chop, Stereo shimmer, Harmonic vibe, Slow swell.
- **Auto-pan** (`mpfree.fx.autopan`): the same LFO pans at constant power (sine and cosine from one polynomial), with Depth and Centre. Presets: Gentle drift, Hard ping, Random space.
- **Ring mod** (`mpfree.fx.ringmod`): the input times a carrier (20 Hz to 5 kHz, capped at 0.45 of the rate). Sine is alias-free; Square and Triangle are band-limited (PolyBLEP and PolyBLAMP), so they don't fold back. Sweep moves the carrier up to four octaves with the LFO. Presets: Robot voice, Metal bell, Sci-fi sweep, Square buzz.
- **Rotary** (`mpfree.fx.rotary`): a Leslie after J. O. Smith's rotating horn and the daisy-leslie project: an LR4 split at 800 Hz, horn and drum each a Doppler delay (cubic reads) with level following the rotor past two microphones, rotors that ramp up and down with their own inertia (the light horn first) when Speed moves between Stop, Slow and Fast, Balance, Width and a little Drive. Presets: Chorale, Tremolo fast, Gritty organ, Quick ramp.
- **Limiter** ([`dynamics.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/dynamics.rs), `mpfree.fx.limiter`): brick wall with a fixed 1.5 ms look-ahead (latency 1.5 ms plus 5 frames, the same with True peak off or on): a sliding minimum of the gain each frame needs, then a box average over the window, so the gain is down before the peak and nothing passes the Ceiling. **Auto release** lets go fast after a short peak and slowly under a sustained push (LazyLimiter's idea). **True peak** estimates the peaks between samples with a 12-tap windowed-sinc at three phases on the detector only. Presets: Safe peaks, Loud master, Drum smash.
- **Gate** (`mpfree.fx.gate`): open and close thresholds apart (hysteresis), so it doesn't chatter; Range instead of a hard mute; Attack from 0.1 ms, Hold, Release; a 1 ms look-ahead (its latency) so a hit's first edge survives; key filters (Key HP, Key LP) and the strip's sidechain as the key. Its audio comes out exactly its latency late (it was a frame early until the channel strip's test caught it, 14 Nov). Presets: Tight drums, Gated snare, Soft cleanup, Kick trigger.
- **De-esser** (`mpfree.fx.deesser`): listens above Freq; **Relative** detection compares that band with the whole signal so the threshold works at any level (Absolute is the plain band level). **Split** turns down only the band (a complementary split, so the rest passes untouched), **Wide** the whole signal; **Listen** plays the band alone. Presets: Male vocal, Female vocal, Harsh hats.
- **Multiband comp** (`mpfree.fx.multiband`): two LR4 crossovers make three bands, each with threshold, ratio and gain (shared attack and release, Solo, Mix, Output and three reduction meters). The low band passes an all-pass at the upper crossover (an LR4 pair sums to a 2nd-order all-pass, x − 2√2·band, so it is one state-variable stage), so the three sum flat in level and phase. Presets: Master glue, Tight low end, Vocal smooth, Drum crush.
- **Room** ([`rooms.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/rooms.rs), `mpfree.fx.room`): eight early reflections panned around the listener, then an eight-line feedback delay network mixed by a Householder matrix, each line damped by a one-pole set from Decay and Damping (Jot), with a slow modulation (on a 16-frame grid) against metallic ringing; **Gate** cuts the tail after the sound stops, the 1980s gated snare. Two pages: Room (Pre-delay, Size, Decay, Damping, Early, Mod, Gate) and Output (Low cut, Width, Mix). Presets: Small booth, Drum room, Big studio, Gated snare.
- **Plate** (`mpfree.fx.plate`): Dattorro's figure-eight tank: four input diffusers, two tank halves (modulated all-pass, delay, damping, all-pass) feeding each other, seven output taps a side. Two pages: Plate (Pre-delay, Size, Decay, Damping, Tone, Mod) and Output (Low cut, Width, Mix). Presets: Vocal plate, Snare plate, Dark plate, Endless plate.
- **Spring** (`mpfree.fx.spring`): after Välimäki, Parker and Abel: two springs of slightly different lengths, each a chain of 40 stretched first-order all-passes (the chirp) in a feedback loop of Length with a loss for Decay; Tension sets the chirp, Drip feeds more of each chirp back (the dub splash). Cut from the paper's 100 to 200 stages to fit the MPC. Presets: Amp spring, Dub splash, Short tank.
- **Tape delay** ([`echoes.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/echoes.rs), `mpfree.fx.tape_delay`): one, two or three playback heads (at 1, 1.95 and 2.9 times Time, a 1970s three-head echo), wow (slow, wandering) and flutter (fast, small), saturation in the loop (which also bounds Feedback up to 110 %), Tone and Low cut losses that grow each pass, Spread, and time changes that slide in pitch. A cheap asymmetric saturator after a low-pass stands in for a hysteresis model, which would need oversampling and a solver. Presets: Slapback, Dub echo, Space heads, Worn tape, Runaway.
- **Multi-tap delay** (`mpfree.fx.multitap`): four taps on the beat grid (1 to 32 sixteenths), each with level and pan, the last feeding back through Tone and Low cut. Presets: Dotted bounce, Triplet scatter, Wide ping, Build up.
- **Amp** ([`amp.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/amp.rs), `mpfree.fx.amp`): mono, as an amp is. Models Clean, Crunch, Lead and Bass set a tightening high-pass and one to three asymmetric tube-like stages run at 4x between polyphase IIR half-bands; the treble, mid and bass tone stack is Yeh's third-order transfer function of the Fender, Marshall and Vox (FMV) circuit from its real pot and capacitor values (bilinear transform), worked out again only when a knob moves; then a power stage, Presence, and a cabinet (Off, 1x12 open, 2x12, 4x12, 1x15 bass) of six filter stages fitted to published speaker responses. Each model's output follows a fixed trim over Gain (measured with a picked note at about -18 dBFS), so the amp comes out about 2 dB above what went in at any Gain: the knob changes the grit, not the loudness, as Drive's Auto-gain does; Level sets the rest. Presets: Sparkle clean, Classic crunch, Modern lead, Bass grit, Lo-fi radio.
- **Exciter** (`mpfree.fx.exciter`): the band above Freq is driven through a shaper at 2x (Colour turns it from odd, crisp harmonics to even, warm ones), high-passed again so only new top end is added (the Aphex idea), and mixed in under the dry sound by Amount. Presets: Vocal air, Hat sparkle, Warm presence.

For scale, the effects budget is about 470k cycles a block and one Cloud reverb in Eco is 185k to 337k.

### The last eight MPC names

Seven more effects (14 Nov 2026, after #315) cover the eight MPC names that had none, so `mpfree_fx::mpc` finds an effect for all 96. Each is a new file with its registry row in its category group, and named pages with chosen Q-Links in `mpfree-fx-ui`'s `pages.rs`. Parameters follow the MPC's where its guide lists them (Auto Wah, AIR Freq Shift, AIR Stereo Width, AIR Talkbox, AIR Channel Strip); the Granulator and Sample Delay follow common practice.

- **Auto wah** ([`wah.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/wah.rs), `mpfree.fx.autowah`, EQ and filter): one Simper state-variable filter swept evenly in pitch between Low and High (100 Hz to 6 kHz) by the sound's level (a follower with Sensitivity, Attack and Release, as the MPC's Auto Wah), by an LFO (free or synced), or by hand (Source Pedal: Center is the pedal, for a Q-Link). A negative Depth sweeps down. Shapes: **Wah**, the band-pass with the low end leaking through at about -9 dB as a pedal's does (the listening render showed a plain band-pass 20 dB down on a bass line), **Band-pass** (normalised, so more Resonance doesn't jump in level), Low-pass and High-pass. It retunes every 16 frames from a smoothed position. Presets: Funk quack, Slow bloom, Down wah, Eighth sweep, Pedal.
- **Talk box** (`mpfree.fx.talkbox`, Pitch and vocal): the AIR Talk Box idea with the sound itself as the carrier: three parallel band-passes at a vowel's formants (Peterson and Barney's adult averages, F2 and F3 levels after Fant), nine vowels from OO to EE in an order where neighbours sound alike, gliding in log frequency. The envelope and an LFO move it along the row (up to four vowels either way); Formant shifts every formant by up to 12 semitones (a smaller or bigger mouth); Resonance makes them half to twice as sharp as speech. Presets: Yeah, Wow wow, Robot choir, Small mouth.
- **Freq shifter** ([`shift.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/shift.rs), `mpfree.fx.freqshift`, Modulation; Frequency Shifter and AIR Freq Shift): single-sideband shifting by 0.01 Hz to 5 kHz. Olli Niemitalo's eighth-order pair of all-pass chains, 90 degrees apart within 0.7 degrees from 20 Hz to 22 kHz, makes the analytic signal, and a phasor (its phase in double precision, so 0.01 Hz still turns smoothly) shifts it; the other side is over 40 dB down. Modes as the AIR one: Up, Down, Up and down, Stereo (left up, right down). Feedback to 95 % through a soft clip, the output scaled by the square root of one minus feedback squared, since each pass lands on new frequencies and the passes add in power. No latency. Presets: Barber pole, Metal drums, Falling spiral, Wide shimmer, Robot.
- **Stereo width** ([`stereo.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/stereo.rs), `mpfree.fx.width`, Utility): the AIR Stereo Width's controls. Width (0 to 200 %) scales the side signal; Low, Mid and High widths act on it split by two Linkwitz-Riley crossovers (Low split 40 to 800 Hz, High split 1 to 10 kHz), and the mid runs through the matching all-passes, so at 100 % everywhere both channels are the input with the same phase turn. Delay (0 to 8 ms) adds delayed mid to the side: it widens a mono sound and cancels in a mono sum, where a one-sided Haas delay would comb; it fades in over its first millisecond (a delay of almost nothing would only pan). Pan (a balance) and Output. Presets: Mono bass, Wide top, Mono, Widen mono, Extra wide.
- **Sample delay** (`mpfree.fx.sample_delay`, Utility): delays left and right by whole samples (0 to 4800) and flips either side's polarity; a delay change crossfades over 10 ms and a flip glides over 2 ms. No latency of its own (the delay is the effect). Presets: Haas right, Haas left, Flip one side.
- **Channel strip** ([`strip.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/strip.rs), `mpfree.fx.channel_strip`, Dynamics; the AIR Channel Strip): MPFree's own Gate, Compressor and EQ in one insert, with the controls a channel needs: the gate's threshold, range, attack and release; the compressor's threshold, ratio (to 20, where the AIR one goes to 100), attack, release and makeup; a low cut (off at 20 Hz), a low shelf, a mid bell with Q and a high shelf; Order puts the EQ first or last; Output; meters for the gate's gain and the compressor's reduction. Each stage's switch crossfades over about 20 ms, a stage that is off and faded out isn't run (it starts fresh when it comes back), and it runs in 64-frame pieces with the dry copy on the stack. The gate's 1 ms latency stays whether the gate is on or not (its dry path is delayed to match). Pages: Gate, Compressor, EQ. Its parameter groups (the chain editor's tabs) put the three stage switches, Order and Output first, on Strip, so the EQ's eight knobs fit one tab. Presets: Vocal, Kick, Snare, Bass, Gentle glue.
- **Granulator** ([`grains.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/grains.rs), `mpfree.fx.granulator`, Delay and reverb): a 4-second stereo buffer read by up to 8 Hann-windowed grains. Size 10 to 500 ms; Density 1 to 80 grains a second, spaced up to a quarter either way at random so the cloud doesn't buzz at the grain rate; Pitch ±24 semitones plus a random Detune; Delay (or synced) back, moved by up to Jitter of a grain's size; a Reverse chance; Width, a random equal-power pan; Feedback through a soft clip; Freeze, which stops writing so the grains play what's there. Each grain's read point is kept inside the buffer for its whole life, whatever its pitch and direction; the level follows how many grains overlap; the random numbers are seeded on reset, so a render repeats. Left and right sit side by side in the buffer so a read works out its point once (12 grains on two mono buffers cost 281k instructions a block at their densest; 8 grains interleaved cost 160k). Presets: Shimmer cloud, Stutter dust, Octave down haze, Reverse swell, Freeze pad.

## XY FX

`XyFx` ([`xyfx.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/xyfx.rs), `mpfree.fx.xy`) is the MPC's XYFX: one effect at a time, played from two controls at once. **X** and **Y** (ids 0 and 1, so Q-Links 1 and 2 on the first page) map onto one of the existing effects, so what it plays is exactly what that effect plays:

| Effect | Engine | X | Y | Style |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Filter | DJ filter | low-pass to high-pass | resonance (0 to 90 %) | insert |
| Echo | Delay, ping-pong, 100 % wet | time in 8 columns, 1/32 to 1/2 | feedback (0 to 95 %) | send |
| Dub echo | Tape delay, one head | time 60 to 900 ms | feedback (20 to 105 %) | send |
| Wash | Plate, 100 % wet | decay 0.7 to 12 s | brightness 1.5 to 16 kHz | send |
| Crush | Crush | sample rate 22 kHz down to 700 Hz | bits 16 down to 2 | insert |
| Flanger | Flanger, no LFO | the sweep by hand, 0.2 to 8 ms | feedback (0 to 92 %) | insert |
| Phaser | Phaser, 8 stages, no LFO | the sweep by hand, 150 Hz to 6 kHz | feedback (0 to 90 %) | insert |
| Ring mod | Ring mod, sine | carrier 30 Hz to 3 kHz | amount | insert |
| Gate | Tremolo, square | rate in 6 columns, 1/32 to 1/4 | depth | insert |

- **Active** (on by default, so the insert plays from its tiles and Q-Links straight away) opens the effect over **Attack** (1 ms to 2 s) and closes it over **Release** (5 ms to 4 s), the MPC pad's envelope. An insert-style effect fades over the dry sound by Active times **Mix**, and once closed and silent it stops running (and starts from a reset when opened again, so the output never depends on where the blocks fell). A send-style effect is fed the sound times Active and added at Mix, so closing it lets the repeats or the tail ring out: lifting is a throw.
- Changing **Effect** crossfades over 30 ms; the new engine is set up and reset as it starts, so an old echo never comes back. All nine engines are made and prepared up front (about 3 MB at 48 kHz, mostly the two delays and the plate), because nothing may allocate when the effect changes on the audio thread. Every engine has no latency, so XY FX has none.
- `xyfx::axes(effect)` gives each direction's label, unit, the engine value at a position and, for a beat division, the column names, for a pad to draw. Its pad page is `XyFxPanel` (see [Effect pages](#effect-pages)); until the app opens effect pages, XY FX plays from its tiles and Q-Links.
- Presets: Filter sweep, Echo throw 1/8D, Dub siren, Washout, Bit crunch, Jet flange, Phase sweep, Robot, Trance gate 1/16.

## The effect picker

Jay picked a category browser (14 Nov, the research doc's option A) so the picker holds more than 40 effects and plugins without shrinking its tiles. The data is in [`mpfree_fx::picker`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/picker.rs) and the screen only draws it ([`mpfree-chain-ui/src/picker.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-chain-ui/src/picker.rs), inside the chain editor):

- **Sections** down the left: Favourites, Recent, then each registry category that has something to offer the strip, in `Category::ALL`'s order, each with its count (`picker::sections`, `members`, `counts`). It opens on the section used last, else Favourites when the strip offers one, else the first category (`first_section`).
- **Tiles** on the right, a fixed 120 px high in one to three columns with pages (never shrinking): name, about line, "N presets" bottom left and a star bottom right to favourite. A tap adds the effect at its defaults; a hold (500 ms) or the presets line (44 px tall, like the star) opens its presets (`picker::presets(id)`: Defaults, then the factory presets), and a preset is applied as the effect is built, so it starts there with no glide. The hold opens the presets while the finger is still down, as other holds do (the app calls `chain_hold` every frame); an effect with no presets waits for the lift, which adds it. Tiles, preset rows and the presets line act when the finger lifts on them, so a slip adds nothing.
- **The wheel:** turning it moves through the tiles, SHIFT + turn moves through the sections, and a push opens the highlighted effect's presets, then adds the highlighted preset. The app hands the wheel and its push to the chain editor while it is open (`ChainWheel`, `ChainPush`), and the hint line shows these bindings.
- **Favourites and Recent** live in `Shelf`, which the app keeps in the store as `files/fx/favourites.txt` and `files/fx/recent.txt` ([storage-spec.md](storage-spec.md)): one effect id per line, in the order shown (favourites as starred, recents newest first), at most 64 and 12. Ids the build doesn't know are kept, so a plugin missing today is a favourite again when it comes back; the picker just doesn't show them.

### Effects with modes

Jay (14 Nov): near-duplicate effects become one effect with modes, as long as two can still be chained (a Room then a Plate). He picked five families, which take the picker from 44 tiles to 36. [`mpfree_fx::families`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/families.rs) holds them:

| Family | Modes (engine) |
|---|---|
| Reverb | Room (`room`), Plate (`plate`), Spring (`spring`), Hall, Cloud, Echoes (`cloud` with Character 2, 3, 4) |
| Delay | Digital (`delay`), Tape (`tape_delay`), Multi-tap (`multitap`) |
| Drive | Soft, Hard, Tube, Foldback, Fuzz, Rectify (`drive` with Mode 0 to 5); Console, Tape, Iron, Soften (`character` with its Mode) |
| Filter | Multi (`filter`), DJ (`dj_filter`) |
| Tremolo | Volume, Harmonic (`tremolo` with Mode 0, 1), Auto-pan (`autopan`) |

- **Nothing underneath changes.** A mode is an engine plus fixed values. Every engine keeps its node id, parameters and presets; an insert saves its engine's id as before, so old projects open unchanged, and two inserts of one family are two separate effects. An engine's first listed mode is the one its defaults make, so a new insert is in it. `mode_of` finds an insert's mode from its values; a Cloud reverb set to its own Plate or Room (Character 0 or 1, from before families) is in none, shows as "Cloud reverb" with its Character knob, and its own Plate and Room presets are left out of the family's list.
- **The picker** shows a family once, as its first engine's tile, where that engine sits in the registry (`tiles`, `tile_id`; favourites and recents store the tile's id). Its presets list is every mode's (`presets`, `picker::preset_rows`), the mode under each name; a preset with no fixed values adds its engine's first mode.
- **Names:** `insert_name` gives "Reverb · Plate" for an insert in a mode, else the engine's name. The chain editor's slot list and Q-Link title use it; the mixer's rows, the Mix tab's Effects look and the Pads Effects row are the app thread's to switch.
- **The Mode chooser** (chain editor, at the right of the page's header while an insert is selected): a button per mode when they fit beside the name, else ‹ and › each side of a chip ("Tube   3 of 10") that opens the list of modes over the page. The mode's fixed values are left off the page (`ChainState::mode`, `families::hidden`), since the chooser stands for them. A tap sends `ChainAction::SetMode { slot, mode }`.
- **Switching** ([`mpfree-app/src/fxmodes.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/fxmodes.rs)): within one engine (Hall to Cloud, Soft to Fuzz) the mode's fixed values are set as knob turns are. Across engines the new engine is built on the builder thread like an added effect (`MixHost::replace_insert`, `Builder::build_switch`) and goes in with `Edit::Replace` keeping the insert's slot id, so the editor keeps it selected, and bypassed if it was. It starts with the controls the two engines share (`switch_values`: same name, unit and kind, a choice only between identical lists, clamped to the new range; for a control the mod engine holds, its own value, not where a macro or row has it), or, if this insert was in that engine before, with what it had then (`Switch::remembered`, kept per strip, slot id and engine for the session, dropped when a project opens). Its macro maps and matrix rows come off as the switch is sent, so nothing drives the old engine's ids into the new one, and go back on the new engine's matching controls (`carried_id`) once it is in (`fxmodes::land`, from `MixHost::poll_built`); ones with no match, and step locks, are dropped. If the new engine never comes, they go back on the old one. A second switch on the same insert before the first is in is refused ("The mixer is busy").
- **Left for later:** a Q-Link learned to one of the insert's knobs keeps its slot id and parameter id through a switch across engines, so it turns whichever control the new engine has at that id (possibly a fixed value the chooser stands for, such as Cloud's Character); learn it again. The app thread is moving learned knobs with `carried_id` in its Mix tab work. The kept values aren't saved with the project.

### Main pages (layout B)

Jay picked layout B for effects with more than eight controls (14 Nov, from the mockups canvas): a hand-picked Main page first, then the groups, with automatic pages of eight for effects without a Main page and for plugins. The effect page redesign (below) has since replaced it in the chain editor for the built-in effects, which follow their page plans; a plugin keeps its pages of eight by group, and the Main pages' four still give the plans their Q-Links.

- **The data:** [`mpfree_fx::main_page`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/main_page.rs) gives each of the 17 built-in effects with more than eight settable controls a Main page. That is up to eight controls by name ("Band 2/Gain" where a name repeats), Main's four (the Q-Links, four of those eight), and for the Channel strip its stages: Gate, Comp and EQ, each a toggle and the group of its controls. Its tests check that every long effect has one and only those, that every name is a real settable control and never a family mode's fixed one, that Main's four are on the page, and that a stage's switch is a toggle.
- **The pages** (`mpfree_chain_ui::state::pages`): Main first, then the groups in the order the effect names them. A group over eight (a plugin's) is split into pages of eight, "EQ" then "EQ 2". The chain editor uses them, without a Main page, for an effect with no plan.
- **The Q-Links:** `ChainState::qlinks` gives the effect's four on every page (`ChainState::main_four`, whose values the app's `ChainPage::set_chain` watches with the page's): its plan's, else the first four of the page shown.

### Page plans (the redesign)

Jay approved the effect page mockups as drawn (14 Nov, "Build it as drawn"), to the same design rules as the instrument pages: labelled sections by job, each in its job's colour, controls sized by how much a player reaches for them, choices as buttons and switches rather than knobs, drawings inside their sections, and a control that does nothing dimmed with the reason. The page is the 844 by 540 pixels between the strip's effects on the left and the Q-Links on the right, under the header (the effect's name, presets and Mode chooser) and over the one-row Modulate strip. Most effects fit one page, so they need no tabs.

- **The data:** [`mpfree_fx::page_plan`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/page_plan.rs) gives every built-in effect a plan. Each plan has its Q-Links (up to four), its pages, and its dimming rules. A page is a layout spec in `mpfree-ui`'s syntax over its sections' keys. A section has a heading, a job (Source for the main process in cyan, Tone in coral, Shape for dynamics in lime, Motion in pink, Voice for output in grey), its controls in order, size tiers (hero, main, trim, else standard; a Q-Linked control is main unless trim), faders, compact rows, print labels ("Gate thresh" reads "Threshold" in the Gate section), the effect's drawings by card title, live meters, and a switch in the heading for a stage. A section can also link to another page and carry a short note. The shape is the instrument pages' own, so the shared section renderer draws both. Sections are a view: no control is renamed, so presets, projects and automation are untouched.
- **Pages:** one for most effects. The Channel strip has a Main page (Gate, Compressor and EQ as switchable sections that open their own pages, and the signal path) and a page for each stage. Perform FX has its play controls, then slots 1 to 8 and 9 to 16.
- **Dimming:** a rule names the controls, the choice or toggle that decides, the values that dim them and the reason ("Sync sets it", "off: pick a slope", "only a bell or a shelf uses it", "the slot is off"). A Sync rule also swaps the Q-Link: while a delay is synced, the Q-Link on Time turns Sync, as the effect pages already do. A section with a switch dims its controls while the switch is off.
- **Q-Links:** Main's four for the 17 effects with a Main page, the effect pages' picks for the others they cover, and a choice for the rest.
- **Tests:** in `page_plan`, every built-in effect has a plan. Every name is a real control: settable ones as controls, read-only ones only as meters. A one-page effect shows each control and each meter exactly once. A family mode's fixed controls (Drive's Mode) sit in compact rows and are never Q-Links. The Q-Links agree with the Main pages, and the dimming rules decide by a choice or a toggle. In `mpfree-chain-ui/tests/plans.rs`, every page's layout parses, places each section once in 844 by 540 pixels and leaves each section room for its controls at their sizes. Every drawing a plan places is one the effect draws or one on `TO_DRAW` (the reverbs' tails, the tape's heads and the Channel strip's stages, which come with the new pages), and every drawing an effect has today is placed.
- **The page the chain editor shows** ([`mpfree_chain_ui::sections`](../../../rust/mpfree-chain-ui/src/sections.rs)): `page_view` turns an insert and its plan into the page shown. It gives the plan's pages, every control, switch and meter on the page once, and its sections with their labels, tiers, Q-Link badges, dimmed controls with the reason in the section's note, and links to other pages. The Q-Links are the plan's, the same on every page, and a synced Time's Q-Link turns Sync. A family mode's fixed controls are left out. An effect with no plan (a plugin) keeps its pages of eight by group, a section per group, with the page's first four on the Q-Links. Its tests run every page of every built-in effect.
- **The chain editor** ([`mpfree_chain_ui::view`](../../../rust/mpfree-chain-ui/src/view.rs)) draws it in the frame the mockups show (layout spec section 4b): the strip's effects 220 px wide at the left (‹ and the strip button over the effects, Add effect, then Up, Down and Remove), the effect column 844 px beside the app's 168 px Q-Link column. The column has a 52 px header (the effect's name, then its Mode chooser or, for an effect with more than one page, its page tabs, with ‹ › when they don't fit), the page (540 px on the MPC) and a 54 px Modulate row. `ChainState::from_chain` fills the state from `page_view` (`groups` its pages, `params` every control on the page, `main_four` its Q-Links, `sections` and `spec` the page), and `ChainView` hands the page to an embedded `SectionView`, a control per parameter (its index in `params` is what the widget's events carry) with the drawings its plan names, picked by title from every card the effect page draws (`picture` with page `""`). ## The insert chain

`Chain` runs effects in series, the way a track, bus or master will:

- **Bypass is click-free and keeps tails:** switching a slot off fades its input out over 20 ms and its dry signal in, so a delay keeps ringing (spillover) and a drive crossfades to dry. A bypassed slot keeps running on silence for exactly its tail, then rests and is reset.
- **Latency never moves:** each slot's dry path is delayed by the slot's latency, on or off.
- **Sleep on silence** (review 14 Nov, item 2): a slot that is on also rests once its input has been silent (within -140 dB) for the node's `Node::rest_frames` and its own output has been under -100 dB for 64 frames: the node is reset and not called, and the slot puts out silence until a frame with sound, or an event other than a parameter change. Parameter changes meanwhile apply at once, and the node is reset again as it wakes so its glides start settled. Every decision falls on a frame (the rest is worked out again after each event), so the output stays the same for any block size, and each slot after a sleeping one sees exact zeros, so a quiet chain falls asleep a slot at a time and then costs a scan of its input. `rest_frames` is how long the node's state lasts, not only its tail: a compressor's is ten release times plus the RMS detector, a transient shaper's ten times its slowest follower, the Limiter's ten of its slowest release, the Gate's Hold and Release after its detector falls, and a reverb's tail to 100 dB down (Spring, and Plate and Room without modulation). Amp, Exciter, De-esser and Multiband sleep too (the newer effects pass their rest to `kit::node_impl!` as a fourth argument). The default, `u32::MAX`, never sleeps; so do nodes that make sound from silence or keep time across it, which reset would change: Chorus, Flanger, Phaser, Pitch, Cloud with any modulation, Plate or Room with modulation, Room with its gate, Crush with dither or a lower rate, a keyed Compressor or Gate, Tremolo, Auto-pan, Ring mod, Rotary, the Tape and Multi-tap echoes (wow and flutter, or taps that follow the tempo), the XY FX, the Ducker, Perform, Master safety and CLAP plug-ins. The De-esser's filters and the Multiband's crossovers, which follow a glide every 16 frames, now also land on the new frequency the frame the glide ends, as a reset puts them (they could stop a hair short before). A slot that holds a lead or could (`Node::can_lead`) never sleeps, so late returns stay the on-time output, bit for bit. `Chain::set_sleep(false)` turns it off; `Chain::sleeping` counts. `tests/sleep.rs` runs every effect through bursts and silences with and without sleep: the worst difference is under -98 dB (most are exact), and the output is bit-identical across block sizes. Measured on the build host (`examples/sleep_cost.rs`): 64 tracks with an EQ and a compressor each, 8 playing at a time, 148 µs a block kept awake, 82 µs with sleep (the mixer with no inserts: 10 µs); every insert awake costs the same as before. (Settled slots with no latency also skip the per-frame fade and dry-path loop now: the same 64 tracks kept awake went from 175 to 137 µs.)
- **Events:** each slot has a queue of sample-timed events (256 per block); a full queue drops and counts.
- **A NaN guard:** a node that puts out a non-finite sample is reset, its slot plays dry for that block, and the chain counts it.
- **Level meters for one slot:** `Chain::watch(Some(slot))` keeps that slot's input and output peaks, falling 20 dB a second, and `Chain::levels()` reads them in dBFS (two peak scans a block for the watched slot, nothing for the rest). It follows the slot id, so the meters stay on the effect when it moves. The effect pages show them as a Levels card when the host puts them in `FxState::levels`.
- **Block-size independent,** including where a tail starts and ends. The one exception: a node with an endless tail (a frozen delay) that is bypassed and then unfrozen picks up its new tail at the end of that block.

### Saved with the project

Each insert is saved in the project's `mixer` part with its slot id, on or off, node id, sidechain key, parameters and, for a node that keeps more than its parameters, its state: `Node::save_state` (bytes, called on the audio thread between periods, never blocking) and `Node::load_state`. `Node::save_extras` names what it needs to be made again (a plug-in's file). An open makes each effect by id (`project::make_node`) on the screen thread and puts the chain in through the mixer's queued edits (`MixHost::open`). See [browser.md](browser.md#the-parts).

## The mixer

`Mixer` (in `src/mixer.rs`; the module docs have the details) is the one place audio is summed. It covers what the MPC 3.6 mixer can route, in fixed stages so nothing can feed back:

1. **Inputs** (2 by default, up to 8): the line input as a stereo pair or either side as mono (Source), heard through the strip's inserts when Monitor says so (Off, On, or Auto: heard while the track is armed).
2. **Pad channels** (a pool of 32 by default, up to 128): pads that need their own inserts, sends or output. The sampler renders those apart; every other pad stays in its kit's track.
3. **Tracks** (16 by default, up to 160): each instrument adds into its track; inputs and pad channels can be routed in.
4. **Submixes** (8): sum what is routed to them; a submix can go on to a higher-numbered one or the master.
5. **Returns** (A to D): every strip above has four sends, each before or after its fader.
6. **Master**: inserts and fader, then Master safety, always last.

Every strip has an insert chain (8 slots by default) and a fader (volume -80 to +6 dB, balance pan, mute, solo); every strip but a return and the master has an output: the master, Sub 1 to 8, a track (inputs and pads), or none. Its parameters are a `ParamInfo` list like any effect's, so the GUI and the automation see them the same way.

- **Solo in place:** soloing a strip keeps it, every strip it feeds and every strip that feeds it; returns and the master are never cut, and mute wins.
- **Sidechain keys per insert slot:** a compressor on the bass can listen to the kick, a gate on the pads to the hats. The key is the other strip after its inserts and before its fader and mute, so a muted ghost kick still pumps the mix. A key may come from an earlier stage or the same stage (the mixer orders each stage so the key is ready, and refuses a loop); it costs no delay.
- **Latency compensation:** every path into a track, submix, return and the master is delayed to line up with the slowest path that could reach it, so routing and fader moves never shift anything in time. Live inputs are the exception: they are never held back, so playing through the mixer stays as immediate as it can be. Compensation stops at 50 ms (a setting); delays crossfade over 20 ms when an effect with latency comes or goes.
- **Click-free:** controls glide over 4 ms; an output change crossfades from the old destination to the new one; adding, removing, swapping or moving an insert while playing (`Edit`, built on the control thread) ducks that strip for 10 ms around the change while the rest plays on, and what it takes out comes back to be dropped off the audio thread.
- **Capture points** for the recorder: any strip before its inserts, before its fader or after it, or the master after Master safety: resampling, the looper, recording an input dry or through its effects, stems. `capture_latency` says how far to slide a take. There are 64 (`CAPTURES`), enough for a stem of every strip at once; the ones in use are kept first in the mixer's list, so a block looks only at those and an idle slot costs nothing.
- **Sample-timed and block-size independent,** including mute, solo and output changes that affect other strips: the same output, sample for sample, at any block size.
- **Guards:** a non-finite sample in any input silences that strip for the block and is counted; Master safety catches a hot sum.
- **Cheap when quiet:** a strip silent for longer than its delays, with nothing moving, skips its work; one nothing wrote to this block does not even scan its input. Its inserts sleep on silence (above).
- **A second core** (`set_lane`, off by default; Jay 2026-11-11: using every core is a top priority): pad channels' and tracks' inserts run partly on another core with a lane from `mpfree-lanes`. At the start of each of those two stages the mixer hands the lane the inserts of the stage's last strips (from the end back, while the lane's share stays at half the stage's inserts or under), runs the strips before them itself meanwhile, and takes the inserts back just before those strips' faders. The inserts and their blocks move to the lane and back (a swap of pointers, no copy, no allocation), so nothing is shared. A strip goes only if its inserts have no sidechain key, it keys nothing and it is not frozen. The output is the same, bit for bit, with or without the lane, on this thread or a worker, at any block size (`tests/mixer.rs`). If the lane is later than the wait given to `set_lane` (about a period), or than the block's one deadline when the host gives one with `set_lane_deadline` (the app does, from the sound card's queue, for every join in the block: each stage and the returns a block late), those strips are silent for that block and play their input dry until their inserts come back; meanwhile edits and latency changes wait and those inserts refuse events (`queue_insert` says false). `MixerStats::lane_posts` and `lane_late` count. On the build host, eight tracks of Drive (HQ) and a compressor took 211 to 235 µs a block on one core and 129 to 143 µs with a lane (a worker that spins or sleeps); the bench song (6 tracks of EQ and compressor, the reverb on a return) only goes from 162 to 160 µs, because its track inserts are light next to the reverb, which a lane cannot take (returns need every send first). On the MPC the engines are about 6.6 times slower, so the device round says what it is worth there; `mpfree-bench --lanes` has the song on one core and two. Returns one period late on another core (pipelined returns) take the reverb too: see the next item.
- **Returns a block late** (`set_pipelined_returns`, off by default; Jay 2026-11-12: reverb returns only): a reverb return's inserts run on another core one block behind and still sound on time. At the start of each block the mixer hands a lane those inserts with the input they kept the block before, runs the pad channels, tracks and submixes itself while the lane works, and at the return takes back that output and keeps this block's input for the next. The reverb takes the block back from its pre-delay: a node can run with its input late (`Node::can_lead`, `lead_in`, `lead_out`), and Cloud does when it is all wet and its pre-delay is at least the block, reading the pre-delay that much nearer. `Chain::lead_slot` finds such an insert (fully on, no latency); the slots before it work on the late input, the slots after it on time. So the output is the on-time output, bit for bit, at Full, Light and Eco, on this thread or a worker. A return goes late only when that holds and it has no sidechain key and keys nothing; every other return stays on time: a delay, parallel compression or drive, a reverb with no pre-delay (Dark plate, Echo chamber) or a dry mix, because a copy of the dry signal a block late comb-filters against it (12-13 dB notches from about 172 Hz in the A/B renders, project files `fx/late-returns-ab/`). A return moves between the two at a block's start whenever that changes (a knob, bypass, a change of block length, an odd block at Eco, the lane turned on or off), and seamlessly: starting puts out a block from what the pre-delay holds without taking input, stopping hands the inserts the input they kept. `tests/late_returns.rs` measures every case against the mixer on time: all are exact except a mix turned below 100 % while late, whose one block of dry signal comes out late, 67 dB under the reverb (60 dB below full scale at most). The inserts are home between blocks, so the app reads them, and knob turns, bypass and edits work as usual. A lane later than the wait silences the returns for that block and the next, and a block of the return's input is lost when it goes back on time. `MixerStats::pipe_posts`, `pipe_late`, `pipe_starts`, `pipe_stops` and `pipe_lost_input` count. In `mpfree-bench --lanes` on the build host (cores 3 and 2) the bench song's audio thread falls from about 175 to 125 µs a block with the Cloud reverb at Full and from about 138 to 98 µs at Eco, a third or more off; the idle mixer costs 0.9 % more instructions with it off, Cloud under 1 % more for the nearer read. Turning it on in the app (mpfree-app `cores.rs`) is a separate change.
- **Profiling:** `set_profiling(true)` times the whole mixer, each strip and each insert slot into `mpfree-prof` probes (off by default; two clock reads each).

**The mixer screen** is [`rust/mpfree-mixer-ui`](../../../rust/mpfree-mixer-ui): the panel the Mix tab holds (TRACK MIX, or a tap on the tab; layout spec section 3a). Views for Tracks, Pads (SHIFT + TRACK MIX), Inputs, and Submixes with returns, each in two looks (`Look`, the toggle left of the page arrows; the widget keeps it, so the tab opens on the last one, and `set_look` puts Faders back for TRACK MIX). **Faders:** pages of strips with the master always on the right. Each strip has its name and colour, four insert rows (a tap opens the slot, "+ FX" adds one, "SC" marks a keyed slot, "+N more" past four of the strip's 8 slots), sends A to D (drag; a tap on the letter takes it before the fader), pan (drag; a tap centres it), a fader beside a stereo meter (drag, relative, with a 0 dB detent; a tap on the level sets 0 dB), Mute and Solo (and Arm on an input, whose meter shows what comes in), and its output, which opens a picker of only the allowed destinations. The master shows Master safety's mode, ceiling, what it caught and its limiting. While profiling, the master's name row shows the mixer's share of a period and each insert row a cost bar. **Effects:** one panel over the strips and the master, a row a strip (a page is the rows that fit) with its 8 slots in chain order and + FX in the first free one, and the master's row pinned at the bottom. A tap on an effect picks it (`MixAction::PickInsert`; the app keeps that strip's `ChainHost` without the editor, `Panels::pick_insert`, and sends its `ChainState` while the mixer shows, so the Q-Link column turns its first four knobs); a tap on the picked one opens it (`OpenInsert`); a drag along the row moves it (`MoveInsert`, `Edit::Move` through the chain host, the pick following it). The app sends `MixAction::Unpick` when the pick goes. Meters aren't drawn on Effects, so a meter-only update redraws only a take's progress (and the costs while profiling). A meter-only update redraws only the meters. While the mixer is open the four Q-Links work on the selected strip: Volume, Pan, Send A and Send B (`mpfree_mixer_ui::qlinks` gives each card's label and text, and turns a knob into a parameter change; SHIFT gives fine steps). `cargo run --release -p mpfree-mixer-ui --bin mpfree-mixer-preview` draws the sample screens with a real mixer playing behind them. Wiring it into mpfree-app is the Hello thread's, which owns the layout.

## Freeze and print

`Print` ([`print.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/print.rs)) records a take from one of the mixer's capture points (`Mixer::set_capture`), lined up for the capture point's latency, so the take is exactly what the strip played.

- **A job** (`PrintJob`): where the take starts (a song beat), how many beats it lasts (a clip's loop), whether it must be a **steady** take (the transport rolling for at least one whole loop first, so delays and reverbs are already ringing at its start), and its tail: cut at the loop's end, or run on until the strip has been silent (below -90 dB) for 100 ms. `PrintJob::freeze(start, beats)` is a steady take with no tail.
- **While it records** (`Print::process` after the mixer, each period) it fails rather than give a wrong take: on a stop, a jump (a locate or a loop that is not the clip's), a tempo change, or no room left (`PrintError`). `progress` gives 0 to 1 for the screen. `take` hands the take over without copying.
- **A print** (`Printed`) is the take and its tail: `wav` writes it as a 32-bit float WAV for a new sample, and `frozen` folds the tail back onto the loop's start so it plays seamlessly round and round.
- **A frozen strip** (`Mixer::freeze`, tracks and pad channels only) plays its take in place of its inserts, before its fader, so the fader, pan, mute, solo, sends and anything keyed by it still work. It crossfades in and out over 10 ms (`FREEZE_FADE_MS`); once the inserts have rung out they rest and their queued parameter changes are applied, so a thaw (`Mixer::thaw`) picks up where they are. `needs_input` turns false once the strip is frozen, so the host can stop the instrument feeding it. The take follows the song's beats, at another tempo too (it is read at the new speed, so its pitch moves with it, as varispeed). `pop_thawed` hands back a thawed take for freeing off the audio thread.
- **Cost:** with the six tracks that have inserts frozen, the bench's song mixer drops from 185 to 131 µs a block on the build host (`mpfree-bench --filter frozen`, which also prints a bus all the while). A frozen strip costs a copy and its fader; a print costs a copy per period.
- **Held quiet** (`Mixer::set_frozen_quiet`): the frozen audio fades out and back in as at a stop and start of the transport, without a thaw. The app uses it while the track a frozen instrument took is muted.
- **On the mixer screen** a second tap on the selected strip's name opens its menu: Freeze or Thaw (where `StripView::freezable`), Print to a new sample (any strip), or Cancel while a take is under way. A frozen strip shows Frozen over its inserts, and a take shows Freezing or Printing with its progress (`StripView::frozen`, `StripView::take`; touches give `MixAction::Freeze`, `Thaw`, `Print` and `CancelTake`).
- **In the app** ([`takes.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/takes.rs)): `Desk::screen` hands those four actions to `MixHost::take_action` with what each track plays (`TimeHost::now_playing`, from mpfree-play's `Player::now_playing`), and the sound loop calls `MixHost::takes_period` after the mixer each period (player_session, the smoke session and `rt_safety` alike).
  - *The loop.* A track's own instrument (`Feed::Track`) takes its track's clip. Drums, Synth and the pad channels (`Feed::Pads`) take the tracks with no instrument of their own: the longest clip playing there, and for a freeze every other clip there must fit it a whole number of times, so the sum repeats with it. Loop and Audio follow no clip and are not freezable (`StripView::freezable` false); a print of them, or of a submix, a return, the line input or the master (`Feed::All`), takes the longest clip on any track. A take is at most `MAX_LOOP_S` (64 s).
  - *One take at a time,* on capture point `TAKE_CAPTURE` (the one below the sampler's; the export's streams stay below it) at the strip's Before fader point. A freeze is `PrintJob::freeze`; a print starts at the loop's next start, not steady, and its tail runs until silent (`PRINT_TAIL_MS`, 10 s at most). The `Print`'s memory is made on the builder thread (`Builder::print`), and spent prints, thawed takes and unsaved prints are dropped there.
  - *Following.* What a strip took is one number (`Song::sig`): each of its tracks' clip slot, length and edit stamp (`NowPlaying::stamp`, a hash of the compiled clip), its effects' ids and on/off, and a track's instrument; for several tracks also where each clip lines up and its mute. A change thaws the strip and says so; so does a change during a take, which stops it. A track's own clip launched elsewhere moves the loop (`set_frozen_origin`), and its mute holds the take quiet. `MixHost::open` thaws everything: frozen strips are not saved.
  - *The instrument rests.* `mpfree-track`'s `Outputs::wants` (the mixer's `needs_input`) is false for a frozen track, so `TrackHost::render` skips that slot's instrument: its events are dropped and nothing counts as held, an old instrument ringing out is retired at once, and an AllOff goes first when it plays again (`SlotStats::rested` counts the blocks).
  - *A print* goes to the screen as `ToUi::Printed`, kept by the loop until the screen has room; the screen writes it on a thread as a 32-bit float WAV in Samples › Prints (`takes::save_print`, `Library::write_new`), named after the strip and the time, and says where it went.

## Bouncing (the export's mixdown and stems)

`bounce` ([`bounce.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/bounce.rs)) streams capture points to WAV files while the song plays, for the export's mixdown (the master after Master safety) and stems (each strip into the master, after its fader). The export itself (the pass, Fast or Listen, the files and the MIDI file) is [export.md](export.md).

- **Two halves.** `bounce(streams, ring_frames, rate)` makes them off the sound thread, before the capture points are set. `BounceIn` is the sound thread's: once the capture points are set, `begin(latencies)` takes their `capture_latency` (pushes before it are dropped); after the mixer, `push(stream, l, r)` copies each capture point's block into a ring of atomics made beforehand (no lock, no allocation, no unsafe code). `BounceOut` is a writer thread's: `pump(files)` writes what the rings hold as stereo WAV in the bounce's format (below) and, once `BounceIn::finish` is called, writes each file's lengths into its header (`WavFormat::header`).
- **Lined up.** Each stream drops its first `latency` frames, so every file starts on the song's first frame, and the writer holds back each stream's last frames (the longest latency less its own), so every file ends on the same frame: a pass of P frames gives files of P less the longest latency. An edit that changes a latency during the pass would move a stem, so the export's pass checks the latencies each period and fails the pass if one changes ([export.md](export.md#the-engine)).
- **When the files can't keep up.** A ring with no room drops the block and counts it (`overruns`); `pump` then answers `Overrun` and the host stops and removes the files rather than hand out audio with a gap. Two seconds of ring a stream covers a stick that stalls now and then. A pass that isn't held to real time (the export's fast mode) asks `room()` before each block and waits for the writer instead.
- **The tail.** `quiet(stream)` counts the frames since a stream was last above about -90 dBFS, so the host can run the pass on until the reverb has died away, and `BounceOut::loud()` says where the last sound in any file is, so the silence after it can be cut off. `BounceOut::heard()` says, for each stream, whether it ever went above that level, so the export can leave out a stem whose track never sounded.
- **Formats** (`BounceOut::with_format(WavFormat { bits, rate })`, set before the first pump; 24-bit at the mixer's rate when not set). Everything here happens on the writer thread, so the sound thread pushes the same float blocks whatever the files are:
  - `Bits::I24`: rounded, clipped at full scale, no dither (the 24-bit step is far below the mixer's own noise).
  - `Bits::I16`: triangular (TPDF) dither of up to one 16-bit step each way, two xorshift numbers added, then rounded and clipped. Each stream has its own seed, so the stems' dither isn't the same noise in every file.
  - `Bits::F32`: the mixer's floats as they are, over full scale included (IEEE float WAV: format tag 3, an 18-byte fmt chunk and a `fact` chunk, so a 58-byte header; `WavFormat::header_len`).
  - **Another rate** ([`resample.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx/src/resample.rs), `Resampler`): a polyphase windowed-sinc converter for the ratio in lowest terms (160/147 from 44.1 to 48 kHz), 80 taps a phase, Kaiser window (β 10.06), each phase scaled to add up to 1 so a steady level stays exact. Flat to 20 kHz within about 0.0001 dB and about 100 dB down where anything would fold back. Output frame n sits at input time `n * down / up` exactly, so every file still starts on the song's first frame, lined up with the other files and the MIDI file; at the end the converter is flushed, and `WavFormat::frames_for` gives the file frames for a length at the mixer's rate (the export's tail cut uses it). It allocates, so it runs only on the writer.
- **Cost** on the build host (Xeon, 2.1 GHz): pushing 17 stereo streams takes 14 µs a 256-frame block. The writer turns 20 s of 17 streams into files at 82 times real time as 24-bit, 64 times as 16-bit with dither, and 11 to 12 times at 48 kHz (the converter's 80 taps are most of it). Not yet counted on the MPC, where a 48 kHz export may make Fast, quiet slower than the mixer alone would.

## Per-step FX locks

The time engine's parameter locks (PR #122, the Elektron method) reach every effect on a track by its parameter address (`mpfree_param::RelAddr` for an insert on the clip's own track). For effects that is all a lock needs, plus two things here:

- **A Hold per Perform pad.** Perform FX has 16 switch parameters, Hold 1 to 16 (ids 69 to 84, `perform::ids::hold(k)`): Hold on plays pad k as a finger would, Hold off lets it go (a finger still on the pad keeps it). So a pad's effect can be locked to a step, or recorded as a move. A lock ends at the track's next note without one (the p-lock rule), so on a sparse track lock Hold off on the next step for a one-step effect.
- **Lock mode on the effect pages** ([`lock.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-fx-ui/src/lock.rs)). While a step is held the app passes its locks with `set_locks`; a turn (a tile, a Q-Link, the wheel) then gives `Lock { id, value }` for the step instead of setting the effect, starting from the step's lock if it has one. The page shows what the step plays: its locks over the effect's values, locked tiles in amber and the drawings from the locked values. `unlock` takes the selected tile's lock off and `unlock_all` all of them (proposed: ERASE while a step is held). On the Perform page a pad tap locks that pad's Hold on for the step, or takes it off, and the XY pad locks the DJ filter and the echo where the finger is.
- The master's Perform FX is not on a track, so a clip cannot lock it; it can move it (below).

## FX moves

[`rust/mpfree-moves`](../../../rust/mpfree-moves) records the moves made on effects (knob turns, the XY pad, the DJ knob, Perform pads) into clips, and plays them back. The time engine's thread wires it into clips, recording and undo; this crate is the engine.

- **Lanes.** A clip keeps one lane per parameter (`Moves`, `Lane`): points on the clip's ticks, each a knob position (0 to 1 through the parameter's `Map`, so a cutoff moves evenly in pitch) and how it leads on: a **ramp** glides to the next point, a **step** holds, an **end** lets the parameter go back to its own value. A lane names its parameter as `Target::Track(RelAddr)` on the clip's own track (it follows the clip to another track) or `Target::Fixed(ParamAddr)` anywhere else, such as the master's Perform FX.
- **Playing.** `play(from, to, emit)` gives every value due in a stretch of clip ticks, in tick order across lanes: each point, and inside a ramp a value every 8 ticks (`GRID`; 120 a beat, about every 4 ms at 120 BPM, which the effects smooth). The ticks never depend on the period, so the output is the same at any period size. `chase(at, emit)` gives every lane's state for a clip launched or located into its middle. Neither allocates.
- **Writing a gesture.** `put` writes one gesture over what the lane had on its ticks: a gesture crossing the loop's end is split there, one longer than the clip keeps its last pass, an end where an older gesture was still moving hands back to it, a gesture punched into the middle of an older ramp keeps that ramp's line up to it, and the result is thinned (points within 0.2 % of the line go). With room reserved it does not allocate, so a recorder can write on the audio thread; `fits` says whether a write would. `put_saving` hands over what a write replaces, and `restore` puts it back exactly, for undo.
- **The move log** (`MoveLog`) keeps every move made while the transport rolls, recording or not, in a fixed ring of 65 536 moves (minutes of busy knob work), on the song's ticks and on a clock that carries on over loops and locates. A recorder follows it with `since`; `phrase` gives the moves since the last bar with no gesture under way, one `Gesture` per parameter, and `Gesture::in_clip` puts them on a clip's ticks for `put`: that is how CAPTURE keeps FX moves, as it keeps notes.
- **Step edits** move ticks around: `copy_range`, `cut` (a stretch out and the gap closed), `insert` (a gap opened, holding what came before) and `truncate`. Each keeps what every lane does on the ticks it leaves alone; `check` says whether a clip's moves are sound.
- **Who wins:** a step's p-lock beats a lane on the same parameter for that step, and a hand on a control beats a lane until it lets go (the mod engine's Touch layer); a hand beats a step's p-lock only while recording (Jay, 2026-11-11 20:42Z: when not recording a locked step keeps its lock, as on an Elektron box). Two clips moving the same fixed parameter: the last value wins, and an end only lets go once no clip holds it. The mod engine (PR #129) tells a lane from a step's lock: the player sends a lane's values apart from its p-locks so the host can hand them on as `ModEvent::Lane`, and `ModEvent::hand` turns a hand on a control into `Touch` (over a lane), `Touch` over locks (while recording) or a plain `Set`.
- **Cost:** 16 lanes of a busy 4-bar clip with the log taking three controls, 1 µs a block on the build host (`mpfree-bench --filter moves`).

- `cargo run --release -p mpfree-fx --example fx_bench` times each effect on 128-frame blocks. On the build host (an x86 desktop core) Drive costs 0.7 % of real time in Eco and 0.9 % in HQ, the Delay 0.4 %, the Compressor 0.2 %, the EQ 0.5 % with every band and both 24 dB cuts working (once its settings stop gliding it skips the per-sample upkeep, and an EQ that does nothing, cuts off, every band flat and output at 0 dB, leaves the block untouched), the Chorus 0.55 % (two voices) to 1.0 % (four), the Flanger 0.35 %, the Phaser 0.45 % (it works out its coefficients every 16 frames and glides between them, instead of an exp2 and a tan every frame), Lo-fi 0.5 % (it, the Delay and Crush work out their saturation gain or bit levels only while those glide, not with a `powf` or `exp2` every frame), Master safety 0.1 % (its window average multiplies by a reciprocal instead of a double divide every frame), the filters 0.05 to 0.15 %, and the Cloud reverb 3 to 5 % at Full (Echoes 1.1 %), 1.4 to 1.7 % at Light and 0.9 to 1.8 % at Eco. This VM's clock is noisy at that size, so the reverb's qualities are compared by instruction counts under cachegrind (with an A17-sized cache model), per 128-frame block, Full / Light / Eco: Plate 786k / 365k / 240k, Room 576k / 301k / 224k, Hall 750k / 356k / 264k, Cloud 939k / 406k / 270k, Echoes 295k / 261k / 184k (Eco with the IIR half-bands; the FIRs cost about 9k more a block). Eco also misses the cache about a sixth as often as Full (Plate: 1470 last-level misses a block at Full, 250 at Eco). Turning NEON on for the device build does not vectorise float code by itself (see [profiling.md](profiling.md)), so the device run is what counts.
- **Instruction counts on the MPC's ARM (2026-11-13).** The VM's clock is noisy and x86 counts miss what is slow on ARMv7 (no hardware divide in the target, `floor` and 64-bit conversions as library calls), so each effect is also counted as ARMv7 code. Build `fx_bench` for `armv7-unknown-linux-gnueabihf` (the cross linker `arm-linux-gnueabihf-gcc`), run it under `qemu-arm -L /usr/arm-linux-gnueabihf -one-insn-per-tb -d exec,nochain`, count the `Trace` lines with `FX_BENCH_ONLY=<effect> FX_BENCH_BLOCKS=10` and with `FX_BENCH_BLOCKS=0`, and divide the difference by 10. That gives instructions per 128-frame stereo block, not cycles: cache misses and VFP stalls come on top, so the device round still decides. A 128-frame period at 44.1 kHz is about 4.7 million cycles of one A17 core at 1.6 GHz. Per block: Multi filter 13k (LP 12) and 44k (LP 24 with drive), DJ filter 17k, Crush 11k, Character 21k to 32k, Compressor 23k (32k with RMS and the SC filter on), Ducker 20k, Transient shaper 29k, Master safety 34k, Pitch shifter 49k, Flanger 51k, Tuner 45k, Pitch correct 74k (77k on Low), Harmonizer 97k, Delay 58k, Phaser 76k (4 stages) and 120k (12), EQ 87k (four bands and both 24 dB cuts working), Chorus 91k (2 voices) and 144k (4), Lo-fi 105k, Drive 136k (Eco; 196k with the FIR half-bands) and 350k (HQ), and the Cloud reverb (Plate, Hall, Cloud) 1.26M, 1.28M and 1.84M at Full, 472k and 517k at Light, and 269k, 308k and 337k at Eco (Echoes 185k). The FIR half-bands were about two thirds of the Drive's cost, which is why Eco moved to IIR ones. Two bit-exact fixes came from these counts: the Delay's reads wrapped their index with `rem_euclid`, four integer divisions a read (a library call on ARMv7), and now wrap with one compare (88k to 58k, the same output byte for byte, checked with `fx_render`); the Ducker's tempo cell used `floor` and a 64-bit conversion on every frame (28k to 20k).
- **The reverb on the MPC (an estimate until the next device round):** round 7 measured Full at 40, 27, 38, 56 and 12 % of a period on average (Plate, Room, Hall, Cloud, Echoes), with code from before PR #94. Fitting those five numbers to that build's instruction counts and cache misses (the fit matches each within 1.5 points) puts Full now at about 36, 25, 33, 51 and 11 %, Light at 14, 11, 14, 17 and 10 %, and Eco at 9.2, 8.5, 9.8, 11.4 and 7.4 % (the IIR half-bands took about 0.3 points off each). So one Eco reverb takes about the whole 10 % effects budget by itself and leaves little for the other effects. The lever left is fewer diffuser stages in Eco (a change of sound, so Jay's call, and only if the device round says Eco is over budget).
- **The mixer's cost**, from `mpfree-bench --filter Mixer` (128 frames at 44.1 kHz, the mixer alone, on the build host; the VM is noisy, so means): idle with 63 strips 4 µs a block (0.15 % of a period), 12 tracks and 8 pad channels with sends and submixes 23 µs (0.8 %), 16 tracks and 32 pad channels 55 µs (1.9 %), and the 12 and 8 with a song's inserts (EQ and compressor on 6 tracks, drive and glue on submixes, a Cloud reverb, a delay, a master EQ) 230 to 280 µs, almost all of it the inserts (the Hall reverb alone is about half). The mix budget is 1 % of a period on the MPC (29 µs), set before the mixer had sends, submixes and compensation; without NEON the A17 runs this kind of summing many times slower than a desktop core, so the 12 and 8 case may well cost 3 to 6 % on the device. The device run decides; if it is over, the summing loops (a multiply, a multiply-add, a max and a copy) are the place for hand-written NEON.
