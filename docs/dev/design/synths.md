# Synths: the node interface, Poly, FM, Drums, Pluck, Wavetable, Keys, Drawbar, Squelch, Analog and pad pressure

**Status:** 2026-11-12, at the synth thread's handoff (mpfree main `0ada61a`). The code is [`rust/mpfree-node`](../../../rust/mpfree-node) (the interface every instrument and effect uses), [`rust/mpfree-synth`](../../../rust/mpfree-synth) (native synths: Poly, FM, Drums, Pluck, Wavetable, Keys, Drawbar, Squelch and Analog), [`rust/mpfree-tuning`](../../../rust/mpfree-tuning) and [`rust/mpfree-synth-ui`](../../../rust/mpfree-synth-ui) (the Synth tab). All five engines play on the pads since round 8, with scales, pad layouts and tunings (see "In the pad player"), and the Synth tab's options are saved in the project (13 Nov). Round 7's bench timed the engines on the MPC (1 to 11 % of a period at 8 voices); the same-output cost passes since (PR #118, #137, #197) have not been timed on the device yet. The full design, the roadmap and the mockups are in the design doc "MPFree synths and plugins: design" and in mpfree-claude (`synths/mockups/`, `skill-tree/synth-skill-tree.html`, and the handoff `handoffs/2026-11-12-synth-thread.md`).

## The node interface (`mpfree-node`)

One trait, `Node`, for anything that runs on the audio thread on a stereo bus: a synth, the sampler (later), a hosted plugin (later), an effect (the FX work builds `mpfree-fx` on it).

- `process(ctx, events, left, right)` once per block. `events` are `Timed { offset, ev }`, sorted, with offsets inside the block: `NoteOn`, `NoteOff`, `Pressure` (poly aftertouch), `Bend`, `AllOff`, `Param { id, value }` (automation, parameter locks, macro moves) and `Expression { note, kind, value }` (one note's own slide, tone or release velocity; see [Tunings and per-note expression](#tunings-and-per-note-expression)).
- `set_tuning(&Tuning)` hands an instrument its tuning between blocks. The node copies it (plain data, no allocation), so it may run on the audio thread. The default does nothing, so effects and plugins ignore it.
- `Ctx` carries the rate, the absolute frame of the block's first frame, the tempo, the transport position in beats, whether it plays, and an optional sidechain.
- `params()` describes every parameter with `ParamInfo`: id, name, group, range, default, unit, curve (linear, log, stepped, named choices, toggle) and a read-only flag for meters. A GUI page, Q-Link mapping, automation and p-locks can be built for any node from this list alone. `check_params` checks a list.
- `prepare(rate, max_block)` runs on the control thread and may allocate. `reset()` silences the node and keeps its parameters. `tail_frames()` and `latency()` tell the host how long the node rings and how much delay it adds. `rest_frames()` (default `u32::MAX`, never) is how long its input must be silent before it is as good as reset, so an effect chain may reset it and stop calling it until sound comes again (sleep on silence, [fx.md](fx.md)); a node that makes sound from silence or keeps time across it keeps the default. `can_lead`, `lead`, `lead_in` and `lead_out` (default: never) let a host feed an effect late and still hear it on time, as the mixer does with a reverb return a block late (see [fx.md](fx.md)).

Rules every node keeps:

- Stereo `f32`, in place. Instruments **add** into the buffers; effects transform them.
- 1.0 is 0 dBFS. No node clamps or saturates unless that is its job, so deliberate distortion stays possible. Instruments aim for about -12 dBFS peak for one full-velocity note at their defaults and fade every voice's end. The master node catches accidents with a limiter that counts overs and never errors or mutes.
- No allocation, free, lock, block or panic in `process`.
- The same output, sample for sample, for any block size from 1 to `MAX_BLOCK` (8192) frames. Control-rate work is aligned to the absolute frame, never to the block's start.
- Parameter values are plain units, clamped, never refused; each node smooths its own continuous parameters (`Smooth`).
- A node must not output non-finite samples. If one does, the host resets it and counts it.
- `mpfree_node::pressure` is the shared pad-pressure shaper every engine runs pressure through (see [Pad pressure](#pad-pressure-poly-aftertouch)).

## Poly (`mpfree-synth`)

A subtractive synth:

- **Oscillators:** two per voice (saw and pulse band-limited with PolyBLEP, triangle, sine), octave, semitone and fine tune, pulse width; a square sub one octave down; white noise.
- **Unison:** 1 to 4 stacks per voice, detuned symmetrically and spread across the stereo field. Each voice has a left and a right filter, so the spread stays stereo.
- **Drive, filter and distortion:** a `tanh` **Drive** into a zero-delay-feedback ladder (LP 24) or a trapezoidal state-variable filter (LP 12, BP 12, HP 12), then a **Distortion** after it, the same `tanh` stage with its own amount (0 dB of either is fully clean). Both are there since live test 3 ("Drive has almost no effect"; Jay, 13 Nov 17:58Z: "add both types of drive and distortion"): the drive before the filter is the classic analog filter overdrive, warmth the filter then shapes, but a low cutoff takes out what it adds, so on Grit Bass (260 Hz) the harmonics from the 5th up go only from about 2 % to 5 % of the power at 18 dB; the distortion after the filter keeps its grit whatever the cutoff (21 %; `grit_bass_distortion_is_heard_through_its_low_cutoff`). Grit Bass's grit is its distortion (18 dB, pressure +6 dB); the other presets keep the drive they were made with. Pressure can push either (Poly's targets Drive and Distortion). Each stage keeps the level the same way, with its own `DriveLevel`, and the two corrections multiply into the voice's gain. Each keeps the voice's level (live test 2: "Drive seems to make synths quieter"; the old stage divided by the gain's square root and took up to 14 dB off a full voice at 24 dB). It drives the mix as if one full oscillator fed it (`dsp::mix_level` from the unison stacks, oscillator, sub and noise levels; `dsp::drive_scales`), clipping at that oscillator's peak (`DRIVE_REF`), and `dsp::DriveLevel` sums the power into and out of the drive and, once a control step, folds `sqrt(in / out)` (smoothed over about 40 ms, at most 18 dB either way) into the voice's ramped gain, so the correction costs two multiply-adds a frame and never clicks. Every Poly and Wavetable preset stays within 2 dB of its undriven level from 3 to 24 dB of drive, and of distortion (`turning_the_drive_up_keeps_every_preset_about_as_loud`); a drive or distortion of 0 is bit-identical to none. The drum voices keep the old stage (`drive_sq`): a hit reaches it well under full level, where it lifts the hit. Cutoff follows the filter envelope, key tracking, velocity, the LFO and pad pressure (see [Pad pressure](#pad-pressure-poly-aftertouch)).
- **Envelopes:** filter and amp ADSR with exponential segments; a retrigger starts from the current level, so it never clicks.
- **LFO:** sine, triangle, saw, square or sample-and-hold, free (0.05 to 30 Hz) or synced to the transport (4 bars to 1/16 triplets, phase-locked to `Ctx::beats` while playing), to pitch, cutoff, pulse width and level.
- **Voices:** poly (1 to 16), mono or legato, glide, bend range. A stolen voice fades out over 3 ms in one of 4 spare slots. Stealing takes the quietest releasing voice, then the oldest held one. Mono and legato keep a note stack.
- **Presets:** Init, Grit Bass, Acid, Supersaw, Dust Pad, Pluck, Brass, Sub Bass and Lead.

Timing: oscillators, drive, filters, distortion, the amp envelope and the steal fade run every frame. Smoothing, the LFO, the filter envelope, pressure, glide, pitch and the filter coefficients run once every 16 frames, on frames divisible by 16. Each voice's gain is ramped across a step, so a pressure swell has no zipper.

Cost (after round 7's device bench): each voice renders a control step in passes (gains, each unison stack, sub and noise, drive, filter, distortion, out), so the choice of wave, filter type and drive is made once per step, not once a frame. A centred voice (one stack, or no spread) works out one side and uses it for both, since its left and right are the same to the bit. Pitch, pan and filter coefficients are only worked out again when their inputs move. The output is the same to the bit as before; `cargo run --release -p mpfree-synth --example poly_cost` prints a hash per preset to check that after any change. Under qemu, the ARMv7 build runs 12 to 37 % fewer instructions per preset (Init 30 %, Supersaw 20 %, Dust Pad 12 %).

## FM (`mpfree-synth`, `Fm`)

A six-operator FM synth in the DX7's mould. It is DX7-style, not a bit-exact clone: the numbers come from published descriptions of the DX7 (the operator level table, the envelope rate formula, the keyboard scaling curves), and where the original is not public (the LFO speed curve, the pitch envelope's tables, detune) the values are close approximations.

- **Operators:** six sines, each with a ratio (coarse 0 to 31, fine) or a fixed frequency, detune, an output level (0 to 99), a four-rate, four-level envelope, velocity sensitivity, keyboard level scaling (break point, left and right depth and curve), rate scaling and amplitude modulation from the LFO. Each operator can be switched off.
- **Algorithms:** the DX7's 32 ways of wiring them, with feedback 0 to 7 on one operator or, for algorithms 4 and 6, around a loop of two or three. Feedback averages the last two samples, as the DX7 does, so it stays stable at 7.
- **Envelopes:** in decibels, falling at a constant rate, with the attack curving over as it nears its level. Rates and levels from 0 to 99 mean what they mean on a DX7.
- **Global:** a pitch envelope, an LFO (triangle, saw down, saw up, square, sine, sample-and-hold; speed, delay, pitch and amplitude depth, key sync), transpose, oscillator key sync, poly (1 to 16) or mono with a note stack, bend range, pad pressure (see below) and an output level.
- **Level:** one carrier at full level is about -12 dBFS. A voice with more carriers is scaled by 1/sqrt(carriers), so changing the algorithm doesn't jump the volume. The output level glides while notes sound and jumps straight to a new value when nothing sounds, so loading a preset never starts with a swell.
- **Voices:** a stolen voice fades out over 3 ms in one of 4 spare slots, like Poly.
- **Presets:** Init (one sine), Glass Keys, Bell Tower, Rubber Bass, Drawbars, Brass Section, Log Drum, Silk Pad and Steel Pluck. Each plays a chord between -11 and -6 dBFS peak.
- **DX7 banks** (`dx7`): reads a player's own .syx files, a 32-voice bank (4104 bytes) or a single voice (163 bytes), checks the checksum and maps every byte onto FM parameters; it can also write a bank back. Names are 10 characters, as on a DX7, and the bank has no output level, so imported voices play at 0 dB. Only voice data is read; nothing is sent to any device. MPFree ships no Yamaha voices.

Timing: operators run every frame. Envelopes, pitch and the LFO run once every 16 frames, on frames divisible by 16, with operator gains ramped across each step.

A knob (`set_param`) reworks only what it feeds. An operator or global knob reads the voice settings again; a pressure shaper setting (mode, threshold, curve and so on) works out the 128-step pressure curve; a pressure slot's target or depth works out just the slots. Before 12 November every knob also worked out the curve, which costs more than everything else together: on a desktop a knob turn took about 1.55 µs, now 0.15 µs (0.07 µs for a slot), against about 9 µs to render 32 frames of 8 voices. That matters when a Q-Link, a macro or an LFO in the mod matrix moves an FM parameter every few frames.

**On the Synth tab** FM has its own pages: Algo (algorithm, feedback, transpose, level, voices), Mod (LFO and pitch envelope), Pressure (see below), Op 1 to Op 6 (each operator's frequency, level, envelope and velocity; the Q-Links turn output, coarse, fine and velocity) and Kbd 1-2, 3-4 and 5-6 (keyboard scaling, two operators a page). Every FM parameter is on exactly one page.

Every FM page has a visual strip, drawn from the values:

- **The algorithm** as six boxes: carriers (green) along the bottom over the output line, each modulator (blue) a row above what it modulates, joined by down-across-down lines like the DX7's panel, and the feedback loop on the right when feedback is above 0. A switched-off or silent operator is faded. The layout is worked out from the algorithm's wiring, so all 32 draw the same way (`fm_visual::algorithm_layout`).
- **Algo page:** the algorithm and the six output levels as bars, carriers green.
- **Op pages:** the algorithm with that operator ringed, and its envelope as the engine runs it (L4 up through L1, L2 and L3, held, then the release), with its role in the caption ("modulates 1 · output 58").
- **Kbd pages:** the algorithm with the two operators ringed in their curves' colours, and their keyboard level scaling across A-1 to C7 in dB (the card's scale is the smallest of ±12, 24, 48 or 96 dB that holds both).
- **Mod page:** the pitch envelope (on a ±2, 12, 24 or 48 semitone scale, so a small scoop still shows) and the LFO's wave after its delay.

A tap on a box opens that operator's page; a tap elsewhere on the algorithm card opens Algo. `cargo run --locked -p mpfree-synth-ui --bin mpfree-synth-preview` draws the samples `synth-fm-algo`, `synth-fm-mod`, `synth-fm-op`, `synth-fm-kbd` and `synth-fm-pressure`. FM isn't in the pad player yet; Poly is the pads' synth for round 6.

## Drums (`mpfree-synth`, `Drums`)

A drum synth with a model per pad. Sixteen pads, each one of eleven models: **Kick, Snare, Clap, Closed hat, Open hat, Tom, Rim, Cowbell, Cymbal, Zap and FM perc**. Every pad has the same fourteen knobs, ids `pad * 16 + field` (`drum_params::pad_id`), grouped "Pad 1" to "Pad 16":

- **Model**, **Tune** (±24 semitones from the model's own pitch: kick 50 Hz, tom 120 Hz, snare 185 Hz ...), **Decay** (10 ms to 5 s, the time to fall 60 dB).
- **Tone** (the kick's shape, the snare's and hats' filter, the FM perc's index), **Snap** (a short noise click; for the clap, how sharp its bursts are), **Noise** (the noise layer: the snare's wires, the hats' noise against their six square oscillators).
- **Sweep** and **Sweep time** (a pitch drop from up to 48 semitones above), **Drive** (soft saturation), **Level**, **Pan**.
- **Vel > tone**, **Vel > level** and **Choke** (Off or group 1 to 4).

How it plays:

- **Notes:** pad 1 is the kit's *Pad 1 note* (36 by default, the MPC's usual kick note) and each pad after it a semitone higher; other notes are ignored. Note-offs are ignored too: every hit is a one-shot that rings for its decay.
- **Hits:** a new hit on a pad fades its last hit out over 3 ms. A hit in a choke group fades out the group's other pads, so the closed hat (pad 4) closes the open hat (pad 5) in the Init kit. AllOff fades every hit over 30 ms.
- **Knobs** apply to the next hit, as on drum machines: a hit takes its pad's settings when it starts. The kit Level moves at once, smoothed.
- **Level:** at full velocity the Init kit peaks from about -15 dBFS (hats) to -9 dBFS (the kick). Pressure and bend are ignored.
- **Kits:** Init Kit (kick, snare, clap, closed and open hat, three toms, rim, cowbell, cymbal, zap, FM perc, a long kick, a tight snare, a shaker), Boom Kit (long 808-style booms), Tight Kit (short and bright), Grit Kit (everything driven) and Electro Kit (zaps and FM blips for the toms).

Every part of a voice runs every frame (no control rate), so the output is the same for any block size. 32 hit slots; nothing allocates after `prepare`.

**On the Synth tab** Drums has a page per pad (the tab strip shows 1 to 16) and a Kit page. The Q-Links turn the pad's Tune, Decay, Tone and Snap. `pages::drum_page(pad)` gives the page of a pad, so the app can follow the last pad hit. The preview draws `synth-drums-kick`, `synth-drums-boom`, `synth-drums-hat` and `synth-drums-kit`. `Drums::pad_name(pad)` gives the model name for pad labels.

Its visual strip has four cards. **Kit**: a box per pad, laid out as the pads are (pad 1 bottom left), tagged with its sound (BD, SD, CP, CH, OH, TM, RS, CB, CY, ZP, FM) and coloured by what it is made of (green a tuned body, amber noise over a body, blue metal); the pad shown is ringed, a silent pad faded, and a tap on a box opens that pad's page. **Hit**: the pad's body, noise (a clap's four bursts over its tail) and click over time, each at its level in the mix, until the longest is 60 dB down. **Sweep**: the body's pitch sweep over 250 ms, against the knob's 48 semitones ("no body to sweep" for the hats, clap, cowbell and cymbal). **Levels**: every pad's level, the pad shown in white. On the Kit page Hit and Sweep show pad 1. `drum::hit_shape` works the shapes out with the same code that builds the hits the engine plays (`build`), and a test renders a kick and finds the body's fall and the sweep's pitch as drawn.

## Pluck (`mpfree-synth`, `Pluck`)

Plucked strings and struck bars: two physical models behind one set of pages, picked with **Model**.

- **String** is a Karplus-Strong string (the Jaffe-Smith extension): a delay line one period long with a one-pole damping filter and a first-order allpass in the loop. The tuning takes the filter's phase delay into account, so notes from C1 to C7 play within 2 cents (within 6 at C1 for the darkest presets). A pluck fills the line with a burst of noise shaped by **Pick** (brightness of the burst) and **Position** (a comb: plucking near the bridge is thin, near the middle hollow). **Brightness** sets the loop filter between 12 and 112 harmonics of the note.
- **Mallet** is a modal bank: up to eight two-pole resonators per voice at a **Material**'s mode ratios (Marimba, Vibes, Bell, Glass, Kalimba, Steel drum), struck by a half-sine mallet pulse. **Hardness** is the pulse length (a harder mallet reaches higher modes); **Strike** is where the bar is hit, **Stretch** spreads the ratios, **High damp** shortens the upper modes.
- **Decay** is the ring at middle C (higher notes ring shorter, lower ones longer); **Release** is how fast a note damps once its key is let go (1.5 s by default, so pad taps ring). **Vel > tone** makes soft notes darker. Transpose, Fine, Voices (1 to 16), Bend range, Level, Pan and Spread (alternate voices left and right).
- **Notes:** playing a note that is still ringing re-plucks it in its own voice, adding to what sounds, as a real string does. Past the voice limit the oldest note (released ones first) fades out over 3 ms. Pressure is ignored.
- **Level:** every preset peaks between -16 and -9 dBFS at full velocity across C2 to C6.
- **Presets:** Init, Nylon, Steel String, Harp, Koto, Muted Bass (strings), and Marimba, Vibes, Bell, Glass, Kalimba and Steel Drum (mallets).

Timing: the strings and resonators run every frame; pitch (transpose, fine, bend) runs at a control step of 16 frames aligned to the absolute frame. The delay lines (one per voice, 4096 frames at 48 kHz) are allocated in `prepare`.

**On the Synth tab** Pluck has three pages: String (with the model, decay and release; Q-Links Decay, Brightness, Pick, Position), Mallet (Q-Links Hardness, Strike, Stretch, High damp) and Voice. The preview draws `synth-pluck-string` and `synth-pluck-mallet`.

Its visual strip has three cards for the model it plays, all for middle C. A string: **Pick** (the string pulled up where it is picked), **Harmonics** (the first 16, in dB, at their average level over many plucks, since each pluck's noise burst gives them random levels around it) and **Decay** (harmonics 1, 2, 4 and 8 falling in dB, higher ones sooner, more so when darker). A mallet: **Mallet** (the pulse, as long as the mallet stays on the bar), **Modes** (the material's modes on a log scale of the note, as struck: placed by Strike, weighed by the pulse's spectrum) and **Decay** (the first four modes). `pluck::partials` works them out with the engine's own formulas (shared helpers, the output unchanged to the bit), and tests render notes and find each harmonic's and mode's decay, and the harmonics' average levels, as drawn. One finding: Position's comb works within the burst's one period (the first frames are not subtracted), so its notches are partial; plucked at the middle, the even harmonics come out about 7 dB under the odd ones on average rather than gone.

## Wavetable (`mpfree-synth`, `Wavetable`)

Two table oscillators that scan through stacks of single-cycle frames, with warps (Bend, Sync, Mirror, FM), unison, a sub and noise, Poly's filter, a mod envelope and LFO that move the positions, and pad pressure. Ten factory tables are built from formulas; a player's WAV tables and a pad's sample can become user tables. Tables are band-limited at 17 half-octave levels, so high notes don't alias. The ten factory tables are built once, by the first `Wavetable::new` (the pad player makes it before the sound starts): about 0.9 billion ARMv7 instructions under qemu, most of them FFTs, so it adds to start-up time, never to a block. The full description, the memory and cost numbers and the demo are in [Wavetable synth](wavetable.md).

## Keys (`mpfree-synth`, `Keys`)

An electric piano: a struck steel tine beside a magnetic pickup (**Tine**) or a struck steel reed in an electrostatic pickup (**Reed**), picked with **Model**. The design follows the measurements and models collected in [Electric pianos](../research/electric-piano.md): the vibrating part moves almost as a sine, and the pickup makes the tone.

- **Voice.** A small modal bank of two-pole resonators. A tine has the fundamental (Decay at middle C, falling as f^-0.75, the slope of measured Q factors) and the cantilever's bending modes at 7.11 and 20.25 times it, heard only in the attack (Bell sets their level). A reed has its fundamental in two parts, long and short, so the decay is not a single exponential; its second mode as a pair 0.1 % apart, so it beats; and its third mode. Each strike moves the upper modes by up to 0.3 % and 1.3 dB at random, and each key has a small fixed offset in its voicing.
- **Hammer.** A half-sine contact of unit area (so the fundamental rings at the strike's level whatever the hammer): 0.4 to 4.5 ms at full velocity by **Hardness**, longer for softer notes as v^-0.47 (Hunt and Crossley contact, exponent 2.8), never more than half the note's period. A shorter contact reaches higher modes. **Vel > tone** sets how much velocity shortens it.
- **Pickup.** A Hammerstein model, as measured on magnetic pickups: a static curve of the displacement, then its change from frame to frame (Faraday's law; a capacitor's current), divided by the note's angular frequency so every note comes out at the same level. Tine: the flux `1/(1+u²)`; **Voicing** moves the rest point from the field's axis (0: the octave above the note dominates) to the curve's inflection at 1/√3 (100: nearly pure). Reed: the capacitance `1/(1-y)`, the reed held off the plate by a soft limit at 0.9 of the gap; Voicing moves its rest point from 0.4 to 0. **Bark** sets how far a full-velocity hit swings across the curve, and low notes swing wider ((261.6 Hz / f)^0.5, limited to 0.5 to 1.6), so they bark sooner. Putting the sum of the modes through the curve intermodulates them, as the real pickup does.
- **After the voices.** The voices sum to a mono bus, then: key noise (a filtered noise burst on each strike and a softer one on each release, **Key noise**), the pickup network's high-pass (30 Hz tine, 120 Hz reed), a tilt **Tone** around 900 Hz (up to ±9 dB treble against ∓3 dB bass), then for a tine a tanh **Drive** (Poly's shape, scaled so a hard chord on the bus is full scale) and for a reed an asymmetric preamp curve that always colours a little and that Drive pushes. **Tremolo** at **Trem rate**: **Pan** follows a rounded square between the speakers at equal power; **Amp** a sine on the level, which for a reed works before the preamp so the tone moves with it. The reed's output gets 3.5 dB of make-up gain.
- **Notes.** A ringing note struck again uses its own voice and adds to what sounds. Past Voices (1 to 16, 12 by default) the oldest note, released ones first, fades out over 3 ms. Letting go damps the note over **Release**. Pressure is ignored.
- **Level.** A full-velocity note peaks between -20 and -6 dBFS from C2 to C6 on every preset, and a six-note full-velocity chord stays under 0 dBFS.
- **Presets:** Init, Soft Tines, Bright Tines, Barking Tines, Bell Keys, Hollow Tines (voiced near the axis), Pan Tines, Ballad, Dusty Keys (tine), and Reed, Reed Bite, Trem Reed.

Timing: the resonators, pickup and bus run every frame; pitch (transpose, fine, bend), the tremolo and retuning run at a control step of 16 frames aligned to the absolute frame, so any block size gives the same output to the bit. Nothing is allocated, even in `prepare`. **Cost:** 117k instructions per 128-frame block for Init with 8 voices, 170k with 16, 220k for Reed with 16, 189k for Dusty Keys with 16 (callgrind, x86; CI's instruction gate). For comparison Poly Pluck with 8 voices is 209k and Pluck Harp with 16 is 249k. With the MPC calibration file the bench estimates Init at 8 voices near the synth budget's worst block (18.7 % of 20 %), in line with Poly; a device run should set the default voice count.

**On the Synth tab** Keys has four pages: Strike (Model, Hardness, Bell, Decay, Release), Tone (Voicing, Bark, Tone, Vel > tone), Amp (Tremolo, Trem rate, Trem mode, Drive, Key noise) and Voice. Its Q-Link sets are Strike, Tone and Amp. Hardness and Bell are in the Tone group, but the Strike page takes them (`pg_with` in `pages.rs`): a preset file names each parameter by its group, so moving them to another group would drop them from presets already saved.

Its visual strip has four cards, all for middle C, drawn by `inst_visual` with the engine's own formulas. **Hammer**: the force over the contact for a soft hit (velocity 40) and a full one, over 2.5 ms, a little over the longest contact middle C allows (half its period); Hardness and the velocity set the length (`keys::contact_secs`). **Decay**: the fundamental of C2, C4 and C6 falling in dB (the reed's two parts summed), 1.5 times middle C's ring long. **Pickup**: the tine's flux or the reed's capacitance across the range the voice can reach, the stretch a velocity-100 hit swings across drawn bright, and a dot where Voicing and Bark put the rest point (`keys::pickup_shape`). **Tremolo**: two cycles of the left and right gains for Pan, one curve for Amp (`keys::tremolo_gains`, which the engine calls too). The preview draws `synth-keys-strike` and `synth-keys-tone`.

`cargo run --release -p mpfree-synth --example keys_demo [out.wav]` writes `~/mpfree-work/keys-demo.wav` (103 s): every preset in turn, 8.5 s each, playing four chords from velocity 40 to 127 over a bass note, a velocity ladder on C2 and C4, and a melody in the top octaves.

## Drawbar (`mpfree-synth`, `Drawbar`)

A tonewheel organ and the rotating speaker it is usually played through, built from the measurements, papers and service documentation collected in [Tonewheel organs and rotary speakers](../research/tonewheel-organ.md). Velocity, pressure, bend and slides are ignored, as on the organ.

- **Wheels.** 91 wheels turn all the time, each tuned as the organ's gears make it: 20 turns a second × one of twelve gear ratios × its teeth (2 to 128, then 192 for the top seven, which take the gears of the note a fourth up). **Wheels** Gears puts those small offsets from equal temperament (G# 0.71 cents flat, the top octave up to 1.9 cents sharp) on top of the project's tuning; Exact plays the tuning as it is. A key never starts an oscillator: it sets how much of each running wheel is heard, so no two notes start in phase, and a wheel two keys share (one key's 4', the next octave's 8') sums once instead of beating. The lowest octave of wheels is nearly square (1×, 3× and 5×), as cut on the organ.
- **Drawbars.** Nine, at -12, +7, 0, +12, +19, +24, +28, +31 and +36 semitones, each 0 to 8 at 3 dB a step, taking the nearest wheel (so the fifths are 2 cents flat of pure and the 1 3/5' about 14 cents sharp, as on the organ). The 16' folds back up an octave in the lowest manual octave (C2 to B2), and anything past the top wheel folds down an octave.
- **Click.** Each drawbar has its own key contact. Closing, each waits a random moment (up to 3 ms at **Click** 100) and then switches its running wheels in over 0.3 ms (at 100) to 6 ms (at 0), and at higher Click half of them bounce open for a moment. Letting go opens them the same way, half as spread and twice as slow. There is no added noise: the click is the sound of running sines switched abruptly.
- **Leakage.** Each wheel is heard in its compartment partner, 48 wheels (four octaves) away: off at 0, -40 dB at 40 (the default, setBfree's figure), -26 dB at 100.
- **Percussion.** A decaying second (**Perc harm** Second, the 4') or third (Third, the 2 2/3') harmonic, falling 60 dB in 1 s (**Perc decay** Fast) or 4 s (Slow), at a drawbar's full level (**Perc volume** Normal) or 6 dB under (Soft). It is single trigger: it fires only when a key goes down with no other key held, and re-arms when every key is up, but a chord struck together gets it on every note. It takes the 1' drawbar's contact, so the 1' is silent while percussion is on, and Normal lowers the drawbars by 3 dB.
- **Vibrato and chorus.** The scanner: a delay line read at the taps of the organ's 19-section ladder (44.7 µs a section), a scanner at **Scanner** Hz (6.9 by default) sweeping back and forth across the first 9, 13 or 19 taps for V1, V2 or V3 and crossfading between neighbouring taps as its plates do, then a 7 kHz low-pass. C1 to C3 are the same depths mixed with the dry sound (0.6 of each). V1 swings about ±4 to 14 cents and V3 about ±12 to 30 cents, depending on the note.
- **Drive and Tone.** Drive is the smooth curve u/√(1+u²) with a bias, so it adds even harmonics, followed by a 10 Hz DC blocker; it is scaled so a signal at 0.4 comes out at 0.4 whatever the drive, and at 0 it is nearly linear (more than 50 dB below the note). A sharp-cornered curve aliased audibly on full drawbars, so it was replaced. **Tone** is a tilt around 900 Hz, up to ±9 dB treble against ∓3 dB bass.
- **Rotary speaker.** A 4th-order (Linkwitz-Riley) split at 800 Hz. The horn and the drum are each a source turning on a circle (19.2 cm and 22 cm), heard by two microphones (**Spread** sets how far round the cabinet the second stands; 0 is mono) through a delay that follows the source's distance (the Doppler shift; Hermite reads for the horn, linear for the drum). Each rotor's level follows where it faces (35 % quieter facing away for the horn, 30 % for the drum), the horn's treble falls from 9 kHz facing the microphone to 3 kHz facing away, the horn gets one cabinet reflection (1.1 ms, 0.3) and leaks 15 % of its sound unmodulated. Speeds and inertia are setBfree's: horn 40.32 and 423.36 rpm, speeding up with a 0.161 s time constant and slowing with 0.321 s; drum 36 and 357.3 rpm, 4.127 s up and 1.371 s down. **Speed** Stop lets both coast to a standstill wherever they are. **Rotary** Off crossfades to the dry sound over 15 ms, in mono.
- **Notes.** Up to 64 keys down or letting go at once; past that a new key takes the place of the one let go longest. A key struck again while its contacts are still opening closes them from where they are. **Keys held** (read only) shows the keys down. `active_voices` counts the keys still sounding, then one while the speaker and filters ring out, so the host keeps the instrument running through the tail (`tail_frames` is 0.2 s).
- **Level.** One key at the defaults (Init, C4) peaks at -15.6 dBFS; one key on every preset peaks between -19 and -11.7 dBFS, and a six-note chord between -9.7 and -1.8 dBFS.
- **Presets** (drawbar settings as players write them, 16' first): Init (888000000), Jazz Perc (888000000, third percussion, C3), Gospel (888800000, Fast speaker, C3, some drive), Full Organ (888888888), Rock Drive (888600000, Drive 70), Ballad (008800000, V3), Flute (008000000, V1), Church (868868446, no speaker, C1), Reedy (008080800, odd harmonics), Bright Perc (800000888, second percussion), Hollow (808000000, speaker stopped, V2) and Soft Bass (850000000, an octave down, no speaker).

Timing: wheels, the scanner, drive, tone and the speaker run every frame; contacts, percussion, the rotors' speeds and retuning run at a control step of 16 frames aligned to the absolute frame, with each wheel's level moving smoothly across the step, so any block size gives the same output to the bit. Nothing is allocated, even in `prepare`. **Cost:** a sounding wheel is a phasor turned by a fixed rotation each frame (four multiplies), started from the wheel's own phase when it comes in and pulled back to unit length once a step, about 10 instructions a wheel-frame; a silent wheel only counts its phase; a key whose contacts have all closed skips them; with no key and no tail the engine returns after its control step. That makes the cost depend on the wheels heard, not the keys: 134k instructions per 128-frame block for Init with 8 keys, 174k for Full Organ with 8, 167k for Gospel with 16 and 129k for Church with 8, and about 7k when silent (callgrind, x86; CI's instruction gate). The speaker is about a third of it. With the MPC calibration file the bench estimates Init at 8 keys at 9.7 % of the synth budget's 20 % worst block and Full Organ at 14.9 % (Keys Init at 8 voices: 18.1 % in the same run).

**On the Synth tab** Drawbar has four pages: Drawbars (all nine; the Q-Links take the 4', 2 2/3', 2' and 1', since the 16', 5 1/3' and 8' usually stay out), Percussion (Percussion, Perc harm, Perc decay, Perc volume, Vibrato, Scanner), Rotary (Rotary, Speed, Spread with Click, Leakage, Drive, Tone and Wheels; the Q-Links take Drive, Tone, Click and Spread) and Voice. Its Q-Link sets are Low bars, High bars, Tone (Drive, Tone, Click, Leakage) and Play (Scanner, Spread, Fine, Level). Its visual strip has four cards. **Drawbars**: the nine as bars, amber below the note (16', 5 1/3'), white the note and its octaves, grey the other harmonics, as an organ's drawbars are brown, white and black; the caption is the registration. **Wave**: one cycle of the 16', the drawbars' sines at their levels (`drawbar::bar_level`), the 1' left out while percussion is on. **Percussion**: its level against a drawbar at 8 over 4.5 s, so Fast and Slow compare on one scale (`drawbar::perc_level`). **Rotor**: the horn's and the drum's speeds over 10 s after Speed is switched to where it is now, from Slow to Fast or from Fast to Slow or Stop (`drawbar::rotor_ramp`, with the engine's speeds and time constants). ## Squelch (`mpfree-synth`, `Squelch`)

A mono acid bass: one oscillator into a resonant four-pole diode ladder, with a decaying filter envelope, accent and slide, built from the circuit analyses collected in [Acid bass](../research/acid-bass.md). Pressure and per-note expression are ignored.

- **Oscillator.** A sawtooth with a polyBLEP at its reset. **Wave** Square is not a second oscillator but the saw through a limited-gain shaper, as on the original: `tanh` of six times the saw, with its threshold a little off centre, so the reset edge stays sharp, the middle transition is an S-curve, the two halves differ and some even harmonics come through. A change of Wave crossfades over about 10 ms.
- **Filter.** The diode ladder's nodal model: four capacitor voltages, the input pair's current driving the first (its capacitor half the others'), a diode pair between each pair of neighbours and one from the last to a fixed point, every pair's current through `tanh` of the voltage across it. Its linear part is Stinchcombe's transfer function to the third decimal (tested within 0.5 dB). It is solved each sample with zero-delay feedback: each pair's `tanh(d)/d` (a Padé form, `1/|d|` past ±3) is taken from the previous frame, which leaves one linear system, solved by elimination from the top with every division done once a frame. The frequency is prewarped so the ladder's resonance lands on **Cutoff** (30 Hz to 5 kHz; the original tops out near 2.5 kHz).
- **Resonance.** The output fed back to the input, k from 0 to 18.5. The bare ladder oscillates past 17 (tested: it grows at 17.6 and dies at 16, at the cutoff within 6 %); with a note through it the input pair's compression keeps it at the edge, so at 100 the harmonic nearest a mid or high cutoff rings out (more than 25 dB up at 1.2 kHz) rather than a free whistle. The feedback goes through a 120 Hz one-pole high-pass (our reading of the measured loop high-pass), so low cutoffs ring far less (under 8 dB at 80 Hz). Resonance thins the bass, as on the original; half of that loss (√(1+k)) is made up in the amplifier.
- **Envelopes.** The filter's envelope jumps to full at each new note and falls exponentially to a tenth over **Decay** (200 ms to 2 s), or over **Acc decay** (200 ms by default) on accented notes. **Env mod** sweeps up to 4 octaves above the base and lowers the base by a quarter of the sweep, as turning it up does on the original. **Key track** moves the cutoff with the note around C3 (0 by default; the original doesn't track). The volume envelope rises in 3 ms from wherever it is, falls to -60 dB over **Amp decay** (3.5 s by default) while the key is held, and closes in 8 ms when it is let go.
- **Accent.** A note at or above **Accent at** (velocity 100 by default) is accented. Its filter envelope goes into the amplifier through a 1.55 ms lag (up to 1.5 times more level at **Accent** 100), and into the cutoff two ways mixed by Resonance, as the original's dual pot does: directly (a sharp rise and fall, up to 2 octaves) and through a capacitor charged through a diode with a 147 ms time constant (a smooth swell, weighted twice). The capacitor empties with a 200 ms time constant (our reading), so accents close together build on each other, more with more resonance (tested: of four accents 125 ms apart, the fourth's cutoff peaks at least 15 % higher than the first's at Resonance 100, and within 5 % of it at 0).
- **Slide.** A note played while another is held glides there over **Slide** (10 to 500 ms, 60 ms by default; an exponential glide of three time constants, as the original's RC) without starting the envelopes, and keeps the accent of the note it slid from. Letting go of it glides back to the key still held; letting go of the last closes the gate. Up to 16 held keys are remembered.
- **After the filter.** The amplifier, then a 47 Hz one-pole high-pass (the original's weak low end: -5 dB at 32 Hz), then **Drive**: the smooth curve u/√(1+u²) with an off-centre rest point, so it adds even harmonics, scaled so the level stays near the clean one (tested: harmonics 6 to 25 more than 6 dB up, the peak within 6 dB), then a 10 Hz DC blocker. **Level** and **Pan** at the end.
- **Oversampling.** Below 64 kHz the oscillator, filter, amplifier and drive run at twice the rate and come down through a polyphase IIR half-band (six all-pass sections, mpfree-fx's design: flat to 0.4 of the rate, -80 dB from 0.6). Without it, Distorted on C6 had its loudest alias at -24.8 dB, and with it -32.6 dB; Screamer on C7, -32.4 and -43.2 dB.
- **Level.** One unaccented note on C2 peaks between -16.8 and -11.9 dBFS on every preset, an accented one between -15.6 and -6.3 dBFS, and an accented line stays under -1 dBFS.
- **Presets:** Init, Acid Line (the classic squelch: Resonance 85, a fair sweep, short decay), Deep Bass (low and round), Square Acid (the square through a nearly closed, very resonant filter), Distorted (Drive 70), Rubber (long decay and 140 ms slides), Plucky (Amp decay 400 ms), Acid Lead (Key track 70, an octave up, some drive) and Screamer (Resonance 100, Accent 100).

Timing: the oscillator, ladder, amplifier, drive and half-band run every sample; pitch, bend, slide, the envelopes, the accent's capacitor and the cutoff run at a control step of 16 frames aligned to the absolute frame, with the pitch, the ladder's frequency and the level moving linearly across the step, so any block size gives the same output to the bit. Nothing is allocated, even in `prepare`. With no key and no tail it returns after its control step, and once the tails fall under 10⁻⁶ the states are set to exact zero. **Cost:** 59k instructions per 128-frame block for Acid Line playing a sixteenth-note line with accents and slides, 64k for Square Acid and 62k for Distorted (callgrind, x86; CI's instruction gate). That is under half of Drawbar Init with 8 keys (134k) or Keys Init with 8 voices (117k). With the MPC calibration file the bench estimates each at 9.1 % of the synth budget's 20 % worst block (the same in four runs), and about 3 % of a period on average.

**On the Synth tab** Squelch has three pages: Synth (Wave, Cutoff, Resonance, Env mod, Decay, Accent; the Q-Links take Cutoff, Resonance, Env mod and Decay), Play (Slide, Accent at, Acc decay, Amp decay, Key track, Drive; the Q-Links take Slide, Acc decay, Amp decay and Drive) and Voice (with Level and Pan). Its Q-Link sets are Filter (Cutoff, Resonance, Env mod, Decay), Accent (Accent, Acc decay, Slide, Amp decay) and Tone (Drive, Key track, Fine, Level). Its visual strip has three cards. **Wave**: two cycles of the saw, or of the square the engine's shaper makes from it (`squelch::square_of`). **Filter**: the ladder's response (`squelch::response`: Stinchcombe's transfer function with the feedback through the loop's high-pass) where the filter rests between notes, with a dot there, and dashed where a plain note's envelope takes it. The loop's high-pass is why the peak is small at low cutoffs, as on the original. **Sweep**: the cutoff over four sixteenths at 120 BPM, played plain and played accented, in octaves above where it rests (`squelch::cutoff_sweep`, which steps the envelope and the accent's sweep capacitor as the engine does), so the accents' build-up shows. The preview draws `synth-squelch` and `synth-squelch-play`.

`cargo run --release -p mpfree-synth --example squelch_demo [out.wav]` writes `~/mpfree-work/squelch-demo.wav` (39 s): every preset in turn, 4.24 s each, playing two bars of the same sixteen-step line at 125 BPM, with accents (velocity 127) and slides, gates 3.5/6 of a step as on the original's sequencer; in the second bar the cutoff sweeps up two octaves and back. ## Analog (`mpfree-synth`, `Analog`)

A vintage analog poly: a card per voice, each with two oscillators, a resonant filter, two envelopes and an amplifier, notes handed to the cards in turn, with poly-mod and a bucket-brigade chorus. It is built from the datasheets, papers and makers' notes collected in [Vintage analog polysynths](../research/vintage-poly.md). [Poly](#poly-mpfree-synth) already does clean subtractive synthesis; Analog is about what makes the old instruments sound old. Pressure and per-note expression are ignored.

- **Voice cards.** Sixteen cards, each given fixed unit-normal offsets when the engine is made (a seeded generator, clamped to ±2.5, so every instance and every run is the same): both oscillators' tuning, the cutoff, the envelopes' times, the pulse width and the level. **Age** scales them; at 100 their standard deviations are 4 cents, 0.084 octave (about 6 %) of cutoff, 8 % of each time, 2.5 % of pulse width and 0.5 dB (our reading of a maker's "+/- 5-10 cents variation per voice"). Age also scales drift: each oscillator follows its own Ornstein-Uhlenbeck walk, 2 cents rms at Age 100 with a 5 s time constant, stepped each control step (tested: over 5 s a held note wanders between 0.5 and 20 cents, never more than 1.5 cents in 100 ms; at Age 0 under 0.01).
- **Taking turns.** A note goes to the next free card after the one used last, so a repeated chord lands on other cards (tested: four notes in a row take cards 0 to 3). A note still sounding keeps its card; with none free the card let go longest ago is taken, then the oldest held. **Voices** limits the cards in use (8 by default). **Unison** starts every card in use on one note, spread evenly across ±25 cents at **Detune** 100; letting go of the note goes back to a key still held (up to 16 are remembered). **Glide** moves each card from the last note it played (three time constants over the time; tested: a third of the way in, it is 1 − e⁻¹ of the way there), so in Poly chords smear into each other as on the old instruments.
- **Oscillators.** Two per card, each **Saw**, **Pulse**, **Saw+Pulse** (0.6 of each) or **Triangle**, band-limited with polyBLEP steps placed a sample late: each step's correction is split between the sample before it, held back and not yet heard, and the sample after, so a step anywhere in a sample (a wrap, a pulse edge, a sync reset) gets both halves. **Sync** restarts oscillator 1 at oscillator 2's wrap, with the jump's own step. A square **Sub** an octave under oscillator 1 and white **Noise** complete the mixer, whose sum past one full source is brought down by its power (two full oscillators are 3 dB up, not 6). The oscillators run on while a card is silent, so each note starts at another phase.
- **Poly-mod.** Each card's filter envelope (**PMod env**, ±100: 4 octaves of pitch, 45 % of pulse width or 4 octaves of cutoff) and its oscillator 2 at audio rate (**PMod osc 2**: 2 octaves of pitch, 45 % of pulse width or 3 octaves of cutoff) go to oscillator 1's pitch, its pulse width and the cutoff, as the three **PMod >** switches choose, as on the Prophet-5's Poly-Mod (tested: on a triangle, PMod osc 2 at 60 puts more than 30 % of the rms off the note's harmonics, against under 5 % at 0). The global **LFO** (Sine, Triangle, Saw, Square or S&H, 0.05 to 30 Hz) goes to the pitch (**Vibrato**, up to 100 cents), both pulse widths (45 % each way) and the cutoff (2 octaves each way), faded in over **LFO delay** after a note played with no other key held.
- **LP 24.** Four OTA stages, each `dv/dt = ω·tanh(x − v)` (the input pair saturates, unlike the transistor ladder's `tanh(x) − tanh(v)`), the output fed back with k up to 4.1 at **Resonance** 100. It is solved each sample with zero-delay feedback: each stage's `tanh(d)/d` (a Padé form, `1/|d|` past ±3) from the previous frame makes it a one-pole of share `G/(1+G)`, one division a stage, and the feedback is solved from the output written as `A·u + B` (one more). Four equal stages oscillate at k = 4; k 4.1 is past that, so with no input it whistles near the cutoff (tested: from a burst of noise at Resonance 100 and a 1 kHz cutoff, the strongest tone is within 100 cents of 1 kHz and 30 dB over its neighbours; 4.2 pulled it down to 919 Hz). The passband falls to 1/(1+k) as on the original; half of that in dB, √(1+k), is made up in the amplifier (tested: Resonance 100 loses 4 to 10 dB at 80 Hz). The slope is tested between 18 and 32 dB an octave.
- **LP 12, BP 12, HP 12.** A trapezoidal state-variable filter, damping from 2 at Resonance 0 to 0.06 at 100. Its damping grows with the square of the band-pass's level the frame before, so a loud resonance squashes rather than running away (our reading of an OTA filter's limits; with the integrators' inputs through `tanh` instead it went unstable). The band-pass keeps half its resonant peak in dB. Tested against the saw's own 1/n slope: the low-pass's slope between 8 and 16 dB an octave, the high-pass flat above the cutoff and 20 dB down below it, the band-pass 10 dB over both sides.
- **Cutoff.** **Cutoff** (20 Hz to 18 kHz), the filter envelope (**Env amount** ±100: 5 octaves either way, scaled down for soft notes by **Vel > filter**), **Key track** (at 100 an octave per octave around middle C), poly-mod, the LFO and the card's offset add in octaves, clamped to 16 Hz to 0.45 of the rate (at most 20 kHz) and prewarped. **Drive** raises the input up to 3 octaves into the stages and scales the output back by the same.
- **Envelopes.** The CEM3310's curve: the attack charges toward 1.3 times the peak and stops at it (the datasheet's 6.5 V asymptote and 5 V peak), so it is an RC curve cut before it flattens (tested: 0.675 of the peak halfway, within 0.06); decay and release fall exponentially to 1 % over their time, from 1 ms to 20 s. A new note starts the attack from where the envelope is. **Vel > amp** scales the level by velocity (50 by default).
- **Chorus.** A bucket-brigade delay modelled by its clock: a triangle sweeps the clock, so the delay, stages over twice the clock, sweeps as its reciprocal, `mid / (1 + swing·tri)`, between the mode's ends. **Slow** 0.51 Hz and **Fast** 0.83 Hz sweep 1.4 to 5.6 ms; **Vibe** 9.75 Hz sweeps 3.2 to 3.8 ms (the Juno-60's modes as the community measured them, and Juno-XD's depths). Left and right are swept in antiphase from one delay line, with a 9 kHz two-pole low-pass before it and a 7.5 kHz one after each side, a hiss at 0.15 % of the signal's own level (followed over 50 ms) and the wet 1.62 dB under the dry; the whole is trimmed so switching it on is not much louder (tested: within 3 dB of the dry rms on every mode, and the difference of the sides over 5 % of the level). A change of mode moves the rate and depth over 50 ms.
- **After the cards.** A 10 Hz DC blocker, the chorus, then **Level** and **Pan**.
- **Level.** One note at velocity 100 peaks between -17.1 and -10.0 dBFS on every preset (tested: -22 to -6), and an 8-note chord stays under 0 dBFS (worst -0.3 dB, Poly Brass).
- **Presets:** Init (one saw through the four-pole), Poly Brass (two detuned saws through LP 12 with a quick filter swell), Warm Pad (slow saws and the Slow chorus), Sync Lead (oscillator 1 synced and swept by PMod env, Unison on 2 cards, delayed vibrato and glide), Chorus Strings (Saw+Pulse with the LFO on the width, Fast chorus), Square Keys (a pulse and the sub, plucked), Clang Bell (PMod osc 2 with oscillator 2 an octave and a fifth up), Unison Bass (4 cards), Slow Sweep (the LFO on a resonant cutoff), Hollow Pulse (BP 12 and Vibe) and Worn Cards (Age 100).

Timing: the oscillators, the filter and the amplifier run every sample at the host's rate (no oversampling: the oscillators are band-limited and the filter's nonlinearity is mild); pitch, the envelopes, the LFO, drift and the cutoff run at a control step of 16 frames aligned to the absolute frame, with pitch, pulse width, cutoff and level moving linearly across the step, so any block size gives the same output to the bit (tested on four setups). Nothing is allocated after `new`. Once every card has stopped it lets the chorus and DC blocker ring for 20 ms, then clears them and returns at once until the next note (tested: exact silence after the release, chorus on or off). **Cost:** 265k instructions per 128-frame block for Init with 8-voice chords, 321k for Warm Pad (the chorus adds about 55k), 203k for Poly Brass (the two-pole is cheaper than the four-pole's five divisions a sample) and 466k for Worn Cards on 16 voices (callgrind, x86; CI's instruction gate). Poly Init with 8 voices is 192k. On this build host Analog Init's mean time per block was 6 to 17 % over Poly Init's; the bench's MPC estimates were too noisy here to quote (Poly Init itself read from 27 to 30 % of the synth budget between runs), so Analog needs its own run on the device before its voice count is settled.

**On the Synth tab** Analog has five pages: Osc (both oscillators, Sync and the mix; the Q-Links take Osc 1 PW, Osc 2 PW, Osc 2 fine and Osc 2), Filter (the Q-Links take Cutoff, Resonance, Env amount and Drive), Envelopes (F attack, F decay, A attack, A release), Mod (LFO rate, Vibrato, PMod env, PMod osc 2) and Voice (with Output; Age, Glide, Detune, Level). Its Q-Link sets are Filter (Cutoff, Resonance, Env amount, Drive), Env (F attack, F decay, A attack, A release), Mod (LFO rate, Vibrato, PMod env, PMod osc 2) and Voice (Age, Osc 2 fine, Detune, Level). Its visual strip has five cards. **Osc**: two cycles of oscillator 2, and oscillator 1 against it at its interval, restarting with each of oscillator 2's cycles when synced. **Filter**: the response at Cutoff (`analog::filter_gain`: the four-pole's with the feedback at Resonance, or the two-pole's with its damping), with a dot there, and dashed where the filter envelope's peak takes it. **Env**: the amp and filter envelopes with the CEM3310's attack (`analog::attack_shape`), on a square-root time scale as Poly's. **LFO**: two cycles, faded in over its delay. **Voices**: each card's fixed detune for both oscillators at the Age set (`analog::card_cents`, from the same seeded draw the engine makes its offsets with). The preview draws `synth-analog-filter` and `synth-analog-voice`.

`cargo run --release -p mpfree-synth --example analog_demo [out.wav]` writes `~/mpfree-work/analog-demo.wav` (61 s): every preset in turn, 5.4 s each, over Am7, Fmaj7, C and G: held chords for the pads and keys, stabs for Poly Brass, a legato line for Sync Lead, eighths for Unison Bass, an arpeggio for Clang Bell, and one chord four times for Worn Cards, so each lands on other cards. ## Pad pressure (poly aftertouch)

The MPC One's pads send pressure for each pad while it's held. Players who have per-note pressure rarely give it up, and most complaints about it are about feel: it fires by accident, jumps when a note starts or ends, moves in coarse steps, or can't be tuned. So every engine shapes it the same way before it reaches a sound. The research and the reasoning are in the design doc [Poly aftertouch for MPFree synths](https://claude.ai/artifact/7SWqijpsJoBR2LjEHkNU3m).

**The shaper** (`mpfree-node`, `pressure`) runs once per voice every 16 frames. The sampler can use it without depending on the synths.

1. **Hold-off** (0 to 100 ms, 30 by default): for this long after a note-on the value holds still, so the strike itself doesn't modulate. A fresh voice holds at 0 and then glides in; a voice retriggered while it sounds keeps its value, so it doesn't dip.
2. **Threshold** (0 to 50 %, 8 by default): below it the output is exactly 0, so a resting finger does nothing. The scale starts again from 0 at the threshold, with no step.
3. **Full at** (30 to 100 %, 85 by default): the raw pressure that gives the full effect.
4. **Curve**: Soft, Linear, Firm (the default) or Hard, powers 0.6, 1, 1.6 and 2.5. Steps 2 to 4 are a 128-entry table worked out when a setting changes.
5. **Rise and fall** (0 to 200 ms each, 8 and 40 by default): one-pole smoothing, quicker up than down.
6. **Release**: Hold (the default) keeps the value the voice has at its note-off; the 40 ms fall means a lifting finger has barely lowered it. Follow keeps following the pad. Fade falls to 0.
7. **Mode**: Per note (the default), Highest (every voice follows the highest pressure among the held notes, so a chord swells together without jumps) or Off. Off makes pressure do nothing: a level swell then plays at the preset's full level.

**Slots.** Each engine has four slots, each a target and a depth of -100 to 100 %. A depth reads in its target's unit on the Synth tab (`mpfree-synth`, `pressure::full_scale`):

| Target | 100 % is | Poly | FM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cutoff | 4 octaves | yes | |
| Resonance | 100 % | yes | |
| Level | a 24 dB swell | yes | yes |
| Vibrato | 1 semitone (Poly: its own sine at Vib rate, 5.5 Hz; FM: its LFO, without the LFO delay) | yes | yes |
| LFO rate | 2 octaves faster; the LFO is shared, so it follows the highest pressure | yes | yes |
| Pitch | 12 semitones | yes | yes |
| Drive, Distortion | 24 dB more | yes | |
| Width (pulse) | 45 % | yes | |
| Sub, Noise | 100 % of their level | yes | |
| Brightness | 24 output-level steps (18 dB) on every modulator | | yes |
| Tremolo | a 100 % dip, at the LFO's rate | | yes |
| Feedback | 7 steps | | yes |

**Level is a swell, never a boost.** A positive depth lowers the voice by the depth at zero pressure and plays it at the preset's level at full pressure; a negative depth ducks it as you press. Pressure on level never pushes a patch past the level it was designed at, so it causes no unintended clipping. Deliberate grit goes through Drive or Distortion.

| Pluck (Poly), Steel Pluck, Log Drum (FM) | none: these sounds decay, and pressure would fight the hit |

A swell preset at rest (a pad not pressed, a keyboard without aftertouch) plays quieter by the swell's depth. Mode Off plays it at its full level.

**Ids.** Poly's pressure block is ids 100 to 115 and Vib rate is 75; FM's block is ids 224 to 239. The old fixed amounts (Poly 74, pressure to cutoff; FM 22, pressure to vibrato) are retired, and slot 1 does their job by default.

**On the Synth tab** both engines have a Pressure page: the 8 settings and 4 slots, a drawing of the curve with the threshold and full-at points marked, and a live bar for each sounding voice's pressure. The app passes those values in with `SynthPanel::set_pressures`, from `Poly::pressures` or `Fm::pressures`. Q-Links 1 to 4 turn the threshold, full at, depth 1 and depth 2. With twelve pages, FM's tabs get room by the length of their names.

**What other parts own.** Per-pad calibration is the app's: it scales each pad's raw value before the event reaches a node. Recording pressure into clips, Note Repeat and the sampler pads adopting the shaper belong to the time engine, the arp and the sampler. How the One's pads actually send pressure (rate, a spike on the strike, a 0 before the note-off, evenness) still needs a read-only measurement round before the defaults are final.

## Tunings and per-note expression

**Tunings** (`rust/mpfree-tuning`). A `Tuning` turns a note number, fractional for bends and slides, into Hz. The default is 12-TET with A4 = 440 Hz, worked out with exactly the operations every engine used before (`440 * exp2((note - 69) / 12)`), so every factory sound is unchanged to the bit: all 38 `engine_cost` and 14 `poly_cost` hashes match main on x86 and on ARMv7.

- **Equal tunings:** any number of steps to the octave, each key one step, A4 on key 69 at any pitch from 100 to 1000 Hz (`Tuning::equal(steps, a4)`).
- **Table tunings:** each key from MIDI -64 to 191 has its own pitch. A note between two keys lies between their pitches on a log scale, so a slide or bend moves evenly from one key's pitch to the next.
- **Built in** (`Builtin`): 12-TET, 5-limit just intonation, Pythagorean, quarter-comma meantone and 24-TET quarter tones. The table tunings take a root (their 1/1, which keeps its 12-TET pitch) and an A4, so "just intonation on A at A4 = 432" is one call. `Builtin::tuning` never allocates (the degrees are a fixed array, `Builtin::degrees`), so the audio thread can switch tunings; it gives the same table, to the bit, as the scale through `scala::tuning`.
- **Scala files** (`scala`): `.scl` scales (cents or ratios, any period, so Bohlen-Pierce works) and `.kbm` keyboard maps (middle key, reference key and frequency, a map per repeat with `x` for unmapped keys). With no map, keys follow the degrees from middle C at its 12-TET pitch, as Scala does. Two differences from Scala, so a note never vanishes: every key follows the map whatever its first and last keys say, and an unmapped key sounds between its neighbours.

Poly, FM, Wavetable and Pluck play every note through their tuning; a sounding note moves to a new tuning on the next control step. FM's fixed-frequency operators stay fixed. Drums ignore tunings (a kit is pitched per pad).

**Per-note expression** (`NodeEvent::Expression`, `mpfree_node::Expr`):

- **Slide:** the note's own pitch offset, -48 to 48 semitones, added to the bend. Only voices playing that note move, so one note of a chord can slide (a sus4 resolving, a pitch lock on one note). Engines glide to each new value over 5 ms (`expr::EXPR_MS`), so a stream of values never zips; a sender ramps a long slide itself.
- **Tone:** 0 to 1, MPE's timbre axis. The engines keep it per voice; routing it to destinations, as pressure is, comes next with a Synth tab page.
- **Release:** release velocity, 0 to 1, for the note's coming note-off. The native engines don't use it yet.
- A value sent before a note-on (as MPE controllers do) is kept for that note and starts it in place. A note-off puts the note back to neutral for its next note-on; the releasing voice keeps its values. AllOff and reset clear every note. With nothing sent, nothing changes to the bit.
- **CLAP plugins** get them as CLAP note expressions: a note's slide is its tuning expression (with the bend added when the bend goes that way), its tone is brightness, and its release velocity goes in its note-off. A new note now also starts at a bend held from before it. The plugin sandbox carries `Expression` across (protocol version 2).

A tuning per track slot is in the track host (`TrackHost::set_tuning`, see [Track host](track-host.md)); nothing calls the host yet.

**Not yet.** Tone routing in the native engines, and MPE from MIDI input: MIDI in ([MIDI in and out](midi-io.md)) plays notes and poly pressure on the armed track, but does not read pitch bend or CCs yet (so no per-note bend or CC74 for MPE), and nothing sends Tone. The sequencer and the arp can send slides now. Choosing a built-in tuning or a tuning file is on the Synth tab's options (see [In the pad player](#in-the-pad-player-rustmpfree-app)).

## The Synth tab (`rust/mpfree-synth-ui`)

The instrument page for the MPC One's 1280x800 screen, on `mpfree-ui`. It works for any `Node`, so hosted plugins and effects get the same page.

- **Pages.** A native engine names its pages and what its Q-Links turn (Poly: Osc, Filter, Env, LFO, Voice, Pressure; FM: see above). Any other node gets pages from its `ParamInfo` groups: consecutive groups share a page up to 12 tiles, and a group bigger than 16 is split. Every parameter is on exactly one page.
- **Tiles.** Continuous and whole-number parameters are knobs; a bipolar one fills from the top. Choices of up to four show as buttons when they fit, otherwise as the chosen name with arrows. Toggles are switches. Read-only parameters are live meters. A tile's label carries its group when the group isn't the page's ("Env · Attack" on the Filter page).
- **Visuals.** Poly gets four cards drawn from its values: both oscillators, the filter response (with a dashed line where the filter envelope takes it), the amp and filter envelopes, and the LFO. A tap on a card opens its page. FM gets the algorithm diagram and a card for each page (see above). Drums gets the kit, the hit, the sweep and the levels; Pluck the pick or mallet, the harmonics or modes, and their decays (see their sections; `kit_visual`). On the Pressure page (Poly and FM) the strip shows the pressure curve and the live bars instead. Other nodes get the plain layout without cards.
- **Control.**
  - A finger dragged up or down a knob turns it; 320 px is the whole range.
  - A tap selects a tile, and the wheel then turns it.
  - Q-Links 1 to 4 turn the marked tiles. A detent is one step for whole numbers and choices, and 1/100 of the range along the parameter's curve otherwise.
  - SHIFT + wheel steps through the pages.
- **Q-Link sets** (`qsets`). A sound carries named sets of four controls, so the Q-Links can play it from any screen without opening its editor. Each engine has curated sets, in the order a player reaches for them:

  | Engine | Sets |
  |---|---|
  | Poly | Tone (cutoff, resonance, filter env amount, drive), Env, Filter env, Motion (LFO rate, LFO to cutoff, LFO to pitch, unison detune), Mix, Osc |
  | FM | Algo (algorithm, feedback, transpose, level), Levels 1-4, Ops 5-6 (levels and coarse), Ratios 1-4, Mod (LFO) |
  | Wavetable | Tables (both positions and warps), Tone, Env, Mod env, Motion, Mix |
  | Pluck | String, Mallet, Play |
  | Drums | Hit (tune, decay, tone, snap), Body, Mix, on the pad hit last |

  Any other node, such as a plugin, gets one set per Synth tab page (at most 8). Drums' sets name pad 1's parameters; `qsets::on_pad` moves them to the pad hit last, and `qsets::pad_relative` keeps a knob learned on another pad as pad 1's, so it follows the pad too. `qsets::save` writes the sets as `qset = <id> <id> <id> <id> <name>` lines for a preset file (`-` for a free knob), and `qsets::load` reads them back from a whole preset file: an id the node no longer has, or a meter, loads as a free knob. `qsets::to_banks(sets, params, owner)` makes the sets into `mpfree-mod` banks for `Bindings::set_page_sets` (one bank per set, each knob turning its parameter on `owner`, the sound's `Owner::Inst(track)`, over its whole range). After a learn (`Bindings::take_sets_edited`), `qsets::from_banks(info, banks, params, owner)` reads them back to save with the sound: a knob learned to anything that can't travel with the preset (an effect, a macro, another track's sound) is saved free, and a Drums pad's knob as pad 1's. Wiring this into the app's Q-Link column is the app's; nothing on screen changes yet.
- **The app's part.** The app owns the node on the audio thread. It builds a `SynthState` from the node (`SynthState::from_node`) and adds the panel with `SynthPanel::add(ui, "Synth", state)` after its own `header` and `hint` widgets. It passes touches (`SynthPanel::event`), Q-Link turns (`qlink`) and wheel turns (`wheel`, `step_page`) to the panel, and applies each `SynthAction` it gets back: `Set { id, value }` becomes `Node::set_param`, and `Preset(i)` loads the preset, after which the app calls `SynthPanel::load` with the node's new state. The panel updates its own copy of a value at once, so the screen keeps up with a finger.
- **Colours follow the theme.** The panel builds its tiles, cards and boxes without the theme, so it picks each colour by what it means, written as the default theme's colour for it: `widgets::QLINK_COLORS` (Q-Links 1 to 4, and the curves' blue, green, amber and violet), `TEXT`, `TEXT_DIM`, `TEXT_FAINT` and `ON_FILL` (text on a coloured box). When the widgets paint, `widgets::themed(theme, colour)` swaps each for the shown theme's own: the Q-Link colours for `track` 1 to 4, the rest for `text`, `text_dim`, `text_faint` and `on_accent`, keeping their transparency. Any other colour is left as it is, and the instrument's swatch goes through `Theme::content` like a kit colour. So the default theme draws exactly what the panel wrote, and the colours named on this page (a blue modulator, a green carrier, a white amp envelope) are the default theme's; Console, Synthwave and Spaceship draw their own. The FX tab, which uses these widgets, follows the theme the same way. A new colour in a panel should be one of these, or `Theme::content` for a colour that belongs to the music (a kit's, a track's).
- **Preview.** `cargo run --locked -p mpfree-synth-ui --bin mpfree-synth-preview` draws the sample screens to PNG files in a new folder `~/mpfree-work/synth-preview-<UTC>`. It opens no device. `--theme console` (or `synthwave`, `spaceship`) draws them in that theme.
- **Tests.**
  - Q-Link sets: every engine's curated sets turn real knobs (no meters, switches or choices, nothing twice in a set), Drums' sets follow the pad, the sets save and load with a preset, old or odd preset lines load safely, the sets go to Q-Link banks and come back unchanged (Drums on any pad too), and a learn on the Q-Links saves with the sound only when it is the sound's own parameter.
  - The anti-aliased drawing, value text, page plans and visual curves (every curve stays in its box for every parameter at its minimum and maximum). All 32 algorithms lay out with no boxes overlapping, carriers on the bottom row and every modulator above what it modulates.
  - `tests/panel.rs` runs a Ui at 1280x800 with a real Poly. It checks that dragging, tapping choices and switches, Q-Links, the wheel and presets reach the node, that every Poly parameter can be reached and set, that the cards follow the page, that the Pressure page draws the curve and live bars and reads depths in their targets' units, that FM draws its algorithm, rings the page's operators and opens an operator's page from a tap on its box, that every FM page has its strip, that a node without a plan gets the plain page, that every sample screen lays out inside the screen and redraws only the panel after a turn, and that under Console, Synthwave and Spaceship no sample screen's panel shows a default-theme Q-Link or text colour.

## In the pad player (`rust/mpfree-app`)

The pad player (`play` and `app`) carries Poly, FM, Drums, Pluck and Wavetable; one of them has the pads. `src/synth.rs` holds them all on the audio thread; the Synth tab shows the one on the pads. Round 6 brought Poly; FM, Drums, Pluck, Wavetable and the pad layouts came in round 8.

- **Picking it.** The kit list ends with "Synth: Poly" (or "Synth: FM", "Synth: Drums", "Synth: Pluck", "Synth: Wavetable"). Picking it with the wheel or a tap, or opening the Synth tab, hands the pads to the synth. Picking a drum kit hands them back, and the synth's notes end with their release. The sampler keeps its kit underneath, and its sounds finish.
- **The Synth tab's options** (MENU on the Synth tab, `app/synth_pads.rs`): "Track N instrument" (the selected track's own instrument: Kit, the five engines, then plugin synths; see [Track host](track-host.md#in-the-app)), Scale (Off, the built-in scales and the owner's), Scale root, Pad layout (for the bank playing), In key rows, Tuning (12-TET, Just (5-limit), Pythagorean, Meantone 1/4 comma, 24-TET quarter tones, then the tuning files), Tuning root (the note the table tunings and files are pure on; "(not used)" for the equal ones) and A4 (380 to 480 Hz, 1 Hz a step), then the Play rows as before. Every engine but Drums plays through the tuning; held notes follow on their next control step. The tuning is worked out on the audio thread without allocating (`TuningPick::tuning`), and the pads' log line adds it when it isn't 12-TET at 440 Hz ("tuned Just (5-limit) on D, A4 432 Hz"). On an engine switch the old engine's notes get their note-off and finish their release while the new one plays; each engine keeps its own preset. The shared synth's engine is still kept and restored with the options, but no row sets it since the Instrument row took its place: a track that wants another engine gets its own.
- **Tuning files** (`load_tuning_files` in `main.rs`, `TuningFile` in `synth.rs`): at start the app reads `files/tunings/` in the data store (`Store::list`, read only; names `[a-z0-9._-]`, 1 MiB a file), takes each `NAME.scl` that parses, in name order, at most 64 (`MAX_TUNING_FILES`), with `NAME.kbm` beside it when there is one, and logs `tunings: found dorian (7 notes, with its .kbm), just (12 notes)` and one `left out` line, with the reason, per file it can't use (a scale or map that doesn't parse, a map with no scale). Text that isn't UTF-8 is read lossily, since many published files are Latin-1 and only their descriptions use it. All parsing happens there, before the sound starts. A file with a map keeps the tuning the map gives (`scala::tuning`, which allocates, done once); one without keeps its scale, and the audio thread moves it to the picked root and A4 with `Scale::rooted`, which never allocates and gives the same table, to the bit, as `scala::tuning` with a linear map on that root (a test checks it, and that a `.scl` spelling just intonation plays exactly as the built-in one). The synth holds the list (`Synth::set_tuning_files`, before the sound starts) and the menu's list is built from the synth's, so a pick (`TuningPick::file`, an index) always names the file that plays; a pick the synth doesn't have falls to the built-in tuning and the settings and log line say so. The Tuning row cuts names to 20 characters; with a map, the root and A4 rows read "set by its .kbm". With a file picked, the built-in tuning underneath is 12-TET.
- **Kept in the project** (`App::synth_settings` and `App::open_synth` in `app/synth_pads.rs`; Jay, 13 Nov: a song's key and tuning are the song's, so they don't follow you from one project into the next): every option above and each bank's layout, in the project's `synth` part under `[options]`, by name (a scale by its name, a tuning file by its file name), so a list that grows or shrinks doesn't move a pick. A change with the controls is an edit for the unsaved-work check (`App::take_project_edit`, `ToUi::Edited`); switching banks isn't, since the bank playing isn't kept. An open puts every option back over the defaults (an option the project lacks, or a scale or file that's gone, is the default and said in the log, never the last project's) and reaches the synth as an `Effect::SynthPads`. New project starts chromatic in C at A4 = 440 Hz. They are no longer kept in Prefs ([Storage spec](storage-spec.md)).
- **Notes.** With no scale, pad 1 plays C3 and each pad after it a semitone higher, so the pads play C3 to D#4, in every bank. With a scale the pads follow `mpfree_scale::pads` (see [scales.md](scales.md#the-pads-mpfree_scalepads)): each bank has its own layout, out of the box A Chromatic (the scale lit), B In key, C Chord pads and D In key an octave up. A chord pad plays three or four notes. SHIFT + wheel on the Play page moves the pads by an octave, from C0 to C6 at pad 1 (with a drum kit it still moves the kit). With no scale the pads light C in purple, the other white keys in light blue and the black keys in dark blue; with a scale the root (or the home chord) is purple, the scale's notes light blue and the rest dark blue, all colours Akai's app was seen sending. The screen labels each pad with its note, or its chord ("iv7 Dm7"), and the header chip reads "FM · D Dorian · Chord pads · D3 to D4" (or "Poly · C3 to D#4" with no scale).
- **Drums.** A kit, not a keyboard: pad 1 plays the kit's Pad 1 note (on the Synth tab's Kit page, C2 by default) and each pad after it a semitone higher, whatever the scale, layout, bank or octave; SHIFT + wheel does nothing. Each pad is labelled with its sound ("Kick", "Closed hat") and lit in the kit's colour, the chip reads "Drums · Boom Kit", and the Synth tab shows the page of the pad hit last (`mpfree_synth_ui::pages::drum_page`). A new Pad 1 note, a pad's sound or another kit sends the pad grid and the arp the new names and notes. Pluck and Wavetable play the pad layouts as Poly and FM do. Neither takes pad pressure yet.
- **The layout owner's part.** `App::synth_toggle_layout` (the quick switch, Chromatic to In key and back) and `App::synth_bank` (a bank's layout) hand the synth new settings; which button or touch does each is the Hello MPC thread's to place.
- **Playing.** Note Repeat, Latch, Full and Half Level and the velocity curve work as for drums: the note engine hands the synth each note-on and note-off on its exact frame. Pad pressure goes to every note its pad plays. Two pads can share a note (two chords): a shared note takes the harder press and ends only when the last pad holding it is let go.
- **The Synth tab.** It sits between Clips and Looper. Q-Links 1 to 4 turn the page's marked knobs while it shows; elsewhere Q-Link 1 is still the level. The wheel turns the selected knob. SHIFT + wheel, - and + step the pages. A touch, Q-Link or wheel change goes to the audio thread and applies from the next period. After a preset load the audio thread sends the node's values back; every 250 ms it sends the meters and each voice's pressure (for the Pressure page's live bars) when they change.
- **Sound.** Each period the sampler and the synth render into their own tracks of the pad player's mixer ([`mixing.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/mixing.rs)), whose master fader is the Level and whose Master safety keeps the output under full scale. Every engine plays on the synth's track. An engine not on the pads renders only while a note of its own still rings. The synth's render uses fixed buffers and never allocates (a test watches it with a counting allocator, through engine, scale, bank and tuning changes). A block with a non-finite sample is dropped, that node reset and the reset counted. STOP fades every engine out over 10 ms and resets them, so no release rings on.
- **The log.** At the start, `synth: Poly, preset Pluck, as kit list entry N (C3 to D#4)`. Then a line when the pads move to the synth, by an octave or to another layout (`pads: FM synth, D Dorian, Chord pads (bank A), D3 to D4 (pad 1 = D3; …)`), one on an engine switch (`synth: FM on the pads, preset Glass Keys`), one per preset (`synth: preset 4 of 9, Supersaw`) and one per knob once it rests for a second (`synth: Filter Cutoff 2.5 kHz`). Every 30 s a second status line gives the synth's engine, render time per period (mean and largest, also as a share of a period), notes, the most voices at once, notes dropped and resets. The sampler's line times the sampler alone.
