# Playback: clips on the transport

**Status:** roadmap step T2 (merged in #47), a track through the arp (#51), the clip recorder (T3, #55), replace recording (#56) and take undo, 2026-11-11; pad pressure in clips, 2026-11-12. The code is [`rust/mpfree-seq`](../../../rust/mpfree-seq) (the model) and [`rust/mpfree-play`](../../../rust/mpfree-play) (the playback engine). Both are tested on the host. The pad player runs them in `TimeHost` (`rust/mpfree-app/src/time.rs`): the player and the recorder on the audio half, the project and every compile on the control half ([time-threads.md](time-threads.md)). The app's first live test on the MPC (12 Nov, main c8ef977) skipped most of its phases, so clips, recording and everything newer are still to be tried there.

This builds on the [transport](transport.md). The full plan is the time engine design document. Its mockups and skill tree are in the mpfree-claude notes (`sequencer/`).

## The model (`mpfree-seq`)

A project holds up to 128 tracks (`MAX_TRACKS`; Jay, 14 Nov 2026: "Up to 128, like Akai's MPC", raised from 16). A track has a lasting id (`TrackId`, the instrument redesign's step 2c: not its place in the list; the project gives each new track the next one, `Project::next_track`, and never gives one twice: [instruments.md](instruments.md#2c-as-built)), a destination, an optional swing, a mute flag and its clips by row (`Track::slots`, a `Rows`: up to 64 rows, `ROWS`, kept as long as the last clip; the Clips grid shows the first 16, `SLOTS`: [arrange.md](arrange.md#the-model-mpfree-seq)). A clip has:

- a lasting id (`ClipId`, the Arrange refactor's 1.1: kept when it moves, new for a copy, never given twice: [arrange.md](arrange.md#the-model-mpfree-seq))
- a length in its own ticks (from a 1/32 note to 256 bars)
- a speed (1/8 to 4 times)
- an optional swing and an optional quantise
- its notes, each with a tick, a length, a note or pad number, a velocity, a chance (1 to 100 %, 100 for a recorded note), a trig condition (`Always` for a recorded note), a ratchet (1 to 8 hits, 1 for a recorded note) with its velocity ramp (0 for a recorded note), whether its step is on (a recorded note is), how it starts (its own attack for a recorded note, or tied or sliding into the note before it: [steps.md](steps.md#ties-and-slides-notejoin)) and its pad pressure, if any (`Note::touch`, see "Pad pressure" below)
- its pad pressure (`Clip::touch`): every note's pressure values, one run a note
- the order it plays its steps in (`Clip::direction`, sequencer S6): forward, reverse, ping-pong or random; see "Play direction" below
- what its note numbers are (`Clip::holds`, the instrument redesign's step 5c): pad hits (`Holds::Hits`, pads 0 to 15 or a kit's 0 to 127, as every clip held before) or notes (`Holds::Notes`, MIDI notes 0 to 127 for the track's instrument). A clip of notes saves a `holds notes` line; a clip of hits saves nothing new, so old projects read as before and a project without notes clips saves as before. See "What a clip holds" below

These are plain values, edited on the control thread and checked with `check()`.

- **Everything is a clip.** A step pattern and a recorded phrase are the same thing. The step grid is a view of a clip, not its own data: see [steps.md](steps.md).
- **Any length.** Clips of different lengths drift against each other (polymeter), and no clip is tied to 16 steps.
- **Quantise is non-destructive.** Notes keep their recorded ticks, and quantise (grid and strength) applies on playback, so it can be changed or undone later.
- **Swing:** the clip's own swing wins, then the track's, then the transport's.

Parameter locks, trig conditions and ratchets are in the model too (see [steps.md](steps.md)). So are FX moves: a clip's `moves` holds a lane of points per parameter (the FX thread's `mpfree-moves`), see "FX moves" below. Scenes ([scenes.md](scenes.md)) and the arrangement ([arrange.md](arrange.md)) add to this model without changing it. Audio clips come in a later step.

## Compiling (`PlayView`)

The control thread compiles the project and the playing clips (`Launch { track, slot, origin, start }`) into a read-only `PlayView`. `start` is the tick the clip started on, which trig conditions count passes from: the origin, unless the origin was anchored back from a later bar line. For each clip it:

- sorts the notes
- applies quantise and speed
- picks the swing to use

The audio thread only reads the view. After an edit, the control thread compiles a new one and swaps it in, the same pattern as the sampler's `EngineView`. A clip plays from `origin` on and loops.

**Recording edits in place.** Recording is the exception, because it changes a clip on every note. A recorded or erased note or lock goes into the view in place, where compiling again would put it:

- `PlayView::add_note(track, slot, &note)` and `add_lock(track, slot, &lock, replaced)` for what `Takes::add` and `Takes::add_lock` put in (`add_lock` returns the lock it replaced);
- `remove_note` and `remove_lock` for what `Takes::erase` and `Takes::erase_lock` took out.

They update the clip playing and the clip cued, whichever shows that slot, and in song mode the song's copies of it (Arrange 2.3). `PlayView::make_room(notes, locks)` sets room aside in the armed track's clips after a compile, so these never allocate; each returns false when the view has no room left, or does not hold what is erased, and then the host compiles instead. Step edits use the same calls: a tap, a turn or a lock on the pattern playing goes into the view in place too, with no compile ([time-threads.md](time-threads.md#step-edits-in-place)). A note goes after the notes at its place, as the clip's newest, so the order is a compile's (a stable sort), and a test compares the two after thousands of random edits.

**Recorded changes, numbered (`mpfree_play::recorded`).** For a time engine on two threads ([time-threads.md](time-threads.md)), the view and the project must each make a recorded change without asking the other. Each is a `Rec`: a note taken (with its pressure run beside it), a note erased, a lock taken, a lock erased, or a part of a gesture (with its points). `PlayView::record` makes it to the view in place and `Takes::record` to the project, and they decide alike, from the same rules in `mpfree-seq`:

- a note's pressure goes on the end of its clip's pressure when it fits (`touch_fits`: in order, and no further than `MAX_TOUCH_POINTS`), else the note has none; the run stays even if the note is then refused, as `Takes::add_touched` leaves it;
- a note goes in when the clip can hold it (`Note::fits`, and fewer than `MAX_NOTES`); one past the clip's end, say, is left out by both;
- a lock takes the place of the lock in its place (`Lock::same_place`: the same parameter, tick and row), else goes in as the newest when the clip can hold it (`Lock::fits`, fewer than `MAX_LOCKS`);
- an erase of a note or lock the view no longer holds needs nothing, since an edit took it out of the project too.

`PlayView::record` returns false only when there is no room in place, and then the host compiles. The pad player makes every recorded change this way, and numbers it.

**What a clip plays, stamped (`ClipView::stamp`).** Each compiled clip carries a 64-bit hash of what it plays: its length, speed, quantise, swing, and every note, lock, pressure value and moves lane, but not where it plays (slot, origin, start). It is a sum of a hash per note, lock, pressure value (with its place) and lane, each fed whole words with every field of the note or lock named (`note_hash`, `lock_hash`: a field added to `Note` or `Lock` doesn't build until it is hashed or said to play nothing), so a recorded change in place adds or takes away its own share and the view still equals a fresh compile, stamp and all; a recorded gesture hashes its lane again before and after. `Player::now_playing(view, track, tick)` gives a host what a track plays now, as `NowPlaying { slot, origin, song_len, stamp, muted }`: the clip launched or switched in, else in song mode the placement under `tick`. The mixer's frozen strips follow it ([FX](fx.md), "In the app"): a new stamp thaws the strip, a new origin moves its take, and a mute holds it quiet.

**What a view includes.** A view can say what it was compiled with (`PlayView::set_includes(Includes { recorded, switches, decided, mutes_decided, cues, timeline })`): the number of the last recorded change made to the project, how many started cues were applied on each track, for the timing contract (Arrange 2.5, below) each track's newest decision with the frame the host had heard when it made it, and the song's timeline revision, which changes with every edit that moves time in the song (a timing change made against an older one is cancelled: [time-threads.md](time-threads.md#one-timing-change)). A view compiled a little late then catches up before it plays: `Recent` keeps the last `RECENT` (1024) changes with their points (`RECENT_TOUCH` pressure values and `RECENT_MOVES` move points, four full notes' or parts' worth), and `Recent::catch_up(view, recorded)` makes the ones after `recorded` to it in order. If the view needs changes `Recent` no longer holds (`Behind::TooOld`), or has no room for one (`Behind::NoRoom`), the host keeps playing the view it has, which holds everything, and compiles again.

## Launching on the bar line (cues, T9)

A clip launched while the music plays does not cut in at once. It waits for the next bar line, the way the MPC's Next Sequence mode switches sequences at the end of a bar, or the next line of the launch quantise (below).

- **A cue.** `PlayView::compile_cued` takes the launches and the `Cue { track, slot }` values waiting on the tracks, one per track. `slot: None` is a stop.
- **The next bar line the playhead plays.** That is usually the next bar. In a loop whose end comes first, it is the loop start's bar line, right after the jump back. A cue made while stopped waits for the first bar line played after PLAY.
- **Launch quantise** (sequencer S4, the transport bar's "Launch 1 bar"). `mpfree_seq::LaunchGrid` is the line a cue lands on: `Now` (the next tick played), `Ticks(n)` (a note grid, 1/16 to 1/2 on the screen) or `Bars(n)` (1 to 8 bars on the screen, 16 in a file). The project has one (`Project::launch`, `Bars(1)` by default) and a clip can have its own (`Clip::launch`, None for the project's). `compile_cued` gives each cue its line (`CueView::line`): its clip's own, else the project's; a stop takes the project's. `Switches::due` starts the cue on `line.next(span start, bar)`, the first line in the span, counted on song ticks from bar 1. The host can set a cue's line on a fresh view (`PlayView::set_cue_line`): a scene's cues and a follow's use it (see [scenes.md](scenes.md)), and so does a cue that was waiting when Launch changed, which keeps the line it had (`Perform::hold_line`; the design's edge case). A new tap on the track replaces the cue and takes the line Launch gives now (`Perform::forget_line`).
- **Legato** (`Clip::legato`, the clip card's Mode row). A legato clip launched over a playing clip doesn't start from its top: it starts as far into its own pass as the playing clip had got into its pass (`pass_into`, in song ticks, so a clip at another speed keeps the place in the bar), so a variation takes over mid-phrase. `Started::from` is the tick its pass counts from (`at - into`), and `Started::apply` makes that the launch's `start`, so trig conditions and follow actions count from it too. Launched with nothing playing, or a stop, it starts from its top as before.
- **The switch.** On that tick the track's clip stops and the cued clip starts from its first tick. Notes of the old clip still sounding end on the same tick, before the new clip's notes start. A stop does the same with no new clip.
- **Then it keeps its place against the song.** A started clip's origin is `anchored`: the bar line, moved back by whole passes to before any position the song can reach (`EARLIEST`, about 12 days at 120 BPM). So after a loop jump back or a locate it plays on in phase, and doesn't go silent until it reaches the bar line again.
- **The control thread catches up.** `Player::started()` names each switch (track, slot, the tick, the new origin). The app applies it with `Started::apply`, which makes the clip the track's launch (starting on that tick) and removes the cue, and compiles again. Until that view arrives, the player and the recorder carry the switch themselves, so when the view arrives changes nothing that plays. A view that says what it includes stops the switch once it shows as many switches on the track as the player made (`Includes::switches`); one that doesn't stops it once its cue is gone. The count matters on two threads: a late view can both apply the switch and cue the same clip again, and the player must take that cue for a new one.
- **The timing contract** (Arrange 2.5, design D4 in the plan). A host that compiles on another thread can be behind the music, so its views say when and in which revision each cue was decided. `Decisions` is the host's side: on every view it compiles, a track whose cue (its clip, mute, line and legato) or scene program change differs from the last view's is a new decision, and gets the next revision (`Includes::cues`, rising across the tracks) and the absolute frame the host had heard, the end of the last period it took (`Includes::decided`); a cue the player carried out (`Started::rev`) that the host makes again before compiling is a new decision too. The mute queue gets the frame it began at (`Includes::mutes_decided`), so its cues land together. The player keeps the last 32 spans it played (`Switches::played`; 32 bytes each, so the look-ahead copies them cheaply) and, for each cue, mute cue and scene program change, looks for the first line of its grid played since it was decided (`Switches::crossed`). None: it waits for its line, as before. One: the view came after the line, so it is **late**: it starts at once, on the span's first tick, in phase with the line it missed (its clip anchored from the line, legato measured there; `Due { at, line }`), and `Stats::late_cues` counts it. A cue the player has carried out is never carried out again (its revision is at most `done`), so a view that still shows it while the host hears of the switch changes nothing; a newer revision takes its place, and one older than a cue carried out is dropped and counted (`stale_cues`). A newer cue in a view that does not yet show a switch means the switch's clip is not in it: the track is silent until a view shows it, counted once a span played so (`lost_switches`); the cue still lands on its line. A view that says no revisions (0) follows the old rules. On one thread (the facade together) the host has heard every period before it compiles, so nothing is ever late; with the control half on a thread of its own (`--time-thread`, Arrange 2.6b) it can be, by up to a poll (a millisecond) plus a compile. The app's control half adds the three counts to its status line ("cues whose view came late") whenever one is not 0. A press within the control half's lag after a line (a few ms) catches that line, in phase.
- **Recording.** On the armed track, notes held at the switch end on its bar line, in the clip that was playing. Notes played from that tick on go into the new clip. A switch ends a replace pass, and the take goes on as overdub.
- **Song mode.** `PlayView::set_song(project, on)` adds the arrangement's placements as regions on each track, each naming one of the view's song clips: each clip the song plays is compiled once, not once a block (Arrange 2.3), and an edit to it goes into that copy in place. A track with no clip launched plays the song, and a launched clip takes the track over until it stops: see [arrange.md](arrange.md#song-mode-mpfree-play).
- **A clip's program change** (sequencer S5, the review's "program change and bank per clip"). `Clip::program` (`ClipProgram { program, bank }`, the clip card's Program and Bank rows, saved as a `program 4[ bank 300]` clip line) rides into `ClipView::program`, outside the stamp, since it plays nothing on the pads. The player sends `Event::Program` once each time a clip starts on a track: at a cue's switch, at a song placement's start, and, for a clip that started before the span (a launch at once, the music starting, a locate or loop jump into it), on the span's first tick. `Player::programmed` keeps, per track, the clip start (slot and start tick, as `seen`) it was sent for, so a loop of the clip, the control thread applying the switch later, or the span split by a mute cue sends nothing more; a stop cue, a stop of the transport and a locate forget it, so the gear is set again when the music starts, but a loop jump keeps it (the clips that start on the far side send their own). It rides on the clip's reset start (`Start::program`), so in `play_span` it goes after the note-offs on its tick and before the clip's first note-on there. A muted track still sends it (in song mode, a placement starting while its track is muted sends it on the next span's first tick), so the gear is right when the track comes back. Everything but MIDI Out ignores it: the pads, the arps (it passes through `TrackArps` and never enters a MIDI effects chain), the recorder, CAPTURE, CV. `Chords::messages` turns it into bank select (CC 0, then CC 32) when it has a bank, then the program change, for MIDI Out (`MidiOut::send`, on the track's port and channel, beside the track's own program from its Plays on settings, which still goes first at an output change and the song's start) and for a MIDI file export's tap.
- **A scene's program change** (sequencer S5, [scenes.md](scenes.md)). `PlayView::set_scene_program(track, Some(SceneProgram { program, line, id }))`, set by `Perform::dress` on every view. `Player::play_track` sends it (`scene_due`, `land_scene`) on the first line of `line` the playhead plays, or on the span's first tick once it has landed (`scene_landed`, kept across a stop) or when it has no line (a scene launched while stopped), and once until the music next starts or jumps (`scene_sent`, forgotten with `programmed` on a stop or a locate, kept on a loop jump). It is skipped (marked sent all the same) when the clip playing on the track then has a program of its own: the cued one when the track's switch is on or before that tick, else the playing clip or the song's region. It is a `Start` of its own (`Start::scene`: not a note, no clip start, so no reset of PRE and NEI, no cut of a ratchet, and no say in whether a step's lock plays), pushed before the track's notes, so it goes after the note-offs on its tick and before the notes that start there, as a clip's does, and it comes out as the same `Event::Program`.
- **Play direction** (sequencer S6, the Deluge's and the Hapax's play direction; `mpfree_seq::Direction`, the clip card's Direction row, saved as a `direction reverse` clip line). A clip of two whole 1/16 steps or more (`Clip::direction_steps`, `DIRECTION_STEP` = 240 clip ticks) plays its steps in another order: **Reverse** (the last step first, every pass), **Ping-pong** (forward then back, the end steps once each turn, the turns running on across passes: 0 1 2 3 2 1 0 1 ...) or **Random** (every step once a pass, in an order of its own each pass: a Feistel shuffle keyed by the track, the slot and the pass, so a pass plays the same every time, an export too). Passes count from the one the launch started in, as the trig conditions do. Another clip, a loop cell and the song lanes play forward; the clip's moves (FX lanes) always do. `ClipView::direction` and `ClipView::steps` (0 going forward) carry it, and both are in the stamp. **Where the steps play** is `mpfree-play`'s `steps` module, shared by the player and the recorder: counted in play ticks (the clip's own ticks from its origin, as if forward), play step `S` holds the ticks within half a step of its line, and plays the clip step its direction picks for that pass (`Direction::step`), whose notes are those within half a step of the clip step's line (the clip's last half step belongs to its first step, before its line). Each note keeps its distance from the line, so a note played a little early or late moves with its step, and swing is applied where the step plays. `Player::collect` plays a forward clip as one run a pass (`Run`, unchanged), and a clip with a direction as runs of a step (`collect_steps`: the step's ticks, shifted to where it plays; the first step's two runs), nothing before the origin. Erasing (the replace pass, live erase) and trig conditions go by the pass the step plays in. **Recording** maps what is heard to the step: a note recorded while a direction plays goes on the clip step playing where it was heard, at the same distance from its line (`Steps::heard`, after record quantise, which works on the heard position), so it plays back where it was played; a knob move locks the pad held, else the note heard last within a 1/16 by where each note played, else the step line heard. The replace pass erases what each step plays as it plays it, so a ping-pong pass that doesn't reach a step leaves it. **What a frozen strip follows** (`Player::now_playing`): a reversed clip repeats every pass; a ping-pong clip comes round the same every 2n − 2 steps, so its `NowPlaying` loop is that many passes (the lowest whole number, counted from its launch's first pass); a random clip never repeats (`NowPlaying::repeats` false), so Freeze refuses it with why (`takes::Song::loop_of`), and Print takes one pass. **The step playhead** (the Play page's pattern and the step pads with STEP SEQ) lights the step heard: `StepOrder::of` builds the player's order from the project's clip and the track's `Launch` (its origin and start, so passes count from the same one), and `StepClip::playing` in `mpfree-app`'s time module asks it for the clip step of each play step, from its line to the next, as a forward clip's playhead moves.
- **A mute with the cue.** A cue can carry its track's mute (`PlayView::set_cue_mute`, for a scene's mutes). The player switches the track's mute on the cue's bar line, and `Started::mute` tells the app. Scenes and follow actions use this: see [scenes.md](scenes.md).
- **The mute queue** (sequencer S4's third part; [make-perform.md](make-perform.md)). A track can carry a mute cue of its own (`PlayView::set_mute_cue`: `MuteCue { silent, line, id }`), for TRACK MUTE's taps that wait. `Player::play_span` plays each track on its own (`play_track`), and splits the span where the track's mute cue is due (`mute_due`: the cue's id is newer than the one that last landed, and `line.next` falls in the span): the part before plays, `land_mute` switches the track there (a track going silent has its sounding notes end on the line, and its hits, touch, locks and moves let go), and the rest plays. Every track of one queue shares its id and line, so they land on the same tick. `Player::mutes_landed()` names each landing (`MuteLanded { track, silent, at, id }`), and the app applies the whole queue on the first one. A landed cue holds while the view keeps its id; where a clip switch's mute (a scene's) also lands, the later tick wins, the mute cue on a tie.

## Playing (`Player::process`)

Each period the player walks the transport's spans and sends events in frame order to a `Sink`. Each event says which track it came from and which destination it is for. The host maps destinations to the sampler, the arp or MIDI out.

- **Clip positions come from the tick.** A clip's position is worked out from the song tick every time (origin, length, speed), never counted up. After a loop jump, a locate or a tempo change, every clip is in the right place.
- **Whole ticks.** A note's song position after quantise, speed and swing is rounded to the nearest tick. A tick is 1/960 of a beat, 0.26 ms at 120 BPM, the same resolution as the MPC. A note plays when the transport plays its tick, on that tick's frame. The transport plays every tick exactly once between jumps, whatever the period size, so no note is lost or doubled at a period edge or a tempo change.
- **Note-offs** come on the tick where the note ends.
  - A loop jump, a locate or a stop ends every sounding note on that frame, and the sound keeps its release tail.
  - STOP while stopped (panic) also sends `AllOff`.
  - If a note starts while the same note is still sounding on the same track, the old one ends first, on the same frame.
- **What a clip holds.** Every note event says whether its number is a pad or a note (`Out::notes`, from `ClipView::holds_notes`, which is in the stamp): a note's start, its end, its ratchet hits and its pressure, so the host reads each right. The player keeps a sounding note's kind with it (`Sounding::notes`, `Hit::notes`, `Touching::notes`), so a note-off or a tie only ever meets a note of its own kind. The arps keep a clip of notes as a source of its own (`arp::CLIP_NOTES`), so its pressing and letting go never mix with a clip of hits on the same track; what an arp plays is marked as pads (`notes: false`) and the host's live map reads it, as before. `Out` is 40 bytes with the flag (asserted). The app's host plays a clip of notes by number ([instruments.md](instruments.md#5c-as-built-clips-of-notes-playback)). Convert to notes turns a clip of hits into a clip of notes that plays the same events ([recording.md](recording.md#convert-to-notes)), tested here by `tests/convert.rs`.
- **Count-in and mute.** Nothing plays during a count-in, or on a muted track.
- **Chance.** A note below 100 % rolls each time it comes round, on a hash of the note and the frame it would play on: the same for any period size, different on every pass. Notes left out are counted in `Stats::skipped`.
- **Trig conditions.** A note's condition is decided before its chance; `Stats::unmet` counts the notes it leaves out. A:B and 1ST count passes from the clip's `start` by song position, so they are right after a locate or a loop jump. `Player::set_fill(on, offset)` turns FILL on or off on an exact frame of the coming period. Each track keeps its last result for PRE, and NEI reads the track to its left. The player decides a span's notes in the order they play (tick, then track, then the clip's order), so the results are the same for any period size. A track's result starts again when its clip starts, and on a stop or a locate, but not on a loop jump. See [steps.md](steps.md#trig-conditions-cond).
- **Ratchets.** A ratchet's first hit is decided like any note; if it plays, its later hits wait in a table (256 for all tracks) and play on their ticks, after the note-offs and locks there and before the new notes. A stop, a locate or a loop jump drops them, a switch drops those from its bar line on, and a muted track drops its own. Each later hit's velocity follows the note's ramp (`mpfree_seq::ramped`, [steps.md](steps.md#ratchets-noteratchet)).
- **Steps that are off, ties and slides** (sequencer S6, [steps.md](steps.md#ties-and-slides-notejoin)). A step that is off starts out decided against: it is left out with its locks, sets no last result for PRE and NEI, and counts in `Stats::off`. A tie or slide that plays looks first, before anything on its tick ends (the locks and note-offs there come after), at its track's sounding notes started before its tick that end on it or later (`Sounding::from`), and at a ratchet's hits from before its tick that the span hasn't played yet, so a long period joins what a short one would. A note of its own pitch takes the tie's end, the tie sends no note-on and no unlocks, and `Stats::tied` counts it; with a slide, the other notes' ends move to one tick after its tick, so its note-on comes first and they overlap by a tick (0.26 ms at 120 BPM).
- **What conditions cost.** Counted in instructions with callgrind on the bench's Time case (8 clips of 16ths with 3-note chords and 2 locks a step, through the arp): 7,380 instructions a period before conditions existed, 8,060 with them built in and none used (+9 %), and 7,580 with a condition on every note, a ratchet of 3 on every fourth step and FILL switching (fewer notes play). A note with no condition and full chance skips the deciding.
- **Real-time safe.** There is no allocation, lock or panic in `process`. A full table is counted in `Stats::dropped`, never dropped silently: 256 sounding notes, 256 note starts per span.
- **A copy that plays ahead** (sequencer S5, the look-ahead for slow MIDI gear, `mpfree_play::Ahead`; [midi-io.md](midi-io.md)). `Player::copy_from` makes a player what another is, copying only the used part of each table (about 15 kB of 140 kB, in place, no allocation), and `Player::only` limits a copy to some tracks (a bit a track). Each period the look-ahead copies the transport and the player and plays the copy on up to 100 ms in one period, for the tracks on slow gear alone: it plays what the player will, since both play the same events for any period size and the transport's queued commands go with the copy.

## Parameter locks

The player sends a clip's locks as `Event::Lock { addr, value }`, with `addr` the full `ParamAddr` on the clip's track, and ends them with `Event::Unlock { addr }`. The mod thread's resolver (`mpfree-mod`) applies them: a lock sets the parameter, and an unlock lets it go back to its knob, automation and modulation.

- **Before the note.** A lock is sent on its tick's frame, after the note-offs there and before the note-ons, so the step's note starts with the locked value.
- **Until the next note without one.** The lock holds until the track plays a note with no lock on that parameter on its own tick; the `Unlock` comes on that note's frame, before it. A sampler pad's parameter is only ended by that pad's notes. A trigless lock (no note on its tick) holds the same way. A note its condition or chance leaves out ends nothing.
- **A left-out note leaves out its locks.** The locks on its tick and row are not sent, and a whole-step lock is not sent when every note on its tick is left out. Trigless locks always play. The player decides the span's notes before it sends anything, so it knows this when it reaches the lock.
- **Jumps and switches.** A stop, a locate, a transport loop jump or a panic unlock everything, after the notes end. When a cue switches a track's clip, the old clip's locks end on the switch, before the new clip's start. A muted track, or one with nothing playing, lets its locks go at once and sends none.
- **Made for another instrument** (step sequencer refactor F1; [steps.md](steps.md#parameter-locks-lock)). A clip's locks and move lanes on its track's instrument (`OnTrack::Inst`) are left out of what the track plays while it plays another kind of instrument than the clip's were made for, or a control of that number has another name there (`Clip::inst_silent`): `view_of` takes the clip's track's instrument (`Track::plays`; a song cell's, its lane's track) and compiles the clip without them. Nothing else changes: its notes, its other locks and lanes play.
- **Every Unlock follows a Lock.** The player keeps what it has locked (up to 256 held locks, 256 lock starts a span; more is counted in `Stats::dropped`). `Stats::locks` counts locks sent. A pass being replaced sends none of its locks: it erases them (see "Replace and overdub").

## FX moves

A clip can hold moves: Perform FX gestures (the XY pad, the DJ knob, a pad's Hold) recorded as automation, one lane per parameter (`Clip::moves`, the FX thread's `mpfree-moves`). A lane's points sit on the clip's own ticks: a ramp glides to the next point, a step holds, an end lets the parameter go. A lane on a parameter of the clip's own track is track-relative (a copied clip moves its new track); any other (the master's Perform FX, a macro) is fixed.

- **Lane events.** The player sends a lane's values as `Event::Lane { addr, value }` on the clip's own ticks: every point, and inside a ramp every 8th clip tick (about every 4 ms at 120 BPM). A value on song tick x sounds on x rounded half up, so every value lands on the same frame at any period size. An end sends `Event::Unlock`. A value equal to the one held is not sent again. `Stats::lanes` counts values sent. The mod resolver applies them (`ModEvent::Lane`, the mod thread's #129), and a hand on the control beats the lane until it lets go (`Touch`), which needs nothing from the player.
- **A step's lock wins its step.** Lanes and step locks share one held slot per parameter. A step lock on a parameter a lane is moving takes it, and the lane's later values wait under the lock. When the lock ends (the track's next note without one), the lane takes the parameter back with its value there (`Lane`, not `Unlock`). A lane that ends under a lock leaves the parameter free when the lock ends. Moves are sent after the locks of the same tick, so a lock wins a tie. Notes never end a lane.
- **Chase.** When a clip's moves did not just play up to this span (a start, a launch, a jump, a loop, a switch, an unmute), every lane's state on the first tick comes first: its value, or nothing where it is free. A lane moving at the end of a pass goes on from the start of the next.
- **Jumps, switches, mutes.** A stop, a locate, a loop jump or a panic unlock everything (the chase brings back what the new place has). A switch ends the old clip's moves before the new clip's start. A muted track sends no moves and lets go of its own.
- **Real-time safe.** No allocation (`Moves::play` and `chase` walk fixed tables of 64 lanes). Lock starts per span went from 256 to 1024 to make room for lane values.
- **Cost.** In the bench's time case, which has no moves, the player and recorder cost 3.5 % more instructions (395 a period, about 0.3 µs on the MPC One). The moves case of the FX thread's bench plays 16 lanes.

## Through the arp (`ArpHost`)

A track whose destination is an arpeggiator plays its notes into an `mpfree-arp` engine instead of a sound. The held notes press and release keys on the arp, and the arp's own notes go on to the sampler (or any other destination).

- The host's sink passes the arp track's events to `ArpHost::input`, with their offsets, plus a panic's `AllOff` (sent to every destination). After `Player::process`, `ArpHost::process(block, out)` runs the engine over the same period and gives every note it plays, with its offset, in time order.
- Before every engine call the host gives the engine the span's clock with `Engine::sync` (the hook from #42): the span's anchor, tempo, play state, meter and swing. A loop jump or a locate at the start of a span says `jumped`, so the arp lands on the grid again. Transport swing above 50 swings the arp's steps. Straight transport swing leaves the arp's own swing setting in charge.
- The host splits a span at each note from the player, so a press lands on its exact frame. The engine's timing does not depend on how its calls are cut, so the arp plays the same notes on the same frames for any period size.
- Notes from several sources can come in any order in a period: the player's, and live pad notes for the track. They are kept in offset order. Each source sends its own in order and pairs its own: a note-off from one never ends another's note (see [Endings always get through](#endings-always-get-through-refactor-a2)). A note held by a pad and a clip at once is pressed on the engine once and released when the last of them lets go.
- No allocation: a table of 256 notes a period, by the rule below, so a note-off of a note it let in always fits. `ArpHost::dropped()` counts the notes left out of a busy period, endings lost (`ArpHost::lost`, a bug) and engine notes past its 128 per call. It should stay 0.

## One arp per track (`TrackArps`)

The owner's arp decisions of 2026-11-11 give each track its own arp, and each track a switch, "Clips through the arp" (`Track::through_arp`, off by default). `TrackArps` (the arp thread's [`arps.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-play/src/arps.rs)) holds an `ArpHost` for each of the 128 tracks a project can have (about 1.5 MB, made once at start) and sits between the player and the host's sink.

- **On (melodic tracks).** The track's clip notes go into its arp, as the live pads do. Recording keeps the pads as pressed (the chord), so the clip is arpeggiated again on playback.
- **Off (drum tracks).** The clip notes skip the arp and play as recorded. Recording keeps what the arp played, so a roll becomes notes.
- Either way a part is never arpeggiated twice. CAPTURE follows the same rule.
- A through track's parameter locks skip its arp, which has no use for them. They reach the sink as the player sent them, ahead of the arp's notes on the same frame. A panic's `AllOff` clears every arp in use and passes through. Each arp ends only the notes it has sounding, so a panic costs what they sound. Before, every arp ended all 128 notes: 2 049 events in one period, more than the arps' table (1 024) and the time engine's (512) hold, so the 12 Nov live test counted 1 025 events and 512 notes lost on every STOP while stopped (`time::tests::a_panic_through_the_arps_loses_nothing` reproduces those numbers with the old panic).
- Each period: `player.process(view, block, &mut arps.through_sink())`, then `arps.finish(block, sink)`, which runs every arp over the block and sends everything in frame order.
- **MIDI effects** ([note-fx.md](note-fx.md)). A track with any (`Track::note_fx`) runs a chain around its arp: the effects above the arp's place change the notes going into it, the rest what it plays. What reaches the arp reaches them, so the switch's rule holds for them too: a part recorded after them never goes through them again. A chain is about 400 KB, so the time engine's control half makes it the first time a track has an effect and sends it (`ToAudio::Chain`); `TimeAudio::dress_arps` hands it to `TrackArps::give_chain` and gives each period the track's effects (`TrackArps::set_notes`, which never allocates). A track with no chain yet plays as it is. A chained track runs every period, so what its effects hold back (an echo, a strum) leaves on time. A panic, or the switch changing, cuts the chain where it lands: what it has on ends there and nothing waiting plays after it. A mute does the same where it lands: `TimeAudio::run` reads the mute cues that landed going silent (`Player::mutes_landed`), finds their frame in the period, and calls `TrackArps::cut(track, offset)` before the arps finish (sequencer S4).
- **Cost.** A period visits only the tracks up to the highest one anything has asked for (`TrackArps::used`: a note, a pad, the arp's switch or destination, or its engine), so a project of 8 tracks pays for 8 arps, not 128. An idle arp with nothing given to it skips its period (`Engine::skip`). Settings changed on every engine at once (the tempo, a project's arps) go through `TrackArps::engines_mut`, which wakes none: settings alone never start an engine. An engine no period has visited has never been given anything, so it is as a new one, and its first sync lines it up with the transport. On the bench (callgrind), the Time case costs about 8,100 instructions a period and 4 latched arps about 3,100, a little less than with 16 arps always visited.

## Recording (`Recorder`, T3)

Notes played while the transport records go into the armed track's playing clip.

- **Feeding it.** The control thread arms a track in the view (`PlayView::set_armed`) before swapping it in. Each period the host passes the track's live pad notes to `Recorder::input` with their offsets. On a track whose arp output should be kept, it passes the arp's notes instead. Then `Recorder::process(view, block)` runs after the player and the arp. Notes held while the arm changes are not recorded.
- **Exact ticks.** A press lands on the nearest whole tick to its frame (a tick is 0.26 ms at 120 BPM). The tick maps into the clip by its origin, speed and length, so a note played in the clip's fifth pass lands where that pass is. The clip keeps that tick. The clip's quantise applies on playback, so it can change later.
- **Lengths.** A note ends when its pad is released. A punch out, a loop jump, a locate or a stop ends every held note on that tick. A pad pressed again while held ends the held note first. Nothing is recorded while playing without recording, during a count-in, before the clip's origin, on an unarmed track, or with no clip playing.
- **Handing over.** Finished notes come out of `Recorder::taken()` with their track and slot, in the clip's own ticks. The control thread adds each with `Takes::add`, which calls `Clip::add_note` (checked, at most 8192 notes) and remembers the note for undo, and compiles a new view.
- **No double notes.** Quantise or swing can move a note later than the moment it was played. Such a note waits until the playhead has passed the place where it will play, so the player never plays it again straight after it was heard live. It plays from the next pass on.
- **Period size.** Every tick comes from the span the note fell in, and waiting notes come out in a fixed order. The recorded notes are therefore the same, in the same order, for any period size. When the control thread swaps in the new view can depend on the period size.
- **Real-time safe.** Fixed tables, with no allocation: 256 inputs a period, and 256 notes waiting or handed over. `Recorder::dropped()` counts what did not fit. It should stay 0.

### Knob moves become locks

While the transport records, the host passes the armed track's knob moves to `Recorder::input` as `Event::Lock { addr, value }`, in frame order with the pads. Each lands on a step, the Elektron way:

- **On the note being played.** If a pad is held, the lock goes on its note. Otherwise it goes on the note that started last, less than a 1/16 (`LOCK_REACH`, 240 ticks) before the move: one the clip holds, or one this take just recorded that the clip does not hold yet. A sampler pad's parameter looks only at that pad's notes. Old notes the replace pass has erased are not locked.
- **Otherwise trigless.** With no note that close, the lock goes on the nearest step line (the clip's quantise grid, or a 1/16).
- **Waiting like a note.** A recorded lock waits until the playhead has passed where it plays, as a recorded note does (see "No double notes"). So a replace pass never erases it, even when quantise puts it a little ahead of the playhead.
- **A turn is one lock.** A knob sends many values. While a lock waits, each later value for its step and parameter replaces the one it holds. Then `Recorder::taken_locks()` hands it over. The control thread puts each in with `Takes::add_lock`, in place of a lock of the same parameter on the same tick and row. A later value of a lock the same take recorded replaces it in the take too, so the take holds one lock for the whole turn, and UNDO puts back what was there before the take. The clip ends up with the same locks in the same order for any period size.
- **Only the armed track.** A knob on another track's parameter, or a project parameter, is not recorded (`Recorder::stray_locks()` counts them). Nothing is recorded while not recording.
- **What the app records** (sequencer roadmap S1's knob recording): `record_knob` in `main.rs` (the smoke session's `route` too) passes a screen action's turn to `TimeHost::knob` while the transport records, as `knob_turn` reads it: a knob of the selected track's instrument (`SynthAction::Set`, the Synth tab and its Q-Links), a mixer strip's control or an insert's knob (`MixAction::Set`, `SetInsert`), and an effect's knob in the chain editor (`ChainAction::Set`), each on a track. Still to come: the Play page's Q-Links (swing, tune, the Drums' filter and delay send) and a kit pad's sound knobs on the Sample tab.
- **Real-time safe.** Up to 64 recorded locks a period after joining; more are counted in `Recorder::dropped()`.

### Perform FX gestures become moves

The host gives the recorder the same Perform FX gestures it gives the move log: `Recorder::input_move(offset, Input::Move { addr, value, map })` while a control moves, `Input::End { addr }` when it lets go. While the transport records:

- **On the clip's ticks.** Each gesture lands on the armed track's playing clip, from where the clip was when it began, at the clip's speed. A gesture that goes on past the clip's end keeps counting on; writing it splits it at the end (`Moves::put`).
- **Where it ends.** A gesture ends where its control lets go, or at a punch out, a stop or a switch of the armed track's clip. After a loop jump or a locate it goes on from where the playhead landed, as a new part.
- **Handing over.** `Recorder::taken_moves()` names each finished gesture (or each part of 1024 values of a long one) with its track, slot, target, map and take; `Recorder::move_points(k)` gives its points. The control thread writes each with `Takes::put_move`, which replaces what the lane had over the ticks the gesture covers, and with `PlayView::put_move` into the view in place.
- **Which lane.** A parameter on the armed track gets a track-relative lane, any other a fixed one. Knob moves are not gestures: they still become parameter locks (the owner's decision, and an opt-in setting to record knobs as moves can come later).
- **Undo.** `Takes::put_move` keeps what each write replaced (`Moves::put_saving`), so UNDO puts the lane back exactly and REDO writes the gesture again. Step edits that move lanes (a page copied, a clip made longer or shorter) keep the lanes before and after.
- **Real-time safe.** 256 gesture inputs a period, 16 gestures at once, 32 handed over a period, in tables made when the recorder is made. `Recorder::dropped()` counts what did not fit.

### Pad pressure goes on its note

The pads send pressure (polyphonic aftertouch, 0 to 127) while they are held. While the armed track records and its "Pressure" switch is on (`Track::record_touch`, on for a new track; the Record list's sixth row), the host gives the recorder each value: `TimeHost::pad_pressure(offset, pad, value)`, which feeds `Recorder::input(offset, Event::Pressure { note, value })`. Another armed track's routed keys give their pressure to that track's recorder the same way when its switch is on (`TimeAudio::routed`, step sequencer refactor E3; [rec-arm.md](rec-arm.md)). The decision (the owner's, 12 November, from the synth thread's "Poly aftertouch for MPFree synths" doc, part 7) is thinned pressure, a switch per track, and raw values played back through each instrument's own pressure curve.

- **Thinning.** A value 2 or more steps from the last one kept is kept at once (`TOUCH_STEP`). A value one step away is kept once it has lasted 10 ms (`TOUCH_SETTLE_MS`), on the frame that time is up. So a slow drift is kept about every 10 ms, and a finger's one-step jitter is not kept at all. A value one step away that ends before 10 ms (it goes back, or the pad lets go) is dropped. A pressed note starts at 0, as the instruments do.
- **On its note.** The values a note keeps are timed in clip ticks from the note's own start (`TouchPoint { at, value }`, at the clip's speed). The recorded note names its run in the clip (`Note::touch`, a `Touch { first, len }` into `Clip::touch`). Quantise, swing and note edits that move the note (`..note` copies) take its pressure with it. A split leaves it on the first part only; a shorter note stops sending what falls past its end. Half and double speed (`notes::half_speed`, `notes::double_speed`) stretch each note's pressure with it: the edit carries the notes' runs again with their times doubled or halved (`Edit::touch`, added after the clip's pressure, which is `Edit::touch_at` long when the edit is made), and the new notes name them. The clip's old runs stay, so undo puts back notes that still name theirs. A clip with no room for the new runs refuses the whole edit; an edit made on a clip whose pressure has grown since is refused (`BadTouch`) and changes nothing.
- **The same for any period size.** Every time comes from the frame the value came on, and a one-step value's 10 ms is checked against later values and at each span's end, inside the span where it falls.
- **Its own queue.** Pressure values have their own input queue (`MAX_TOUCH_INPUT`, 512 a period), so a flood of pressure never crowds out notes. A value on the same offset as a note comes after it.
- **Buffers.** Each note's values wait in one of 32 buffers of 2048 values (`TOUCH_BUFS`, `TOUCH_POINTS`, made when the recorder is made) until the note is handed over. The taken note's `touch` then names them in the recorder, `Recorder::touch_points(&note)` gives them, and the buffer is free from the next period on. A full queue or buffer, or a note that finds no free buffer, is counted in `Recorder::touch_dropped()` (it should stay 0) and shown in the status line.
- **Into the clip.** `Takes::add_touched(project, &taken, points)` adds the run to the clip first (`Clip::add_touch`), then the note naming it. A clip's pressure only grows while it is played into (at most `MAX_TOUCH_POINTS`, 65536), so undo and redo of the note never touch it; pressure a full clip can't take is left off the note and counted. Saving writes only the values a note names, its own or one Restore would bring back (refactor B4a, `TouchKeep` in `mpfree-seq`): each run is renumbered as the unused values drop out, so a clip reloads with no pressure a note it erased left behind (`save::tests::only_the_pressure_a_note_names_is_saved`). In memory too (refactor B4b): once a recording stops, each clip it put pressure into that holds more than half the room (`TOUCH_RECLAIM_AT`, 32,768) frees the values nothing can play again (`Takes::reclaim_touch`, called from `TimeControl::reclaim_touch`). It keeps what a note names, its own, one Restore would bring back, or one an undo or redo would put back in this clip: the takes back to the one that put the clip in its slot, and the ones redo would bring up to the one that would swap it, since past those a take's notes name another clip's pressure, kept with that clip. Every kept run is renumbered alike in the clip, its original and those takes, so undo and redo still find their notes by value, and the view is compiled again. Never while recording, since a replace pass names the notes it erases by value, pressure run and all, as the playing view holds them. The status line says "unused pressure values freed (N times)" once it has run. Tests: `takes::tests::reclaiming_pressure_keeps_what_undo_redo_and_restore_bring_back` and `time::tests::unused_pressure_is_freed_once_the_recording_stops`. `PlayView::add_touch` puts the run in the view in place, before `PlayView::add_note`, in room set aside at each compile (`TOUCH_ROOM`, 8192 values in the armed track's clips).
- **Playback.** A note with pressure sends `Event::Pressure { note, value }` while it plays: each value on the song tick its time from the note's start lands on (the note's start as played, after quantise and swing, plus the value's clip ticks at the clip's speed, rounded to a tick). It stops at the whole note's end (a ratchet's later hits keep it going) or wherever the note is cut. A new note on the same sound and pitch ends the old note's values. A stop, a locate or a loop jump ends them with the notes; a switch ends the track's on its bar line; a track going quiet drops its own. On a tick, a value comes before the note-offs there. 64 notes send pressure at once (`MAX_TOUCHING`); past that, it counts in `Stats::dropped`. `Stats::pressure` counts the values sent.
- **Where it goes.** The raw value goes to the pads (`Pads::pressure_at`): the synth's `pressure_at` (every note the pad plays, on its frame) or the sampler's. On a track whose clips play through its arp, the arp's repeats follow it, and the sound hears it too. Each instrument shapes it with its own pressure curve (`mpfree_node::Pressure`), so changing a preset's curve changes the playback too. `mpfree-track`'s host sends it to the node as `NodeEvent::Pressure`.
- **CAPTURE** keeps it too: its history has a ring of thinned values of its own, and a kept phrase's notes carry them (see [capture.md](capture.md)).
- **Live to song** places the clips it heard, each block naming its clip by id, and a song clip (one only the song plays, kept in `Arrangement::clips`) is a whole clip: either plays its notes' pressure (`tests/pressure.rs`, `a_song_placement_plays_its_clips_pressure`).
- **The note editor** draws it on the roll ([note-editor.md](note-editor.md)).
- **Not yet.** Editing pressure in the note editor, and FX-move-style lanes of pressure.

### Replace and overdub

With the transport's overdub on, a take only adds notes. With overdub off (the default, like REC on an MPC), the take replaces:

- **One pass.** The first time in a take that the transport records with overdub off, the player erases the armed track's notes as the playhead reaches them, until the clip wraps. A take is everything from a punch in to the next punch out. A loop jump, a locate, a stop, a punch out or overdub turning on also ends the pass. After that the take goes on as overdub, so a looping clip never erases what was just played into it. A punch in a moment before the clip wraps erases only that moment.
- **Erased notes.** They are not played. `Player::erased()` names them after each period with their track and slot, as the clip stores them, in the order the playhead reached them. The control thread removes each with `Takes::erase`, which calls `Clip::remove_note` (it removes one equal note), then adds the recorded ones, and compiles a new view. Naming notes by value, not by position, keeps this right when notes are added in the same period. Until the new view is swapped in, an erased note is still in the view, but the playhead has passed it for this pass.
- **The new notes are safe.** A note recorded in the replace pass is handed over only after the playhead has passed where it plays (see "No double notes"), so the pass never erases it.
- **Locks too.** The pass erases the old parameter locks it reaches in the same way, trigless ones included, so the new take does not play the old take's locks. `Player::erased_locks()` names them and `Takes::erase_lock` removes them; UNDO puts them back. The control thread applies erased locks before the recorded ones.
- **Muted tracks.** A muted armed track is still replaced; nothing on it plays.
- **Live erase** (ERASE held on a pad while recording, sequencer roadmap S1; while only playing too since the button combos of 19 Nov). `Player::set_erasing` names sounds, a bit each by note; while the transport records or plays, the armed track's notes of those sounds are erased as the playhead reaches them, on every pass and in overdub too, with the locks scoped to those sounds, through `erased()` and `erased_locks()` as the replace pass's are, so the take's undo brings them back. While only playing there is no take, so each hold (`set_erasing` going from nothing to something) starts a take of its own, numbered from `ERASE_TAKES` (the top bit) so it never joins a recorded take; the takes list and UNDO call it "erase 1", "erase 2" (`TakeRow::shown`). The app sets it through `TimeHost::erase_sound` from the main loop and the smoke session (`live_erase_starts`, `live_erase`, `live_erase_let_go` in `main.rs`): ERASE held and a pad pressed while recording, or while playing with the kit on the pads, which then neither sounds nor records, until the pad or ERASE is let go. Stopped, ERASE + a kit pad clears that pad's notes from the clip at once (`erase_pad_notes`, `StepAction::ClearPad`, one edit take). With STEP SEQ on, ERASE + a step pad stays the step pads'.
- **Real-time safe.** Up to 256 erased notes a period; the rest are counted in `Stats::dropped`. `Stats::erased` counts erased notes.

### Take undo (`Takes`)

A take is undone and redone as a whole: the notes it recorded come out and the notes it erased go back.

- **Take numbers.** Each record-on starts the next take, from 1. The recorder and the player count the same record-ons, so every `Taken` and `Erased` note carries its take's number (`Recorder::take()` is the latest).
- **The history.** `Takes` lives on the control thread. `Takes::add` and `Takes::erase` change the clip and remember the note with its take. `Takes::undo` and `Takes::redo` return the take's number, or None. A new take ends redo. It keeps the last 32 takes by default (`Takes::new(limit)`); older ones stay as they are.
- **Late notes.** A note that quantise moves past a punch out waits for the playhead, so it can arrive after the next take has begun. Its number puts it in its own take.
- **By value.** Notes are named as the clip stores them, so editing other notes between takes does not disturb undo. When undo or redo can't apply a note, it skips it. That happens when the clip is gone, or no longer has room or reach for the note (it was shortened). A take undone while it is still recording goes on as a new take, and redo can no longer bring back the undone part.
- **A drop on the Clips grid** (Jay, 14 Nov: drag and drop; `mpfree_play::takes::drops`). `Takes::drop_clip(project, from, to, copy)` moves a clip into an empty slot, swaps it with the clip there, or copies it into an empty slot (a copy onto a clip is refused: `NoRoom`). `Takes::drop_row(project, from, gap, copy)` moves a row (every track's clip in it) to a gap, the rows between shifting one, or copies it in with the rows under it moving down (refused while the last row is in use). Each is one edit take holding its clips in `removed` and `made` and the drop in `Take::dropped` (a `ClipDrop`), named "Move N", "Swap N" or "Copy N" in the takes list. The song's blocks name their clip by id, so a drop leaves them as they are and they play the clip wherever it lands, through their own lane ([arrange.md](arrange.md#the-model-mpfree-seq)); a row's scene and the morph's rows follow the row. Undo and redo work these out again from `ClipDrop::before` and `ClipDrop::after` rather than from a copy, so a song edited since is kept. The control half moves the launches and cues the same way (`TimeControl::follow_launches`): a playing clip plays on where it lands, on another track only if that track plays nothing, and a waiting launch follows its clip on its own track.
- **A deleted clip** (ERASE + a clip on Arrange). `Takes::remove_clip(project, track, slot)` takes the clip out of its slot as an edit take of its own ("Delete N" in the takes list) that holds the clip. Undo puts it back if the slot is still empty, and redo takes it out again. A clip a song block plays goes to the song's own clips instead (its id in `Take::kept_by_song`), so the block plays on; undo takes it back from there into its slot, and redo gives it to the song again. The control half compiles afterwards, which drops the clip's launch and cues.
- **A Song view edit** (a block moved, copied, made longer or deleted, a section moved, Scenes → song, Make unique). `Takes::song_edit(project, change, before)` keeps the song before it and after it as an edit take of its own (`Take::song`, a `SongEdit` with a `SongChange`). Undo puts `before` back only while the song is still `after`, and redo the other way, so a song changed since (live to song, say) is left alone. See [arrange.md](arrange.md#in-the-pad-player-timehost).
- **A clip's settings** (the clip settings card, sequencer S3). `Takes::set_clip(project, track, slot, to, join)` gives a clip new `ClipSettings` (its speed, swing, quantise and follow action, read with `Clip::settings` and checked as one by `Clip::set_settings`) as an edit take of its own ("Settings N" in the takes list, `Take::settings` holding the clip's place and its settings before and after). A change with the same `join` key on the same clip, with nothing undone since, joins the latest take, so a card row turned through many values is one undo (the control half keys it by the card's row). Undo puts the settings back only while the clip still has the ones it was given, and redo the same way. A note edit never joins a settings take.
- **A pasted clip** (COPY + an empty slot on Arrange, sequencer S3). `Takes::paste_clip(project, track, slot, clip)` puts a copy in an empty slot as an edit take of its own (`Take::pasted`, the clip in `made`), named "Paste N" in the takes list; undo empties the slot and redo puts it back if the slot is still empty. The control half keeps the clip COPY + a clip copied (`TimeAction::CopyClip`, not a take; `TimeState::clipboard` names it for the hint line) and refuses a paste onto a Loop track.
- **A first step's clip** (the Sequencer's first step on a track with no clip). `Takes::new_clip(project, track, slot, clip, launched)` does the same for a new clip that already holds the step's notes, with `Take::launched` set so redo launches it again; named "Edit N". `Takes::withdraw` takes back an edit what plays could not follow. See [steps.md](steps.md#undo-takesedit).
- **What UNDO says.** The control half names the take it undid or redid as the list does ("take 3 · Drums / 1"); the app shows it in place of the hint ("Undid take 3 · Drums / 1").

## Listening

`cargo run --release -p mpfree-play --example play_render` plays a demo beat through the transport, the player, the arp and the real sampler engine (`mpfree-sampler`), and writes `~/mpfree-work/play-demo.wav`. The beat is 84 BPM with heavy 1/16 swing: a four-bar loop played three times, then a final hit that rings out. The sounds are saturated and bit-crushed:

- drums, with ghost snares at low velocity
- hats at double speed with their own swing, and a 1/32 roll
- a distorted bass line on gated pads, so note lengths matter
- a 7/16 glitch clip that drifts against the bar
- dark chords held for a bar each, played through the arp in swung 1/16s on gated pads
- vinyl dust
- two live takes on an empty one-bar clip that quantises to 1/16:
  - **Take 1.** The transport records the first pass. A distorted chord stab is played on the pads a little off the grid, one or two hits a bar. From the next bar on, each hit plays from the clip on the grid, so the stab riff builds up. The transport punches out at the loop's end.
  - **Take 2.** In the second pass, bars 5 and 6 play the riff. Bar 7 punches in with overdub off. The stabs are erased as the playhead reaches them, so they stop, while a metal clank is played on the off-beats. Bar 8 and the third pass play the clanks alone.
- the end: the transport stops on the last bar line, and a kick, stab and crash ring out. The tool then undoes take 2 on a copy of the song and checks that the stab riff is back (take undo).

All the sounds are synthesised in the tool. It prints the take track bar by bar from the notes the player and the pads actually sent (which steps played from the clip, which were played live, which were erased). It checks that the stabs play from the clip only in bars 2 to 6, the clanks only from bar 8, and every erased note falls in bar 7. It prints an events hash for the player's and the arp's notes, the recorded notes and the erased notes. That hash is the same on every computer, and the tool checks that the events are identical at periods of 1, 128 and 4096 frames. On the build host the WAV bytes are also identical at 128 and 1000 frames. Every sound fades out at its end, so none ends with a click. The master is a plain gain with about 1.5 dB of headroom. The tool counts samples that clip at the master and warns if there are any. Drive and bit crushing inside a sound are deliberate and are not counted.

`cargo run --release -p mpfree-play --example steps_demo` plays a beat programmed only through the step grid; see [steps.md](steps.md#listening).

## Endings always get through (refactor A2)

Every table between the player and the sounds has a fixed size, and a busy enough moment (16 tracks of chords, a crowd of locks, a STOP at full polyphony) can bring more than it holds. Since the step sequencer refactor's A2 (20 Nov 2026), what gives way is always something new, never an ending: a note that started always gets its note-off, a held parameter its unlock, and a STOP its panic. Before, a full table dropped whatever came last, endings included, and left notes hanging.

- **The rule** (`mpfree_play::room`). A `Table` holds one period's events in the order they come, and its `Room` says which go in. Note-ons get `NOTE_STARTS` (512) places a period and values (locks, move values, pressure, program changes) `VALUES` (512), apart, so a crowd of locks never keeps a note out. Past that the new event is refused and counted (`Room::refused`). The room keeps, by stream, track and note, how many notes it let start that haven't ended. A note-on gets in only while those and the period's note-offs stay under `NOTE_ENDS` (1 024, twice the note-ons, so a period can end every note and start as many again), so the note-off of every note it let in has a place. A note-off whose note-on was refused is left out (`Room::unowed`): nothing sounded, so nothing hangs. The player asks before it holds a new parameter (`Sink::hold`; refused, the lock is left out and counted in `Stats::busy`), and the room grants `MAX_LOCKED` (256) new holds a period, so a period's unlocks are at most what was held as it began and what it held since: `LOCK_ENDS` (512). A panic has `ALL_OFFS` places, one for each command the transport takes in a period. The table has `ROOM` (2 576) places, enough for all of it (about 120 KB with its room, made once at start). `Room::lost` and `Table::lost` count an ending that found no place anyway: a bug, which must stay 0.
- **Streams.** The player's notes and the arps' pair up apart (`PLAYER`, `ARPS`), so a live note-off never ends a clip's note on the same track and pitch.
- **The arps** (`TrackArps`). The period's notes for the sink wait in a `Table`. Each arp's input (256 places) keeps the same rule by source: the clips (`CLIPS`), the live pads (`PADS`) and what a track's MIDI effects above the arp make of them (`EFFECTS`), with 17 places kept for panics (16 STOPs and the switch). A note-off goes where its note-on went: into the arp if the arp has it from the clips, to the sink if not, whatever "Clips through the arp" says now. Before, turning the switch on while a clip's chord sounded sent its note-offs into the arp, and the chord hung. The switch changing several times before a period sends one panic, and a panic from the pads lets go of the pads' notes only.
- **The time engine** (`TimeAudio`). Its period table is a `Table`. In `TimeHost::period` the player's events go through its room; in `period_arps` the arps' table has let them in already, and they pass on as they are. The lock list for the mod engine has a place for every lock and unlock the table lets in (`MAX_LOCKS`, 1 024), and the lane list for every value (512).
- **Said.** `Counts::busy` (new notes and locks left out: the time engine's table, the arps' and the player's holds) and `Counts::ends_lost` (should stay 0). The 30 s status line's `time:` part says `N new notes or locks left out of moments too busy, N endings lost`, and the Dev tab's Performance page has a "Note endings lost" row, green at 0. `TrackArps::refused`, `lost` and `unowed` count the arps' side; `TrackArps::dropped` still adds up everything that gave way.
- **The instruments' queues (A2b).** The same rule holds where the events reach a sound (`mpfree_node::room`, 20 Nov 2026): each engine of the shared synth (`synth::ROOM`) and each slot of the track host (`mpfree_track::ROOM`) has a `NodeRoom` of its own, and its queue has a place for everything the room lets in (`Limits::room`). A period takes 128 note-ons and 256 values (pressure, bend, the other controllers, a knob's value). A note-on goes in only while the notes the queue owes and the note-offs it passed this period stay within 256, so every owed note's note-off has a place; a note owed already (a pad and a clip on one note) is owed once, and its last holder's note-off ends it. The sustain and sostenuto pedals hold notes, so a pedal going down is owed like a note and coming up is its ending. A note-on left out is still counted as a holder by its host, so the counts stay paired, and its note-off is left out when nothing owes the note. A clip's lock asks before it holds a parameter it didn't hold (`Synth::lock`, `TrackHost::hold`), and a period grants 128 new holds, so the 256 places for values put back (`TrackHost::unlock`) are always enough. An all-off past its 16 places with nothing owed ends nothing and is refused, not lost. A render of no frames keeps the queue for the next one, and an instrument reset (a non-finite block, STOP's fade, a swap, a frozen track) leaves nothing owed. Said: the synth's 30 s status line adds `N new notes or locks left out of moments too busy, N endings lost` (`Counters::busy`, `ends_lost`), a track's instrument counts them in `SlotStats::dropped` and `ends_lost`, and the Dev page's "Note endings lost" adds the synth's and the track host's to the time engine's. *Tests:* `room::tests` in `mpfree-node`; `synth::tests::a_busy_period_leaves_new_notes_out_and_every_ending_in` and `a_lock_put_back_always_finds_room_and_new_locks_ask_first`; `tests/host.rs` `every_ending_gets_in_however_busy_the_period` in `mpfree-track`.
- **The mod engine (XY controller plan, phase 1).** Its own queue (256 events a period) used to drop an `Unlock` that came past it. Now a let-go that finds it full is kept on its target and done first next period, and opening a project lets go of every lock ([modulation.md](modulation.md#let-gos-are-never-lost)).
- **MIDI out (A2b).** Each port's queue keeps the rule by channel and note (`mpfree_midi::room::PortRoom`), with one note-off owed for each note-on, as gear may give a note struck twice two voices, and keeps a place in the port's writer for every owed note-off, however far behind the writer is ([midi-io.md](midi-io.md), M3). The arp engines, the MIDI effect chains (`note-fx.md`) and the sampler's queue (its let-gos kept aside) already keep room for their endings their own way.
- **Tests.** `room::tests` (a full period, a crowd retriggered on one frame, the streams, 400 random periods far past the limits), `tests/arp.rs` `a_busy_period_leaves_new_notes_out_and_never_an_ending`, `tests/arps.rs` `turning_the_switch_while_a_clip_note_sounds_leaves_nothing_hanging`, and in the app `time::tests::a_crowd_on_one_frame_leaves_nothing_hanging` and `a_crowd_through_the_arps_leaves_nothing_hanging`: 16 tracks switch clips on one bar line while their notes hold (256 endings, 256 starts and 768 lock values on one frame), play 16 notes and 48 locks a track on every sixteenth for two bars, then STOP. Nothing hangs, no note-off comes for a note that never sounded, no lock stays held, and a panic after it all still gets through. With the old 512-place table they fail.

## In the pad player (`TimeHost`)

`mpfree-app`'s [`time.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/time.rs) runs the engine in the audio loop, in two halves behind one facade since 12 Nov: an audio half that plays and records, and a control half that holds the project and compiles ([time-threads.md](time-threads.md)).

1. Each period the loop hands `TimeHost::pad` the pad notes it is about to play, after the arp, at their frame offsets. Once the app plays the pads through the per-track arps, it also hands `TimeHost::pad_pressed` the pads as pressed, before any arp. The armed track records the pressed pads when its clips play through its arp, and the heard notes otherwise. The capture history keeps the same side.
2. `TimeHost::period` runs the transport and the player. The clips play the current kit's pads through the sampler (`hit_at`, `release_at`); a clip's note numbers are pad numbers, 0 to 15. `TimeHost::period_arps(frames, pads, arps)` does the same with the per-track arps between the player and the pads. The app owns the `TrackArps` with its arp panel and lends it for the period. Each period every arp first takes its track's destination and switch. `TimeHost::set_through_arp(track, on)` turns the switch. Nothing calls `period_arps` or `set_through_arp` yet: the arp thread moves the app's arp onto the per-track arps.
3. The armed track records, and the taken and erased notes, the recorded locks and the recorded moves go into the clips and the take history. `TimeHost::gesture(offset, input)` takes a Perform FX gesture: the recorder records it while recording, and a move log (`mpfree_moves::MoveLog`) keeps it while rolling, so CAPTURE keeps the moves made over its phrase as part of the same capture. Nothing calls it yet: the Perform FX page calls it once the pad player runs it. `TimeHost::knob(offset, addr, value)` takes a knob move for recording; the main loop calls it for the screen's knob turns while recording (`record_knob`, see "Knob moves become locks"), and the smoke test `knob_turns_while_recording_become_locks` turns a strip's pan and a synth knob while recording and checks both land as locks, and that nothing does while only playing. `TimeHost::set_fill(on, offset)` turns FILL on or off for the trig conditions; no button calls it yet (the pad player picks the FILL button).
4. While counting in and recording, a click plays on each beat when the metronome is on: 1760 Hz on the bar and 1320 Hz on the other beats. The metronome is off by default and plays at −24 dBFS (Settings `metronome` and `metronome_db`, −36 to −6 dB; `TimeHost::metronome` and `click_gain`). It was on, at about −12 dBFS, until live test 3 ("far too loud").

Pad pressure goes to `TimeHost::pad_pressure` as it comes from the pads (main.rs, before the live instrument hears it), and the player's `Pressure` events go back to the pads with `Pads::pressure_at`. The Record list's "Pressure" row (`TimeAction::RecordPressure`) flips the armed track's switch; the row scrolls into view when a lower row is picked. `TimeHost::touch_dropped()` counts lost values, in the status line.

The player's `Lock`, `Lane` and `Unlock` events go to the mixer's mod engine: `TimeHost::locks` and `TimeHost::lanes` each period, then `MixHost::lock` and `MixHost::lane`. On the way, one on "this track's strip" goes to the strip the track's instrument sounds on, and ends there ([instruments.md](instruments.md#strip-locks-follow-the-instrument-as-built)). A move lane's value holds its parameter as a lock does, but a hand on the control beats it.

A track that plays somewhere other than the pads, such as a track on MIDI OUT (the MIDI thread's M3), is offered each event the clips play first, through `Pads::out(&Out) -> bool`: notes, a panic's `AllOff`, pressure, locks and lanes. When it returns true, neither the pads nor the mod engine get the event. When it returns false, the event goes on as usual, which is what an `AllOff` should do so the pads stop too. By default it takes nothing. The arps' notes, the live pads among them, are not offered to it: they go to `Pads::live`, which sees the whole event (track included) and says what the armed track records, so the pad player sends a MIDI OUT track's live notes from there. The pad player's `SeqPads` answers both from the track's "Plays on" setting. A track on slow gear (sequencer S5) has the locks and lanes on its own MIDI out controls sent through `Pads::out_ahead` instead, with its notes and not by the mod engine ([midi-io.md](midi-io.md), Controls ahead).

It starts with 8 empty tracks, with track 1 armed. A clip made from an empty slot is as long as the loop, or 2 bars without one. REC when the armed track has no clip playing launches the clip its pattern shows, or makes one in its first empty slot (`TimeControl::ready_to_record`), so REC on a fresh project keeps what the pads play. PLAY from stopped does the same when no clip plays or waits anywhere and song mode is off (`TimeControl::ready_to_play`): the pattern the Play page shows starts with it. A project opens with nothing launched (at start too), so before this, PLAY after an open moved the Beats grid's line over steps that never played (the 16 Nov device round). A step put on that pattern while its track plays nothing and has nothing cued launches it too (`launch_edited`), at the next bar line while the music plays, as a new pattern's first step always has; a clip launched on Arrange is left as it is. The tempo belongs to the app, so TAP TEMPO, the menus and the Clips page all set it, and the transport follows. Only the position (about 30 times a second) and real changes go to the screen.

Cues that start become launches right after the period they started in (`Started::apply`). Every view the pad player compiles says what it includes: the recorded changes made so far and the cues started on each track (`PlayView::set_includes`). Edits (launches, cues, arming, undo and redo, a capture, a step edit that changes a pattern's length) compile a new `PlayView` on the control half, which runs in the audio loop by default and on a thread of its own with `--time-thread` (Arrange 2.6b). A step edit that only adds and erases notes and locks of the pattern playing is made to the view in place instead ([time-threads.md](time-threads.md#step-edits-in-place)). That allocates, but only on an edit, not every period. Recording does not compile: each recorded or erased note, lock or gesture is a numbered change (`Rec`) made to the view in place (`PlayView::record`) and to the clip (`Takes::record`), deciding alike, in room set aside at each compile (`RECORD_ROOM`, 1024 notes and 1024 locks in the armed track's clips, and room for two parts of a gesture in each of their lanes; a new take's lists have room for `TAKE_ROOM`, 256). A gesture on a parameter the clip has no lane for yet compiles once. Once a take has begun, a recording period allocates nothing, and the screens hear about the recorded notes at most four times a second. It is also far cheaper: on the workstation, with 8 clips of 500 notes playing, compiling after each recorded note took 66 to 90 µs (2 to 3 % of a 128-frame period, and several times that on the MPC One); putting the note in place takes 0.3 µs.

**A compile costs what plays.** `PlayView::compile_cued` checks the tracks (`Project::check_tracks`: how many, their swing) and then each clip it compiles, launched or cued, as it compiles it; `set_song` checks the arrangement and each placed clip once, with song mode on. It no longer checks every clip in the project, which was more than half of a compile's time and grew with clips that weren't playing. A clip out of range is refused when it is launched, cued or placed (`CompileError::Model`), so the rest of the project still plays. On the workstation (release build, other jobs running), a project of 64 clips of 256 notes with 8 playing compiled in 49 µs before and 17 µs after; 128 clips of 512 notes with 16 playing, 307 µs before and 159 µs after. What is left grows with the notes playing, and on the MPC One it can still take a good part of a period, so the other edits move to a control thread: the two halves are built, and `--time-thread` runs the control half on a thread of its own, off by default until a device round compares the two ([time-threads.md](time-threads.md#apart-the-control-half-on-its-own-thread)). The status line every 30 s adds the transport state, the takes, any notes or parameter locks lost to full tables (should stay 0), and the new notes or locks left out of moments too busy and the endings lost (should stay 0; [Endings always get through](#endings-always-get-through-refactor-a2)).
