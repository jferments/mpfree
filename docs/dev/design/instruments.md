# Instruments apart from tracks

**Status:** step 1 on main (Play on the pads, #722, train 124, 20 Nov 2026); step 2a built (the shared synth as instrument 1 of the project's list, project format 2, `rust/mpfree-app/src/instrument_list.rs`, 20 Nov 2026); step 2b built (the tracks' own instruments in the list, 20 Nov 2026); step 2c built (tracks get lasting ids, `rust/mpfree-seq`, 20 Nov 2026); step 3 built (pad focus: the synth chosen for the pads stays whichever track is selected, saved with the project, `app/pads_focus.rs`, 20 Nov 2026); step 4a built (a track plays another track's own instrument, `instruments.rs`, 20 Nov 2026); step 4b built (knob owners, `locks.rs`, 20 Nov 2026); step 4c built (Duplicate: a copy of another track's instrument as its own; New instrument track: the preset picker's New track, 20 Nov 2026); step 4d's audit done and the instrument channels named for their instruments (20 Nov 2026); a clip's "this track's strip" follows the track's instrument (`time/strips.rs`, 20 Nov 2026). Jay approved the plan and its five answers on 20 Nov 2026 (11:16 UTC). The plan, its rules and the reviews it went through: "Instruments, pads and tracks: the redesign" (https://claude.ai/artifact/A2iE1bDqPxk2uSJABCgTJg). Nothing here has run on the MPC One yet.

## The model

Jay's rule (20 Nov 2026): **instruments make the sound, tracks organize and record performances, and the pads are controllers that play instruments.** A track is a place you record to. The pads, MIDI and scripts play instruments; tracks record what is played and play it back to instruments; every instrument's sound goes to the mixer, recorded or not. So the code names and routes by instrument, never by track, and a track's mixer channel is not an instrument's output.

Today an instrument belongs to a track: a track plays a kit, the shared synth (the Synth kit) or its own instrument (`instruments.rs`), and the pads play the armed track's. The redesign takes that apart in steps, each of which ships on its own, keeps old projects playing as they did, and gives the musician something they couldn't do before:

1. **Try a synth on the pads** (on main): Play on the pads plays the shared synth while the armed track keeps its kit, and REC records it on a track nobody set up ([rec-arm.md](rec-arm.md), `app/pads_focus.rs`).
2. **Instrument identities** (this page, below): instruments get lasting ids in a list in the project. No visible change.
3. **Pad focus** (built, below): the pads follow the selected track or play an instrument chosen for them, which stays whichever track is selected.
4. **Tracks send to instruments** (4a built, below): a track plays any instrument, several share one; each knob has one owner at a time (4b); Duplicate and New instrument track (4c, built); instrument channels in the mixer (4d).
5. Recording by source, with one recording plan behind both the REC chip and REC.
6. The instrument picker and banks.
7. MIDI and scripts play instruments.
8. Layers, mutes and stems by instrument.
9. Audio points.

## Step 2: instrument identities

Anything that must point at an instrument (a track, later a knob's owner, a script or a MIDI port) needs a name for it that stays put when tracks are added, moved or taken away. An **`InstrumentId`** is that name: a number from 1, which is not a track number, a mixer channel, a slot or a place in a list. A project gives each new instrument the next number, keeps that counter in its file, and never gives a number out again once its instrument is gone, so something that named an old instrument can't reach a new one by mistake.

An instrument's **saved definition** (what it is: its id and kind, later its name and sound) is kept apart from its **running engine** (built, loaded and silent, or sounding). The list holds definitions only; engines are built and freed off the audio thread, and the loop swaps them in, as projects open today.

The step goes in three slices, each proven with the shared Poly before the next:

- **2a (built):** the shared synth becomes instrument 1 of the project's list, and tracks on it point at it by that id. It saves, loads and plays exactly as before.
- **2b (built):** the tracks' own instruments join the list, each with its own id.
- **2c (built):** tracks get lasting ids too, so knob owners (step 4) and anything else that must survive moving tracks has something steady to point at.

### 2a as built

- **The list** (`instrument_list::InstrumentList`): the instruments, each an `InstrumentId` and a `Kind`, and the next id. In 2a it always holds one: `Kind::SharedSynth` as `InstrumentId::SHARED_SYNTH` (1), the pads' synth, whichever engine the Synth tab plays. Its sound stays in the `synth` part and it sounds on the Synth strip; its running engine is still `synth::Synth`.
- **Its part**, `instrument-list`, saved with every project:

  ```text
  mpfree-instrument-list 1
  next 2
  instrument 1 shared-synth
  ```

  `InstrumentList::parse` refuses a part with a line it can't read, an id listed twice or not below `next`, or without the shared synth as instrument 1.
- **Tracks point at it by id.** The `track-kits` part went to version 2: `track 4 instrument 1` where version 1 said `track 4 synth` (`sampler::TrackKits::text`). `project::track_kits_parse` reads both versions, each with its own word only, into `TrackPick::Kit` or `TrackPick::Instrument`. The opener turns an instrument the list has into the shared synth's entry; a track whose instrument isn't in the list plays kit 1, and the open's log says which and why (`project::tracks_play`).
- **The project's format.** `project.txt` says `format = 2` (`project::FORMAT_KEY`, `FORMAT`), written by every save and by New project. A project with no format line is format 1, saved before instruments had ids: it has no list, and its shared synth is instrument 1, as in a new project. Nothing is said for that, because nothing was lost. A format newer than this build's, a format line that doesn't read, a format 2 project with no list, or a list that doesn't read are each said in the log, and the project opens with the new project's list. The library's own first line (`mpfree-project 1`) is the folder's format and doesn't change.
- **Threads.** The list is read and made on the screen thread with the rest of the open (`project::opening`, `Opening::instrument_list`). The audio loop swaps it in whole between two periods, and its old one goes back in the box to be freed on the screen thread (`main.rs`, mirrored by the test session). A save copies it out as text with the other parts (`Collected::instrument_list`). The loop never makes or frees one.
- **What an older build does with a format 2 project:** it can't read the track kits part's version 2, says so, and plays every track on kit 1; it keeps the list as a part it doesn't know. Projects are not promised to open in older builds.
- **Tests:** `instrument_list::tests` (the new list, the counter kept, broken lists refused); `project::tests::a_project_from_before_instrument_ids_opens_and_saves_as_it_played`, `a_track_whose_instrument_is_not_listed_plays_kit_1_and_says_so`, `the_project_format_and_the_list_are_written_and_a_problem_is_said`; `sampler::tests::each_track_plays_its_own_entry_and_a_kit_lives_on_its_first_track` (the part's text); and through the whole session, `smoke::song_workflows::tests::a_project_from_before_instrument_ids_opens_saves_and_plays_the_same`: a song saved as the build before wrote it, with track 2 on the synth and track 3 on kit 2, opens from BROWSE with those tracks on them, saves as format 2 with track 2 on instrument 1, and after New project opens again with every track on what it played and the same notes.

What plays is unchanged: the track kits a project opens with are the same as before, and nothing that plays reads the list yet.

### 2b as built

- **When an own instrument has an id.** A track's own instrument (`instruments.rs`) gets the next id when it goes into a track that had none, keeps it while the track has one (a new sound or preset loaded in its place is the same instrument, as the slot it plays in is), and loses it for good when it is taken out: a new one gets a new id, never an old one. `Kind::Own { track }` says whose it is.
- **Fixed storage, so the audio loop can follow.** The list keeps an id for each of the 128 tracks (`own`) and the counter; the shared synth is always instrument 1 and needs no entry. Each period the loop calls `InstrumentList::follow(Instruments::held_tracks())`, a bit for each track that holds one (not one only ringing out an old one), and the list gives and drops ids from that alone. It never allocates, so whatever puts an instrument in or takes it out (the Synth tab's Instrument row, the builder landing one, an open) needs no change.
- **Its part** names each one's track, by id:

  ```text
  mpfree-instrument-list 1
  next 4
  instrument 1 shared-synth
  instrument 2 own track 2
  instrument 3 own track 4
  ```

  `InstrumentList::parse` also refuses a track with two, a track out of range, or instrument 1 as an own one. An older (2a) build refuses a list with own lines and says so; the instruments themselves still open there, since their sounds are where they were.
- **Their sounds stay where they were**, in the instruments part, named by track (`track 2 mpfree.fm`): a track has one at most, so the list's track tells which block is which instrument. A track that plays another's (step 4a) is saved in the track kits part by the instrument's id, so the blocks can stay by track.
- **On open** (`project::own_ids`): every track the instruments part gave an instrument keeps the id the list gives it, or gets the next one. In a project with no format line (saved before instruments had ids) that is every one, in track order, and nothing is said. In a newer one, a track's instrument with no id in the list, and an id whose track has no instrument (not in the part, or it couldn't be made), are each said, and the list is put right. A track in the track kits part pointed at another track's own instrument plays it (step 4a, below).
- **Tests:** `instrument_list::tests::a_tracks_own_instrument_keeps_its_id_and_ids_are_never_given_twice`, `the_next_id_is_kept_through_the_text`; `instruments::tests::the_list_of_instruments_follows_the_tracks_own` (with real instruments: a new sound keeps the id, out and in again is a new id); `project::tests::the_tracks_own_instruments_get_ids_that_come_back` (an old project's FM on track 2 and Poly on track 4 become instruments 2 and 3, saved and opened again), `a_list_that_disagrees_with_the_instruments_part_is_put_right_and_said`.

### 2c as built

- **`mpfree_seq::TrackId`** on every `Track` (`Track::id`), and `Project::next_track`, the id the next track gets. `TrackId::NONE` (0) is a track with none yet: one just made with `Track::new`, or read from a project saved before tracks had ids.
- **Given in one place each.** `Project::give_track_id` gives a track going in the next id (or keeps its own if no other track has it), never allocating: `Takes::add_track_as` (ADD TRACK, Rec's new track, a Loop track) and `loops::place` (a Loop track a recording makes). `Project::give_track_ids` gives every track without one, or sharing an earlier track's, the next id in track order: `TimeHost::with_project` calls it, so every track in the time engine has one. The take keeps the track with its id, so UNDO takes it away and REDO brings back the same track; a track added after the UNDO gets the next id, never its.
- **Saved in the clips part:** `nexttrack <n>` before the tracks and `id <n>` after each track's name. An older build skips both lines. A project without them gets 1, 2, 3 ... as it opens, saying nothing; one that has `nexttrack` but a track without an id, or two sharing one, gets new ones for those, said (`project::opening`).
- **Nothing reads them yet.** Tracks can't be moved or deleted from the middle today (UNDO takes away only the last added), so a track's place still names it, and the list of instruments, the track kits and the instruments part keep naming tracks by place. Step 4's knob owners are the first to use the ids (their priority), and anything that lets tracks move will switch those parts to ids as it goes in. `Project::track_with(id)` finds a track by its id.
- **Tests:** `mpfree_seq` `track_id_tests::tracks_get_lasting_ids_that_are_never_given_twice`, `save::track_id_tests::track_ids_and_the_next_id_come_back`; `mpfree_play` `takes::tests::an_added_track_has_a_lasting_id_that_undo_and_redo_keep`; `project::tests::tracks_get_lasting_ids_as_a_project_opens`; the whole-session test above also checks that an old song's tracks get 1, 2, 3 ... and keep them through a save and an open.

## Step 3: pad focus

The design's rule 2: **the pads follow the selected track until you choose for them.** Selecting a track puts its instrument on the pads, as on the MPC ("Follow track"); an instrument chosen for the pads stays there whichever track is selected ("Pads: Poly") until Follow track. Rule 1 says what never moves them: arming or selecting a track, a row of REC's list, REC.

### As built

- **`app::PadFocus`** (`app/pads_focus.rs`): `Track` (a new project's) or `Instrument(InstrumentId)`, in the app's state (`App::pads_focus`), apart from the track and the arm. Only the shared synth can be chosen yet (`PadFocus::SYNTH`, instrument 1): the Synth tab's button, while the selected track plays a kit (`App::toggle_pads_synth`). The picker of step 6 chooses others.
- **What moves it:** Follow track (the Synth tab's button again, or the header's chip, `App::pads_follow_track`); a kit picked for the selected track in the list of kits, or turned with SHIFT + the wheel (`App::kit_pick_follows_track`: a choice of what the pads play, as on the MPC, so they follow the track and play it); the arp coming on over a track that plays something else (`App::arp_ends_pads_synth`, below); a project opened (its own). **What doesn't:** `App::set_track` (selecting or arming), a row of REC's list (`desk.rs`, `ArmTrack`), REC (`App::rec_synth_on` keeps it).
- **How the pads play it** (`main.rs`, `preview_on`, mirrored by the test session): once a period, after the kit has followed the armed track, the loop reconciles. While the synth is chosen and the selected track plays something else (a kit, its own instrument), pad hits go to the shared synth on their own, before the arp and the recorder, as step 1's preview did; while the track plays the shared synth itself, they play it the usual way, through the track (the arp, the recorder, the synth's layout). The pads' colours follow, sent once. A pad held across a change lets go where it started.
- **REC** (step 1's rule 3): the desk's `rec_synth` acts only while the armed track plays something else. It points a track nobody set up at the shared synth (`Effect::SynthOnTrack`) and arms it; the pads keep the synth. The press comes again next period, when the armed track plays the pads' synth (`App::plays_pads_synth`, from `TrackKits::on_synth` each period), so it is REC's own, as is every later press there: a second take goes on the same track, not on another free one.
- **The arp:** it plays through the selected track, so while the synth plays on its own it can't play the pads: the button won't choose the synth while the arp plays them, and the arp coming on gives the pads back to the track, said. Over a track that plays the synth the arp plays it, and the pads keep it. The arp by instrument is for a later step (MIDI and scripts play instruments).
- **On screen:** the header's **Pads: Poly** chip on every page while the synth is chosen (warn, the shared synth's name from the list of kits; a tap is Follow track, `FromUi::FollowTrack`); Play's transport line reads "Pads: Poly · C3 to D#4" (`PadsPlay::chosen`) whichever track Play's rows show; the Synth tab's button reads **Follow track** over "The pads play Poly on every track". The Synth tab still shows the selected track's own instrument when it has one; which instrument the tab edits is the picker's (step 6).
- **Saved** in the `pads` part, written with every save:

  ```text
  mpfree-pads 1
  play instrument 1
  ```

  (`play track` while the pads follow the track). No part (New project, or a project saved before) follows the track, saying nothing; a part that doesn't read (another first line, a line it doesn't know, two `play` lines, none, or an instrument other than the shared synth) is said, and the pads follow the track (`App::open_pads`). An older build keeps it as a part it doesn't know.
- **Tests:** `app::tests::the_synth_chosen_for_the_pads_stays_until_follow_track`, `the_pads_focus_is_saved_and_a_part_that_doesnt_read_is_said`, `the_arp_and_the_synth_on_the_pads_never_play_apart`; `desk::tests::rec_while_the_pads_play_the_synth_records_it_on_a_track_nobody_set_up` (the second press records on the same track); `gui::tests::the_synth_chosen_for_the_pads_is_named_and_follow_track_gives_them_back` (the chip, the button, Play's transport, the chip's tap); `project::tests::what_the_pads_play_is_kept_and_comes_back`; `sampler::tests` (`TrackKits::on_synth`); and through the whole session, the design's proof, `smoke::workflows::tests::the_pads_play_poly_while_a_drum_clip_plays_on_whichever_track_is_selected` (a drum clip plays on Track 1 while the pads play Poly with Track 1, Track 3 and, from REC's list, Track 2 armed: every hit on the synth, none on a kit or a track, no track or clip changed; then a kit picked and the chip give the pads back), and `smoke::song_workflows::tests::the_synth_chosen_for_the_pads_comes_back_with_the_project` (saved, New project follows the track, opened again with Poly on the pads).

## Step 4: tracks send to instruments

A track's output is any instrument: several tracks share one, and the pads and a clip can play one Poly together. The step goes in slices: **4a** a track plays another track's own instrument; **4b** knob owners (each knob has one owner at a time, by a fixed priority over lasting track ids; the hand wins while it touches the knob); **4c** Duplicate and New instrument track; **4d** instrument channels in the mixer, after the audit of its strips.

### 4a as built

- **A send** (`Instruments::set_send`): a track with no instrument of its own plays another track's. Its clip's notes, the pads and the arp while it is selected, a keyboard's keys, pedals, wheel and pressure, and its clips' locks all go to that instrument (`Instruments::owner`, `Instruments::plays`); the sound stays in the instrument's own track's channel, so the track that sends needs no channel or slot of its own. `holds`, `held`, `node` and `stats` stay about a track's own instrument, so what saves, counts places (16 at once) and shows channels is unchanged. A send to a track with no instrument, to itself, or from a track that has its own is refused (`SendRefused`), so there are no chains.
- **Notes end where they started.** The notes a track's pads started, and the keys it holds by number, are kept by the track that played them, not by the slot (`started`, `keys`, fixed arrays made with the host). Two tracks on one instrument each end their own, even on the same pad moved apart by their transposes. A send that moves or ends first ends the notes the track left sounding on the old instrument. A panic on a track that sends ends only its notes; on the instrument's own track, every note on it.
- **The shared synth too** (`synth::Player`): the notes its pads start are kept by who started them, the pads straight on it (`Player::Pads`: the synth chosen for the pads, a step's or scale row's audition) or a track (`Player::Track`: its clip, or the pads through its arp), in fixed tables made with the synth (`Synth::note_on_shifted`, `note_off_by`, `pressure_at`). Two Synth-kit tracks on one pad, or a track and the pads, each end their own; a note two of them hold ends with the last. A track's clip and the pads through the same track still share its row, as before: telling them apart is step 5's sources. The pad's own pressure still reaches every note held on that pad. Found by the step sequencer refactor's review (20 Nov 2026).
- **What ends a send:** taking the instrument out (Kit on its track: its id is gone for good, so the tracks that sent to it play their kits again, said: "Track 3 plays its kit again: Track 2's instrument is gone"); the track getting its own instrument (`Instruments::put` ends its send); Kit on the track itself; a project opening (`Instruments::clear_sends`, then the project's). A new sound or preset in the instrument keeps its sends, as it keeps its id.
- **Locks** from a track that sends move the instrument it plays, kept under its own track (`lock_key(owner, id)`), so two tracks on one knob share the value under it; which of them moves it is 4b's knob owners (below).
- **On screen:** the Synth tab's Instrument row offers the other tracks' own instruments right after Kit ("Track 3's FM", `SoundChoice::Track`, `app::sent_name`), then the engines and plugin synths for one of its own. A pick sends `Effect::TrackSend`; the loop takes the track's own instrument out first (its senders go back to their kits, said) and sets the send (`send_track`, main.rs and the test session). The Synth tab, its presets, a plugin's skin and the knobs then show and edit that instrument (`instrument_track`), titled with its own track. Arrange writes "Track 3's FM" under the track, the Pads tab's Kit row reads "Track 3's FM, no kit" (`ToUi::TrackSends`, `gui::played`), and the app keeps a copy of every send (`App::set_track_sends`). The pad focus button counts a track that sends as one that plays a synth already.
- **Saved** in the track kits part, beside the track's kit (which plays again if the send ends), by the instrument's id in the project's list:

  ```text
  mpfree-track-kits 2
  track 6 kit 3
  track 6 instrument 2
  ```

  (`project::track_kits_text`; read into `Opening::sends`, set by the loop once the project's instruments are in, `open_sends`). A send to an instrument the list hasn't got (its sound wasn't made) plays the kit, said; one to the track's own instrument is said and left out. A step 2 or 3 build reads `track 6 instrument 2` as an instrument it can't play from another track, says so and plays the kit.
- **Not yet:** an export's stem of a track that sends plays in the instrument's track's channel (step 8 makes stems by instrument); muting a track that sends ends its notes but not the instrument's own (mute scopes are step 8); the pads can be chosen to play only the shared synth (the picker is step 6).
- **Tests:** `instruments::tests::a_track_plays_another_tracks_instrument_into_that_tracks_channel`, `a_send_is_refused_where_it_cant_play`, `two_tracks_on_one_instrument_each_end_their_own_notes`, `a_send_that_moves_ends_its_notes_where_they_started`, `a_new_sound_keeps_the_sends_and_taking_it_out_ends_them`, `a_track_given_its_own_instrument_stops_sending`, `a_panic_on_a_sending_track_ends_only_its_notes`, `a_lock_from_a_sending_track_moves_the_instrument_it_plays`; `synth::tests::two_tracks_on_one_pad_each_end_their_own_notes`; `app::tests::the_instrument_row_plays_another_tracks_instrument`; `project::tests::a_track_that_plays_another_tracks_instrument_is_kept_and_comes_back`; and through the whole session, `smoke::song_workflows::tests::a_track_plays_another_tracks_instrument_and_the_project_keeps_it` (Night Market's Track 3 picks "Track 2's Poly", Arrange names it, saved by id, back after New project, and Kit on Track 2 gives Track 3 its kit, said).

### 4b as built

Jay's answer to question 5: each knob has one owner at a time, chosen by a fixed priority, never by timing; when the owner stops the next takes over the way the knob allows; the hand wins while it touches the knob.

- **Where:** `locks::Under`, the table the shared synth (`Synth::lock`) and the tracks' own instruments (`Instruments::lock`) already kept the value under each lock in, now with each track's lock (128 at once per table, beside its 64 parameters). A lock comes with the track whose clip played it (`Owner::Inst(t)`; `Synth::lock` takes the track now). Nothing allocates or locks on the audio thread.
- **The owner** is the locking track with the lowest lasting id (`mpfree_seq::TrackId`, step 2c), handed to both tables every period by the loop (`track_ranks`, `set_ranks`); a track without one comes after every track with one, then by place. It doesn't change when tracks move, and timing never decides. The owner's locks play with their exact value and timing, a better-placed track's first lock taking the knob on its own frame; every other lock is kept with its value (`Under::waited` counts them), not played.
- **When the owner stops**, the next owner's value comes back: on a smooth knob by a glide of 30 ms (`GLIDE_S`, run once a block by `Under::tick` before the instrument plays), on a stepped one (`Curve::Stepped`, `Choice`, `Toggle`: `locks::glides`) at once on the end's frame. The last lock's end gives the value under them back on its frame, as before, and stops any glide.
- **The hand** (`Under::hand`: the Synth tab's knobs, a learned control, UNDO of a turn) is the value under the locks from then on, and holds the knob for 0.5 s after its last move (`HAND_S`), even with no lock on it yet: a lock then is kept, not played, and a lock's end sets nothing. When the hand lets go, the owner takes the knob back the same way, gliding or at once. The Q-Links' touch sensor (`qlink_touch.rs`) doesn't hold it longer yet: the rule for a controller that can't sense touch (while it moves and a moment after) holds for every hand now.
- **Counted on the status line** (added with the strip locks): the locks kept waiting and any lost to a full table, `Under::describe`, at the end of the synth's part and of the tracks' own instruments' part (`synth_status`, `Instruments::describe`). They were counted from the start but not written anywhere.
- **Not yet:** the screen doesn't yet say that a locked knob is shared (the design's last line on this; it belongs with step 6's instrument card); priorities you can set are for later, the lasting id is the written-down stopgap.
- **Tests:** `locks::tests::two_tracks_on_one_knob_take_turns_by_their_lasting_ids` (whichever locks first, the lower id owns; the next owner at once on a stepped knob, by a glide on a smooth one; the last end gives the value under back and stops a glide; a track without an id last), `the_hand_holds_a_knob_then_gives_it_back_to_its_owner`, and the earlier three on the value under; `instruments::tests::two_tracks_locking_one_knob_take_turns_by_their_lasting_ids` (Poly on track 2 played by track 5 too: track 5's lower id owns, its end glides back to track 2's value, the last end gives the sound's own) and `a_lock_from_a_sending_track_moves_the_instrument_it_plays`; `synth::tests::a_clips_lock_plays_on_its_frame_and_its_end_puts_the_sound_back` (a turn holds the knob, then the lock takes it back by the glide).


### 4c as built (Duplicate)

- **Duplicate** is the Instrument row's **Copy of Track 3's FM**, right after each other track's own instrument (`SoundChoice::Copy`, `App::copy_track_sound`): the selected track gets an instrument of its own with the same engine and the sound it has now (its values and extras, read from the running node by `sound_of_track`, main.rs), made by the builder as a new sound is (`Effect::TrackCopy`, `instrument_job`). It goes in as any instrument does (`Instruments::put`), so it gets its own slot, channel and lasting id, and a send from the track ends; a track with its own already takes the copy's sound in place and keeps the tracks that play it, as a new sound does. The preset name comes with it (`TrackPresets::copy`). The screen says "Track 4 plays its own copy of Track 3's FM: change one and the other stays" (`copied_note`). It counts among the 16 at once: with no room the row says so and changes nothing (`App::no_room_for`).
- **The row stays on it** while both play the same engine (`App::copied_from`), so stepping back from a copy goes on to the send and Kit rather than round the copy again; it reads "Copy of Track 3's FM". The mark isn't saved: an open forgets it (`App::forget_copies`), and the row then reads "FM".

### 4d audit: the mixer's strips (20 Nov 2026)

What the mixer gives instruments today, read from the code before building instrument channels:

- **Room.** The mixer has 132 track strips, not the 160 the plan counted: the engines' 4 (Drums, Synth, Loop, Audio) and one held for each of the 128 sequencer tracks (`MixHost::new`, `tracks: TRACKS + instruments::TRACKS`). There are no spare ones. The pads have 16 strips plus 48 to lend, and there is one input. Giving the 16 loaded instruments strips of their own means growing the mixer to 148 (the parameter addresses, `mpfree_param::Strip::Track(u8)`, have room), with the strip count's cost checked against the instruction gate.
- **Where each instrument sounds.** A track's own instrument: that track's channel (`Instruments::channel`; track 1's is the Drums strip, or its old own channel in a project saved with settings there). The shared synth: the Synth strip. Kit 1: the pad channels into Drums; another kit: the channel of the first track that plays it (`TrackKits::homes`). A track that plays another track's instrument (4a): the owner's channel, and it holds none of its own. MIDI out: none. So each loaded instrument already has exactly one channel, its holder track's, and a track that only sends notes holds no audio channel, which the plan wanted either way.
- **What the Mix tab shows.** The engines' strips, then a track's channel only while in use (its own instrument, or a kit it homes), named "Track 3" whatever plays there (`mixing::name`). Most other screens name strips the same way (the chain editor's strip list, the Looper's Records from, Perform's On, the undo lines); export's stems by channel use the track's own name.
- **Saved.** The mixer part keeps each strip that isn't plain by its strip number, with a `track1` line for where track 1 plays (`project::mixer_text_lent`). Instruments are saved per track, with lasting ids (step 2). Opening an old project puts every setting back where it was.
- **Locks.** A clip's lock on "this track's strip" reaches its own track's channel (`OnTrack::Strip`, `track_strip`). On a track that plays another track's instrument that channel is not in use, so such a lock changes nothing you hear; its instrument locks do reach the instrument (4a). (Now it follows the instrument: "Strip locks follow the instrument" below.)
- **Stems and takes.** A stem or a frozen take of a track's channel holds its instrument, with every track that plays it; a track that sends has no stem of its own.

**What follows** (asked of Jay, 20 Nov 16:20Z; his reviewer's answer 16:36Z, which this follows): the strips stay where they are and nothing moves. 160 is the mixer's most (`mpfree_fx::mixer::MAX_TRACKS`) and 132 are built, so the 16 strips instruments without a track will need (step 6) fit under it. Now: each instrument's strip is named for it, and where an instrument sounds is read from the instrument, not worked out from its track (below). A separate slice makes "this track's strip" locks on a track that plays another's instrument reach that instrument's strip: only those relative targets follow (a named strip and the synth's own knobs stay put), the lock is resolved where its track is still known and ends on the strip it began on, and two tracks on one strip follow the knob owners' rule (4b).

### Strip locks follow the instrument (as built)

The slice the reviewer asked for (20 Nov 16:36Z; Jay's go 16:47Z): a clip's "this track's strip" reaches the strip the track is heard on.

- **Where it is resolved.** In the time engine's audio half, as each lock leaves the player (`TimeAudio::send_to_mixer`), where the track it came from is still known (`Out::track`); the mod engine further on sees only the address. The host says where a track is heard (`time::Pads::sounds_on`; `SeqPads` asks `Instruments::heard_on`, the strip of the instrument the track plays, its own or the one it sends to). `time::strips::StripLocks` keeps each lock with the strip it went to.
- **Only "this track's strip".** A lock is moved when it names the playing track's own strip (`Owner::Strip(track_strip(t))`), which is how a clip recorded on that track names it (`Target::for_track`). The player gives no other sign of a relative target, and a fixed target only names the playing track's strip when a clip was copied onto the track whose strip it names, where the two mean the same strip. Fixed targets elsewhere (the master, a submix, a return, another track's strip, the old projects' rewritten lanes, which name Synth, Loop, Audio or a strip four tracks down) go on as they came. So do locks on a strip's effects (`OnTrack::Insert`): an effect's number is its own strip's, so the same number on the instrument's strip would be another effect. They stay on the track's own strip, where they change nothing heard, as before.
- **A lock ends where it began.** The end goes to the strip the lock went to, even when the instrument moved in between. A new value for a lock still held after the instrument moved (a move's next point, the next locked step) ends it on the old strip first, then starts it on the new one.
- **Two tracks on one strip take turns** by the knob owners' rule (4b): the lowest lasting id owns the control (`TrackSet::id`, sent to the audio half with the tracks' settings), and the others' locks are kept, not sent. The instrument's own track's locks on its strip are in the same line. When the owner's lock ends, the next owner's value is sent as a lock (the mixer smooths its levels over 4 ms, so there is no glide of our own); when the last ends, the control is let go. A hand on the control is the mod engine's, as before.
- **Not moved:** a track that plays a kit another track plays first, or the shared synth, is heard on that kit's home channel or the Synth strip, and its "this track's strip" still reaches its own strip, which carries nothing. The same rule could take them in (the host would say where they are heard); it is left for the step that gives kits and the synth their instrument cards (step 6), since it would change what the many tracks that share kit 1 do today.
- **Counted:** locks moved, kept waiting, and the ones that found the table (256, the player's own most) full and went on as they came, on the time engine's status line (`StripCounts`, sent to the control half when they change, `ToControl::Strips`, as the clock-follow counts are; the period-end message had no room left under its 128 bytes).
- **Real-time safe:** fixed tables, boxed once with the audio half; the end-to-end test counts no allocation.
- **Tests:** `time::strips::tests` (moved and ended there, only the track's own strip, a reroute mid-lock, turns by lasting id, no id last, a full table), `time::tests::a_lock_on_this_tracks_strip_goes_where_its_instrument_sounds` (from a clip, with an effect lock that stays, the counts on the status line), `instruments::tests::a_track_is_heard_where_the_instrument_it_plays_sounds`, `rt_safety::a_lock_on_this_tracks_strip_moves_the_strip_its_instrument_sounds_on` (track 5 playing track 3's Poly moves track 3's strip's volume and lets it go, track 5's strip untouched, no allocation).
