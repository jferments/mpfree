# Export: mixdown, stems and MIDI file

**Status:** 2026-11-14. Built and tested: the engine, [`rust/mpfree-export`](../../../rust/mpfree-export) (the pass, Fast or Listen, the files), on `mpfree_fx::bounce` ([fx.md](fx.md#bouncing-the-exports-mixdown-and-stems)) and `mpfree_midi::smf` ([midi-io.md](midi-io.md)); and the app's part (the Export button and panel in BROWSE, Prefs > Export, the pass in the sound loop, the notes for the MIDI file), which the FX thread wrote with the app thread's agreement (14 Nov). Listen runs in a test of the sound loop's steps; Fast, quiet stops and restarts the app's own sound card stream, which has not run on the MPC yet. The file formats (16-bit with dither, 32-bit float, 48 kHz; Jay's pick "Both" of 14 Nov 07:51Z: Prefs sets them, the panel changes them for one export) are built and tested too, and so are stems by track (Jay 14 Nov 15:16Z, "one stem per track", and his pick of 15:20Z, "Yes, a Stems by row": by track the default, by channel kept): [below](#stems-by-track-replays). The plan Jay reviewed is the doc "Export plan: mixdown, stems and MIDI file". The musician's page is [Exporting your music](../../user/src/making-music/exporting.md).

## What an export makes

One pass over the song makes three things, lined up to the sample, in one new folder:

| File | What is in it | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Mixdown | The master after Master safety: what the speakers get | Stereo WAV in the export's format ([below](#file-formats); 24-bit at the session's rate unless changed), not normalised |
| Stems | By track (the default): one file per sequencer track that sounds and per audio track that sounds, then the Loop and each return; by channel: one per engine track (Drums, Synth, Loop, Audio), per track instrument in use and per return. Each after its strip's fader, pan and mute, without Master safety. A stem that never went above about -90 dBFS is left out (the mixdown never is) | One stereo WAV each in the same format, all the same start and length |
| MIDI file | The notes and controls each track played in that pass, as the player sent them (quantise, swing, chance and ratchets as they came out), with the tempo map and meter | Standard MIDI File type 1, 960 ticks a quarter |

- **Range.** The arrangement from its start to its end, or, with no arrangement, the clips playing now for a number of bars (8 by default).
- **Tail.** After the range, the pass plays on until every file has been silent (below about -90 dBFS) for 2 s, so a slow delay's next echo isn't lost, and at most 10 s. That silence isn't kept: the files are cut where the last sound ends, never inside the range.
- **Where.** A new folder in the library's Recordings, named after the project and the time ("Night drive 2026-11-14 01.32", UTC as the project's own names are; " 2" and on when taken), with the MIDI file named after the project. Names are made safe for a FAT or exFAT stick: no `/ \ : * ? " < > |`, and no two files that differ only in case (a second "Drums" is "Drums 2").
- **Cancel or failure** (Cancel, STOP before the range's end, the stick fills or can't keep up, or a capture point's latency changes because a look-ahead effect went in or out) stops the export and removes its files; the panel says why.

## Fast or Listen

Jay's decision (14 Nov 2026, 01:32Z): build the listening mode first, then the fast quiet mode, make fast and quiet the default, let Prefs switch between them, and switch to listening by itself when the song needs outside gear.

- **Fast, quiet** (the default): the sound loop runs the song as fast as the processor allows, the speakers quiet. Estimated, not yet measured on the MPC One: about 2 to 3 times faster than real time for a typical song, close to real time for one that already works it hard.
- **Listen**: the song plays in real time (each pass, with replays) and you hear it as it records, faders and all.
- **When an export listens anyway** (`mpfree_export::choose`, from a `Rig` the sound loop fills in): a track plays outside gear over MIDI, the line input is on, the CV or gate jacks are in use, MIDI clock goes out, the tempo follows MIDI clock coming in, part of the sound is made on another computer (offload), or the song has plugins (until the plugin host can wait for a late block instead of dropping it). The panel says which, in words (`Why::words`).

## File formats

Jay's pick on the card "Where should the export file format be picked?" (14 Nov 07:51Z): **Both**. Prefs sets the format every export starts from, and the Export panel's File row changes it for one export.

| Choice | What the writer does | For |
|---|---|---|
| 24-bit (the default) | Rounded and clipped at full scale, no dither | Nearly everything |
| 16-bit | Triangular dither of up to one step each way, a seed per file, then rounded and clipped | CDs, older gear |
| 32-bit float | The mixer's floats as they are, over full scale kept (IEEE float WAV with a `fact` chunk, 58-byte header) | Stems that go over full scale before the master's limiter |
| 44.1 kHz (the default) | As the mixer made it | |
| 48 kHz | Converted on the writer thread ([fx.md](fx.md#bouncing-the-exports-mixdown-and-stems), `mpfree_fx::resample`), every file still starting on the song's first frame | Video |

The sound thread pushes the same float blocks whatever the format, so a format can't make the pass drop a block; only the writer works harder. On the build host the writer makes 17 streams at 82 times real time as 24-bit, 64 times as 16-bit and 11 to 12 times at 48 kHz, so a 48 kHz export in Fast, quiet will be held to the writer's pace on the MPC sooner than a 44.1 kHz one (not yet measured there).

## Stems by track: replays

Jay's decision (14 Nov 15:16Z, in the demo songs thread): "Yes, definitely one stem per track then"; and his pick on the card "Keep per-channel stems as a choice next to per-track stems?" (15:20Z): "Yes, a Stems by row". The panel's **Stems by** row picks Track (the default) or Channel (the stems above, one pass, adding up exactly to the mix); the last choice is kept with the rest. That row is in the layout spec's section 3.

A track's stem by track comes from (`export::track_stems`):

- **its own strip** in the first pass: a track with its own instrument (`StripId::Track(TRACKS + k)`), or the only track on the pads, or the only audio track that sounds;
- **a replay** (`mpfree_export::Replay`): the range played again with only that track playing into the strip it shares: each track on the pads (the drum kit's strip, or the synth's while the pads play the synth) when two or more are, and each audio track that sounds (the Audio strip) when two or more do;
- **nothing**: a track on outside gear (MIDI Out), a muted one, one with no notes in any clip. A frozen pads or Audio strip plays its take whatever the tracks do, so it gets one stem, named after the strip, in the first pass.

The Loop and the returns follow the tracks, from the first pass. Audio tracks play only in song mode, so a range that doesn't play song mode gives them no replay (`Facts::track_count` counts each range for the panel, which says how many passes it takes).

How a replay plays the same notes as the first pass:

- **Only that track:** while a replay plays, `Host::only` names its track. For a sequencer track, `SeqPads` takes every other track's note-on, note-off and pressure on the pads (`export::quiets`) so nothing plays or records it; panics, parameter locks and lanes play as in the first pass, and so do tracks with their own instrument and on MIDI Out, so an effect keyed from another strip hears what it heard. For an audio track, `AudioTracks::set_only` keeps the others quiet from the replay's first frame (their gains are set to 0 at once rather than ramped).
- **The same chance rolls:** with more than one pass, `Host::begin` anchors the player's chance rolls to the frame the transport starts on (`Player::anchor_dice`, through `TimeHost::anchor_dice`), so each pass rolls the same; the trig conditions already follow the song position. `main.rs` reseeds every track's arp as each pass begins (`TrackArps::reseed`).
- **The same start:** `Host::begin` clears the mixer (`Mixer::reset`: delay lines, smoothers, free-running LFOs back to their start) before attaching each pass, and every pass starts rolling on a period's first frame.
- What can still differ: round-robin sample picks and an analog-style synth's drift, which keep their own state; and an effect on a shared strip that reacts to everything in it (a compressor on the kit) hears one track at a time, so those stems don't add up exactly to the mix, as on the MPC. With the tempo following MIDI clock in, each pass follows the clock as it comes.

The engine's side (`mpfree-export`): `Plan::replays` lists them after the first pass's `streams`; `start` opens every file (names safe and different across all of them) and starts the first pass. Each pass that comes back goes to `Job::next`, which waits for its writer, keeps what its files gave, and makes the next replay's pass (one stream, its own bounce and writer thread, `Pass::only`, `Pass::index`, `Pass::passes`), which the screen sends to the sound loop as it sent the first (`Next::Play`); `Next::Finish` gives the pass back for `Job::finish` after the last, or after one that didn't play to its end. `finish` makes every file as long as the longest pass's sound (cut at the tail's silence, never into the range); a replay whose sound died sooner gets silence added, and none is made longer than a pass whose tail was cut at 10 s still sounding (`capped`), since each pass's look-ahead differs. A cancel or failure in any pass removes every file. `Progress` says the pass ("1:12 of 3:50 · pass 2 of 6") and `Progress::part` gives each pass an equal share of the bar.

## The engine

[`rust/mpfree-export`](../../../rust/mpfree-export):

- **`Plan`**: the folder (new or empty), the song's name, the files' `format` (`mpfree_fx::WavFormat`), the streams (a mixer capture point and a WAV file each: `Stream::mixdown()`, `Stream::stem(name, strip)`), the MIDI file's track names, tempo map, meter and end (`MidiPlan`), the mode, the rate, the range's length when known (for the time left), and the first of the mixer's capture slots the export may use.
- **`start(plan)`**, off the sound thread: makes the folder and the files, the bounce's rings (2 s a file) and a writer thread (`mpfree-export`, emptying the rings every 20 ms and syncing the files to the stick at the end). It gives back two halves.
- **`Pass`**, the sound thread's, which never allocates:
  - `attach(&mut mixer)` sets the export's capture points and takes their latencies (once the mixer has played, as the app's always has). A refused capture point fails the pass at once and leaves none set.
  - `period(&mixer, frames, ended)` after the mixer each period: checks every capture point's latency against the one `attach` took (a change fails the pass with `Failure::Latency`, since the stems would no longer line up), then copies every capture point's block into its ring. `ended` says the range is over; the pass then plays the tail and says `Done` once the range's last frames have reached every stream and the tail is silent or 10 s long. `Cancelled` when Cancel was pressed (`Job::cancel`, or `Pass::cancel` from the sound thread); `Failed` when blocks were lost or a capture point was refused.
  - `note(tick, track, msg)` logs a message the player sent (`mpfree_midi::smf::NoteLog`, fixed room, the overflow counted).
  - `wait_for_room(frames, most)`, in fast mode only, before each block: waits for the writer, so a fast pass never loses a block. A pass in real time is paced by the sound card and never waits.
  - `detach(&mut mixer)` frees the capture points; the pass then goes back to the screen.
- **`Job`**, the screen's: `progress()` ("1:12 of 3:50", "3:52 · the tail"), `cancel()`, `next(pass)` for the replays ([above](#stems-by-track-replays)), and `finish(pass)`, which waits for the writer, cuts the tail's silence (at the files' own rate: `WavFormat::frames_for` turns the frames to keep at the mixer's rate into file frames, and the cut uses the format's header and frame sizes), removes the stems that never sounded (`BounceOut::heard`), writes the MIDI file (only when notes played) and gives the `Outcome` with a line for the panel that names the format ("3 WAV files (24-bit, 48 kHz) and a MIDI file").

## The app's part

- **Prefs > Export** (`Settings::export_mode`, saved as `export fast|listen`): Fast, quiet | Listen, a row before Touch screen (layout spec section 5, Jay's ask of 14 Nov 01:32Z).
- **Prefs > Export file and Export rate** (`Settings::export_bits` and `export_rate`, saved as `export-file 24|16|float` and `export-rate 44100|48000`; a rate the list doesn't hold is ignored): the format each export starts from, rows after Export (layout spec section 5, Jay's pick of 14 Nov 07:51Z). `export::panel_format` turns them into the panel's `ExportFormat`, and `export::wav_format` the panel's choice into the plan's `WavFormat`.
- **The Export button and panel** ([`mpfree-browser-ui/src/export.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-browser-ui/src/export.rs), layout spec section 3, Jay's pick on 14 Nov 01:39Z): Export in the Projects place sends `BrowseAction::ExportAsked`; the panel ticks Mixdown, Stems and MIDI file, picks Stems by (Track or Channel, with how many passes it takes), the range (Arrangement, or 1 to 999 bars) and the File row (`ExportFormat`: 24-bit, 16-bit or 32-bit float, 44.1 or 48 kHz, starting from Prefs each time it opens), says how it will play and where, and Start sends `ExportStart(choice)`. While it runs it shows the time done with Cancel (`ExportCancel`); when it is over, the outcome with Show in BROWSE. The panel remembers the last choice while the app runs. BROWSE hides it and a running export carries on.
- **The facts and the plan** ([`mpfree-app/src/export.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/export.rs)): `FromUi::ExportAsk` and `FromUi::Export` are the audio loop's own (`Desk::screen` hands them back, since they need MIDI out, CV out and the card). On `ExportAsk` the sound loop fills in the `Rig` and calls `Facts::gather` (the rate, the arrangement's end, the playhead, the meter and tempo map, the track names and the stem strips, from the time engine and the mixer), and sends the facts to the screen, which opens the panel. The smoke tests' session can call `Facts::gather` with `Rig::default()`. On Start the screen makes the `Plan` (`Facts::plan`: the streams, the MIDI file's tracks and tempo map from the range's start, the range's length), calls `mpfree_export::start` and sends the `Pass` to the sound loop in a `Run`.
- **The pass in the sound loop** (`export::Host`): `begin` (with replays, after clearing the mixer and anchoring the chance rolls) attaches the pass, remembers the transport (song mode, loop, position), stops it, clears the loop, sets song mode for the arrangement, locates to the range's start and plays. Each period after the mixer, `notes` logs what the player sent (`Tap`, below) at its tick from the range's start, and `period` hands the block to the pass with `ended` once the playhead reaches the range's end. In the period before the range ends, the host forecasts the frame of the range's end from the block's last span (`Span::frame_of`) and queues STOP on it (`TimeHost::command_at`), so the song stops exactly there: the clips play nothing after the range and the MIDI file gets nothing after it, while what still sounds (a release, an echo, a reverb) rings out as the tail. A transport that doesn't roll within 400 periods, or stops before the end (STOP), cancels the pass. When it is over the host detaches it, puts the transport back as it was and sends the `Run` to the screen, which sends the next replay's pass back (`Job::next`) or calls `Job::finish` and shows the outcome. A second export while one runs is refused.
- **Fast mode in the sound loop:** while a Fast pass runs, the sound card stream is dropped (`Playback::pause`, so no writes and no underruns), each period waits for the writer's room instead of the card (`Pass::wait_for_room`, at most 5 s, with a 1 ms rest every 64 periods so the screen and MIDI threads get the cores), and the lanes on other cores are given 250 ms instead of the card's deadline, so a slow lane is waited for rather than dropped. When the pass is over the stream is prepared and refilled with silence (`Playback::resume`), as the underrun recovery does.
- **The MIDI file's notes** (`export::Tap`): each event the player sends a track (a clip's note, a pad as its note, the arp and live keys) goes through a `midi_out::Chords` as MIDI Out would send it, into a log with fixed room (1024 events a period), which `notes` hands to `Pass::note`.
