## What it is for

Four composing tools share one engine and one set of controls:

- **Continue** carries a clip on for 1 to 16 bars, in the note editor, on Steps and from the clip card.
- **Add a part** listens to a row of the clip grid and plays a bass, chords, a melody or drums with it.
- **Improvise**, a fifth tab on the Arp panel, plays a line over a held chord.
- **Answer** replies to a phrase when you stop playing.

There is no trained model: the engine is procedural. It reads what was played (key, chords, groove, motifs), plans what each bar is for, then picks chords, then each phrase's rhythm and notes, then how they are played. Every result is heard before anything is written, and Keep writes it as one take that UNDO takes back.

## Two crates

- **`mpfree-context`** holds the Musical Context's types: one reading of what was played, made once and read by every stage. It is its own crate so that other features can use the key, chords or groove later. Nothing else in mpfree changes for it until one does.
- **`mpfree-compose`** is the composer: a pure library with no screen and no sound. A result depends only on what it was given, the settings and a seed.

Both are plain data and functions. Nothing here runs on the audio thread; from phase 0C the composer runs as an idle-priority background job with a fixed budget per request.

## The Musical Context

- **Key** (`key.rs`): 72 keys (12 tonics, six modes). A `KeyReading` keeps up to three candidates with their odds, used inside for ranking only. The screen speaks in words: "Likely D minor, also possible A minor, not sure yet", "C minor, fairly sure", "C minor", or "No clear key", which is an answer in its own right. Fewer than four different notes never read better than "not sure yet". The lines between the words (`FAIRLY_SURE`, `SURE`, `NO_CLEAR_KEY`, `TIE`) are provisional until phase 0C checks them against labelled melodies; until then no number is shown.
- **Chords** (`chord.rs`): 12 qualities on 12 roots. A `ChordTimeline` is spans with their own start and length from tick 0, with no gaps; a span may last a beat or the whole clip (drones), and may say "no clear chord". Each span says whether it was heard, suggested (shown dashed) or set by the musician.
- **The harmony question** (`harmony.rs`): every part asks "what is the harmony here?" through the `Harmony` trait. A `Resolver` answers from the first source that applies: the chord held (live tools), then in song mode the song section's chords, on the clip grid the row's Harmony line, then suggested chords, else no clear chord. A row's chords run for the row's length (as `mpfree_seq::scene_length` defines it) and a line shorter than the row repeats; `row_tick` places a shorter clip's notes on each pass, so a two-bar bass in an eight-bar row meets four stretches of chords.
- **Groove** (`groove.rs`): played drums as residual timing. See the timing contract below.
- **The listener** (`listen.rs`, phase 0C): notes read as a key and a chord timeline, with the second prototype's numbers. Every note is weighted by how long it sounds and where it starts (half as much again on a bar line, a fifth more on a beat) and compared with the six modes in all 12 keys; the home note is picked by the cues a player uses (the last note, the first strong note, the lowest and longest, each bar's first note, a note held at a bar's end that is longer than the bar's other notes, and an opening on the home chord's three notes, such as F A C in F major), the evidence is capped at 24 weighted notes, and a mode prior favours major and minor (Phrygian and Lydian least). A note played up to a 1/32 off a bar line or a beat counts as on it, so a player's loose timing keeps its cues. Over more than four bars each bar's cues share four bars' weight, so a long clip's many downbeats don't outvote the rest, and when the budget cut the reading short its last note says nothing about home. Chords are a semi-Markov search over beats: every span from one beat to the whole of what was read, against 144 chord shapes and a 145th, no chord, scored by the notes that fit, the notes that don't, the chord notes missing and the lowest note on the root, plus how long chords usually last. A single line (hardly two notes at once) only implies its chords, so there a power chord, a sus4, a 6th or a diminished chord costs more, as does a chord outside the key (but a minor key's major V), and a tune's lowest note (its middle note G3 or higher) is not taken as a root. A beat's rest stays inside its chord; a bar of silence, or notes that fit no chord (`NO_CLEAR_CHORD`), is no clear chord. `chord_of` names the one chord a handful of notes make. The tests hear the prototype's examples as it did: D F A is "Dm" and "Likely D minor, also possible D Dorian, not sure yet" (since the opening cue; A minor shares its notes but not its opening), the riff "C minor, fairly sure", the Dorian tune "D Dorian, fairly sure", and Cm7 F7 | Bbmaj7 Ebmaj7 | Am7b5 D7 | Gm6 exactly. A full reading (16 bars, 2,048 notes) takes about 6.5 ms on one desktop core; MPC timings come with the rest of 0C.
- **The context** (`context.rs`): the key, chords, groove, the pitch classes played, motifs (their place only, until 0C), and every clip read with its `Revision`, so a stale reading is never used (`is_stale`). `ReadBudget` caps what a reading reads. The context only compares revisions; the composer makes them (see "Previews and Keep" below).

`read` (`read.rs`, phase 0C) reads clips into a Musical Context as they play: notes after quantise and speed, a shorter clip looping over the longest as a row plays them; clips of notes give the key and chords, drum clips the groove (heard with playback's swing, which the template then takes out); every clip goes in with its revision, and a pitched part made of pad hits is left out and said so, since a pad's pitch depends on the kit.

## The model

A part keeps its **decisions** apart from its **notes** (`event.rs`). A `NoteDecision` is everything about a note except its pitch: when, how long, how loud, its `Role` over the chord (a chord tone, colour, scale note, approach, passing, neighbour, ghost or drum hit), its `Motion`, the motif it comes from, a pitch that must not change (a locked note, a note held into a selection, a drum pad), the drum lane whose groove it follows, its timing offsets and its `Source` (you, mpfree, or an arp pattern). Only `Source::You` notes are ever learned from.

`realize` turns decisions into notes under a harmony, a key (or none) and an instrument, with no dice: the same input always makes the same notes. Anchors (chord tones, colour and scale notes) are placed first, each following its motion from the anchor before it; approach notes then take a half step into the next note, and passing, neighbour and ghost notes lean on the notes either side. With no clear chord, chord-tone roles keep to the pitch classes played, so nothing is invented. Lengths follow the instrument.

`decisions_of` reads decisions back out of plain notes: each note's role over the chord it was heard on, and its motion. A passing note needs a note to pass into, and an in-key note right after a note outside the key reads as a scale note. Realising them under the same harmony gives the same notes for diatonic material, so "Fit to the new chords" works on any clip, including one written by hand.

## The plan and the chords (phase 0C)

`Settings` (`settings.rs`) carries the controls in the design's words: Style, Similarity, Variation, Activity, Range, Keep, Bars, Ending and the seed.

`plan` (`plan.rs`) gives every bar a job, a tension aim, an activity and a similarity aim before any note. Jobs are yours, repeat, loop, vary, sequence, answer, develop, contrast, return and land (home, open, or leading back). A style's plan shapes are of two kinds: its pattern sets the jobs (Minimal loops, Groove calls and answers, Jazz-ish builds sentences), and its tension curve, picked by the seed from the style's own (one climax, small waves, a slow build), sets the rise and fall. Sixteen bars or more get a contrasting phrase and a return. With the second prototype's numbers, the test's 16 Jazz-ish bars contrast in bars 9 and 10 (similarity 0.31), peak in bar 12 (tension 0.975), return in bar 13 (aiming at 0.91) and land home in bar 16 (0.125), as the prototype measured.

`progression` (`progression.rs`) makes the chord timeline over every bar. Your bars keep the chords heard in them; a repeat or a return plays your bar's chords; every other new bar takes chords from the style's palette (diatonic triads or sevenths on its degrees, plus a borrowed V in minor for Jazz-ish), cut into the style's harmonic rhythm (two bars, one bar, half a bar). A landing bar's last beat takes the ending's chord. The chain is sampled by forward filtering and backward sampling from your last chord, weighted by the style's stay and root-motion weights, so it never dead-ends and Try again (the `attempt` in the dice name) gives another good progression; Variation flattens or sharpens the weights. New chords are marked suggested, shown dashed. A loop bar plays your riff over the style's chords rather than copying yours. A state of the chain is a chord and how long it has lasted, so no new chord outstays the style's longest hold (`max_hold_bars`: four bars in Minimal and Groove, two in Jazz-ish), and a new phrase's first bar leans to the home chord (`home_pull`: 12 times as likely in Minimal, 2.5 in Groove, 1.5 in Jazz-ish), so the chords come home between the landings: Minimal now starts at least 70 % of its free phrases on the home chord. Minimal and Groove suggest no diminished chord (a degree that makes one gives way to the V, and an ending on a diminished V lands on the major V); Jazz-ish keeps them. A harmonic rhythm of a bar or more is whole bars, so a 3/4 waltz's chords change on bar lines.

Minimal's pattern plays your bar round at most twice running: a phrase is loop, loop (or vary every other phrase), a sequence a step up or down, then leave open or vary. A vary, answer or contrast bar moves up the scale with the tension (up to three steps at the peak, one down when calm), so a long continuation climbs to a high point and comes back for the returns and landings. With no clear key, the new bars play your bars' chords again in order, and nothing is invented.

## Phrases (phase 0C)

A phrase is written as a tune of places in the scale on the 1/16 grid (`motif.rs`): `Key::position` turns a pitch into a scale place and how far it is raised, so moving a motif two places is a sequence that stays in the key. `motifs` cuts a line at its phrase breaks (Cambouropoulos's boundary model: changes in leap, time between starts and rests); `similarity` says how alike two stretches are, whatever key they're in (0.35 leaps and lengths as an edit distance, 0.25 shape, 0.3 rhythm, 0.1 accents); `transform` has the classic changes (move, invert, backwards, augment, diminish, head, tail, shift, widen, end on).

`rhythm.rs` keeps, varies, reshapes and makes a bar's rhythm on any meter's grid (`Grid`: the 4/4 table of strong and weak steps generalised to any bar and beat). `vary` splits, joins and nudges notes; `feel` is the style's: Minimal keeps the strongest notes, Groove pushes beats a 1/16 early and chops long notes (never into a rest), Jazz-ish moves notes to 1/8s and anticipates beats, and rests of an 1/8 or more that you played stay rests; `fresh` makes a new rhythm for a contrast near the style's syncopation (Longuet-Higgins and Lee) and away from yours; `density_to` thins a rhythm (the weakest notes go, never the bar's first) or fills it (the longest notes split at their strongest step, on 1/8s for a swung line) to the bar's activity.

`phrase.rs` writes the new bars. `Phrasing::new` reads your notes (only notes a person played; anything the composer or an arp made is left out), learns how you move from note to note (a variable-order model of scale steps), and finds the middle of your tune and the notes you use most. For each new bar, `bar_material` takes the rhythm and a target from the bar's job: your bar again (a loop varies half the time), varied, moved up or down the scale, the first half of the call with the second half of your other bar ending elsewhere, the opening motif twice a step apart, or a new rhythm under your tune upside down. `fit` then picks the notes with a beam search (12 lines kept at each note) over every pitch in range, scoring your steps blended with the style's, the chord under it, leaps and a turn after one, repeats, register following the bar's tension, the target's place and steps, and, for the last note, the way into the next bar and the ending; Gumbel noise scaled by Variation makes each try different. The finished line nearest the bar's similarity aim wins. With no clear key, new notes keep to the notes you played.

Since the second listen: a note outside the key passes quickly (a 1/16, or an 1/8 in a style as chromatic as Jazz-ish) and never on a beat; striking the same note again a 1/16 later costs a lot and an 1/8 later a little (across the bar line too), unless your bar did it, and a note a busier bar split in two that stayed on one pitch is joined again (`rejoin`); the register the line aims at climbs with the tension, from a step below your tune's middle to a fifth above at the peak; and a note outside Minimal's few notes of yours costs a little less (`OUTSIDE_SET`), so a lifted bar finds the notes above. `fresh` rhythms always start on the downbeat and pay for each 1/16 after a beat (`LATE_COST`), so a contrast never starts a bar a 1/16 late.

Contract 6's kinds hold: the Range you picked (`RangeSetting::steps`, scale places either side of your tune's middle), the instrument's range, locked bars and the Keeps never give way, and when they can't all be met the request is refused with the setting and one change ("Can't fit a new bar 3 between your bars with Range Narrow. Try Range Medium?", or the instrument's range when no wider setting helps). Keep Rhythm holds every new bar's rhythm as your bar's; Keep Tune shape makes every step go the way yours did. A leap over an octave, a chromatic note that doesn't move on by a half step and the ending's last note (home: the home note, or less well its 3rd or 5th; open: the 2nd, 5th or 7th; leading back: near your first note or the 5th) were hard rules in the prototype and are preferences here, weighed by the style; when one gives way the result says so (`Line::gave_way`), so Jazz-ish enclosures show up there and nothing is hidden.

`continue_line` writes every new bar in order, each with its own dice (`Job::Rhythm` and `Job::Pitch`, by bar and try), and ends each phrase with a breath (`breath_share`, `breathe`); `try_again` remakes one bar with everything else locked; `decisions` reads the new bars back as decisions under the chords, so a later chord change re-fits them, and realising them gives the same notes for all three styles. A 16-bar line takes 1 to 2 ms on one desktop core (9,400 to 21,000 candidates scored); MPC timings come with the rest of 0C.

## Playing, and Continue end to end (phase 0C)

`playing.rs` plays the new notes once their pitches are set, after the KTH performance rules as the second prototype tuned them, and writes the result into each note's decision, so a chord change re-fits the notes and keeps how they were played. Your touch (`Touch`) comes first: the new notes start from how you played your bars, their mean loudness moved three quarters of the way from the style's to yours, and the style's gate three quarters of the way to your notes' length against the gap to the next note (less when you played fewer than eight notes to go by). The first renders for the listening tests showed why: Groove carried a soft legato riff on louder and shorter, like another player, and repeating the riff would have won on that alone. Loudness: the style's level and accents per 1/16, a rise and fall over every four bars, the bar's tension, melodic charge over the chord (Friberg), contour peaks and notes after a leap, higher notes a little louder; how far these move a note is the instrument's velocity response, and Keep Accents leaves your loudness. Length: the gate, repeated notes shorter, a little air before a leap, short notes a little shorter and softer (long ones a little louder), and a landing note held for all its planned length, never past it, so the breath after it stays; the first note after a breath comes in a little louder. Timing (`timing`): with drums read, each note on the grid takes the drummer's residual at its step for its lane (a bass the kick's, the rest the hats'), moved onto the swing the clip plays with; with none, the style's slow drift where it has one (Groove and Jazz-ish, 6 ms; never Minimal): one smooth 1/f curve (`drift`) drawn from the request's seed alone, so every part of a request drifts together.

`request.rs` is Continue end to end. `continue_notes` takes your notes in the clip's ticks, a reading and the settings: the plan for your bars plus the new ones, the chords (Keep Chords plays your chords again), the phrase, the decisions, the playing, and the notes Keep writes, with the clip's new length. `try_bar` makes one new bar afresh and keeps every other note exactly as it was, played as it was. `continue_clip` reads a clip and the rest of its row from a project (a pitched clip of pad hits is refused, since a pad's note depends on the kit), maps the chords onto the clip's own ticks at its speed, takes its notes after quantise, and returns the `Proposal` a preview plays and Keep writes, bound to every clip it read. A refusal says why in words (`Refused::words`). Carrying 16 bars on takes 1 to 2.2 ms on one desktop core after the reading.

## Simpler ways and the listening tests (phase 0C)

Each stage has to beat a simpler way of doing its job by ear, or it is reworked or dropped for the simpler way. `baseline.rs` makes the simpler ways, each returning the new notes as Keep would write them:

| Job | Played beside |
|---|---|
| Continue a melody | your phrase repeated exactly as played (`Simpler::Repeat`), or moved a scale step up, then down (`Simpler::Moved`) |
| Styles | random notes from the key's scale around your tune, in rhythms from the style's density and syncopation, played with the style's accents on your touch (`Simpler::Random`) |
| Groove | the same notes on the grid (a fixed swing when the clip swings), or with ordinary humanize (`regroove`) |
| 16 bars | the first four bars repeated with small random changes (`motif_repeated`) |
| Try again on a bar | the same bar made without looking at the bar after it (`try_bar_one_sided`) |

A bass beside root notes on the kicks comes with Add a part (phase 2). Their tests check the shape of each, and that mpfree's new bars keep closer to the chords and to your tune than random notes do.

`rust/mpfree-compose/examples/listen.rs` renders the listening set as files (workstation only; it reads only the MIDI file it is given and writes into one new folder):

```text
cargo run --release -p mpfree-compose --example listen -- --demo
cargo run --release -p mpfree-compose --example listen -- --midi my-clip.mid [--from 1] [--bars 2] [--style groove]
cargo run --release -p mpfree-compose --example listen -- --demo --gate --sounds a   # S1's blind listen: 8 bars three ways
cargo run --release -p mpfree-compose --example listen -- --demo --critic 20        # the critic's table on 20 seeds; writes nothing
cargo run --release -p mpfree-compose --example listen -- --phrases                 # the arp's 64 phrases as they play, a file each
```

`--demo` uses four built-in phrases (the prototype's C minor riff, an F major tune, a swung A minor line, a D Dorian waltz in 3/4); `--midi` reads your own clip from a MIDI file (its first track with notes, the top note where notes start together). For each phrase it writes every case as a WAV and a MIDI file of what is heard: Continue beside each simpler way, unlabelled and shuffled into A and B; the three styles as X, Y and Z; mpfree's feel beside the plain ones; 16 bars beside the repeated motif; two Try again pairs; a 16-bar continuation for "do you hear your motif?"; and about 32 bars (Continue by 16, then by 16 again) with the chords as a quiet pad. Every file of a phrase plays on the same sounds at the same loudness (-18 LUFS; `--sounds` picks the set, [below](#sounds-you-can-trust-s0)), picked for the phrase's style since the sound and rhythm pass: the melody on Keys "Funk Clav" (Groove), Keys "Soft Tines" (Minimal), Wind "Warm Clarinet" (the waltz) or Wind "Tenor Sax" (Jazz-ish), since the second listen heard the plucks as shrill; a drummer on the style's kit; a plate reverb and a dotted-1/8 echo; and in the long listen the chords on Strings "Ballad Pad" and a bass in the style's way, on the chord's own third and fifth. Each half of the long listen plays over the chords it was written over. With `EXPLAIN=1` set, the long listen's two runs are explained on stderr: the key read, the chords, and each bar's plan with every note's role. The pairs have no chords under them, so neither side is helped by the composer's own. `answers.json` says which file is which and `report.txt` lists every case. The owner's listening page plays a selection of 20 of them blind, asks "which would you keep?" and the eight ratings (intentional, keeps the idea, suits the style, harmonic sense, groove, develops, avoids monotony, would you keep it), and reveals the answers only at the end.

### The sound and rhythm pass (after the first listen)

The owner's first listen (2026-11-22) heard the 32 bars as "very mechanical sounding, chiptune style music" that "needs a better sense of rhythm and variety", and asked for FX, envelopes and filters for texture. Measured on the long renders (31 new bars each), the lines hardly ever stopped (rests of an 1/8 or more: riff 2, tune 4, swung line 1, waltz 7), their loudness moved little (a spread of 5.5 to 7.3 velocity), the Activity setting barely changed Groove and Jazz-ish (6.5 to 7.0 notes a bar from Sparse to Busy, against Minimal's 2.3 to 6.2), and the test beat was one bar of 1/8 hats, kick and snare at the same loudness for 32 bars. The pass changes:

- **Breaths.** Each phrase ends with a rest before the next (`RhythmStyle::breath`: a beat in Minimal and Groove, half a beat in Jazz-ish): a whole breath on a landing, every fourth bar and the bar before a contrast or a return, half of one now and then halfway through a phrase and on a last bar that leads back. Notes starting in it are dropped (never the bar's first) and the rest end before it; a landing note keeps its first beat. Keep Rhythm has none. Rests of an 1/8 or more: riff 20, tune 19, swung line 12, waltz 16.
- **Busier and sparser stretches.** The plan's activity now moves within a phrase (its second bar a little sparser, its third busier), rises with the tension, and each phrase is a little busier or calmer by the seed (0.85 to 1.15). Every job but a contrast and a landing makes its bar about that busy against your bar (within 15 %, so the feel's own changes stay), through `density_to`; Minimal's feel already keeps as many notes as the activity asks. From Sparse to Busy, Groove now goes from about 4.8 to 8.2 notes a bar and Jazz-ish from 4.7 to 6.6 (on 1/8s, it never passes eight).

### Phrases with intent (S1)

The first engine gave every bar a job and searched for its notes bar by bar, hoping a whole would appear; two listens heard no purpose or movement. S1 plans the new bars as one phrase before any note ([`sketch.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-compose/src/sketch.rs)), fills it in ([`draft.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-compose/src/draft.rs)) and judges whole drafts ([`critic.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-compose/src/critic.rs)). It is the engine behind `continue_notes` (the owner's go, 2026-11-23 03:49Z, ahead of the blind listen's answers); the first engine stays as `first_engine`, played beside it by `listen --gate`, `--critic` and the bench.

- **The phrase plan.** One gesture per new bar, from how many there are: answer your idea, reach up to a landing, develop your opening, reach again, hold back, set up, the high point, settle, come home (or leave it open, or lead back to your first bar). Odd-numbered drafts put the high point a bar earlier and settle after it. Anchors are fixed before any other note: each landing (on a downbeat or the middle of the bar, mostly on the five chord's root or fifth, now and then the four or six chord), the high point (above anything you played, on the middle, a beat in, or in a groove an 1/8 early) and the last note. Rests follow a landing (an 1/8), the high point (a beat) and a held-back bar (its last beat). One surprise sits in the set-up bar: a chord from the other mode (iv in major, IV in minor, bVI in Dorian) where chords change every bar or sooner, otherwise a sudden silence for the bar's second half.
- **Chords led to the landings.** `progression_led` takes the plan's needs: the chords that may sound under each landing (`Need::Degrees`), a chord that holds the high point's note (`Need::Holding`) and the surprise's borrowed chord (`Need::Chord`). The chord then picks each anchor's exact note, the nearest chord note to its aim: the high point first, then every landing on the way up above the one before (the first above your opening), and nothing else as high as the high point.
- **Notes between the anchors.** Each bar's material comes from its gesture: your tune moved so it arrives a step from the bar's anchor, a short pickup after a landing, the way down after the high point towards the next bar's first note, and a bar with no anchor of its own ending a step from the next bar's downbeat landing. The plan's rests stay silent. The same beam search as before (`phrase::fit`) fills the notes, with `Phrasing::music_first` costs: the note before an anchor leads into it by step and never sounds its note early; the note after it doesn't strike it again; a free note isn't struck again within a beat; no quick trill of two notes; an outside note is reached and left by a half step and appears only with a job (a half step into the high point or the last note in a style that uses them, or a borrowed chord's own note). Drafts are played without the first engine's random drift.
- **The small critic.** Six checks on the notes alone, each saying in words what it found: *goes somewhere* (the highest new note first heard 40 to 92 % of the way through, 3 semitones or more above the new bars' opening, not hammered), *lands* (the planned landings on time; the last note on a beat, held, on a chord note, closing as the ending asks), *breathes* (an 1/8 rest or longer in every four bars, the planned rests kept, at most 90 % of beats struck), *yours* (your opening heard, changed, in some new bar; at most half the bars copied), *not too repetitive* (no longer run of copied bars than the style allows: one for a sentence, two otherwise) and *flows* (no leap wider than a sixth, at most one wider than a fourth in every two new bars, no two leaps the same way, no quick trill). Four drafts are made (two above 8 bars) and the one passing most checks, then scoring best, is offered.
- **Locked bars and Try again.** A result keeps its phrase plan (`Continued::sketch`). With bars locked, every draft keeps them exactly (notes, chords, playing): the chords are led to the other bars' landings around them, a locked bar's anchor keeps its note, and the high point goes above every locked note. Try again on picked bars (`draft::try_bars_s1`) fills each picked bar again around its anchor, which keeps its note, with the other bars, the chords and the high point's ceiling as they were; its free notes steer off the notes the bar had where another fits, and its dice start past the drafts'. Try again on picked notes works as before.
- **Its listen.** Over 20 seeds of 8 bars the four built-in phrases pass all six checks 19, 20, 20 and 19 times with S1 and 1, 1, 0 and 0 times with the first engine (`listen --demo --critic 20`). The checks were written beside S1, so this shows it does what it sets out to do, not that it sounds better. The owner's blind listen plays each phrase carried on by 8 bars three ways (S1, the first engine, and your bars looped over your chords looped; seed 2, sound set `a`), with "which would you keep?" and each file rated on "goes somewhere" and "would you keep it"; the owner chose to go ahead with S1 before answering it, and later set it aside (2026-11-23 08:50Z).

### Parts that play together (S2)

S1 made one part go somewhere; S2 adds a part to a row that plays with the parts already there (the plan the owner approved on 2026-11-23 04:26Z). Three files carry it: [`row.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-compose/src/row.rs) reads the row, [`cells.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-compose/src/cells.rs) makes rhythm, and [`parts.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-compose/src/parts.rs) makes and judges each part.

- **The row as one picture (S2.1).** `Row::hear` takes the row's parts as they play (notes, or drum hits by kick, snare, hats and the rest), its length, key, chords and groove. The *lead* is the melody with the most movement, unless one is picked. The lead is read back into a plan: a landing at each phrase end (a gap of a beat or more, or a strong break after a longer note, at least half a bar apart), the high point (its highest note), the close (its last note), its rests (split at the loop's end) and each bar's energy. With no tune, S1's gestures give the plan over the row's length.
- **Rhythm cells with named changes (S2.2).** A part's rhythm is a riff of one or two bars of one-beat cells (on, e, and, a), drawn from a library per style and kind of part. Cells are sampled, weighted by how well they fit what the row wants at each step, and scored on fit, the style's liking, density, syncopation and playing in every bar. The riff then comes back with named changes where the plan asks (`develop`): a note on each landing, pushes, a split at the high point, the end of the part by feel, and one change every fourth bar. The changes are keep, shift, push, split, join, drop, ghost, echo and mirror. Ghost notes (about 0.38 of the note's loudness) sit on the 1/16 before an on-beat note, never on a kick or the backbeat.
- **Parts by role (S2.3).** `add_part` makes a bass, chords, a second line or drums by role, and offers the best of four drafts. The roles are Support, Answer, Interlock and Stay out.
  - *What each role wants.* Each role is a weighting of what the row asks at each step. Support wants the kick, chord changes and the plan's landings. Answer wants the tune's gaps: its rests, and the tails of notes held half a beat or longer. Interlock wants the steps between the others. Stay out plays one note per chord.
  - *Cells that change with the role.* A walking bass plays in two on Support and answers like a line. Held pad chords play the off-beats on Interlock and Answer.
  - *How the notes are picked.* Bass notes are picked together, by the cheapest path through each note's options: roots and fifths on the strong beats and on each chord's first note, and a step into each new chord or bar (a half step outside the key only when the bass walks). The register rises with each bar's energy and with the high point. Octaves and fifths in parallel with the tune are avoided, and no note a half step from one the others hold. Chords are voiced with the least movement, under the tune's lowest note and over the bass: the 3rd and 7th are kept, and rootless voicings are used in Jazz-ish. A second line sits under the tune in 3rds and 6ths, with no unison on a strong beat. Drums play the style's step maps by role, with fills before the high point and at the end, only on pads the kit names, and never doubling a hit already in the row.
  - *When it refuses.* A pitched part needs chords. Answer needs a tune.
  - *When a chord changes.* `refit` keeps a part's rhythm and picks its notes again under the new chords.
- **The row critic.** Each part gets six checks, in words: *plays together* (the role can be heard), *no clash* (no minor 2nd or 9th against a held note on a strong beat, no parallels with the tune), *room* (in its range, under the tune), *lands with the row*, *returns, not stuck*, and *flows*. Drums have their own versions of these: a kick under the tune's accents and a backbeat, fills in the tune's gaps, percussion between the others, the plainest beat, no doubled hits, and the time kept. Over the riff with a beat, the riff alone, a tune that rests and a beat alone, every style, part and role plays its role and passes five of the six checks or more (`every_role_and_part_plays_its_role_on_four_rows`). The checks were written beside the parts, so the blind listen is what judges them.
- **Its listen (S2.4).** `listen --demo --parts` plays four rows twice through, three ways, blind, in a Latin square (no way sits in the same place in every row). The rows are the C minor riff with its beat, the F major tune alone, the riff's beat alone, and the swung line with its beat. The three ways are:
  - S2's parts: a bass on Support, chords on Answer (on Interlock with no tune) and, over the tune alone, drums on Support first;
  - the same parts with every part on Support (roles off);
  - the simple way: the chord's root on every kick and a block chord on each change, with the phrase's own drummer.

  The riff and the beat play over Cm7 and Fm7, picked: heard alone, the riff reads as one Fm7 for both bars. The other rows play over the chords heard, each looped with its phrase (contract 4). S2 was to pass if it was kept over the simple way on most rows, the owner's own included, and roles beat roles off, with a role that doesn't win simplified or removed; the owner set the listen aside (2026-11-23 08:50Z), so S2 goes in as built and the set stays for later listens.

A part searches cells, not single notes. The Harmony line stored with the row, contract 4 connected to Continue and Add a part, Fit to the new chords, and the screens come next.

### New ideas: phrases made to order (P2)

P1 gave the arp a phrase player and 64 written phrases ([arpeggiator.md](arpeggiator.md#phrases)). P2 makes a phrase to order: a bass line, a riff, chord stabs or an arp line of one, two or four bars, in a style, as busy as asked. [`idea.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-compose/src/idea.rs) is its front door, with one file a maker under [`idea/`](../../../rust/mpfree-compose/src/idea): `new_idea` takes a request (kind, style, major or minor, bars, how busy, a seed and Try again's count) and returns an `Idea`; `next_idea` is Try again; `Idea::phrase` is the arp's `Phrase`. The engine is built with no screen yet. Each choice below says what was weighed.

- **An idea is Add a part for a band that isn't there.** A phrase plays from one held note, so an idea is written from a home note in C, major or minor, over a short loop of chords from home that the style's own chord moves suggest (one bar is the home chord alone; two bars move one time in two, four bars three times in four), led home at the start and back at the end. The band is that loop heard as a row with no tune, with the style's beat added first as Add a part adds drums: a bass line is written against a kick, so it is made against one. Weighed: a generator of its own for phrases. Left, because S2 already makes a bass and chords that play with a beat and judges them with the row, and a second engine would have to learn the same things again. A bass and chords are therefore Add a part's own drafts for that row (`parts::draft`, `parts::judge`). A bass plays with the kick or between the kicks as its style's bass does, and takes the other way on every second try.
- **Written first, then judged.** Every kind is made as four whole drafts and the best is offered (most checks passed, then the best score). A draft is first written as a phrase writes notes (`written`): on a grid of 1/8s when it swings or every note starts on one, else 1/16s; each note as long as whole steps with the last one short (a quarter of a step, when it was made shorter than half a step) or plain (four fifths), or held to the next note (when it was made to reach it); plain, accented or a ghost. Then it is judged on what was written (`Draft::heard`). Weighed: judging the notes as made, as Add a part does for a clip. Left, because a phrase cannot hold a note for nine tenths of a step, or play a ghost off a swung grid, so checks on the notes as made passed rests and closes that nobody would hear.
- **A riff has a tune of its own** ([`idea/riff.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-compose/src/idea/riff.rs)). Its rhythm is the style's cells, as a part's is. Weighed for its notes: Add a part's second line. Left, because that line sits under a tune and has none of its own, so riffs came out on one or two notes. A riff's tune is one period (a bar, or two) chosen whole along a shape (an arch, a rise, a zigzag or a plateau, reaching three to five scale places up), every note of it at once by the cheapest path, so the way from its last note back round to its first, which it takes every time, is weighed with the rest. A place off the shape costs 0.45 a scale place and the style's taste in moves weighs 0.4: at half that pull and half as much again of the taste, a style that likes to say a note again (Minimal) made riffs of one note. Saying a note again costs 0.7 after a note of an 1/8 or more and 1.2 after one of a beat or more, and nothing after a quicker one: quick notes may hammer one pitch, and slow ones on one pitch are a tune standing still. A tune that still came out on fewer than three notes, or with one note more than two times in three, is made again along the other shapes and reaches, and the most varied is kept.
- **Later bars bring the tune back, placed by its middle.** Every later bar plays the tune's note for its place, moved in key with its chord's root, as the phrase player itself moves a phrase, and a note that lands off its chord on a beat moves onto it. The whole bar then sits in the octave that keeps it nearest the tune's own middle. Weighed: the root's shift the short way round alone (a bar under a high tune landed a fourth below it, and the high point then leapt out of the bar by more than an octave), and the octave nearest the note before (the register crept up bar by bar).
- **The plan in a riff: a note on the high point, rests that are heard, a close that leads back.** A part under a tune only plays more at the plan's high point; a riff is the tune, so it gets a note there when its rhythm has none, lifted above everything else and accented. Its notes end where the plan rests, so the rest is heard. The close takes the chord note nearest the first note, and what follows it walks back into the first note, three scale places off, then two, then one, from the close's side; when the close is already right beside the first note the walk comes from the other side, so the two enclose it. A note of the walk takes its chord's own note when that is a half step away (the leading note over a dominant), unless that would say the note before it, the note after it or the first note itself. A landing leaves the home note for another note of its chord.
- **One bar has no plan.** A one-bar row's only mark is a close at the half bar, and a close with its walk back would leave half a bar of tune. One bar is all tune, its accent on the top of the tune when the tune rises to one, and its last note, when it is longer than an 1/8, ends an 1/8 before the riff comes round.
- **A riff is judged as a riff.** Six checks: *has a tune* (three different notes, none more than two in three), *goes somewhere*, *leads back*, *breathes*, *not too repetitive* (the critic's) and *flows*. Three of them are the critic's questions with a riff's answers. The critic measures a tune's high point against its opening bar; a riff's high point is in its opening bar when it has two, so a riff *goes somewhere* from its first note (three semitones or more above it, first heard late, not struck over and over). The critic allows a tune one leap wider than a fourth in every two bars; a riff comes round, so a leap in its tune is in every bar, and up a fifth to an accent and back is what many riffs are, so a riff *flows* with up to two such leaps in any bar, one wider than a sixth in every two bars and none wider than an octave (and, as for a tune, no leap followed by another the same way and no quick trill), and a move across a rest of a beat or more is a new start, not a leap. Under the tune's limit four-bar riffs passed every check 41 times in 100. *Leads back* asks that the last note steps into the first, or is the key's fifth, or is a note of the chord the first note comes in on, so the loop's seam is a step or a chord and never an accident. A tune and a riff count the same things: `critic::moves` is the one measure of leaps and trills, and each sets its own limits on it.
- **Two bars come round by themselves.** Add a part asks a bass or chords to come back within the row. A phrase of one or two bars comes back by going round, which a row played once in a song does not, so that check is not asked of them.
- **A line is a pulse that moves** ([`idea/line.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-compose/src/idea/line.rs)). One beat's rhythm on every beat (all the 1/16s or some of them; on swung 1/8s the beat and its "and"), and a figure of chord notes that starts again in every bar and follows each chord by the nearest of its notes: up the chord, wide, up and back, one note held to with the others climbing round it, in thirds, or low and an octave up by turns. The wide figure and the octaves are drawn for three notes to the octave, so they climb the triad's ladder whatever the chord: on a seventh chord's four notes the octaves came out as root and seventh by turns. From the plan's landing and from its high point to the end of their bars the figure plays a chord note higher, and the high point itself two, never lower than a place above the figure's top, so it is the highest note whichever note of the figure falls there. Four bars alike are then two alike, one that opens and one that peaks.
- **Lines also fall, and start off the root.** A one-bar line over the home chord had some ten figures in a swung style, and Try again ran out of new ones on one tap in five. One figure in three now falls, and one in two starts on the chord's third or fifth (the octaves keep to the root). A falling figure is its rising twin from the top down, the same notes in the same room. Weighed: a room of its own below the rising one (it would have needed space under the line's lowest note, and a second register to keep in range). One bar of a line may fall, so for one bar *goes somewhere* asks only for a note three semitones or more from the first, either way (`moves_off`, the riff's one-bar rule too); with two bars or more it is the critic's high point, which is the plan's lift heard.
- **The home note is where the phrase weighs.** The held note plays the phrase's home note, so which one it is written from decides where the phrase sits under the hand. Chords are shapes over the held note: the home note under the first chord's lowest note. Any other kind: the home note it sounds longest, in the octave it sounds it, the lower of two as long; one that never plays the home note is written from the one under its lowest note. Weighed: the lowest home note it plays (a riff that touched a low home note once and lived an octave above played an octave above the hand).
- **The last note ends with the phrase.** A phrase's text has no way to say a note that hangs over its end. A last note written to reach the first note of the next round was heard long, and came back shorter once the idea was kept as text. It is written to the phrase's end and no further, and a test reads every idea back from its text.
- **Try again never offers a phrase again.** `next_idea(request, offered)` returns the idea of the request's attempt, or of the first later attempt (of eight) whose phrase is none of `offered`, with the attempt it was, so the next Try again asks from one past it. `offered` is what the musician can still page back to. It returns nothing when eight attempts in a row made only phrases already offered, so the caller can say there is nothing new for these settings and never offers one again in silence. Weighed: more dice for each attempt (fewer repeats and no promise), and an object that remembers a sitting (state the app keeps anyway, as the last eight). Measured over sittings of 24 taps with the last eight kept (`print_try_again_sittings`): of 1,296 taps for each kind and length, one found nothing new (one-bar chords); before lines fell and started off the root, 294 did on one-bar lines. An idea takes up to 1.2 ms to make on the workstation (a four-bar riff; a line under 0.1 ms).
- **One place an idea becomes a phrase.** `Idea::phrase` returns the arp's `Phrase`, written for either scale when every note is the home note, its 2nd, 4th or 5th. `mpfree-compose` therefore depends on `mpfree-arp` for that one type. Weighed: the app turning an idea's notes into a phrase, as it does a clip's (`Phrase::from_notes`). Left, because the listening tool would then turn them into one its own way, and the owner would judge a phrase the app does not play. The arp still knows nothing of the composer.

## Continue in the time engine (phase 1)

Phase 1's first step puts Continue in the app with no screen yet: `TimeAction::Continue` carries a `ContinueAction` (`rust/mpfree-time-ui/src/continuing.rs`): Ask (Continue, or Try again on everything, with the settings, the kind of instrument and the kit's drums), Step (‹ ›), Keep and Back. The time engine's control half runs it (`TimeControl`'s Continue section in `time/control.rs`, with `Composing` in `time/compose.rs`) and publishes `TimeState::continuing`: the state, the shown result (what was made, with each bar's job and the chords, and the clip as heard with it), where the new bars start, which of the last 8 is shown, whether it plays and the bar line the music changes on next, and why nothing can be heard or kept when that is so.

**The composer off the music's threads.** An ask copies just what the reading needs into a `ContinueJob` (`job.rs`): the tracks it reads, each with only its read clip, at its place, with the track's swing; nothing else of the project. That goes to a thread of its own, started on the first Continue, at idle priority on the helper cores (`mpfree_lanes::os::idle_job`), which runs only the newest job waiting. The control half takes the answer on a later period and checks it against the project as it is then (`Preview::arrived`); one overtaken by a newer ask, or made from a clip that changed meanwhile, is dropped and counted. The rest of the row is read from the clip's row on every other heard track of notes, a clip of pad hits as drums; the kit's drums (`Lanes`) come with the ask, from the screen that knows the pad names.

**Heard in place of the clip.** While a result is in the view, `TimeControl::compile` swaps the result's clip into the clip's slot for the view's compile only, and back (no copy of the project). The music changes on the next bar line while the clip plays and the transport rolls (a line within the next period is as good as now, as Reload clip does), and at once otherwise:

| What happens | When the music changes |
|---|---|
| A result arrives (or Try again's next one, or ‹ ›) | On the next bar line; the last result plays on until then |
| Back | On the next bar line: the clip as it was |
| Keep | At once: the view is compiled from the kept clip, which plays the same notes |
| The clip, or a clip the reading listened to, changes (an edit, a recording, UNDO) | At once: the clip as it now is, with "The clip changed: try again?" |
| REC | At once, before the take starts; the result stays ready, and none comes in while recording |
| Stopped, or the clip doesn't play | At once |

A result's tick 0 falls where its clip started (the launch's `start`): the launch's origin is anchored for the longer clip, so a riff carried on from 2 bars to 4 lines up with the 4-bar part that started with it, as the reading heard them. Without it the new length would play from wherever the old length's origin happened to fall (a 1-bar clip's origin is any whole bar back), which could put your first bar over the other part's third. Back gives the launch its origin back; Keep leaves it, in time with the kept clip.

While Continue is under way, edits compile rather than go in place, so each compile checks the result against its clip (`Preview::clip_changed`) and no result in the view is edited in place. A recorded change checks it each period it lands.

**Keep.** The shown result's clip goes in as one take, `Takes::replace_clip` with `Replaced::Continue { bars }` beside Mutate and Restore, as the design asked; the Takes list says "continue" and "2 more bars". Phase 0B kept through `Takes::edit` with the same `Edit`; the whole clip replaced is the same notes and length, puts the clip back whole on UNDO, and says what it was. A refused Keep (the clip changed, or its own limits) writes nothing and leaves no result playing. Keep is refused while recording.

**What gave way is counted** and goes on the status line once anything was asked: "continue: 3 asked, 3 made, 1 stopped by a change, 1 stopped by REC" (asked, made, refused, overtaken, made from a clip that changed, stopped by a change, clips left out of readings, keeps refused, stopped by REC). They move to the system status monitor with the rest when it takes the time engine's counts.

**Agreed with the piano roll** (22 Nov): Continue becomes a tool in the note editor's Edit options drawer (`ToolKind::Continue`), working like Strum or Humanize: the Q-Links while you hear it, Done for Keep, Cancel for Back. Its preview is the clip `TimeState::continuing` publishes, not a tool edit; the roll takes its length from it and draws the new bars from `new_from` in the faint mix the other clips use, with each bar's job on the ruler and the tries under the options where Takes sit. No new bottom button. That is step 2, with its layout spec change for the owner.

### The composer side of steps 2 to 4

Steps 2 to 4 put Continue on screen: a tool in the note editor's Edit options, Try again on the new bars or notes you pick with the bars you lock, and a Continue row on Arrange's clip card. They are built, their screens approved as drawn, and come to main after the piano roll's note editor and Arrange's clip card they are drawn on. What they ask of the composer is here:

- **What each track plays.** The screen knows each track's instrument and the kit's pad names. Where it gives no kind, `kind_of_notes` (`profile.rs`) reads it from the notes: chords where a third of the notes sound together, a bass under note 48 in the middle, else one note at a time. `lane_named` (`job.rs`) gives the drum a pad's sound name stands for ("BD Deep" a kick, "Clap Wide" a snare, "Shaker" the hats).
- **What an ask makes.** A `ContinueJob` carries a `Making` (`request.rs`), which `continue_clip_how` makes: `New`, a new result; `Keeping { before, locked }`, a new result with the locked new bars of the one shown kept; `Bars { before, bars, attempt }`, the picked bars again with the shown result's seed and a new attempt number, so the rest of the piece is the same piece; `Notes { before, notes, attempt }`, the picked notes again, each found among the clip's notes as heard with `before`. Step 1's asks are all `New`.
- **Picked bars.** `try_bars` fills each picked bar again, a music-first result's from its phrase plan (`draft::try_bars_s1`, each bar keeping its anchor's note; [S1](#phrases-with-intent-s1)), a first-engine result's scored on its inside, its way in and its way out as contract 1 says. Every other bar is spliced back from `before` (events and decisions, `splice`).
- **Picked notes.** `try_notes` fits each picked note's bar again with the others fixed (`NoteAs::Fixed`) and the picked ones free to move (`NoteAs::Change`, the same pitch costing more), keeping each note's place, length, velocity and timing; only the pitch changes. Picking only your own notes is refused (`Refused::NoNewNotes`).
- **Locked bars.** `continue_notes_keeping` makes a new result with the locked bars' notes, chords and playing kept, every draft leading into and out of them ([S1](#phrases-with-intent-s1)); the first engine plans its chords around them as fixed slots (`progression_keeping`).

### What a change re-fits (contract 2)

Two kinds of change: making new decisions (regenerating) and playing the same decisions under new conditions (re-fitting). `effect` is the table: a chord, groove, instrument or playing change re-fits; a motif, style or activity change regenerates what it touches. No entry redoes what it keeps, and every entry keeps locked notes and the Keeps. `action` says what happens to one part: in a preview it is re-fitted or regenerated at once; locked notes are left; a kept clip is never rewritten. When a chord change leaves a kept clip's notes off the new chords (`misfits`: chord tones on a beat that no longer are), it is marked and offered "Fit to the new chords", as one step UNDO takes back.

Chance comes from stable names (`DiceName`): the seed, the part, the phrase, the job, the bar and the try, mixed with SplitMix64. Rolling more dice for one bar never changes another, so trying bar 6 again leaves bars 7 to 16 as they were. New jobs are added at the end of `Job` so existing rolls stay the same.

### The timing contract (contract 3)

Every event's time is built in one order:

1. its musical position, in ticks on the clip's grid;
2. its groove offset: the drummer's residual timing at that step, as a share of a 1/16;
3. its playing offset, in ticks; 2 and 3 together stay within ±30 ms (`LIMIT_MS`);
4. playback's own quantise, speed and swing, as the play view applies them (`ClipView::note_view`, then the player's run collection in `mpfree-play`).

Steps 1 to 3 are written into the clip (`written`); step 4 is playback's (`heard`). A note pushed before the clip's start is written at its end, where playback plays it just before the next pass, as quantise already does with a note at the end.

Swing is a playback setting (the clip's, the track's or the transport's), so a `GrooveTemplate` never holds swing: each cell is the drummer's timing once the swing playback applies has been taken out (`unswing`, the exact inverse of `Swing::apply`). `Timing::from_groove` moves a template read against one swing onto the clip's swing first (`GrooveTemplate::against`), keeping where every hit lands. The tests check both ways round: grooved notes land within a millisecond of the drummer whether the groove was read against straight or swung playback, and offsets measured from the straight grid would have swung the hats twice (more than 15 ms off).

A clip whose quantise has a strength above 0 pulls grooved notes back towards its grid; `check` says so before Keep, in words. A style's slow drift is used only where the style asks for it (0 in Minimal) and never over a played groove (`drift_on`).

Keep writes `clip_notes`: the written ticks, all or nothing inside `MAX_NOTES` and `MAX_CLIP_TICKS`. The preview plays the same written notes through playback, so the preview and the kept clip play every note at the same time; a test compares them note by note, and phase 0B's tests repeat it through the real play view and player: `heard` lands on the player's frame for every note, with quantise, half, double and 3:4 speed, the clip's swing or the transport's, and a clip started off the bar line.

### Previews and Keep (contract 7)

A preview is bound to one clip at one revision (`Binding` in `bind.rs`): its track and slot, its lasting id and its `Revision`. A revision is a hash of what the clip plays and what the composer reads (every field of every note, the length, speed, swing, quantise, direction, what the notes stand for), worked out from the clip when asked; its name, locks, moves and launch settings are left out, and every field of `Clip` and `Note` is named so a new one must say whether it is in. It is a sum of one hash per note, so notes stored in another order, as UNDO puts them back, are the same revision. A counter kept with the clip was the other way, but every path that edits a clip (step edits, recording in place, undo, captures, Mutate, loading) would have to move it on, and one that forgot would let a stale result be kept; a hash can't forget.

`Preview` (`preview.rs`) is the state machine the app's control thread will drive:

| State | What the host does |
|---|---|
| Idle | Nothing |
| Preparing | A request is out, numbered; a result for an older request is dropped. A result that was playing (Try again while listening) plays on until the new one arrives |
| Ready | A result is in, not playing yet: auditions it at the next bar line |
| Auditioning | Compiles the play view from `audition`'s project: the result's edit applied to a copy of the clip with the same `Clip::apply` Keep's take uses |
| Committing | Writes the edit as one take (`mpfree_play::Takes::edit`, the transaction step edits use) and compiles; a refused Keep writes nothing and leaves no preview running (Ready again, with the refusal in words) |
| Cancelling | After Back while it played: compiles the clip's own view, then says it is in |
| Invalidated | The clip, or a clip the reading listened to, changed or went: nothing can be kept, and Try again asks afresh |

The last 8 results for the clip are kept, and ‹ › steps through them (`step`); Keep writes the one shown. Try again while a result plays keeps it playing until the new one arrives, so the music doesn't drop out (the design's table had the clip come back while preparing; this is the one change, made because a gap on every Try again would be heard). The binding is checked when a result arrives, after every edit (`clip_changed`) and once more at Keep, against the bound clip and every clip the reading listened to. Continue's edit (`keep::continuation`) makes the clip longer and adds the new notes as one `Edit`, so one UNDO takes back the length and the notes together. Phase 0B's test auditions by copying the whole project; the app doesn't (phase 1, below): its compile puts the result's clip in the clip's place just for the compile. The piano roll's tool previews turned out to be drawn only, so there was no shared audition layer to play through, and phase 1 built the substitute; the piano roll will use it for its tool previews.

### Rules, styles and instruments (contracts 6, 10, 11)

`Rule::kind` sorts every rule into structural (a real pitch and time, the instrument's range, the clip limits), yours (locks, chords you set, the Keeps, the Range you picked) and preferences (leap size, chromatic motion, the four parts of an ending, repeats). Only preferences give way, weighed by the style's `Weights` (a leap costs `leap` per scale step beyond a fourth, more again over an octave), so a leap over an octave is rare rather than banned and Jazz-ish can use enclosures. When the others can't all be met, `Unmet::words` says which setting is in the way and suggests one change: "Can't fit a new bar 6 between your bars with Range Narrow. Try Range Medium?"

A `Style` is components (harmony, rhythm, bass, melody, playing, development) plus weights and drift; `Style::with` swaps one component, so jazz harmony can go without the walking bass. The three presets (`MINIMAL`, `GROOVE`, `JAZZISH`) carry the second prototype's numbers, tuned by ear, with each style's swing as a suggestion for the clip's playback swing and a few plan shapes each.

Five `InstrumentKind`s give a `Profile`: polyphony, range, articulation, overlap, velocity response, slides and whether length matters. `realize` keeps notes in the profile's range and takes lengths from it: a bass and a one-note lead end before the next note (the lead legato), pads may overlap, hits get a fixed short length.

### Try again on one bar (contract 1)

Trying again on a bar is filling a gap: each try is scored on its inside, its way in and its way out, everything outside the selection stays (including a note held into it), and a loop's last bar leads into its first. The second prototype measured it: over 40 seeds, four bars each and three tries, big steps (more than a major third) into the next bar fell from 28 % with the way out ignored to 17 % with it, the same as music written in order (16 %); at the end of a four-bar loop, big steps back into bar 1 fell from 53 % to 17 %. Phase 0C built the scoring (`try_again`), and its test repeats the measurement on the same riff and plan: over 40 seeds, trying bars 3, 6, 10 and 13 three times each, big steps into the next bar are 14 % with the way out scored and 28 % without (8 % in the line as first written), and at the end of a four-bar loop big steps back into bar 1 are 16 % with the wrap and 39 % without. The other bars stay exactly as they were. The listening set counts how often scoring the way out changes the bar at all: over every new bar of a 16-bar continuation and 20 tries each, 15 tries in 100 on the riff and 1 to 7 on the other three phrases; the rest pick the same bar either way. Its blind pairs are tries where the two differ.
