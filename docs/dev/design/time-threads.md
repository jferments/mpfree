# The time engine on two threads

**Status:** steps 1 and 2 built, 2026-11-12. The time engine is two halves that speak only through two queues, with a facade (`TimeHost`) that by default still runs both on the audio thread. Step 3 is built as a second mode (Arrange 2.6b, 22 Nov): with `--time-thread` the control half runs on a thread of its own ([apart](#apart-the-control-half-on-its-own-thread)), off by default until a device round has compared the two modes. Measured on the desktop in a big song, an action that compiles cost the audio thread 0.4 to 0.8 ms together and under 0.02 ms apart. Live test 2 (13 Nov) found no slow period on the audio thread with small projects ([concurrency.md](concurrency.md), plan step 2). Since 17 Nov a step edit no longer compiles (sequencer S1, [step edits in place](#step-edits-in-place)), and the status line gives what compiles and step edits cost, so the `step-speed` device check (Gate 0) measures both on the MPC. The app thread, which owns `main.rs` and the control thread ([concurrency.md](concurrency.md), plan step 2), agreed to the shape on 12 Nov (07:20Z) with three asks, which are rules 1 to 3 below. Nothing here has run on the MPC yet.

## Why

The pad player ran the whole time engine (`TimeHost` in `rust/mpfree-app/src/time.rs`) in its audio loop. Recording is cheap there, because it writes notes into the play view in place. Every other edit ran on the audio thread, inside a period:

- **A compile** of the `PlayView` on every launch, stop, cue that starts, follow action, undo, redo, arm, capture, mute, song edit and note editor edit. It allocates, and it costs time proportional to the notes playing. After the first step, a project of 128 clips of 512 notes with 16 playing still took 159 µs on the workstation. The MPC One's Cortex-A17 is several times slower, against a 128-frame period of 2.9 ms whose busiest round 7 period already used 44 %.
- **Song edits** clone the whole project (`TimeAction::ScenesToSong` and its kin keep a copy to put back on an error).
- **Capture** builds its clip and takes there.

The time this takes is unbounded, because it grows with the project. On the MPC it can make a launch in a big project cost a period's deadline, which is a click.

## The split

`TimeHost` is two halves with one facade, in `rust/mpfree-app/src/time/`:

| Half | Owns | Runs |
|---|---|---|
| **Control** (`TimeControl`, `control.rs`) | The project, the takes, the launches and cues, scenes and follow actions (`Perform`), live to song, the note editor, the morph's settings, the selection, the armed track, CAPTURE's history and the move log, the steps for the Play page, every compile, and the `TimeState` for the screens | At its own pace: on each screen or MIDI event, and on a timer of a few ms (the app thread's control thread, step 3). Never needed once per period. |
| **Audio** (`TimeAudio`, `audio.rs`) | The transport and its block, the follower of another device's clock (`follow.rs`, see [midi-clock.md](midi-clock.md#following-the-time-engine)), the player, the recorder (and a notes-only one for each other armed track, [rec-arm.md](rec-arm.md#several-tracks)), the view it plays, the last recorded changes (`Recent`), the click, the pad hits and the locks and lanes going out | Once per period, on the audio thread, with no allocation and no free |

They talk through two bounded queues (`link.rs`), one each way.

CAPTURE's history and the move log moved to the control half, not the audio half as first planned. The audio half sends each pad note and gesture it takes, with its offset, and each period's spans; the control half feeds them to the history and the log against a copy of the period's spans (`Block::start_copy`, `Block::copy_span`), so they keep the same phrase as before. Keeping a capture reads the whole history, so this way no phrase has to cross the queue in pieces.

### Rules

1. **The queue.** `link::link` makes a bounded `std::sync::mpsc::sync_channel`, the type `main.rs` already uses between the screen and the audio thread, with all its room. The two halves only `try_send` and `try_recv`, so nothing blocks. A full queue hands the message back and counts it, never a silent drop (the fallback rule). If the device numbers call for it, an SPSC ring goes in behind `link` in one place.
2. **The audio half allocates and frees nothing.** Every message is a fixed size, except a compiled view, which comes boxed. The audio half swaps the box in at the start of a period and sends the old one back, and the control half drops it. While the queue back is full, up to 8 views wait in the audio half; past that one is kept rather than freed there (counted; it should never happen). Messages from it carry no `Vec` or `String`.
3. **The control half keeps up at its own pace.** It may run a few ms late, so nothing it does may need to land within a period. The two places where time matters are below.

### Messages

**To audio** (`ToAudio`, 256 slots): a new view, which says what it includes; a transport command; the tracks' settings when they change: how many tracks and which is armed (`ToAudio::Tracks`), and, one message per track that changed, its "Clips through the arp", destination and "Record pad pressure" (`ToAudio::Track`); a track's MIDI effects, one place a message (`ToAudio::NoteFx`, and `ToAudio::NoteArp` for where its arp sits), and, the first time the track has one, a chain to run them in, made here and boxed (`ToAudio::Chain`: about 400 KB, it stays with the track's arp; one the audio half has no use for comes back to be freed, `ToControl::Chain`); and a step edit's changes, one a message (`ToAudio::EditPart`), then its end (`ToAudio::EditEnd`, below). Each view sent carries the next number (`ToAudio::View(view, n)`), so a step edit can say which view it was made on. Both halves keep a table of all 128 tracks (`link::Tracks`), but a message carries only what changed, so it stays small; one that finds the queue full goes at the next poll.

**To control** (`ToControl`, 8192 slots, a busy second of periods), in the order things happened: each pad note played, each pad pressure value that changed (both for CAPTURE's history) and each gesture taken, with its offset; each cue that started (`Started`); each recorded change (`Rec`) with its number, after its pressure or gesture points in fixed chunks; a request to compile again; each of the period's spans; the clock follower's counts when they change; the timing contract's counts when they change (`ToControl::Cues`, Arrange 2.5); the period's end, with the transport as it left it and the audio half's counts; and each view to free.

The pad player's audio loop calls the audio half directly for what it hands the time engine each period: the pads, their pressure, the knobs, the gestures, FILL, the tempo it follows, the followed port's clock bytes, and the metronome. It can also queue a transport command on an exact frame of the coming period (`TimeHost::command_at`), where a command through the queue waits for the next period: the export stops the song on its range's last frame with it ([export.md](export.md#the-apps-part)).

### Where time matters

**Recording.** The audio half writes recorded and erased notes, locks and gestures into its view in place, numbers each one and keeps the last 1024 with their points (`Recent`), then sends it on. The control half makes them to the project and the takes in the same order. Each view it compiles says the last number it includes. When the audio half swaps in a view older than its last number, it first makes the newer ones to it in place. If the ring has run over, or the view has no room, it plays on with the view it has (which holds everything), counts it, and asks for a compile. Nothing recorded is lost or doubled. Live erase while only playing (ERASE + a pad, the button combos of 19 Nov) goes the same way: the player erases into the view, and the control half makes it to the clip as a take of its own, numbered from `mpfree_play::ERASE_TAKES` ("erase 1" in the takes list), so UNDO brings it back.

**Cues and follow actions.** A launch made while the music plays already waits for the next bar line, and the player carries a switch itself until a view with it arrives. Each view says how many switches it includes. A view compiled before a switch that has since happened gets the switch put in by the audio half before it plays, the same way as recorded notes. Follow actions are cued for their bar line as soon as their clip or scene starts, a bar or more before they are due (`Perform::due` cues one within the bar before its point), so the control half has that long to compile. **The timing contract** (Arrange 2.5): each view says, for each track, the revision of its newest decision and the frame the control half had heard when it made it (`mpfree_play::Decisions`, stamped in `TimeControl::compile` from `heard`, the end of the last period it took). A cue whose line the audio half played before its view came starts at once, in phase with that line; a newer launch replaces a waiting one; an older one than a cue carried out is dropped. The audio half counts each (`CueCounts`: late, stale, lost), sends them when they change, and the status line says them when they are not 0. See [playback.md](playback.md#launching-on-the-bar-line-cues-t9).

### Step edits in place

A tap on a step, a Q-Link turned on a held step and a lock change one or two notes or locks of the clip playing. Compiling the whole view for each (every detent of a turn) cost the audio thread a compile per edit while both halves run on it, and a compile's time grows with the notes playing. So `TimeControl::step_action` makes the edit to the project and the takes as before, and then, when it can, sends its changes instead of compiling (`TimeControl::in_place`): `ToAudio::EditPart { id, change }`, one per change, with `link::InPlace` naming it (a note or lock added or erased), in the order the clip took them (`Clip::apply`: the notes erased, the locks erased, the notes added, the locks added), then `ToAudio::EditEnd { id, track, slot, view, n }`: the edit is whole, `n` changes made on view number `view`. The audio half keeps the parts aside (`TimeAudio::staged`, room for `MAX_IN_PLACE`) until the end comes, then makes them all to its view in place at once with `PlayView::edit_in_place`, or none (refactor A3). That first checks every change fits in every place the clip shows (`ClipView::fits`, a dry run that counts room and what is there, including what the edit's earlier changes add or erase), then makes them with the calls recording uses (`add_note`, `remove_note`, `add_lock`, `remove_lock`), so the view holds them where a compile would, stamp and all. That allocates and frees nothing: the room is what each compile sets aside for recording (`RECORD_ROOM`, 1024 notes and 1024 locks in the armed track's clips), and the same in the clips of a track selected apart from the arm (`PlayView::make_room_on`, refactor C3: a track picked while REC's arm is pinned elsewhere, [rec-arm.md](rec-arm.md)), so its step edits go in place too. Selecting such a track compiles once, for that room.

They need no catch-up, unlike recorded changes: they go through the same queue as the views, so a view sent before them was compiled without them and one sent after them has them. But the view they were made on must be the one playing: one the audio half sent back to compile again (it could not catch up, `Counts::behind`) leaves it playing an older view, which an edit made on the newer one would put wrong. So the audio half keeps the number of the view it plays (`TimeAudio::view_n`, set when it takes one) and makes an edit only when its `view` matches. An edit it cannot make whole (a part missing, made on another view, no room left, or what it erases is not there) changes nothing, is counted (`Counts::missed`, "step edits compiled again" on the status line, which should stay 0) and asks for one compile until a new view comes; the compile brings it and every edit after it. So it never plays half an edit: a velocity change is never the old note gone with the new one missing.

It compiles, as before, when the edit makes a new pattern or launches the pattern on a track playing nothing, changes the clip's length, pressure or moves, has more than `MAX_IN_PLACE` (64) changes or would cost the audio half more than 8192 notes and locks searched and moved (`IN_PLACE_WORK`: its note changes times the notes the clip holds after it, plus its lock changes times its locks, since the view keeps the two apart and a lock change never touches the notes; so a big erase or copy compiles, while a lock detent on a full clip of 8,192 notes goes in place, refactor B3; the rule is `mpfree_play::InPlace::fits`, which the bench's step cases follow too, refactor B1), in song mode (the song's placements play clips the view would also need changing), while recording or counting in (the audio half edits the view itself), while a view waits to be sent, on a track neither armed nor selected apart from the arm (no room set aside), or when the queue fills partway (the control half stops sending at the first part refused and compiles; the audio half makes none of the parts that went, since their end never comes, and the next edit's id clears them). An edit to a clip neither playing nor cued sends nothing. UNDO and REDO of a step edit still compile.

**What it costs, on the status line.** The time engine's part of the 30 s status line ends with what compiles and step edits have cost the thread the control half runs on since the start (`control::Costs`): "compiles: 12 (slowest 0.21 ms, 96 notes and locks); step edits: 40 in place (slowest 0.02 ms), 3 compiled (slowest 0.25 ms)". A step edit is timed by the facade from the action to the audio half having made it (`TimeHost::step_action`), so it is what a tap or a turn costs the audio thread today. The `step-speed` device check reads it after a run of taps and a lock turn during playback: it passes when the edits went in place, each within a tenth of a 128-frame period (`IN_PLACE_MS`, 0.29 ms), and no `control: slow period` line named the pads or the screen. Its numbers, with the slowest compile and the notes it played, are Gate 0 of the sequencer roadmap: whether step 3 has to come before more step work. Note editor edits go the same road (piano roll plan 0C.1) and are counted after them, "; note edits: 5 in place (slowest 0.03 ms), 1 compiled (slowest 0.30 ms)", timed inside `TimeControl::edit` from the touch to the edit sent, on whichever thread the control half runs; coming last, they leave the device check's pattern as it was.

### The facade

`TimeHost` keeps the API `main.rs` used. A call on the control half (an action, a step edit, a capture, a mute) is followed by the audio half taking what it sent, and a period by the control half taking the period's news and the audio half taking any view it compiled (a cue that started, a follow action, a recording with no room left). So on one thread nothing plays differently, and the old time tests pass with only their field paths changed (`h.control.project`, `h.audio.view`).

**Starting on a note.** `TimeHost::start_at(at, bpm, take, into)` starts the song on frame `at` for the looper's Starts: When I play ([looper.md](looper.md#when-i-play-starting-on-your-note)), so bar 1 is the note. `into` is 0 but for smart loop end's Auto ([looper.md](looper.md#smart-loop-end)): the loop is already that many ticks round when the song starts, so the song starts that far into the bar (the `Locate` goes to the bar's start plus `into`, counted as a command when it isn't where the song stands), and what was due before it is skipped, never played late. It is the audio half's (`TimeAudio::start_at`), and since smart loop end step 0C it is a [timing change](#one-timing-change)'s stopped case, landing at once, on the transport's own queue at `at`: back to the start of the bar the song stopped in (`Locate`), `SetTempo(bpm)` if given (before `Play`, so the whole song takes it: the tempo from the first loop, step 4c), then `Play`, with no count-in. A `take` ready (adding or replacing, as for `take_waits`) records from the same frame the way a followed clock's start does: `ToggleOverdub` if the mode differs, `Play`, then `Record`, a punch-in; the control half readies the clip it records into first (`take_started_on_note`), and with no clip to record into the song starts without the take (so no punch-in needs taking back) and only plays. Refused while the song plays or counts in, while another device's clock leads (its tempo arriving, or PLAY waiting for its Start), or when the transport's queue hasn't room for every command (`Transport::queue_room`, and `room_on` for the 16 a period applies: all go in and apply together or none, so a refused start leaves no `Locate` or tempo behind), and counted (`starts_refused`); the reason comes back for the screen (`TimeAudio::start_refusal` asks without counting). `TimeAudio` keeps the tempo last asked for (a `SetTempo` or `start_at`'s) with the frame it is due on until the transport plays at it (a change while rolling waits for the next 1/16 line; stopped, once its period has run), and `follow_tempo` compares the app's tempo with that, so a tempo on its way is asked for once.

**Notes for the looper.** Every note started in a period, live on a track (as the arps give them, in the sink loop) or by a route (`routed`), is kept with its frame on the transport's clock, its track, note and velocity (`TimeAudio::note_started`, at most `NOTES`, 64), for smart loop end ([looper.md](looper.md#smart-loop-end)). After the period the pad player takes them (`TimeHost::take_notes`, which also gives the newest frame of a note there was no room for, so the looper knows its notes there aren't whole); notes nobody took are forgotten when the next period starts (`advance`). Keyed on what was played, the track and the note, as CAPTURE's history is; no instrument ids.

`TimeHost::split` hands over the two halves, already linked, for step 3: the audio loop keeps `TimeAudio` and calls it as the facade does (the pads' input, `advance`, `dress_arps` and `run`, then the hits, locks and click), and the control thread keeps `TimeControl` and calls `poll` every few ms and after each screen or MIDI event. The queue to the audio half holds 256 messages and the one back 8192 (`link::TO_AUDIO`, `link::TO_CONTROL`). Neither half ever waits: a full queue hands the message back to its sender, which counts it (`Counts::lost` for the audio half's, `TimeControl::lost` for the control half's; a view waits and is sent again).

What passing between the halves lost goes in the status line when it is not 0 (`TimeHost::describe`): messages lost to a full queue (either way, and spans past a copy's room), late views compiled again, views kept unfreed, and transport commands the transport's queue refused.

### One timing change

Smart loop end step 0C (`time/timing.rs`, `TimeAudio::timing_change`, through the facade's `TimeHost::timing_change`; the interface agreed with the arranger and live shows threads on 22 Nov). A loop that closes may want the song's tempo, its position and PLAY to change together on the frame the loop comes round, all or none, and to hear what became of that. A `TimingChange { at, tempo, position, play, take, made_against }` is made off the audio thread or ahead of time, against the song's timeline as it was then (`TimeHost::timeline`), for frame `at`: the next period's first frame or later.

- **Waiting.** The audio half holds one change at a time, with a ticket. A newer change cancels it ("a newer change came"), and so does anything that moves time first, each with its reason: a locate, a tempo or a tempo map taking effect, PLAY, STOP or REC (`moves_time`, for every command through `command` and `command_at`), a song edit that moves time (a view whose `Includes::timeline` changed: "the song was edited"), or another change landing. Each gives the timeline a new revision (`moved`), so a change made against an older one is refused at once for that reason. The control half raises the view's revision for the song edits that move time and only those (the arranger's list): a section moved, bars put in or taken out, a tempo point added, moved or erased (not picked), song mode on or off, and the UNDO or REDO of a song edit (`moves_song_time`). Markers, block moves and stretches, clip edits, launches, scenes and mutes leave it alone. A project opened has a new engine with a fresh revision (`next_timeline`, never given out twice in the process), so a change made for one project can't land in another: one made against the old timeline is refused ("the song changed"), and the old engine's tickets read None. A song switch in a live show (live shows D2) uses its own engine the same way and is not a timing change. The change's maker can take it back (`cancel_timing`).
- **Landing.** At the top of the period its frame falls in, before the transport runs (`commit_due`), the change goes to the transport's queue on its exact frame as one piece: there must be room for all its commands in the queue and among the 16 the period applies (`Transport::room_on`), or none go in and it is refused. Playing, the tempo takes over on that frame (`Command::TempoNow`, not on the next 1/16) with no jump, so the notes sounding go on and nothing replays or is skipped. A position is a jump on the same frame (`Locate`): each sounding note ends once there (`Player::end_all`), what was due before it in the new timing is skipped, never replayed, and MIDI clock out sends Stop, Song Position and Continue. Stopped, the position, the tempo (before PLAY, so the whole song takes it), the overdub mode for a take, PLAY, then REC for a take, as `start_at` always did. No period is split: the transport places every command on its frame.
- **Answers.** `timing_answer(ticket)` says Waiting, Committed (on its frame), Cancelled (and why) or Refused (and why), for the last four changes. `timing_counts` counts each kind for the status line (smart loop end steps 1 and 2 show them).
- **Allowed.** `allowed(&change, audio)` asks first and changes nothing. Refused while the song records or counts in (a timing change is never a punch-in), while another device's clock leads or PLAY waits for its Start, for a tempo while the song plays and its tempo points lead, for a take while the song plays, or when the frame has played. Ask when the change brings a new tempo and recorded audio playing at its own speed would play out of step (`audio`, which the app knows: audio regions, other loops). Otherwise Safe, since everything on the grid follows the change.

### Apart: the control half on its own thread

Arrange 2.6b (`time/apart.rs`). Together, an action that compiles takes its time out of a period: in a song of 512 blocks (16 tracks, 32 clips of 1,000 notes) a launch, a scene, undo, redo, song mode or a track mute each cost the audio thread 0.4 to 0.8 ms on the desktop, so several ms on the MPC, more than a 128-frame period. With `--time-thread` (`play` and `app`) the facade runs the control half on `mpfree-time`, off the sound's cores at the normal priority (`TimeHost::into_apart`; a project opened gets the same mode), and keeps only the audio half and a copy of what the app reads. Three ways across:

- **Asks.** The screen's and the buttons' actions (`time_action` in main.rs, through `TimeHost::act_then`), TRACK MUTE (`mute_or_solo_then`), and step taps and turns (`step_action_then`, from the step pads and the screen) go to the thread as messages and return at once (None). The answer comes back later with how the caller wants it said (`Say`: a log line with its prefixes, a line on the screen, UNDO's words, PLAY waiting for a clock, or a step's log line or why on the screen), and main.rs says it with the same function together uses on the spot (`time_said`), so the words are the same in both modes. Together these calls answer at once (`Some`), as before. The inputs the app gives every period (its tempo, whether a take records) go only when they change; what comes back unasked (live to song's line, a take turning Rec song on or off, the loops' lines, the mute queue's pad landings) arrives as notes and landings.
- **Lends.** Every other call (a phrase from the Sample page or a MIDI file's clips put on tracks, the morph, a pad's mute key, a track's MIDI effects, a followed clock's start and a start on a note, the export's transport commands, SHIFT + TC, SHIFT + PLAY and SHIFT + OVERDUB, the step pads' less frequent edits such as clear, copy and a held step's length, a loop copied to another Loop track, and a song time edit, UNDO and REDO while the audio tracks hold regions, so the regions move in the same period as the song: Arrange 3.4) borrows the control half back for the call: the thread hands it over and waits, the call runs on the caller's thread exactly as together, and it goes back. Same answer, same order; the light copy and the steps are read again before it goes, so a read straight after sees the change. A lend waits for whatever the thread is doing (an ask's compile, say), so each is counted and the longest wait timed on the status line ("editing on its own thread: 160 asks, 20 lends (slowest 0.06 ms)"); a device run shows which lent calls still cost the audio thread, and those become asks next.
- **The published copy.** What the app reads comes from a copy the thread publishes: the cheap part (`Light`: the armed and selected tracks, the mutes and their lights, the loop plan, song mode, Perform, the redo count and the loss counts) whenever it differs, and the rest (`Heavy`: the project for saving and for the pages that read it, the steps, the screens' state, the mute queue and the status line) after every ask or lend, whatever it changed, after a change the audio half brought, and at least once a second. The facade takes the newest copy once a period and sends the heavy part it replaces back to the thread to be freed, so its period allocates and frees nothing. Apart, a read straight after an ask sees the copy from before it, a millisecond or so behind; the screens follow the flags the copy carries (`take_changed`, `take_steps_changed`) as together.

The thread polls every millisecond when nothing asks (`apart::POLL`), and each ask wakes it. Every queue is a bounded `sync_channel` made once; the facade waits only in a lend. The control half's own view of the transport comes from the audio half's news, so apart it hears a start or a line up to a poll later than the audio half: a mute asked to wait in the first millisecond after PLAY lands at once, as it does together when the transport has not started. Dropping an apart host (a project opened replaces it) stops the thread without waiting for it.

### Tests

- `recording_with_the_control_half_late_loses_and_doubles_nothing` (time.rs) records the pads, their pressure and a knob into a looping clip at periods of 16 to 4096 frames, with the control half waking 0 to 20 ms apart at random and half the times compiling a view from what it knew when it last woke. Every view the audio half swaps in plays just like the one it replaces, every time the control half has caught up the view playing is a fresh compile of its project, and the clip, the takes, the notes played back and the pressure are just what they are with the halves in step. About 30 views a run catch up recorded changes; nothing is lost, sent back or compiled again.
- `follow_actions_land_on_their_bar_lines_with_the_control_half_late` runs every follow action (stay, again, next, previous, first, last, any, other, stop) on 1-bar clips at 300 BPM, at 64, 128 and 512 frames, with the control half up to 20 ms late: each lands on its bar line, playing just what it plays in step.
- `the_audio_half_allocates_nothing_in_any_period_while_the_control_half_works_on_its_own_thread` (Arrange 2.6a) splits the host with eight tracks of busy clips and a song, and runs the control half on a real thread doing what a musician does to a song while it plays: launches, scenes, stops, uncues, step taps, undo and redo, capture, mutes and song mode on and off, a few thousand actions, compiling each time. The audio half plays 4,000 periods meanwhile under the counting allocator: not one allocation or free in any whole period (taking what the control half sent, playing, and sending back what it no longer plays), nothing lost or kept.
- `the_halves_split_onto_two_threads_play_what_the_control_half_launches` splits the host and runs the control half on a real thread, polling every millisecond: the clip it launches plays from the first period the commands reach, on the bar lines, and nothing is lost.
