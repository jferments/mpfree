# The arrangement, the Song view and live to song (T13, T11)

**Status:** built and tested on the workstation, 2026-11-12: the arrangement and the Song view (T13), then live to song (T11), then the audio tracks' lanes in the Song view (layout spec 4c). Nothing here has run on the MPC.

The launch grid plays clips as you trigger them. The arrangement puts them on a timeline, so the same project can also play as a fixed song. A placement in the song (a block) names its clip by the clip's lasting id, so an edit to the clip shows wherever the song uses it, and the block follows the clip wherever it moves, until the block is made unique. Scenes and the song convert both ways, and live to song records what the grid plays into the song.

The model is in `rust/mpfree-seq/src/arrange.rs`, song-mode playback in `rust/mpfree-play`, live to song in `rust/mpfree-perform/src/song.rs`, and the Song view (with the audio tracks' lanes) in `rust/mpfree-time-ui/src/song.rs`.

## The model (`mpfree-seq`)

- **`Placement { track, start, length, offset, clip }`** puts a clip on a track's lane from song tick `start` for `length` ticks (the Arrange refactor's 1.3, D1).
  - `clip` is the clip's lasting `ClipId`, wherever the clip lives: in a cell of the grid, or among the song's own clips (`Arrangement::clips`, below). `Placement::new(track, clip, start, length)` makes one, and `Placement::of(tracks, track, row, start, length)` one of the clip in a cell (none for an empty cell or a clip with no id yet).
  - `Placement::clip(tracks, song_clips)`, `Arrangement::clip_of` and `Project::block_clip` find the clip: the block's own lane's cells first, then the song's clips, then the other tracks'. None when no clip has the id: the block plays nothing.
  - A block plays through its own lane's track (its instrument, routing, swing, transpose and note FX), whichever track's grid holds its clip.
  - A placement longer than its clip loops it.
  - `offset` starts it part way into the clip, in the clip's own ticks.
- **`Marker { at, name }`** names a place in the song, such as "Verse" or "Drop".
- **`Arrangement { placements, clips, markers }`** is `Project::arrangement`. The placements are kept in order by track, then start, and never overlap on a track. Up to 4096 placements, 4096 song clips and 256 markers.
  - **The song's own clips** (`Arrangement::clips`, a `SongClips`): clips no cell holds that blocks play: a block's copy made unique, or a deleted clip the song still plays. Each has its lasting id, kept in order of id, none twice, and no cell's clip has the same id (`Project::check`: a clip lives in one place). `get`, `get_mut`, `keep` (refuses a clip with no id or a taken one), `take`, `retain` and `iter`. Copies of the song share them (an `Arc`) until one changes, so a Song view undo step keeps the song's blocks, not its clips (`SongClips::shares`).
  - `place` puts a placement in its place, and refuses one that overlaps or is empty.
  - `remove`, `on_track`, `at(track, tick)` (the placement playing there) and `end`. `remove` lets go of a song clip the block was the last to play, and `clear` does the same through `drop_unplayed`.
  - `Project::make_unique(i)` gives block `i` a copy of its clip among the song's clips, with a new id (`Project::new_clip_id`), and returns the id it plays now. A block already playing a song clip no other block plays keeps it.
  - `plays(id)`: whether a block plays clip `id`.
  - `mark(at, name)` adds a marker.
  - `clear(tracks, track, from, to)` takes a track's placements out of a stretch. Placements inside it go. Placements across an edge are cut there, and a cut at the start moves the offset on, so the rest plays as before (`Placement::from(clip, at)` is the part after a cut).
  - `move_placement(tracks, i, track, start, copy)` moves placement `i`, or puts a copy of it, on a track from a tick, and returns its index. On another track's lane it stays linked to its clip and plays through that track. It refuses (`BadPlacement`) an overlap or a start before 0, and changes nothing then.
  - `set_length(i, length)` makes a placement longer or shorter, refused where it would run into the next one on its track.
  - **The block tools** (the Arrange refactor's phase 6; the Song view's buttons below, approved as drawn 23 Nov 2026 16:06Z, layout spec 4f). Each is refused (`BadPlacement`), changing nothing, where it can't be done, and none changes what the song plays but where the musician asked. `Placement::offset_by(clip, by)` is the offset `by` song ticks further into the clip, at its speed and going round its length (what `Placement::from` uses).
    - `split(tracks, i, at)` cuts block `i` in two at `at`, inside it: the part after starts there from where its clip had got to, so the song plays as it did, both parts on the same clip. Its start and end are no cut. `split_all(tracks, at)` splits every block across `at` on every lane (Split at the playhead with nothing picked), all or none, and says how many.
    - `duplicate(i)` puts a copy of block `i` right after it: the same clip, offset and length, from its end; refused where a block on its lane is in the way.
    - `trim_start(tracks, i, start)` moves block `i`'s start, its end staying, with the offset moving along, so what plays stays where it was in the song: a later start leaves out the clip's first ticks, an earlier one brings in the ticks before (past the clip's top, its pass before). Refused below 0, at or past its end, or into the block before.
    - `slide(tracks, i, by)` moves what block `i` plays `by` song ticks later under it (earlier for a negative `by`), the block keeping its place and length.
    - **In the time engine** each is a song edit UNDO takes back (`TimeControl::edit_song`): `TimeAction::SplitPlacement(Some(i))` cuts the picked block at the playhead and picks the part after, `SplitPlacement(None)` cuts every block across it ("song: 2 blocks split at bar 2 beat 3"), `DuplicatePlacement(i)` puts the copy and picks it (a `SongChange::Copied`), `TrimPlacement { index, start }` and `SlidePlacement { index, by }`; their takes are `SongChange::Split`, `Trimmed` and `Slid` ("split", "trim", "slide" in the Takes list). A refusal says why ("move the playhead inside Beat (Split cuts at the playhead)", "no room after Bass for its copy") and changes nothing.
    - **A split leaves a part following its scene**: blocks that meet with the clip where the one before had got to count as one when `mark_changed` compares what a part holds, so a block split in phase holds what it did whole. Trim, slide and a duplicate inside a part make it the song's own, as any edit inside does.
    - *Tests:* `split_duplicate_trim_and_slide_keep_what_plays_in_phase` (a half-speed clip's split too), `time::tests::split_duplicate_trim_and_slide_are_song_edits_undo_takes_back`, and the split in `an_edit_inside_a_section_makes_it_the_songs_own_and_a_whole_move_doesnt`.
  - **Several blocks at once** (the Arrange refactor's 6.5; the model and the time engine, and the Song view's picking below, approved as drawn 23 Nov 2026 16:05Z, layout spec 4h). Each takes a set of block indexes (any order, a repeat counts once) and goes all or none: one block that can't go refuses the lot, changing nothing, as does an empty set or an index past the blocks (`BadPlacement`).
    - `move_placements(tracks, set, lanes, by, copy)` moves every block in `set` `lanes` lanes down (up for a negative) and `by` song ticks later (earlier for a negative), or with `copy` puts a copy of each there and leaves the blocks, and gives back where the moved or copied blocks now are, in order. A block may not run into another block, start before the song or leave the lanes (`BadPlacement`), go to another lane from or onto a Loop track's (`BadLoop`), or land on an audio track's lane (`AudioTrack`); the blocks may land where blocks of the same set were.
    - `duplicate_placements(tracks, set)` puts a copy of the whole set right after it, by the set's span (its first start to its last end), each copy on its own lane, so a picked run of bars repeats as it played.
    - `remove_placements(set)` takes every block in `set` out of the song and says how many.
    - `split_these(tracks, set, at)` splits those of `set` that play across `at` there, in phase as `split` does, and gives back the parts after, in order; refused when none plays across it.
    - **In the time engine** each is one song edit UNDO takes back whole (`TimeControl::edit_song`): `TimeAction::MovePlacements { set, lanes, by, copy }`, `DuplicatePlacements(set)`, `DeletePlacements(set)` and `SplitPlacements(set)` (at the playhead), their takes `SongChange::SeveralMoved`, `SeveralCopied`, `SeveralDeleted` and `SeveralSplit` ("move · 3 blocks" in the Takes list). The message names the count, the lanes and the time in bars, beats or sixteenths ("song: 3 blocks moved a lane down and 2 bars later (UNDO takes it back)"); a refusal says why and changes nothing.
    - **What is picked.** `TimeControl::picks` (`TimeState::picks`) holds the blocks picked together, two or more, `selected` being None while they are; one left is `selected`, as a tap picks it (`set_picks`, which also lets go of a region, section or tempo point). `TimeAction::PickPlacement(i)` adds block `i` or takes it out (SHIFT + a tap), `PickPlacements { set, add }` picks a box's blocks or adds them, and `SelectPlacement` picks one and drops the rest. A move or copy picks the blocks where they are now (the copies for a copy), Duplicate picks the copies and Split the parts after; any other song edit lets go of them (the song-edit arm takes `picks` and gives them back if the edit is refused or doesn't compile), as do UNDO, REDO, Rec song's blocks going in and a conversion, as for `selected`. `state()` drops a pick past the blocks.
    - *Tests:* `several_blocks_move_copy_duplicate_and_go_together_all_or_none` (the split too), `time::tests::several_blocks_are_one_song_edit_and_undo_takes_them_back` and `time::tests::several_blocks_are_picked_and_stay_picked_where_an_edit_puts_them`.
  - `section(k, bar)` is marker `k` to the next, the last one to the song's end in whole bars from its marker. `move_section(tracks, k, gap, copy, bar)` moves section `k`, or puts a copy of it, before section `gap` (after the last for the markers' count), on every track, as one `TimeEdit` (below; the Arrange refactor's 3.2): `section_edit(k, gap, copy, bar)` is a `Move` of the section's ticks to the gap's marker (the last section's end for after the last), or a `Copy` there. The sections close up behind it and make room for it, so nothing is written over, and what comes before the first marker stays. Its blocks, marker, tempo points, morph moves and rows go with it; a block across an edge is cut there in phase and joined again where its pieces meet in step. The song list stays (it names rows). Refused, nothing changes: `Refused::BadRange` for no such section or gap, else what the edit says; the app says why in a musician's words (`refused_said` in `time/control.rs`). Takes the project's tempo for the tempo before the first point.
- **One edit of the song's time** (the Arrange refactor's 3.1, D6; `rust/mpfree-seq/src/time_edit.rs`). `Arrangement::edited(tracks, edit, project_bpm)` makes a new song with a `TimeEdit` applied to everything in it that has a time, all at once: the blocks (loop blocks too), the markers, the tempo points, the song's lanes and the morph's rows. Moving and copying a section goes through it (3.2), and so do insert and delete bars (3.3). The audio tracks' regions follow each one (3.4, `TimeEdit::pieces`: below).
  - `TimeEdit::Insert { at, len }`, `Delete { from, to }`, `Move { from, to, at }` and `Copy { from, to, at }`, in song ticks. Each is a list of parts laid end to end: stretches of the old song, each at a new place, and an insert's gap. A move swaps the moved range with what lies between it and `at`, so the song closes up behind it and makes room for it, and nothing is written over. `place_of(t)` says where tick `t` is after the edit (None for a deleted tick).
  - **Blocks** are cut where a part starts or ends, in phase (as `clear` cuts), and go with their part, on their own lane. Where two parts meet after the edit (a seam), two pieces of one block that meet with its clip exactly where it had got to are one block again, so a long block of a one-bar clip stays one block when whole bars move. Blocks placed apart stay apart, even where they meet in phase: an insert and its delete leave the song playing as it did, but a block the insert cut stays two.
  - **Markers** go with their part and keep their names; a copy copies them, a delete drops them.
  - **Tempo:** each part takes the tempo at its start with it, and a gap holds the tempo before it. A ramp cut by a part's end keeps its curve: if what follows starts where the ramp had got to, it arrives there; otherwise its last 1/16 holds, at a tempo worked out so the cut lands on the frame it did (the transport plays a ramp in 1/16 steps anyway). The ramp then arrives at that tempo, a hair off its old line: up to about 0.8 ms ahead at worst in the tests (a three-bar ramp from 70 BPM cut near its end). Points that change nothing are left out.
  - **The song's lanes** go through `mpfree_moves`' clip-lane edits (insert, cut, copy a range), so a lane keeps its values on the ticks the edit doesn't touch and a gap holds the value before it. **The morph's rows** go with their part, each part starting with the rows in force at its start.
  - A loop block plays in step with its loop's own origin (its offset is not built, [loop-tracks.md](loop-tracks.md)), so one moved by other than whole passes plays another part of its loop.
  - Refused, nothing changes and `Refused` says why: a bad range (past the song's lanes, empty, or a move into itself), too many blocks, markers or tempo points, two tempo points closer than a 1/16 (`TempoTooClose(at)`), the lanes, the morph's rows, or the finished song failing `check`.
- **Markers on their own** (the Arrange refactor's 3.3). `add_marker(at, name)` adds one and returns its index, `rename_marker(k, name)`, `move_marker(k, at)` (its index after: past another it changes places) and `remove_marker(k)` (its bars join the section before). Only the markers change, so a section's edge moves and nothing plays differently; moving what plays is a section move. Refused (`BadPlacement`), nothing changing: no such marker, before the start, an empty name, or a tick a marker has already (a section is never empty). `new_marker_name()` is the first letter A to Z no marker has, then "Section N".
- **The song's own lanes.** `Arrangement::lanes` holds lanes for parameters that are not on a track. For now that is the morph crossfader (`MORPH_LANE`, on `ParamAddr::morph_pos(0)`).
  - The lanes are on song ticks and never loop (`SONG_LANE_LEN`, about 72 hours at 120 BPM).
  - `Arrangement::morphs` holds the two rows the morph is between, from each tick on (`MorphRows { at, a, b }`).
  - `put_morph` writes recorded moves over what was there, and the rows the song had after them go on.
- **The song's tempo** (sequencer roadmap, tempo ramps). `Arrangement::tempo` holds `TempoPoint { at, bpm, ramp }`s in order, at least a 1/16 apart, at most `MAX_TEMPO_POINTS` (31, the tempo map's 32 segments less one for the project's tempo); `Arrangement::check` refuses anything else (`BadTempo`). From a point on the song plays its tempo, and with `ramp` it glides to the next point's tempo, arriving on that point; a ramp on the last point waits for a point after it. No points: the project's tempo throughout.
  - `tempo_map(project_bpm)` makes the transport's map: the project's tempo until the first point (unless that is on bar 1), then a segment a point, ramping where the point ramps ([transport.md](transport.md#tempo-ramps)).
  - `set_tempo(at, bpm, ramp)` puts a point in its place, or changes the one on that tick (keeping its ramp when `ramp` is None); refused (`BadTempo`) before the start, out of range (20 to 400 BPM), within a 1/16 of another point, or one too many. `remove_tempo(at)` takes one away.
  - Saved in the clips part as `tempo <tick> <bpm>[ ramp]` lines after the markers (`save.rs`); an older build skips them and plays the project's tempo.
  - The Song view's tempo lane (`mpfree-time-ui` `SongLanes::tempo`, `tempo_base`, `tempo_pick`; layout spec, the tempo lane): under the ruler, the song's tempo as a line with a dot on each point. A tap on the lane reports `Place { index: NONE, row: TEMPO_ROW, at }` (a new point on the nearest bar), a tap on a point `Cell { row: TEMPO_ROW, col }` (pick it), a drag `Place { index, row: TEMPO_ROW, at }` (move it to a bar). With a point picked the last two buttons read Ramp (lit while it ramps) and Delete.
  - `TimeAction::SongTempo(TempoEdit)` is what the lane's events ask for: `Add(at)`, `Pick(k)`, `Move { index, at }`, `Bpm { index, bpm }` (the wheel, with the point grabbed), `Ramp { index, on }`, `Delete(k)` and `AtPlayhead(bpm)` (the tempo turned in song mode). `TimeState::tempo_points`, `tempo_base` (the project's tempo), `tempo_pick` and `tempo_here` (the tempo of the point in effect at the playhead while song mode plays the points, which the Tempo row steps from: a ramp's start, not where it has glided to) carry them to the screen. See "In the pad player" below.
- **Lasting clip ids** (the Arrange refactor's 1.1, [plan](https://claude.ai/artifact/XP6DtDFASumN5TTTBA5Mqq), D1). Every clip in a cell, Loop track cells included, has a lasting `ClipId` (`Clip::id`), a number from 1 in one id space for every kind of clip, as tracks have `TrackId`s. `Project::next_clip` is the id the next clip gets; it only goes up, so no id is given twice, even after an UNDO takes its clip away. Nothing plays or shows differently yet: the ids are what the song's blocks will name in 1.3.
  - A new clip or a copy gets a new id as it goes in: `Project::put_new_clip(track, row, clip)` (whatever id the clip carried), or `Project::new_clip_id()`. `Clip::copied()` is a copy with no id yet. Recording into an empty slot, a capture, a MIDI file's clips, the Sequencer's first step, a paste, a copied clip or row on the grid, Song → scenes and a new loop cell all do this (`Takes::capture_clip`, `capture_clips`, `paste_clip`, `new_clip`, `drop_clip`, `drop_row`, `song_to_scenes`, `loops`, and the host's own puts in `time/control.rs`).
  - A moved clip keeps its id: a clip or a row dragged on the grid, a swap. A clip put in place of one (Reload, Mutate, Restore: `Takes::replace_clip`) is the same clip and keeps its id. UNDO and REDO bring a clip back with its own id, and never wind `next_clip` back.
  - `Project::find_clip(id)` says where a clip is now (`ClipAt::Cell { track, row }`); `Project::cell_clips()` lists every clip in a cell in track and row order.
  - `Project::give_clip_ids()` gives a clip with no id, or one an earlier clip has too, the next id. `TimeHost::with_project` runs it, so every clip has an id from then on; a project saved before clips had ids gets 1, 2, 3 ... in track and row order, saying nothing, and one that says the next id but has a clip without one or two sharing one gets new ones for those, said (`project::opening`).
  - The song's own clips have ids from the same space (`ClipAt::Song`); `give_clip_ids` covers them after the cells.
  - Saved in the clips part: `nextclip <n>` before the tracks and `id <n>` first in each clip's body.
- **Rows by number** (the Arrange refactor's 1.2, D1). A track keeps its clips by row number (`Track::slots`, a `mpfree_seq::Rows`), up to 64 rows (`ROWS`, four banks of 16), and the scenes go to row 63 (`SCENES = ROWS`); a track's list is as long as its last clip and the scenes' as the last one set up, so rows nobody uses cost nothing. Nothing shows differently yet: the Clips grid shows the first 16 rows (`SLOTS`, `Rows::shown`) until scene banks come (phase 5).
  - `rows[r]` reads row `r` (empty past the last clip) and `rows[r] = ...` writes it; `Rows::get` and `get_mut` do the same and say None past row 63; `Rows::iter` lists the clips with their rows, `clips` the clips alone, `first_free` the first empty row of the grid's. Two are equal when they hold the same clips in the same rows.
  - Looked up anywhere: a launch, a scene, a song block's clip in any row, a morph row, a song list step, a saved row (`clip`, `scene` and `place` lines up to 63), a loop cell, Scenes → song (every row with a clip, not only the grid's) and the takes (`Takes::rows_edit` scans all 64).
  - On the grid: what looks for a free row stays within the grid's 16 (recording into a new clip, a capture, a MIDI file's clips, a phrase, Song → scenes' run of empty rows), and a row dragged or copied moves only the grid's rows: rows 16 to 63 and their scenes stay where they are (`ClipDrop::after` and `before`). A copy still needs the grid's last row empty.
  - Follow actions move within their bank of 16 rows (`mpfree_seq::bank`, [scenes.md](scenes.md#the-model-mpfree-seq)).
- **Song blocks name their clips** (the Arrange refactor's 1.3, D1). A block names its clip by id, so nothing about the song changes when a clip moves:
  - **Clip and row drags leave the song alone.** A clip dragged to another cell, of its own track or another's, or its row dragged, keeps its id, so its blocks play it on from their own lanes (`Takes::drop_clip` and `drop_row` no longer rewrite blocks). The rows' scenes, the morph's rows and the song list's steps still follow a dragged row, since they name rows. The exception is a linked list's parts that follow a row the drag changed: they are made again from it (see "The song's parts follow their rows" under phase 4b).
  - **A block dragged to another lane stays linked** and plays through that lane's track; no copy is made.
  - **Deleting a clip the song plays** moves it, with its id, to the song's own clips, so the song plays on: the cell empties, and one UNDO puts it back (`Takes::remove_clip`, `Take::kept_by_song`). REDO moves it to the song's clips again. A cell filled meanwhile keeps its new clip, and the song keeps its own. The log says "erase: clip 2 of track 1 deleted; the song keeps playing it (UNDO brings it back)".
  - **A block whose clip is gone** (an undone capture, say) stays in the song and plays nothing, until the clip comes back with its id. `Project::check` lets it pass.
  - **Rec song** names the clip each block plays by its id (`SongRecorder::finish`), and so do Scenes → song and List → song, which are what Rec song recorded. The rule-based `scenes_to_song` names each row's clips by id, and refuses (`BadPlacement`) a clip with no id yet.
  - **The player** finds each block's clip through a table of where each cell's clip is, made once a compile (`PlayView::set_song`), checks each placed clip once, and keys a block for the player by its row when it plays its own lane's cell clip, else `ROWS + i`, past every row.
  - The Song view marks a block unique (`PlacementInfo::unique`) when its clip is one of the song's own.
  - **Saved as `mpfree-clips 2`** (decision 7): blocks are `place <track> <start> <length> <offset> clip <id>`, and the song's own clips come before them, each once as `songclip <length> <name>` with its body and `end`. An older build doesn't open a part saved this way. An `mpfree-clips 1` part still opens: its clips get ids in track and row order, each `slot <row>` block names its cell's clip (none for an empty cell, which plays nothing as before), and each `own` block's clip becomes a song clip with the next id, in order (`save::name_old_blocks`).
- `Arrangement::check` keeps the values in range (`ModelError::BadPlacement`), and `Project::check` runs it.

## Scenes and the song

- **Every conversion is exact or says how it is not** (plan D7). Each returns its result with a list of `Difference { track, at, what: Differs }`: what plays differently, on which track (or the whole song) and where in song ticks. `Difference::said` puts one in a musician's words ("Bass, bar 9: a note held over the row change is cut there"), and the host's log line ends ", exact. UNDO takes it back." (`time::EXACT`) or ", 2 differences (UNDO takes it back): …" with each one (`conversion_said` in the time engine). One with differences also opens the report card (below). Since phase 4 (4.1) Scenes → song and List → song play the rows through Rec song, so the one thing they name is a scene's program changes.
- **Scenes → song and List → song play the rows** (the Arrange refactor's 4.1, plan D7; `time/offline.rs` in the app, `offline::play_rows`). The steps play offline, faster than real time, through a time engine of their own: a `TimeHost` with a copy of the project (the real transport, player, Perform and its song list) with Rec song armed, and the song is what Rec song recorded. So whatever the rows do when they play comes out right because it really played: the mutes a scene leaves for the next (a track a scene muted gets no blocks until a scene unmutes it), a one-shot's block ending with its pass, a clip's follow action moving it to another clip, and a legato clip taking over its track's place. The copy plays its clips bare (`offline::bare`): what decides where a block starts and ends stays (id, length, speed, launch line, follow action, legato, one-shot, loop cell) and what only sounds goes (notes, locks, moves, pressure), so its periods play the launches and nothing else.
  - **What plays.** Scenes → song plays every row with a clip, in order, once each (every row the project keeps, not only the grid's); List → song plays the song list's steps once from the first, with Loop the list or without. Both play them as the song list plays (`Perform::play_list`), so each step lasts its span (`list_spans`: its scene's `scene_length`, its follow action's bars or else its longest clip rounded up to whole bars, times its times), the scenes' own follow actions wait, and the clips' follow actions play. An empty row in the list is a silent step: the tracks playing stop on its line.
  - **The copy** has an empty song (nothing of the song plays or is recorded over), every track heard and none soloed (the scenes' mutes carry from none: the song can't hold a mute set by hand), and the steps as its song list, not going round. It is located at the first bar line after the song's end, where the blocks go, launches the first step at once, plays, and stops where the last step ends, where the list stops every track. Rec song off puts what it recorded into the copy's song (`SongRecorder::finish`), and the blocks come from there.
  - **The same every time.** The copy's Perform starts from its fixed seed (`Perform::default`), so a follow with a chance rolls the same every time and a project converts the same way twice.
  - **Fast.** Only ticks are kept, and nothing that decides where a block starts or ends counts frames (cues land on bar lines, follow actions and one-shots on ticks), so the copy plays at 400 BPM and 22,050 Hz (`FAST_BPM`, `FAST_RATE`) in periods of 2,048 frames, 594 ticks: under a quarter of a 4/4 bar, so every follow action and step is cued in the bar before its line, as it is live. On the desktop (release, `print_what_the_conversions_cost`), the big song's rows (16 tracks of 4-bar clips of 1,000 notes) took 4.7 ms for 8 bars and 5.3 ms for 200, most of it building the copy. Together that time comes out of a period on the audio thread, like any edit; apart (`--time-thread`) it runs on the control half's thread, since the screen's conversions go as asks.
  - **Markers** go where each step started (from `Perform::list` as the copy plays), named after the scene ("Scene 3" when it has no name).
  - **Named, not carried:** a scene's program changes (`SceneProgram`), which the song doesn't send. Phase 4b's linked list brings them in. A scene's tempo is carried, as a tempo point where its step starts (5.2b, "Scene tempo" below).
  - **Refused, and nothing changes:** rows that play more than 9,999 bars (`offline::MAX_BARS`), a song that would hold more than `MAX_PLACEMENTS` blocks (Rec song stops on the line where it would), or a copy that refuses the project. The blocks and markers go into a copy of the song, checked, which then replaces it, so an error on the way leaves the song as it was. Each conversion is one UNDO.

A block that carries on with a program change sends it again at the edge.

## Song mode (`mpfree-play`)

`PlayView::set_song(project, on)` compiles the arrangement into each track's regions, and turns song mode on or off. **Each clip the song plays is compiled once** (Arrange 2.3, design D2), into the view's song clips (`PlayView::song_clips`, each with what it was compiled from, a `SongKey`: its cell or the song's own clips, its id, its slot and its swing). A block (`RegionView { start, end, origin, clip }`) names its copy by place, so a block costs four numbers and the view holds the clips the song plays, not a copy of a clip for every block. A clip gets a second copy only where its blocks play it differently: on its own cell's lane (by its row) and on other lanes (by a slot past every row, `ROWS` + its id, the same for every such block and through edits that add blocks, so it never mixes with a cell's clip), and once for each swing its lanes play it with (its own, else its lane's track's). An edit to a cell's clip goes into its copies in place (`PlayView::edit_in_place`, `record`, `add_note` and the rest look them up by the cell, `song_copies`), so every block of it hears the change from the next note it plays, and `TimeControl::in_place` no longer refuses song mode: a tap on a clip the song places is sent to the audio half like a tap on the clip playing (`TimeControl::song_places`). `PlayView::make_room_on(track)` sets room aside in the song's copies of that track's clips too, so the armed track and the one the Sequencer edits apart from the arm (step sequencer refactor C3) take song-mode taps in place. A clip of the song's own (`Arrangement::clips`, made unique) is edited from the Song view, which still compiles.

- **A track with no clip launched plays the song.** The player plays the region at each tick, from the placement's start, looping, from its offset.
- **A launched clip takes the track over.** It plays in place of the song until it stops, and then the song plays on there from where it has got to.
- **Edges.** At a placement's end, notes still held end on that tick, the clip's locks end, and ratchet hits after it are dropped. Trig conditions count passes from each placement's start.
- **Moves chase.** A placement that starts part way into a lane starts it at the lane's value there.
- **Jumps.** A locate or a loop jump lands in whichever placement is at the new tick, at the right place in its clip.
- **The song's own lanes** play in song mode as events on track `SONG_TRACK` (one past the last track), after every track's. They chase after a locate. When song mode goes off, their parameters are let go (`Unlock`).
- **Real-time safe.** Song mode allocates nothing in `process`. `tests/no_alloc.rs` plays a track from placements, linked and unique, and the morph lane, under the counting allocator.

## Live to song (T11, `mpfree-perform`)

**Rec song** records what the launch grid plays into the song, so a jam becomes a song to edit. `SongRecorder` is control-side code. In the pad player, `TimeHost` feeds it from its period, which runs in the audio loop until edits move to a control thread. A period where nothing starts or stops allocates nothing (`tests/song_no_alloc.rs`), while a clip starting or stopping and writing the song at a stop allocate, like any edit there today. While it is armed and the transport plays, it follows each track:

- **Launches and stops.** A clip that starts on a track becomes a placement linked to its slot, from that tick and at the right place in the clip. That covers a launch, a scene and a follow action. The placement ends where the track switches clip or stops.
- **Mutes.** A muted stretch is left out of the song. The placement ends where the track is muted, and a new one starts where it is heard again, in phase with the clip. A scene's mutes come with its cue. A mute made by hand comes through `SongRecorder::mute`.
- **The morph.** Crossfader moves go into the song's morph lane, with the rows they morph between. A pause of a 16th note or more (`MORPH_HOLD`) is held, so a move after it starts from where the crossfader sat instead of ramping across the pause. A drag's moves come closer together, so a drag stays one ramp. A pass with no move leaves the lane as it was.
- **Tracks playing the song are not recorded.** On a track with no clip launched, the song stays as it is. Where a launched clip played a track, what the song had there is replaced, like recording over tape.
- **Passes.** A pass ends where the transport stops or jumps (a locate or a loop jump), and a new one starts where it lands. Each loop pass records over the last.
- **When it goes in.** Nothing goes into the project until the transport stops or Rec song is turned off (`SongRecorder::finish`). That way the song does not change under the player while it plays.
  - On an error, nothing changes and the recording is kept.
  - **It is one UNDO** (audio tracks phase 2, step 2d; `TimeControl::keep_song`): what it put in is a song edit (`SongChange::RecSong`, "Rec song N · 12 blocks" in the takes list). The blocks it put on audio tracks' lanes are handed to the audio tracks once (`TimeHost::take_song_placed`: the edit's number and each block's span by lasting id), which replace the regions under them as a step tagged with that number and say so (`TimeHost::tie_audio`); from then on the song's UNDO and REDO take that step back and bring it back with the blocks, as for a time edit (below). No region under them: nothing is tied.
- **It keeps within the song's room** (`MAX_PLACEMENTS`, 4,096 blocks), so a long jam never fails to go in at the stop. The host tells the recorder how many blocks the song has before each period (`SongRecorder::song_blocks`), and `SongRecorder::worst` counts the most the song would hold if the recording went in now: the song's blocks, every block heard and the one open on each track, and one more for each stretch it records over (where a block of the song may be cut in two).
  - Before a line's cues switch anything, it counts what they could add (a block for each clip started, and a stretch for a track that had none), and before a pass after a jump what that pass could add (a stretch and, heard, a block for each track playing). If that wouldn't fit, recording stops on every track at that line or jump: the pass ends there and the recorder disarms, so it never ends halfway through a scene change. The cues still play; only the recording stops.
  - The host says so once (`take_full`): "rec song: the song is full (4096 blocks), so recording stopped on every track at bar 9; what it recorded is kept", and what was recorded goes into the song as at a stop. The first time the count reaches 90 % while armed it warns (`take_warning`): "rec song: the song is 92% full (3770 of 4096 blocks); recording stops on a launch line before it fills". The warning is a log and hint line for now; a gauge on the screen would be a layout spec change.
  - The checks walk the period's starts line by line and allocate nothing (`tests/song_no_alloc.rs` still passes).
- **The song plays back what was heard.** `tests/live.rs` jams with launches, scenes with mutes, stops, a launch over the song and a mid-clip mute, then plays the song alone. The same notes come out on the same frames, at any period size.
- **Tracks muted at the end stay muted.** In the song their gaps already hold the mutes, so turn the mutes off to hear the song as it was played.

The audio tracks (`rust/mpfree-audiotracks`, the audio tracks thread's engine: see [audio-tracks.md](audio-tracks.md)) are tracks in the one track list (`TrackKind::Audio`, phase 1 of the audio tracks rethink), so each gets a lane in its place among the tracks, as Jay approved on 22 Nov (layout spec 4c; the lanes themselves are the 12 Nov mockups A to C in `/mnt/project-files/audiotracks/mockup/`). The Song view draws them; the app wires the engine (`AudioHost`), the track panel, the region tools and the Q-Links.

- **What the app gives.** `TimeState::audio` is a list of `AudioLane`s, one for each audio track in the project, in track order: the engine track, its place in the track list (`row`, which the lane is drawn at), its name and colour (`view::audio_color`), armed, its input (`mpfree_audiotracks::Input`, shown as the chip "L", "R", "L+R" or "ST": `song::input_chip`), the level for the header's bar (0 to 1), its level and pan, monitor mode, mute and solo (for the panel and the Q-Links), its blocks and, while it records, the punch in. `song::audio_blocks(track, regions, takes, map, rate)` makes the blocks from the engine's `regions(track)` and `takes()`: each keeps its ticks, take, source frame and fades, the frames it spans along the tempo map (`frames_from`), and a "take N" tag when it plays an older take than the track's newest (N counts the track's takes from 1, oldest first by group and pass). `TimeState::waves` holds the `TakeWave`s read so far (`AudioHost::wave`). `TimeState::audio_free` (seconds free and in all) goes on the legend as "Audio 0:34 of 1:00 free", and `TimeState::audio_counts` (the engine's refused, dropped and full, and the streaming misses and errors) are added to the legend while not zero, in the warning colour: a loss is never hidden.
- **The lanes.** In track order: an audio track's lane is at its `row`, between the note tracks' lanes, with the same ruler, playhead, bar scrolling and lane height (56 px at most, 32 px at least); its take lanes, when open, come under it. "+ Track" is the last lane (`SongLanes::lane_count`, `audio_at`, `track_k`, `audio_k`). There is no divider and no "+ Audio": the chooser's Audio track adds one (`TimeAction::AddAudioTrack`) while an engine track is free (8). A region is a block in the track's colour with the take's waveform across it (each pixel column shows the lowest and highest sample of the frames under it), its name (with the tag) over its top, and each fade as a slope with the part above it dimmed. While a track records, a red block runs from its punch in to the playhead.
- **The header** (the 96 px name column): an arm dot (filled red while armed), the name in the track's colour, the input chip, and a 3 px level bar along the bottom edge (red from 95 %).
- **The track panel.** While `TimeState::audio_panel` names a track with a lane, the Song view draws its panel over the lanes, right of the names: under the track's lane if it fits, else over it, else on the side with more room (`SongLanes::panel_parts`). It has the name with "armed" or "recording", Close, Arm, Mute, Solo, Takes, the input (`song::INPUTS`), the monitor mode (`song::MONITORS`), and the level and pan, each with a step down and up. The header whose panel is open has an accent outline. A touch on the panel goes to it alone; the names stay live, so another header opens its own panel. Sample screen: `arrange-song-panel`.
- **Take lanes.** While `TimeState::audio_takes` names a track with a lane, its takes (`AudioLane::takes`, from `song::take_lanes`) show as thin lanes (`TAKE_H`, 28 px) under it, newest first, each named in the name column ("take 2", with "· rec" while it records). A take lane shows the take's stretch with its waveform, brighter where the track plays it (`TakeLane::used`). The other lanes share what is left (32 px at least, then they scroll), so lane heights now differ: `lane_rect` adds them up from the first lane shown, and `max_first` is the last lane that can be first. A finger down on a take lane (past the names) starts a swipe, drawn as an outline, which ends on the lift as `Span { index: take, from, to }` in song ticks (`tick_at`), either way round; a lift within 8 px of where it went down is a tap, `Span` with `from == to`. Sample screen: `arrange-song-takes`.
- **Scrolling the lanes.** When the lanes don't fit at 32 px each, a drag up or down the name column scrolls them, and a thin bar at the left edge shows where you are. The scroll is the widget's own (`SongLanes::first_lane`), kept across updates.
- **Touches** (`view::action`), as `TimeAction::Audio(AudioAction)`:
  - a region: `Pick(Some(RegionId { track, index }))` (an index into `regions(track)`) as the finger goes down; dragged sideways more than 8 px and let go (an outline shows where it would land), `Move { region, to, fine: false }` with its new start (never before 0), from the widget's `Marker { index: track * REGION_SLOTS + index, frame: to }`; the app sets `fine` while SHIFT is held;
  - the arm dot: `Arm { track, on }`; the name or the chip: `Panel(track)` (on the lift, unless the finger scrolled);
  - "+ Track": `Tab(ADD_TRACK)` opens the chooser, and its Audio track row is `TimeAction::AddAudioTrack` (there is no `AudioAction::Add` any more);
  - a take lane: a tap `UseTake(take)`, a swipe `Comp { take, from, to, fine: false }` (the app sets `fine` while SHIFT is held);
  - the panel, which asks for its track (`audio_panel`): Close `ClosePanel`, Arm `Arm`, Mute and Solo `Mute { track, on }` and `Solo { track, on }` (on is the opposite of the lane's), Takes `Takes(track)`, an input `Input { track, input }`, a monitor mode `Monitor { track, monitor }`, and the level's and pan's steps `Turn { track, region, knob: 0 or 1, ticks: -1 or 1, fine: false }` (region: the picked region, if it is the track's);
  - with a region picked, the bottom row is Split · Erase · Fades · Gain · Takes · Undo: `Split(r)`, `Erase(r)`, `Fades(r)`, `Gain(r)`, `Takes(track)` and `Undo`. Rec song still works, and empty space picks nothing.
- **The selection has a kind.** `TimeState::pick()` gives `Pick::Placement(i)` or `Pick::Region(r)`; `TimeHost` keeps them apart (picking a region drops the placement, and `SelectPlacement` drops the region). The app's Q-Links follow it.
- **Waveforms are copied only when they change.** `song_lanes` leaves them out, and `TimePage::update` copies `st.waves` only when a take, its length or its detail differs (`song::same_waves`), as they can be large.
- **Sample screen:** `arrange-song-audio` (`sample::song_audio_state`) records real takes into a real `AudioTracks`: a guitar over bars 1 to 4, a vocal over bars 1 to 8 and again over 5 to 8 (that region picked, with fades), the guitar armed.

## In the pad player (`TimeHost`)

- `act(SongView)` swaps the view, and `act(SongMode)` turns song mode on and compiles.
- `act(ScenesToSong)`, `SongToScenes`, `MakeUnique`, `DeletePlacement`, `MovePlacement`, `StretchPlacement` and `MoveSection` edit the project, and compile. If the edit or the compile fails, the project goes back to what it was, and the log says why (the screen says it in place of the hint for a drag or a delete).
- **Undo.** Every one of those but Song → scenes (which changes the rows) is an edit take holding the song before and after it (`Takes::song_edit`, `Take::song` with a `SongChange` naming it: "Move 5 · Bass, to bar 9" in the takes list). UNDO puts the song back as it was and redo as it became, while nothing else has changed the song in between; otherwise they leave it. A moved block stays picked at its new index, and a moved section stays picked; an undo or redo of a song edit drops the pick.
- **Song → scenes is one undo too.** `Takes::rows_edit` compares the project before and after and keeps every cell it changed (a clip put in goes in `Take::made`, one taken out in `Take::removed`) and every row's whole scene before and after (`Take::rows`, a `RowsEdit`). UNDO takes the clips out and gives each row its scene back, and redo fills them again, each cell and scene only while it is as the edit left it ("Song → scenes 7 · rows 5 to 7" in the takes list). A refused conversion (no run of empty rows long enough, or a compile that fails) leaves the project and the takes as they were.
- **The song's tempo.** `act(SongTempo(..))` edits the song's tempo points (`TimeControl::edit_tempo`): a tap on the lane adds a point on the bar with the tempo playing there (rounded to 0.1 BPM) and picks it; a point dragged onto a bar with a point, or within a 1/16 of one, is refused with why. Each is a song edit UNDO takes back (`SongChange::Tempo { at }`, "Tempo 4 · bar 9" in the takes list); the wheel's turns on one point and the tempo's turns at the playhead are one undo (`Takes::song_edit_joining`, keyed by the point's tick, while nothing else changed the song). Tempi are kept to the app's range, 30 to 300 BPM.
  - **Song mode plays them.** Every compile puts `Arrangement::tempo_map(project tempo)` in the view while song mode is on and the song has points (`PlayView::set_tempo_map`), so the map reaches the audio half with the song it goes with. Each period `TimeAudio::follow_tempo` hands a new map to the transport (`Transport::change_tempo_map`: on the next 1/16 while rolling, at once while stopped) and, when the view has none, puts the app's tempo back throughout (a map left in the transport is flattened too). A map on its way counts as the transport's, so it is asked for once; a tempo asked for after it (a followed clock's) forgets it. While a followed clock arrives its tempo wins, as before. The looper's start on a note (`start_at`) leaves out its tempo while the song's points play: they lead.
  - **The project's tempo** is the app's (`App::settings.bpm`: Play's tempo with no points, TAP TEMPO, a project opened), and plays before the first point. `TimeHost::follow_tempo` gives it to the control half each period (`TimeControl::project_tempo`), which compiles again only when it changed while song mode plays points after bar 1.
  - **The tempo turned in song mode** (Jay's card, 18 Nov 10:17Z, "Set the tempo at the playhead"): while song mode plays the points (`TimeHost::song_tempo_plays`), the app turns `SetTempo` from the Tempo row, a typed tempo or a learned tempo knob into `SongTempo(AtPlayhead(bpm))`: the point in effect at the playhead takes it, or before the first point a new one on the playhead's bar. The project's tempo stays. TAP TEMPO still sets the project's tempo.
  - **What follows the transport's tempo.** The synth's tempo and the effects' (`mixing::ctx`) are the transport's (`TimeHost::info().bpm`), so a synced delay and the synth's synced LFOs follow the points and ramps; warped pads already did. Note Repeat and an arp free-running while stopped keep the app's tempo; playing, the arps follow the transport's clock.
- **Bars and markers** (the Arrange refactor's 3.3; the Song view's tools for them below). `act(SongBars(BarsEdit::Insert { at, bars }))` puts empty bars in at a bar line and `SongBars(BarsEdit::Delete { from, to })` takes bars out, each one `TimeEdit` with the project's tempo, so every block, marker, tempo point, morph move and row after them moves; the log says "song: 2 empty bars at bar 5; everything after moved on (UNDO takes them out)" or "song: bar 2 taken out; everything after moved back (UNDO puts them back)", and a refusal says why (`refused_said`). The pick, the section picked and the tempo point picked go, as their indices may change. `act(SongMarker(MarkerEdit::..))` adds a marker on a tick, named with the next free letter and picked (`Add`), renames one (`Rename`, the name trimmed; an empty one refused), moves one (`Move`, picked after) or takes one away (`Delete`), with lines such as "song: marker A at bar 5 (UNDO takes it away)" and "song: A is Bridge now". Each is a song edit take (`SongChange::Bars { at, len, inserted }`, `SongChange::Marker { at }`: "Bars 7 · bar 9", "2 bars put in"; "Marker 8 · Bridge", "at bar 9" in the takes list). The playhead and the loop range stay where they are.
  - `BarsEdit::RoomBefore(k)` puts one empty bar in at section `k`'s marker and keeps the section picked; `BarsEdit::DeleteSection(k)` takes section `k`'s bars out (marker to the next, the last to the song's end). Presses of + Bar in a row are one take (`song_edit_joining` with the key `ROOM_BEFORE_JOIN | k`), and bars put in one after the other add up in its `SongChange::Bars`, so the takes list says "2 bars put in".
  - **On the Song view** (layout spec 4h, Jay's yes 22 Nov 06:55Z): a tap on the ruler past the last name it shows (`Place { index: NONE, row: RULER_ROW, at }`, the nearest bar) is `MarkerEdit::Add`. A section picked by a tap on its name has a grip (`GRIP_W` 28 px over the ruler's height, just left of its marker; `Held::MarkerGrip`) that drags along the ruler snapped to the bar with a "Verse from bar 6" tag and, lifted elsewhere, is `Place { index: k, row: RULER_ROW, at }`, `MarkerEdit::Move`. While a section is picked and no tempo point is, the bottom row's last four buttons are `SECTION_BUTTONS` (+ Bar, Rename, Remove marker, Delete bars: `Tab(2..=5)`; `view::section_buttons(st)`), mapped by `action()` to `RoomBefore`, `MarkerEdit::Delete` and `DeleteSection`; Rename (`view::song_rename`) is the app's: it opens the text keyboard ("Name marker", 32 letters, `TypeTo::MarkerName`) and Enter sends `MarkerEdit::Rename`. Delete bars is guarded in Perform (`guarded`: `section-button` "Delete bars"). The hint line is `SONG_SECTION_HINT` while a section is picked; the app refreshes it whenever `song_hint` changes with a new state. Preview screen `arrange-song-section`.
- **A recording over a block cuts the block** (audio tracks phase 2, step 2d; `TimeHost::audio_took`). A lane holds regions and blocks, never one over the other, so when a take or an import placing regions ends on an audio lane, the blocks under its span are cut at its edges, the parts inside going and the parts outside playing on as before. The cut is a song edit of its own (`SongChange::Recorded`, "Recorded over N · Vocal"), and its number comes back for the audio tracks to tag the take's step with, so either UNDO takes back both. Where cutting a block in two needs more blocks than the song holds, the recording wins: the whole block goes, said and counted in `describe`.
- **The audio tracks' regions move with the song** (the Arrange refactor's 3.4). A section moved or copied, + Bar and Delete bars go through `TimeHost::act_with_audio` while the audio tracks hold a region or a song edit's step (`main.rs`'s `time_action`), as do UNDO and REDO. `TimeControl::act_with_audio` makes the edit's `Retime` from its `TimeEdit::pieces()` (`retime_of`), asks the tracks first (`SongAudio::refused`), edits the song, and then sends the tracks the same edit numbered by the song edit's take. The tracks apply it at the start of the next period, the first one the song's edit plays in, so both move together; a refusal changes nothing anywhere and is said ("song: nothing moved: Vocal is recording (stop it first)"). An edit that moves only audio (bars put in after everything else) is still a take, so UNDO takes the audio back, and + Bar pressed again after the regions changed is a take of its own, since one UNDO couldn't take both presses back from them. Apart (`--time-thread`) the control half is lent for these (`Apart::lend`), so the edit, its answer and the regions still go together.
  - UNDO of such a take takes the tracks' step back with it (`SongUndo`). With the regions edited since, UNDO waits and says to take that back first with the region's Undo ("UNDO waits: …"). With the step gone (the tracks' history ran past it, or was cleared), the song goes back, the regions stay where they are, and it is said and counted in the status line ("1 song edits undone with the audio regions left where they were"). REDO brings the step back (`SongRedo`); gone, it makes the same edits again on the regions as they are now (`TimeControl::audio_edits` keeps them by take).
- Every compile calls `PlayView::set_song`.
- **Launches land in phase even when the view is late** (Arrange 2.5, the timing contract). Every view says when and in which revision each track's cue and scene program change were decided, so a launch, a scene or a follow action whose view reaches the audio half after its line starts at once, in phase with the line, and a newer launch on a track replaces a waiting one. The status line counts what came late. See [playback.md](playback.md#launching-on-the-bar-line-cues-t9) and [time-threads.md](time-threads.md#where-time-matters).
- **The editing side on its own thread** (Arrange 2.6b, `--time-thread`, off by default until a device round). The song's edits, launches, scenes, undo and redo, mutes and step taps go to the time engine's control half on a thread of its own and return at once; what they say comes back with the same words, and the pages read a copy published after each change. In a song of 512 blocks a launch cost the audio thread 0.84 ms together and 0.018 ms apart on the desktop. See [time-threads.md](time-threads.md#apart-the-control-half-on-its-own-thread).
- `act(RecSong)` arms or disarms live to song. After every period the recorder sees the transport's block and the cues that started. When the transport stops, the recording goes into the song and compiles, and the log says how many placements and morph points went in (`TimeHost::take_song_note`).
- While recording, the morph bar's moves go into the song as they reach the mixer's mod engine (`TimeHost::morph`, see [scenes.md](scenes.md#the-scene-morph-t10-mpfree_performmorph)): each crossfader position, and new rows chosen while morphing.
- **Saved and opened with the project.** `mpfree_seq::save::to_text` writes the whole `Project` (tracks, clips, scenes, the song) as the `clips` part, and `from_text` reads it back, checked (`Project::check`), skipping lines it doesn't know. An open builds a new `TimeHost` from it on the screen thread (`TimeHost::with_project`) and the audio loop swaps it in with the transport stopped; the old one is freed on the screen thread. See [browser.md](browser.md#projects-in-the-pad-player).
