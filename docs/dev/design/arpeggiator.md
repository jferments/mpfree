# Arpeggiator, Latch and latch patterns

**Status:** design and first engine, 2026-11-10; owner decisions recorded below. The engine is [`rust/mpfree-arp`](../../../rust/mpfree-arp) (host-only, unit-tested, not yet wired to the pads). Nothing here has run on the MPC.

## What the owner asked for

From the round 4 notes (2026-11-10): get a **Latch** button working, and start separate work on **arpeggiator and latch pattern tools**. Then: research what people value in high-quality arpeggiators, what makes one great, how to make it intuitive yet flexible for many playing styles, and how to let people **easily design custom arp patterns**.

The Latch button itself (and Note Repeat, Full/Half Level, velocity) is being wired into the pad player in the Hello MPC work (`docs/dev/guides/mpfree-app.md` in PR #31). This document designs everything that sits behind those buttons, and `mpfree-arp` is the engine both use.

## What the research says

Sources are listed at the end. Where a point comes from general knowledge of a product rather than a page read for this document, it says so.

### What makes an arpeggiator great

1. **It is immediate.** Press one button, hold notes, and something musical happens with good defaults. Users on KVR asked for arps that "work quickly and are easy to pick up, not one that takes study"; reviewers of the KeyStep mk2 and Hydrasynth praised hands-on control and criticised menu diving and Shift combinations. Cthulhu and Digitone's arp are called powerful but "not very immediate" with "a steep learning curve".
2. **The basics are complete.** Every good arp has: order (up, down, up-down with and without repeated ends, as played, random, chord), octave range, rate including triplets, gate (note length), swing, latch/hold, and played or fixed velocity. Novation's SL MkIII, Logic, Ableton, Akai Force and the KeyStep all share this core.
3. **Note order goes beyond up and down.** Logic offers outside-in and inside-out orders, four variations per order, inversions in place of octaves, and random without repeats. Ableton has Converge, Diverge, Play Order, Chord Trigger, Random Other (no repeat until all notes played) and Random Once (one random order, repeated until the notes change), plus a Pattern Offset that rotates the start.
4. **Latch has more than one meaning, and players want the right one.** Reset (a fresh press replaces the chord), Add (new notes join), toggle (Ableton's Hold: re-pressing a note removes it), and Logic's Transpose and Gated Transpose (a new key moves the whole latched pattern). Akai Force lets a sustain pedal latch, in Reset or Add mode. Sound On Sound notes the Pro One-style latch that adds held keys "found little musical use", so Reset is the right default.
5. **Rhythm is separate from note order.** Novation's rhythm pattern toggles steps on and off without changing the order; step probability (0–100 %) per step; Akai Force has Note Repeat, Rhythm and Pattern actions, and a Spread control that strums held notes. Ratchets (several hits in one step) with a chance setting are a highlight of the Hydrasynth; the KeyStep mk2 review lists missing ratchets and probability as faults.
6. **Variation without losing the original.** KeyStep mk2's Mutate makes new lines from the current one (a long press changes more), and Shift+Mutate restores the original; reviewers called it "invaluable, especially in live jams" but noted it can be "unpredictable" with chords. Its Spice/Dice changes rhythm and velocity without touching pitch. Novation users ask for Deviate and Mutate. Ultra's arp gives each lane its own random amount under a master random knob, and re-randomises on every pass in Cubase's pattern editor.
7. **Pitch tools keep it in key.** Scale snapping is one of the most requested features (Elektronauts, Digitone threads). Per-step semitone offsets turn one held note into a phrase (Elektron offsets, Ultra's sequencer mode, Sylenth1's step mode, Akai Force's Pattern action where the held note transposes a phrase). Digitone users complained that the scale was not applied to the offsets, so offsets landed out of key: whatever we build must apply the scale after every offset. Ableton's Distance and Steps transpose the whole pattern each repeat (by semitones or scale degrees).
8. **Velocity and feel.** Played, fixed or per-step velocity (added to or replacing the played one, in Ultra), accents, a velocity ramp towards a target (Ableton's Decay and Target), and swing per step, not just per pattern (Ultra's swing lane).
9. **Restart behaviour matters.** Ableton's Retrigger: never, on each new note, or on a beat or bar; Ultra's "Anchored" (locked to the timeline) versus starting on the key press; Repeats (play the pattern N times, for strums). The Hydrasynth review complained that turning the arp on silences sounding notes.
10. **What it plays can be kept.** Logic's Capture Live Performance drags the arp's output to a track as editable MIDI; Cubase converts patterns to MIDI parts; SOS suggests recording the arp and deleting notes to break up its regularity. KVR users ask for MIDI out and MIDI file import.
11. **Arps on drums.** SOS suggests letting each arpeggiated note trigger a different drum sound. On a pad sampler this is natural: latch a few pads and the arp becomes a drum pattern generator.

### What MPC users say about Akai's arpeggiator

Threads on MPC-Forums (few posts, so a thin picture): the built-in patterns "get bland after a bit"; users could not make their own patterns on the device; custom patterns **play one note at a time and don't follow the held chord** ("arpeggios are intelligent, patterns aren't"); and users want rhythms that play across a held chord. Akai's own help article says custom patterns are made by exporting a MIDI track into the Arp Patterns folder, and only appear after making a new project. The factory content on the owner's MPC has an `/MPC Documents/Arp Patterns/` folder; per the [factory content policy](../project/factory-content-policy.md) we neither copy nor ship those files.

### What this means for MPFree

- **One button and good defaults first.** Note Repeat behaves as on any MPC; Latch is one button; the arp is one more. Everything else is on one screen page and four Q-Links, never more than one menu deep.
- **Patterns refer to held notes, not fixed pitches.** A step says "the 2nd held note", "the next note in the order", "every held note" or "the lowest", plus an octave and, later, a scale-degree offset. So a pattern follows the chord the player holds, which is exactly what MPC users said Akai's patterns cannot do.
- **Lanes, each with its own length.** Rhythm (on, rest, tie), note pick, octave, velocity, gate, ratchet and chance are separate lanes. Different lane lengths make polymeter, which suits the project's sequencer goals ([architecture](../project/architecture.md)).
- **Make patterns three ways:** tap steps on the screen grid, play them in on the pads (record mode), or type them as text. Import MIDI files later.
- **Variation is safe.** Mutate and randomise always keep the original, one press away.

## Concepts

| Term | Meaning in MPFree |
|---|---|
| **Held notes** | Pads or keys physically down. |
| **Active notes** | What the generators play: held notes plus latched ones. |
| **Latch** | Keeps notes active after release. Modes: Reset (default), Toggle, Add, and later Transpose. Works in every mode, including plain playing, where it acts as a sustain. The sustain pedal can work it (below, Controller tricks). |
| **Modifier** | One controller (the mod wheel, pressure, an expression pedal, breath or a learned knob) that pushes the arp's rate, octaves, gate, swing, rolls and accents while it plays, and lets them back as it comes down (below, Controller tricks). |
| **Direct** | No generator: notes play as pressed. |
| **Note Repeat** | Every active note retriggers on every step, following pad pressure, as on an MPC. A latched note, once let go, goes back to the velocity it was struck with (`Notes::release`): the pressure falls to almost nothing as a finger lifts. |
| **Arp** | One note per step, walking the active notes in an order, over 1–4 octaves. |
| **Latch pattern** | A row of steps that says when to play and which active note(s) to play. Patterns and the arp share rate, gate, swing and octave settings. |

On the pad player, the "notes" fed to the engine are **pad slots** (pad 1 = 0 … pad 16 = 15), not Akai's scrambled pad note numbers, so "up" means up the pads. With a melodic program (later) they are MIDI notes.

## The engine (`rust/mpfree-arp`)

Notes in, sample-timed notes out. It lives in the audio thread: the app calls `press`, `release`, `pressure` and the setters between periods, then `process(frames)` once per period, which returns the note-ons and note-offs inside that period with their frame offsets. It never allocates, locks or does I/O after `Engine::new`, and its output does not depend on the period size (a test runs the same input in blocks of 1, 64, 127, 1000 and 100 000 frames and gets identical events). Its note-ons and note-offs go out frame by frame and, on one frame, in the order they were made: a note that ends on the frame it plays again ends first, and a note pressed and let go inside one period (a quick tap, or a MIDI note with no length) plays and ends instead of hanging (`a_tap_inside_one_period_does_not_hang`).

Built now:

- **Latch** Off, Reset (called `Replace`), Toggle, Add (every press stays until Latch goes: the MPC's pedal Latch Mode Add, 3.10.1 guide p. 83); clear latched notes; panic. A panic, and a hush, end the notes the engine has sounding and only those (`Engine::sounding`, a bit a note, set as a note-on leaves the engine and cleared as its note-off does), so an arp playing nothing sends nothing.
- **Modes** Direct, Repeat, Arp, Pattern and Phrase ([below](#phrases)). Switching mode never leaves a gap: notes sounding in Direct mode are stopped and the generator starts on the same frame.
- **A press on a frame** (sampler R5.3b): `Engine::set_at(offset)` puts the presses and let-gos made before the next `process` that many frames into its period in Direct mode, rather than at its top, and goes back to 0 once the period runs (or an idle period is skipped). The sound loop uses it for a Loop pad's hit that waited for the launch line, so it plays on the line's frame; a running generator keeps its own grid. Test: `a_press_set_into_the_period_plays_on_that_frame_once`.
- **Orders** Up, Down, Up-down, Up-down inclusive, Converge, Diverge, As played, Random (seeded, so it is repeatable), Random with no repeats until every note has played, Random once (one shuffled order kept until the notes change), Chord; 1–4 octaves; notes past 127 are skipped.
- **Feel** strum (spreads chord notes across up to the whole step, lowest first); retrigger (a new note restarts the order and pattern); repeat count (play N steps, then stop, for one-shot runs).
- **Scales** from the shared `mpfree-scale` crate ([scales.md](scales.md)): 49 named scales in nine families plus the owner's own, with any root, applied last so octave and degree offsets always land in key. A held note outside the scale goes to the scale note below (the default), above, the nearest, or is not played (`Settings::snap`). Degree offsets going down use the scale's notes coming down, for scales such as the classical melodic minor. Off by default and for drum pads: the arp has its own scale, and follows a project key only when a project has one (owner, 2026-11-11). **Chords from the scale:** each held pad can grow into a chord built from the scale (`Settings::chord`: power, triad, sus2, sus4, seventh, ninth), so one pad is enough for an arpeggio; a scale run (`Settings::run`) fills the steps with every scale note between the lowest and highest held note. **Chords take turns** (`Settings::turns`: every beat, 2 beats, bar or 2 bars): with two or more pads held, the steps play from one pad's chord at a time, in the order they were pressed, each turn starting the order again from its root. Holding A, F, C and G with triads in A minor arpeggiates Am F C G (owner asked 2026-11-11 09:51Z).
- **Timing** rates 1/4 to 1/32 with triplets, at 960 ticks per quarter; gate 1–100 %; swing 50–75 % on every second step. Free-running, the first step plays on the press. Following a transport (`Engine::sync` with a `Clock`, called before each `process`), the steps sit on the transport's tick grid: notes start on the next step, beat or bar (`Settings::start`), tempo changes and loop jumps are followed, and a transport swing (grid and amount) bends the grid; when the transport stops, the engine free-runs again.
- **Velocity** played (following pad pressure) or fixed; accent velocity.
- **Patterns** up to 64 steps, written as text:

```text
x . x x        gallop: the next note, rest, next, next
1 3 2 3        a broken chord over whatever is held
1 2 3 1+ 2+ 3+ 1++ .   climb two octaves
* . . * . . * .        chord stabs on a 3-3-2 rhythm
x x:2 x x:3    ratchets
x x%60 ? x%30 . x _ ?  chance, random notes and a tie
```

| Token | Step |
|---|---|
| `.` | rest |
| `_` | tie: the step before holds on through this one |
| `x` | the next note in the arp order (with octaves) |
| `1`..`9` | the Nth active note, lowest first (wraps if fewer are held) |
| `*` | every active note (a chord) |
| `?` | a random active note |
| `l` / `h` | the lowest / highest active note |
| after a note: `+` `-` | an octave up / down (up to three) |
| `!` | accent |
| `~` / `'` | full-length / short note |
| `:N` | N hits in the step (2–8) |
| `%N` | N % chance |

**Lanes** follow the steps after a `|`, each with its own length, so they loop against the steps (polymeter):

```text
x x x x | oct 0 1 0 | vel 120 70 90 70 80     octave over 3, accents over 5, steps over 4
1 1 1 1 1 1 1 1 | deg 0 2 4 5 7 4 2 | gate 40 40 80   one held note plays a line
```

| Lane | Values |
|---|---|
| `oct` | octaves added to the step's own, −3 to 3 |
| `deg` | scale degrees (with a scale) or semitones (without), −24 to 24 |
| `vel` | velocity 1–127; 0 = as played; an accent still wins |
| `gate` | note length in percent; 0 = the step's or the engine's |
| `nudge` | moves the step early (negative) or late, −50 to 50 percent of a step, on top of swing; a step never moves ahead of the one before it |

**Random amounts per lane.** A lane name can carry `~N`: each time a step plays, a random number from −N to N is added to that lane's value, so `vel~20` varies velocity by up to 20, `gate~30` the length, `oct~1` the octave, `deg~2` the degree and `nudge~10` the timing. A lane with a random amount needs no values (`x x x x | vel~25` varies the played velocity). Accented steps keep their accent. The engine's seed picks the numbers, so a seed always plays the same way, and a pattern without `~` draws no random numbers at all.

**Velocity ramp.** `Settings.ramp = Ramp { target, steps }` moves velocity from where it starts towards `target` over `steps` steps after the notes start, then holds it (Ableton's Decay and Target). With `retrigger` on, every new chord starts the ramp again. Accents are left alone.

**Variation** that never loses the original: `Pattern::mutated(seed, amount)` returns a new pattern (the same seed gives the same variation; ties stay; at least one step plays) and `Pattern::rotated(n)` turns the steps. The app keeps the original and swaps back on "Original".

Ten original starter patterns are built in (Straight, Gallop, Bounce, Climb, Pulse, Stabs, Rolls, Drift, Three over four, Climbing line). The text form round-trips, so the screen editor and files use the same model.

### Phrases

**Phrase mode** (`Mode::Phrase`) plays a whole phrase from the newest note held: a bass line, a riff, chord stabs or a line, each note with its own start, length and loudness, in the phrase's own rhythm. Hold another note and the phrase moves there without losing its place; let it go and it goes back to the note before. This is the MPC's phrase player (MPC 3.10.1 user guide, pp. 82 and 83: "Hold down a single note to trigger a melodic phrase", which "will be transposed"), with MPFree's own phrases: none of Akai's is copied or shipped. The owner approved phrases on 2026-11-23 (11:43Z) as the composing tools plan has them ([compose.md](compose.md)): the player and its library first (P1), the composer's phrases next (P2). The owner approved the Phrase tab's five boards as drawn on 2026-11-23 (15:48Z), and the tab is built as they show it ([the Phrase tab](#the-phrase-tab), below). The phrases MPFree ships are heard before they ship. Built: the engine, the library with 64 phrases, the app's list, the project's part and the tab. Making a phrase of one's own on the MPC is not built yet.

#### How it is built, and why

- **A fifth mode of the arp's engine, in files of its own.** A phrase needs what the arp already has for its track: the notes held and Latch, the settings and the Modifier, the transport's clock, and the queue that times note-ons and note-offs to the frame. A second engine beside the arp would need all of those again, and a rule for which of the two has the pads. So Phrase is a mode of `Engine`, and its code is apart from the step modes': the engine has two generators, the step modes in `engine.rs` and the phrase in [`engine/phrase_mode.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-arp/src/engine/phrase_mode.rs), which the engine calls where a phrase starts, stops, starts again or changes speed, and once each period.
- **The model apart from the playing.** [`phrase.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-arp/src/phrase.rs) holds what a phrase is (`Phrase`, its text form, how a note's pitch follows a held note) and where one is as it plays: `Playhead` counts in ticks of a clock it is handed, knows nothing of frames or of sound, and says what is due next (`Playhead::turn`: a note, or the line where a waiting phrase comes in; the engine says which line, `Join::Bar` or `Join::Step`, and the playhead only finds it). Both are tested without an engine. `phrase_mode.rs` turns what the playhead says into notes: the pitch, the velocity, the gate, swing, and which notes end when.
- **Who ends what.** A step mode's note is a few steps long at most, so the engine lets every note it started run out. A phrase's note can be bars long, so Phrase mode has three rules. The same note struck again ends the one sounding at that moment (its own ending, still queued, would otherwise cut the new one short). When the phrase stops (nothing held, or the mode changes), what it has sounding ends at once. When it starts again (Retrigger, a transport start or jump), or another phrase comes in, what still sounds ends there.
- **A press a moment late still starts on its line** (`Engine::start_line`). A step mode can start on the next line whatever the hand did, because its steps are all alike. A phrase is written against the bar: pressed on the beat 20 ms late and started a 1/16 on, every note of it would sit a 1/16 off the beat for as long as it plays, and a hand playing on the beat lands late about as often as early. So while the transport plays, a press up to 60 ms after a start line (never more than half a 1/16; a press meant for the next 1/16 comes later than that at any tempo) counts for that line: the phrase is in time from the line, and only the notes the press was late for are late. Such a note sounds at the press and ends where it was written to, as a hand that lands late and lets go in time would end it, but keeps at least half its length (so the first note of a short-gated phrase is heard) and never runs into the next note's start; a note whose place has wholly passed is left out. The other choices were weighed: starting the whole phrase at the press leaves all of it late against the song, and always waiting for the next line makes the player press early, which nobody does on the one. The same window serves a new note with Retrigger, and a transport start or jump that lands a few frames past a line.
- **A note goes into the queue whole or not at all** (`Engine::queue_note`, for every mode): it starts only when its ending has a place in the queue too, so a full queue leaves a note out and never leaves one hanging. A long phrase holds more endings in the queue than a step mode ever did.
- **One clock that never drifts.** While the transport is stopped the engine's own clock is an anchor (a tick on a frame) that moves only when the tempo or the transport changes (`clock_tick`, `clock_frame`), so a phrase lands on the same frames however the host cuts its periods. The step modes still count frames step by step; moving them onto the same clock is a later step, not part of this one.
- **Fixed size, copied in.** A `Phrase` is 3,088 bytes (256 notes of 12 bytes, 8 bars) and `Copy`, as a `Pattern` is, so the engine holds and swaps one on the audio thread without allocating. An engine holds two (the one playing and one waiting to come in), which makes it about 14 KB, so the 128 engines `TrackArps` allocates at the start take about 0.8 MB more. Parsing and writing text allocate, so they belong on the screen's or a worker's thread.
- **Apart from the composer.** `mpfree-arp` knows nothing of `mpfree-compose`. The composer makes notes off the audio thread; the app turns them into a phrase with `Phrase::from_notes`, as it does a clip's or a MIDI file's.
- **In the app a track keeps a pick, not a copy** ([`app/arp/phrases.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/app/arp/phrases.rs)): its place in one list every track picks from. The app copies all 128 tracks' arp state whenever it looks the project's arps over, and a phrase is 3 KB where a pattern is under 1 KB, so the phrase itself lives in the list and in the track's engine.
- **The owner's phrases have a file of their own in the store** (`arp/phrases.txt`, beside `arp/patterns.txt`), in the same library form. One file for both was weighed: the library reads both kinds already, and one file is one save. It was left because an MPFree from before phrases reads a phrase line as a bad pattern and then leaves the whole file unused, so one start of an older build and a pattern Save in it would write over the owner's patterns and phrases alike. With two files the older build never sees a phrase, and a bad line in one file leaves the other in use. The reader is one for both (`App::load_library`): whatever a file holds is used, a phrase line in a patterns file too (the `--patterns` option's file may carry phrases), and Save writes each kind to its own file at the same time (`Effect::SavePatterns`, `Effect::SavePhrases`), so a line read from the other file is in its own from the next Save on and is never lost between the two.
- **A phrase of the owner's with the name of one in the list takes its place,** as a pattern of the owner's takes a built-in pattern's: it keeps the place and the name, and Save keeps it because it differs from the one built in (`App::phrases_text`). The others come after MPFree's and before the open project's. That puts the owner's run in the middle of the list, so a phrase joining it moves the project's own up one; every track's pick on them moves with them (`App::own_phrase`), and every track's engine takes its pick again (`App::send_all_phrases`), since a phrase a track picked may have other notes now. A pick stays a place in the list: places change in that one function, where a number of its own for every phrase would have every reader of a pick look it up.
- **The list as the tab will show it is worked out apart from any drawing** ([`mpfree-arp-ui/src/phrases.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-arp-ui/src/phrases.rs)). `PhraseList` is the app's list with its three runs, sent to the screens whenever it changes (`Effect::Phrases`), with the track's pick and the scale the pads play in the arp's view. `PhraseList::rows` gives a shelf's rows: MPFree's by kind, then the owner's shelf with the open project's own after them, each row knowing whose it is, and in each the phrases that suit the key first (`Written::suits`, by the key's third: a major phrase suits a key with a major third, a minor one a key with a minor third, and a key with both or neither, or no key, keeps the list's order). That is the list the boards draw and the tab shows. The rows are read through `Shelves`, a view over a list it only borrows: the tab reads its `PhraseList` through one, and the app reads its own list through another where it is kept, so the wheel walks the very rows the tab shows, in their order, and no copy of the list is made to ask (64 phrases of 3 KB on every click of the wheel, had `rows` stayed on `PhraseList` alone).
- **The pads' key goes to every track, from one place.** In key follows the arp's own scale or, with that off, the scale the pads play (`Engine::set_key`). The pads have one scale for the whole project, so the app gives it to every track's engine, not only the armed one's (`App::send_key`): a phrase latched on a track the pads have left changes key when the pads do, and no track is left in whatever key the pads had when it was last armed. Like the tempo it is a setting alone, so it reaches all 128 engines without marking any in use (`TrackArps::engines_mut`). It goes out from the one function that hands the synth the pads' settings ([`App::pads_changed`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/app/synth_pads.rs): the Synth tab's Scale and Scale root rows, the pads panel's In key switch, a project opening), so a new way to change the pads cannot forget the arps. Weighed and left: sending it to the armed track alone, when a track is armed or the scale changes (a track's key would then depend on which tracks were armed since), and comparing the two once a period (state kept in step by a poll, which a test could only see by running a period). The key is the pads' scale whatever layout the bank on the pads has, Chromatic too: a latched phrase must not change its notes when PAD BANK brings chromatic pads up, and the approved design has In key mean something whenever a scale is set. A live transpose differs on purpose (`Synth::transpose_key`: by the key only while the pads play In key or chords), since chromatic pads are played chromatically.

#### The text form

A phrase is written from its home note on a grid, one token per step, so it fits on one line of a library file as a latch pattern does:

```text
bass minor : 1 . 1 1 . 1 . 5- 1 . b7- . 5- . b7- 1
chords either 1/8 : 1,3,5! . . 1,3,5' . 1,3,5 . .
riff minor swung : 5 . b7 1+ _ . b7 5 | vel 110 80 90 100
```

The words before `:` say what it is: its kind (`bass`, `riff`, `chords` or `line`, the library's shelves), the scale it was written in (`major`, `minor`, or `either` when it works in both), its grid (`1/8` to `1/32T`, 1/16 by default), its meter (`4/4` or `3/4`), `swung` when it was written to swing (a label: the swing played is the transport's or the arp's), and `N bars` when it is longer than its steps (up to 8). Steps are `.` (rest), `_` (tie) or notes: `1` to `7` are steps of the written scale from the home note, `b` and `#` lower or raise one a half step (named from the major scale, as degrees usually are, so a minor phrase writes `b3`, `b6` and `b7`), `-` and `+` move it an octave (up to four), and `1,b3,5` plays notes together. After a note, `!` accents it (127), `'` makes it short (25 % of its step) and `~` holds it for the whole step; a plain note sounds for 80 % of its last step at velocity 100. Lanes after a `|` loop at their own lengths as a pattern's do: `vel`, `gate` and `nudge` (up to half a step early or late). A phrase has no random lanes: it plays the same every time, so a recording of it plays back as heard. `Phrase::to_text` writes the same form back (it round-trips), `Phrase::from_notes` makes a phrase from notes as played (a clip, a MIDI file or a latch pattern's output) from a home note, and `Phrase::feel` says straight, swung or triplet for a list's row.

#### The library and MPFree's 64

Phrases go in the same kind of library file as patterns (`mpfree_arp::Library`), one to a line. A phrase line says what it is, so no heading is needed to tell the two apart: a line with a `:` on its own, or that starts with a kind or a scale, is a phrase, and any other is a pattern (a pattern has colons only inside a step, as in `x:2`). Patterns and phrases are named apart, so a phrase may take a pattern's name; `[phrases]` is read as `[patterns]` is, a heading for the reader. A bad phrase line is reported as a bad pattern line is, with its line, the token and the reason.

MPFree ships 64 phrases, 16 of each kind, in [`rust/mpfree-arp/phrases/starter.txt`](../../../rust/mpfree-arp/phrases/starter.txt), a library file built into the program (`STARTER_PHRASES`; `Library::built_in` reads it with the ten patterns). Every one was written for MPFree; none is copied from Akai's phrases or from any other product's ([factory content policy](../project/factory-content-policy.md)). They are 1, 2 or 4 bars; most are straight, with swung and triplet ones and three in 3/4; most are written for either scale, with minor and major ones. They are a first writing: the owner hears them all before they ship, and the list changes with what he hears. The listening set comes from the composer's listening tool, which has the real sounds, a drummer and the mix at one loudness (`cargo run --release -p mpfree-compose --example listen -- --phrases`, [compose.md](compose.md)): the arp's engine plays each phrase with the transport playing, from a note held for half the file and then two others of the key, on a sound, at a tempo and over a beat picked for it (the rows of `HEARD` in `examples/listen/phrases.rs`; a phrase has no tempo or sound of its own), with a bass on the held note under it, or a quiet pad over a bass phrase. `--phrases-from FILE` plays a library file the same way, and `EXPLAIN=1` prints the notes each one played. Reading those notes is how the two rules above for notes outside the scale and for triplets were found. The owner's own phrases are kept in the store beside the arp's patterns (`arp/phrases.txt`, a library file of phrases, read at the start); making one on the MPC, from a clip, a MIDI file or a pattern, comes with the Phrase tab.

#### Pitch, time and the other settings

**Pitch.** *In key* (`Follow::InKey`, the default) the held note is brought into the scale (by its snap) and the phrase moves from it by steps of the scale: a 7-note scale step for step, so a line written in major plays in C minor as C, D, E♭, F; a scale of another size takes the nearest of its notes to the written interval, the lower on a tie. A note written outside its scale (a blue note, a leading tone: a `b7` in a major phrase, a `7` in a minor one) keeps the distance it was written at from the held note, In key too. The first rule built moved it by the key's step and then by its half step, which lands it on its neighbour wherever the key's own step is already there: the `b7` of a major walk held on the key's fifth, where the seventh step is lowered already, played the 6th twice, and a minor phrase's leading tone held on the key's sixth step played the held note itself. Kept at its written distance it is the note the writer meant on every step of the key, and in key where the key allows (`a_note_outside_the_written_scale_keeps_its_distance_from_the_held_note`). The scale is the arp's own (`Settings::scale`) or, with that off, the scale the pads play (`Engine::set_key`, which changes nothing in the other modes). *Exact* (`Follow::Exact`) moves by the semitones as written, and so does In key with neither.

**Time.** Free-running, the phrase starts on the press. With the transport playing it starts on the next 1/16, beat or bar (`Settings::start`), or on the one just passed for a press up to 60 ms late, and stays on the transport's grid; a transport start or a jump (a loop going round, a locate) starts it again from its first note on the grid; when the transport stops it carries on in time. *Speed* (`Settings::speed`) plays it at half, as written or double, and a Modifier's push on the rate moves it by the same ratio from where it is (two steps faster from 1/16 is 1/32: twice as fast). Swing is the transport's while it plays with some, otherwise the arp's own on 1/16s of clock time (counted from the press while free-running). A phrase written on a triplet grid is not swung by either: swing moves the second of every two, so it pushes the threes of a triplet apart unevenly (at 58 % on 1/16s, the second and the third 1/8 triplet 13 ms late at 120 bpm), and a triplet has its lilt already; played straight it sits with a swung beat, whose off-beats fall near its third note. A phrase picked while one plays comes in from its first note on a line of the playing phrase (`Engine::set_phrase`): its next bar line while the transport plays, so the bars it keeps with the song go on unbroken, and its next step (of the grid it is written on) while the transport is stopped, when nothing else counts bars and a list of phrases is quick to hear through without the pulse stumbling. Picking the one that plays leaves it playing on, so a track armed again never starts its phrase over. `Engine::phrase_at` says where it is, for a cursor, and `Engine::phrase_waiting` which line a pick waits for, for a screen that shows it coming in.

**The other settings.** Octaves: each time through the phrase climbs an octave, up to the octaves set, then starts again from the bottom. Gate: 50 % plays the lengths as written; less shortens every note in proportion; more holds each towards the next note, and at 100 % a note ends on the very frame the next starts (before it, so the same note played again is never cut). Velocity: the written velocities times the held note's over 100 (held at 100 it plays as written; Fixed uses its value instead of the held one); the ramp, and the Modifier's rolls and accents, apply as on the other tabs. Retrigger: a new note starts the phrase again from its first note (on the 1/16 the note counts for while the transport plays). Repeats: the times through the phrase; then it stays quiet until it starts again (every note let go and one played, or Retrigger). Latch keeps it going after the pad is let go. Order, chord, scale run, turns, strum and the rate (but for the Modifier's push) belong to the other modes.

#### In the app

The app has one list of phrases, in three runs (MPFree's 64, the owner's from the store, then any only the open project has), and each track's pick from it. With a track's arp on Phrase, a held pad plays the pick from the pad's note where the pads press notes into the arp (a synth); on a kit, whose arp works in pad hits, the pads play as pressed, and so does whatever the pads play on their own ("A phrase stays with its track", below). A kit played as notes gives the app a press layout too (`kit_press_layout`, sampler step 15c part 3), so a phrase plays there as on a synth; it has not been heard on one yet. In key it moves by the scale the pads play (the Synth tab's Scale and Scale root, or the pads panel's In key switch) unless the arp has a scale of its own, and a change of that scale while it plays is heard from the next note. While a phrase plays, a new pick waits for its line (the bar line with the transport, the next step without). The project's arp part keeps `follow` and `speed` for every track it lists, and `phrase-name` with `phrase` (the phrase as text) for a track that plays a phrase or picked one. On open, a phrase the list doesn't have under that name with those notes comes back as the project's own (under its name, or the name with "(project)" when the list has another phrase by it), so a project plays what it was saved with, on an MPC whose owner never had the phrase too; the next project opened drops them. Save in the pattern workspace keeps the owner's phrases in their file when it keeps the patterns. The arp chip and the quick band name the track's phrase ("Phrase: Night drive"), as they name a pattern.

#### The Phrase tab

The Arp panel's fifth tab, built as the five boards the owner approved show it ("Yes, as drawn", 2026-11-23 15:48Z; the boards and their notes are in the notes repository, `composing-tools/project-files/phrase-boards/`). The crate's sample screens are the boards (`phrase-browse`, `phrase-next-bar`, `phrase-exact`, `phrase-kit`, `phrase-yours`, with `phrase-yours-empty`, `phrase-triplets-pushed` and `quick-phrase`), and the app's show the tab in the Play frame (`arp-phrase`, `arp-phrase-next-bar`, `arp-phrase-kit`, `arp-quick-band-phrase`).

**What it shows.** Three panels side by side under the mode tabs, 12 px apart:

- **The kinds** (166 px): Bass, Riff, Chords, Arp lines, then Yours a little under them, each with how many phrases it holds. The kind whose phrases the list shows is lit.
- **That kind's phrases:** a row each (50 px, a fingertip), with the name, how long it is ("2 bars"; then its meter and feel when they are not 4/4 and straight, "2 bars · swung") and a small picture of its notes. The track's pick is lit, and the heading says where it is ("8 of 16"). The phrases that suit the key come first; those written in the other kind of scale follow, quieter, and say which ("major"). A phrase only the open project has says "this project". Where the kind is longer than the panel the pick is kept in the middle, with a scroll bar at the right.
- **Plays** (397 px): the phrase's name, what it is ("Bass · 2 bars · straight · written in minor"), its notes large with a line at each bar and beat and a green cursor where it is as it plays, **Follows** (In key or Exact, with what it follows and whose key that is beside the switch: "A minor · the pads' key", "the arp's own scale", "By semitones · every interval as written", or "No key set · plays by semitones"), and the four Q-Link sliders, **Speed · Q1**, **Gate · Q2**, **Swing · Q3** and **Octaves · Q4**.

While the transport plays, a new pick waits for the bar line: its row is outlined in amber and says "next bar", the phrase still playing is marked in green and says "playing", and Plays shows the pick under "Comes in on the next bar", its notes in amber, with no cursor. On a kit track the kinds are faint, and one panel in the place of the list and Plays says that phrases play on a synth, with the track's name ("Track 1's instrument is a kit, so its pads play as pressed.").

**What the controls do.**

| Control | On the Phrase tab | On NOTE REPEAT's band, on Phrase |
|---|---|---|
| Wheel | The pick, along the kind shown; it stops at the kind's ends | The same, along the pick's kind |
| − / + | The kind. − past Bass goes to the Pattern tab; + stops at Yours | The mode: Repeat, Arp, Pattern and, on a synth track, Phrase |
| Q-Link 1 | Speed: half, as written, double | The same |
| Q-Links 2 to 4 | Gate, swing, octaves | The same |
| A tap on a kind | Shows it, and picks the phrase last picked there (its first, the first time) | |
| A tap on a phrase | Picks it | |
| In key, Exact | How the phrase follows the held note | |
| The band's − and + | | The speed, shown as ½×, 1× or 2× |

**The choices, and why.** The code is in [`mpfree-arp-ui/src/phrase_tab.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-arp-ui/src/phrase_tab.rs) (the widgets), [`view.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-arp-ui/src/view.rs) (the panel), [`app/arp.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/app/arp.rs) and [`app/arp/phrases.rs`](../../../rust/mpfree-app/src/app/arp/phrases.rs) (the pad player).

*The screen.*

- **Widgets that only show, and functions that work out what they show.** `PhraseKinds`, `PhraseRows`, `PhrasePlays` and `PhraseKit` draw what the panel put in them and report touches; `kinds`, `rows`, `plays` and `kit` work that out from an `ArpState` and draw nothing, so the tests read those. The arp's other widgets are built the same way. Widgets that read the `ArpState` themselves would be less code, but every rule of the list would then need a canvas to test.
- **Named as the panel's.** The widgets are `arp-phrase-kinds`, `arp-phrase-list`, `arp-phrase-plays` and `arp-phrase-kit`, so the app's routing of `arp-` widgets to the panel covers them unchanged; their touch targets are `kind`, `phrase`, `follow` and `slider`, the keys of [controls.md](../guides/controls.md).
- **The tab takes the pads' room** in the crate's own layouts (`PHRASE_LAYOUT`, `PHRASE_KIT_LAYOUT`): a list with a picture a row and the playing phrase large do not fit the 460 px column the other tabs keep to. In the app's Play frame every tab has the whole width and the pads are not on the panel, so nothing moves there.
- **The four sliders are drawn inside Plays** by a `SliderStack` it holds (`paint_in`, `targets_in`, `touch_in`), as the boards have them. The off-page `arp-qlinks` stack gets the same rows, because the frame's Q-Link cards read that one. A fourth panel for the sliders was not what the boards drew, and sliders drawn by hand would be a second slider to keep like the first.
- **The tab decides what the controls do** (`ArpState::tab`, `App::arp_tab`): the layout, the wheel, − / +, the first Q-Link and the hint follow the tab the panel shows. That is the mode the pads play through, except on a kit track, where the Phrase tab is picked and the pads play as pressed all the same.
- **The first Q-Link is the phrase's speed.** A phrase has its own rhythm, so a rate means nothing to it; half, as written and double do. One sum gives the speed (`mpfree_arp::phrase_speed`: the speed set, times a Modifier's push on the rate), which the engine plays by and the slider and the band show, so the screen can never say another speed than the one heard. Pushed, the slider shows where the push has it ("1.5×").
- **A phrase written in triplets is not swung** (above), so its Swing slider is quiet, is labelled "Swing · Q3 · triplets" and reads "straight", and never shows a value that changes nothing.
- **A widget that would show the same is left alone** (`show` in `view.rs`). The panel hears from the app every time a step plays or the cursor moves, and a widget reached to be changed is drawn again whether it changed or not. While only the cursor moves, Plays asks for its picture alone (`Widget::changed_parts`), so a playing phrase redraws one box and not the panel. The MPC's panel is mounted turned, and a wide redraw is the costly kind there.
- **The cursor moves a 1/32 at a time** (`CURSOR_TICKS`), and only while the panel shows: fine enough to follow by eye (16 moves a second at 120 bpm), and no more often than the arp's screens already hear of a fast repeat. No other page is drawn again for it.
- **Amber only for a wait that can be seen.** `Engine::phrase_waiting` says which line a pick waits for. The tab shows the wait for a bar line; a wait for the next step (transport stopped) is over within a step, sooner than a screen could say so.
- **The chip and the band name the phrase heard** (`ArpState::phrase_heard`): while a pick waits for its bar line, still the one before it. The tab is where the pick that waits is shown.
- **The band shows and steps the speed** where the other modes have the rate, and the step strip says the phrase's notes are on the Phrase tab (`PHRASE_STRIP`): a phrase has no steps of an order to show, so the strip's cells are empty for it.
- **The screen is told, it does not guess.** The app's view carries the kit track (which the screen names as Arrange does) and whether the pads press notes; the band's hint lists four modes only then. Weighed: the screen working both out from what it knows of the synth on the pads, which is a second reading of a rule the pad player owns.
- **On a kit track the Q-Links still turn the track's settings,** and the cards say what they turn; the hint leaves them out, as board D does.
- **The owner's shelf says only what is true today.** Board E's line names three ways to make a phrase (a clip's card, a MIDI file in the browser, a pattern that plays one note). None is built yet, so the shelf says that phrases of the owner's are kept there and that making one comes next (`OWN_NOTE`), and it takes the board's words as each way lands.
- **Board D drew an "Arp off" chip in the top bar.** The top bar's own rule stands (the layout spec: the arp chip shows only while the arp, a repeat or a latch is on), so the app shows none there.

*The pad player.*

- **A tap on a kind picks on it** (`App::show_shelf`): the phrase last picked there, or its first. So what is lit in the list is always what a held note plays, and stepping through the kinds is hearing one of each. Looking through a kind without picking was weighed: the list would then show a kind with nothing lit while another phrase plays, and the wheel would need a place to start from. On the panel − / + change what plays on every other tab too (the mode), so here they are the coarse step and the wheel the fine one. The pick is easy to put back (each kind remembers its last, `phrase_last`, one memory for the session and not one a track), and while the transport plays a pick only waits for the bar line, so picking the phrase that plays again before then calls it off.
- **The kind shown is the pick's,** except an empty one stepped to (`phrase_shelf`: the owner's, before they have made a phrase), which shows with the pick left where it is so that it can say what it is for.
- **− / + step the kinds only on the panel.** Away from it no kinds show (the band), so there they step the modes alone, and the kind the wheel moves along is always the pick's (`App::shown_shelf`): the wheel never stands on an empty kind nobody sees.
- **The wheel stops at the ends of the kind.** Flowing on into the next kind was weighed and left: a kind is a filter, and a list that stops at its ends is the familiar one. The rows are the ones the tab shows, in their order (`Shelves::rows`).
- **− past the first kind goes to the tab before,** so the buttons still lead out of the tab; + past the last kind does nothing, as + on the last tab does.
- **NOTE REPEAT's − and + reach Phrase on a synth track only** (`GENERATING_NOTES`), and a kit track left on Phrase still repeats: the mode NOTE REPEAT turns on is Repeat wherever the tab's own would play the pads as pressed (`App::held_mode`). A tab left on Phrase must never take Note Repeat from a kit's pads.
- **A phrase stays with its track** (`App::pads_own_mode`; found when this work was merged with sampler step 15c). While the pads play something of their own, the pads' arp plays it with the selected track's arp settings ("The pads' arp", below), and it takes the settings and no phrase. Left alone, a track on Phrase handed it Phrase mode with the engine's stand-in phrase, and a held kit pad played on every beat. So the app says which mode what the pads play on their own goes through: the track's, but as pressed where that is Phrase, and Repeat while NOTE REPEAT or Latch is on, as on a kit track (`held_mode`). `PadsArp::follow` and the hit's way into the pads' arp take that mode, and the track's engine is not touched: a phrase latched on a track that plays the shared synth plays on while the pads play a kit. Weighed: a flag in the app that turns `phrases_play` off while the pads are away, which needs nothing new in the audio loop but takes the track's own phrase away with it (a latched phrase would turn into repeats of its notes); and the pads' arp taking the phrase and the key too, so a synth the pads play on their own would play the phrase, which is where this should end but needs the tab's cursor to read a second engine and has been neither built nor heard. Open with it: a track with a synth of its own still leaves Phrase while the pads are away, because its press layout is taken away then (`press_layout` in `main.rs`) and `phrases_play` asks what the pads press where it should ask what the track's instrument takes (`TrackArps::on_notes`); the tab then shows the kit panel, whose words call that track's instrument a kit. Tests: `what_the_pads_play_on_their_own_never_takes_a_phrase` and `smoke::song_workflows::tests::a_phrase_stays_with_its_track_while_the_pads_play_a_kit_on_their_own`, which fails with four hits of the stand-in phrase when the rule is taken out.
- **MIDI Learn:** on the Phrase tab the sliders are in Plays, and gate, swing and octaves learn as on the other tabs. The speed learns nothing yet: it has no parameter of its own, and the rate's would turn the wrong thing.
- **One list of the panel's tabs** (`MODES`) serves the tabs and the modes a project's arp part can name; they were two lists that had to agree.
- **The sample screens play the real pad player.** `App::set_clock` is no longer for tests alone: the "next bar" screen runs the pad player with a playing clock until a pick waits, and does not fake the waiting state. The four live in `sample_phrase`, so the audit test sweeps them without building every screen (`the_phrase_tabs_controls_do_something_and_are_in_the_catalogue`).

*The top bar.*

- **A chip may carry a short form** (`mpfree_ui::Chip::short`). The header laid chips out from the right and left out any that had no room, with every chip after it: a latched phrase's chip ("Phrase: Night drive, latched · 120 BPM") vanished, as a long order's or a pattern's could before phrases, and a long arp chip that did fit pushed out MAKE or PERFORM, the last chip. Now, where the bar has no room for every chip in full, a chip with a short form says that, and where even that is too long its end is cut with "…", so the chip keeps its place and a tap still finds it. The arp chip's short form is its text without the tempo, which the transport bar shows. Weighed: a budget of characters in the arp's own text (a guess that breaks with the font and the number of tabs) and fitting in the app (a copy of the header's layout sums); the header is the one place that knows the room, and only chips that give a short form are changed. The layout spec has the rule as proposed, for the owner's approval with the pull request.

#### Open, for later

- **Browsing over a playing song.** With the transport stopped a pick comes in on the next step, so the list is quick to hear through. With it playing, each pick waits for the bar line, which suits a change while performing and keeps every phrase's first note on the one, but makes the list slow to hear through over a beat (16 phrases take 16 bars). The owner approved the rule as built with the boards. Coming in at once at the same place in the bar would be quicker and still in time, without the phrase's start being heard first, if hearing through a list over a beat turns out to matter.
- **Phrases of the owner's own.** `Phrase::from_notes` is built; choosing the home note (the key reading names it) and the three places to start from (a clip's card on Arrange, a MIDI file in the browser, a pattern that plays one note) wait for their pull requests, and the owner's shelf says so until then.
- **Keeping a project's own phrase.** A phrase only the open project has is on the owner's shelf marked "this project" (`Origin::Project`); a button to keep it with the owner's comes with making phrases.
- **A parameter for the speed,** so that a controller can learn it (the MIDI map's list of the arp's parameters, `mpfree_param::arp`).
- **A latched phrase's chip and MAKE.** Beside the status chip the top bar has room for an arp chip of about 25 characters and MAKE or PERFORM. "Phrase: Night drive, latched" is longer, so it still pushes that chip out, as a long pattern's name does. Cutting the name sooner to keep MAKE, or a shorter way to say latched, is the owner's call.
- **A value as wide as its ring** on a Q-Link card ("as written") touches the ring's dots: the card takes 16 px text up to the ring's inside with no room to spare (`mpfree_ui::qlink::fitting`). A margin there makes 13 other sample screens' values a size smaller, so it is a change for the pages' owners, not for this tab.

## The screen and the buttons

All of this uses `mpfree-ui` widgets and one-line layouts (`docs/dev/design/ui-architecture.md`, in PR #31), so the page can be rearranged later.

**Where the arp lives (owner decision, 2026-11-11).** The arp is a way of playing the pads, and it belongs to one track, so it is not a top-level tab. It has three surfaces over one engine per track: the **quick band** while NOTE REPEAT is held (big rate buttons, Latch, the next steps; the Q-Links become rate, gate, swing and octaves), the **Arp panel** with every setting of one track (on the Sound tab as the first stage of the track's chain, and beside the pads from the arp chip in the header), and a full-screen **pattern workspace** that returns to the page you came from. The full reasoning, workflows, controls map and architecture are in the arpeggiator design doc (a Claude Doc shared with the owner, 2026-11-11).

**Built (on the workstation): `rust/mpfree-arp-ui`.** The panel and the band as `mpfree-ui` widgets: mode tabs (Direct, Repeat, Arp, Pattern, Phrase), the rate, order or pattern list (wheel), the Phrase tab's three panels ([above](#the-phrase-tab)), the Q-Link sliders (rate, gate, swing, octaves), the pattern buttons (Edit pattern, Record on pads, Mutate, Original), the band's rate buttons (at least 76 px) and Latch, the arp chip for the header, and the step strip, which shows the order's notes (with their octaves) or the pattern's steps (picks, octaves, ratchets, chance, accents) and marks the step playing. The strip shows only while the arp is generating. The panel reads an `ArpState` taken from the engine and turns touches into `ArpAction`s; the pad player keeps the pads, the header and the hint line, so the panel drops into its Play page's right-hand column. The **pattern workspace** (`workspace` module) is drawn too: a grid with a column per step and rows for the step (rest, note, tie, accent), the note pick, octave, ratchet, chance and gate; a tap cycles rest, note and accent, and a drag or the wheel changes a value; Rotate, Mutate, Original (back to the pattern it opened with), length, Save and Back; and recording on the pads, with the pads named for what each writes and the grid at 8 steps beside them. Its `Editor` is plain logic with tests. The pattern's lanes are kept as they are; editing them on screen comes later. `mpfree-arp-preview` draws sample screens, each with a real engine behind it, as PNG files.

**In the pad player (`rust/mpfree-app`, for round 6).** The pad player plays the pads through one engine. Its buttons were measured in round 5: MENU 123, NOTE REPEAT 11, TAP TEMPO 53, - 55 and + 54, besides SHIFT 49, FULL LEVEL 39, STOP 81 and PLAY 82 (`mpfree_controller::mpc_one::button`). - The **quick band** is one slim strip (56 px) at the top of the Play page's right-hand column while NOTE REPEAT is held or pads are latched: what the arp plays (tap it to open the Arp panel), the rate with − and + (on Phrase: the phrase's speed, ½×, 1× or 2×), and Latch. The kit list gives up the strip's height; the pads, level and timing don't move (owner request, 2026-11-11: holding NOTE REPEAT or latching at a rate should be a tiny change).
- The **Arp panel** (the arp editor) takes the whole column, step strip included, so the pads never move or resize between Play, the band and the panel. With it open the pads play through its mode, so the NOTE REPEAT light is on; closing it, or picking Direct, plays them as pressed again.
- The **pattern workspace** opens from the panel's Edit pattern and Record on pads (Pattern mode). **Lanes** switches the grid to the pattern's lanes (octave, degree, velocity, gate, nudge), each looping at its own length: drag or turn a value, drag the `+` cell to add one, and Length −/+, Random −/+ and Clear lane work on the selected lane. **Recording** has Step time (one step per press, no clock, at least 8 steps, stops when full; the pads sound as pressed) and Free time (the pattern plays over the latched notes and each press lands on the nearest step; a pad held past the next step writes ties). Undo takes back a step in Step time; Clear starts again. **Save** keeps the pattern in the list and logs an `arp-pattern-saved: Name = pattern` line for the report. Save also keeps the owner's patterns (and custom scales) in the store as `files/arp/patterns.txt` under the `--data` folder (mpfree-store, see [storage-spec.md](storage-spec.md); on the test boot that is the stick's MPFREE-DATA partition), written whole or not at all by the store's own thread, and the next start reads them back. Without `--data` nothing is written and saved patterns last until the power goes off. `app --patterns FILE` reads a library at start in place of the saved one, and never writes it.
- **Saved with the project** (Jay, 13 Nov: the arp and Note Repeat are the song's): every track's arp, its rate (Note Repeat's too), options, scale and the pattern as it plays go in the project's `arp` part (`app/arp/part.rs`, format in [browser.md](browser.md#the-parts)). An open puts every track back (the armed track through the panel, the others straight into their engines) and lets go of Latch on every arp; New, or a project without the part, is every arp at its default. A change made with the panel, the options, the workspace or a Q-Link counts as unsaved work; arming another track doesn't.
- **Arp options** (MENU on the Arp panel) are rows in the existing Options menu, so the layout does not change: Tempo; Accent level (1-127, steps of 8; what accented steps play, FULL LEVEL still wins); Scale (Off, the 48 named scales but Chromatic, then the owner's from the patterns file; Off by default, since on drum pads a scale would move hits to other pads), Scale root (pad 1 counts as C, as on the Synth's pads) and Out-of-key notes (note below, note above, nearest note, not played); Chord on each pad; Scale run; Chords take turns (Off, every beat, 2 beats, bar, 2 bars); Strum (0-100 % of a step, steps of 10); New notes restart (retrigger); Swing from (Transport, the default: while the sequencer plays with swing on, the arp swings with it on the sequencer's grid; Arp: always the arp's own swing); Modifier (opens its section, below); and Prefs' Latch keeps pads and Sustain pedal latches. Each change logs an `arp ...:` line. The panel's own settings (rate, gate, swing, octaves) leave them alone, except that turning Swing (Q-Link 3 or the panel) switches Swing from to Arp, since the owner's decision is that swing follows the transport until it is turned.
- **Following the transport.** `App::set_clock(Option<Clock>)` gives the arp the transport's clock for the next period (the first span of it); `App::process` calls `Engine::sync` with it, so the arp's steps land on the sequencer's grid and follow its tempo, count-in included. Without a clock the arp free-runs at the app's tempo. The time engine calls it from round 7 (`TimeHost::arp_clock`, the transport thread's side).
- The button guide no longer opens at power-on for these buttons. It asks only for buttons nobody has pressed yet: REC, OVERDUB, UNDO and PLAY START, for the time engine. Prefs > Find the buttons again asks for all nine.
- On drum pads an octave is 12 pads: with Octaves at 2, pad 1's octave up plays pad 13, and anything past pad 16 is skipped. Octave steps in patterns (`1+`) work the same way.
- Q-Link turns arrive as steps of 1 or 2. Rate and octaves change every 3 and 4 units, so one detent does not skip values. Without Akai's session the Q-Links send positions (0-127), and those set the value directly.
- **The pads' arp** (sampler refactor step 15c, `main.rs` `PadsArp`, mirrored in `smoke/session.rs`): while the pads play an instrument on their own (`PadsPreview`: the shared synth, a host instrument, a whole kit, a bank's own), NOTE REPEAT, Latch and the arp play it, and never give the pads back (`App::arp_ends_pads_synth` and the pickers' refusals are gone). A hit made while the arp makes notes for them (`App::pads_own_mode` is not Direct: `App::generating`, but for a track on Phrase) goes into an `ArpHost` of the pads' own instead of playing at once: the armed track's arp as the app set it, taken each period (`PadsArp::follow`: settings, latch, pattern, tempo, swing, STOP's hush; the mode is the app's `pads_own_mode`, the track's but never Phrase, since a phrase stays with its track), on the transport's block after the clips' arps (`TimeHost::block`). One engine of its own keeps it apart from the armed track's clips through its arp. A hit goes in as what it plays: the kit pad of the page the pads are on, the synth's notes for the pad (its pad layout, chords too), or the host's pad; each note plays there (`play_pads_arp`: `Synth::note_on_note`, the pads' host player, `play_routed_pad_at` with the note's offset) and flashes its pad. When the pads move to another instrument or back to the track, `PadsArp::leave` ends what it has on where it played, now, and drops what is held or latched; a pad still held lets go quietly. Layers play each hit once, as pressed. Its notes record as heard (step 15c's second part): each period the audio loop gives it the track recording what the pads play on their own (`App::pads_own_rec`, the armed track REC set up for that sound; None while REC only writes steps) and it keeps, for each sounding note, the track its start went into (`fed`), so its end goes there though the pads move on. The `play` closure gets that track with each note; `rec_pads_arp` records a synth's note by its number (`TimeHost::routed_note`, into a clip of notes; a clip of pad hits has none for it, as the armed track's arp), a kit's or a host instrument's as its pad (`TimeHost::routed_played`). Tests: `app::tests::the_arp_never_gives_the_pads_back`, `smoke::song_workflows::tests::note_repeat_on_a_kit_the_pads_play_on_their_own_repeats_it_and_keeps_the_pads`, `smoke::song_workflows::tests::rec_records_every_sound_of_the_pad_map_on_its_own_track_the_arp_too`.
- **MIDI Learn** (18 Nov 2026): rate, gate, swing and octaves are parameters too, `Owner::Arp(track)` with the ids in `mpfree_param::arp`, so a knob on a controller can turn them. SHIFT + a touch on the panel's sliders, or on the Q-Link column's cards while they turn the arp, arms Learn on the armed track's (`LearnValue::Arp`). A learned control sets them through `App::set_arp_param`: the armed track's as its Q-Link does, another track's straight into its `TrackUi` and engine. On the Phrase tab the sliders are in the Plays panel and the first is the phrase's speed, which has no parameter yet and learns nothing; the other three learn as on any tab. See [midi-io.md](midi-io.md) (Setting the value).

**MIDI out of the arp (`mpfree_arp::midi`).** `MidiOut` turns the engine's events into note-on and note-off messages on one channel, through a `NoteMap`: `Same` (the notes are MIDI notes already), `Chromatic { base }` (pad slots: pad 1 sends `base`; `NoteMap::PADS` starts at C1, 36) or a 16-note table for a drum map. It counts the note-ons it sent per note, so each gets exactly one note-off even when a note plays again before it ends, and changing the channel or the map, or `all_off` (STOP), ends every sounding note first. It never allocates. `midi_file` writes the messages as a Standard MIDI File (format 0, 960 ticks per quarter note). No port is opened anywhere yet: which MIDI port the MPC sends on, and whether it may, goes through the Hello MPC thread and needs the owner's go.

## Controller tricks: the Modifier and the sustain pedal

**Asked for** (Jay, 23 Nov 2026, from the MPC comparison): on the MPC a controller can push the arp while you play, and the sustain pedal can latch it. MPFree had MIDI Learn setting rate, gate, swing and octaves, and pad pressure swelling Note Repeat, but nothing that pushes the arp and lets it back.

**What the MPC does** (MPC 3.10.1 user guide, pp. 83-84). The Arpeggiator window's Settings section has **Sustain Pedal Latches** and a **Latch Mode**, Reset or Add, for the pedal. Its **Arpeggiator modifiers** section checks any of Channel Pressure, Expression (CC 11), Aftertouch (pads or MIDI) and Mod Wheel (CC 1) as sources, and the modifier settings pick what they change and by how much at most: Time Division (1-4 steps faster: from 1/8 with 2, 1/8T then 1/16), Octaves (1-4 extra), Note Length (+1-100 %), Swing (+1-50 %), Variation (+1-4) and Probability (+1-100 %).

**MPFree's design.** Built on the branch for this design; the screens wait for Jay's approval.

- **One controller per track's arp** (`Settings::modifier`, `mpfree_arp::modifier`): Off (the default), Mod wheel (CC 1), Pressure, Expression pedal (CC 11), Breath (CC 2) or Learned knob (any control MIDI Learn gives `Owner::Arp(track)` parameter `mpfree_param::arp::MODIFIER`, 4). One rather than the MPC's several at once, so two controllers never fight over the push. **Pressure** is the highest pressure of the notes physically held on that arp now: the pads' aftertouch, and a keyboard's poly or channel aftertouch on the keys held (channel pressure arrives as each held note's, `mpfree_midi::mpe`). Latched notes, once let go, press nothing, so letting go lets the push back.
- **How far its top pushes each setting**, signed so a push can also slow, thin or tighten the arp: rate -4 to +4 steps along 1/4, 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16, 1/16T, 1/32, 1/32T (as the MPC's Time Division counts them: from 1/16, +2 is 1/32); octaves -3 to +3 (1 to 4 in all); gate -99 to +99 points (1 to 100 %); swing -25 to +25 points (50 to 75 %); **rolls** 0 to 7 extra hits in every step (added to a pattern step's ratchet, 8 hits at most); **accents** 0 to 100 % chance a step plays accented at the Accent level. The push grows in a straight line with the controller (`modifier::push`). The defaults are rate +2 and octaves +1, so picking a controller does something at once. MPFree adds points where the MPC's guide examples read as a share (its "50 % with 20 % gives 60 %"), so the screen can say "50 % → 74 %" plainly; Variation has no MPFree counterpart, and rolls and accents stand in for Probability.
- **The settings never change.** The engine plays `Engine::live()`, the settings as pushed, worked out again whenever the controller or a setting moves (`Engine::relive`). Stepped values (rate, octaves, rolls) hold for 0.15 of a step past each half-way point (`modifier::HOLD`), so pressure resting near an edge doesn't flick between two rates. A pushed rate starts with the next step; following the transport, it stays on the transport's grid. A pushed octave count starts the order's walk again, as turning Q-Link 4 does. In Pattern mode a step's own gate (its gate lane) keeps its shape and moves by the push. The accent chance draws a random number only while accents are set, so an arp with no push plays exactly as before: the engine's tests and the 37 render demos are unchanged (`mpfree-arp-render --demo` also has three Modifier demos, a wheel sweep, a drum roll that builds and a push that slows down).
- **Where the controllers come from** (`main.rs`, the keys loop): the armed track's keys' mod wheel, breath and expression pedal go to `App::arp_controller` and still to the sound (gear, instrument or synth) as before; their pressure goes to `App::key_pressure` (which also swells the arp's notes on those keys, as pad pressure always did) and still to the sound; the pads' pressure goes through `App::pressure` as before. A learned knob reaches `App::set_arp_param(track, MODIFIER, 0-100)` on any track. Keys that a route sends elsewhere (the pads playing a synth on their own, a route to another track) don't reach the armed track's arp.
- **Sustain pedal latches** (Prefs and the arp options, `Settings::pedal_latches`, off by default): with it on, the sustain pedal going down while the armed track's arp plays (Repeat, Arp or Pattern) turns Latch on, and coming up puts Latch back as it was, so the notes no longer held stop (`App::arp_pedal`). The pedal then doesn't reach the sound. A pedal comes up where it went down: a half pedal's later values follow its first, the up goes to the sound if the down did, arming another track lets the pedal's latch go on the old one, and turning the row off under a held pedal lets go too. Direct, the pedal sustains the sound as before. **Latch keeps pads** gains **Add** for every latch, not only the pedal's.
- **Screens** (rendered from the app: `arp-modifier`, `arp-panel` with no controller, `arp-modifier-options`, `arp-options`, `arp-quick-band-pushed`, `prefs`). The **Modifier box** (340 px, `arp_view::MODBOX`) sits at the right of the panel's mode tabs, in the panel the app lays across the Play page (`arp_view::with_modifier`; the 460 px column of the arp-ui previews has no room): "MODIFIER", the controller's name, and how far it is now as a percent and a meter in the second accent; with no controller, "Off · tap to pick a controller". A tap opens the arp options' **Modifier section** (Back, Controller, Pushes rate, Pushes octaves, Pushes gate, Pushes swing, Adds rolls, Adds accents), which the arp options' **Modifier** row opens too; SHIFT + a touch on the box arms MIDI Learn for the Modifier. While pushed, each Q-Link slider shows a ring where it plays and the stretch to it in the second accent, its value "1/16 → 1/32"; the quick band's rate is the one playing, in the second accent; the step strip shows the octaves played. The settings themselves (the sliders' knobs, the Q-Link cards, the arp chip) stay as set.
- **Saved**: per track in the project's arp part (`modifier = modwheel`, `modifier-push = rate 2 octaves 1 gate 30 swing 0 rolls 0 accents 0`); in Prefs (`latch-keeps-pads = add`, `sustain-pedal-latches = on`).
- **The XY pad's hand** (`hand.rs`, `Engine::hold`; [xy.md](xy.md), "The arp on the pad"): a layout of yours holds the rate, gate, swing, octaves or the Modifier's push where the pad puts them, laid over the settings as set before the Modifier pushes them, so the settings never change. A change to a held setting as set takes it from the pad until it lets go. The pad's push wins over the Modifier's controller, which waits (`Engine::controller`), and pushes even with no controller picked; a held swing is the arp's own while the transport swings. Stay on the push leaves it there until the controller next moves (`Engine::keep_push`).
- **Later**: a touch on the Modifier box pushing it, for an MPC with nothing plugged in; Phrase mode (the composing tools design) reads `live` and sends its hits through `pushed_hit`, so the Modifier pushes phrases too.
- **Cost** (mpfree-bench, callgrind): the case `Arps: 16 latched arps at 32nds, a mod wheel always moving` (each arp's Modifier on the wheel, which moves every 128-frame block, so every arp works its pushed settings out again each block, and plays the faster rate and rolls it pushes to) counts 14,954 instructions a block, against 9,434 for the same arps unpushed; with the Modifier off the arps cost what they did on main (9,582). In `instructions.txt` and `golden/cases.txt`.
- **Tests**: `modifier.rs` (the top gives the whole amount, as the guide's example; hold near an edge; negative amounts; clamping; `any_push_stays_in_range_and_comes_back_to_the_settings`, 2,000 random settings and amounts, each push in range and never the wrong way, and 0 gives the settings back), the engine's (`the_wheel_pushes_the_rate_from_the_next_step`, `an_unmoved_modifier_changes_nothing`, `pressure_pushes_from_the_notes_held_now`, `rolls_and_accents_follow_the_push`, `a_pushed_rate_stays_on_the_transport_grid`, `a_pattern_step_keeps_its_shape_under_the_push`, `add_latch_keeps_every_note_pressed_until_latch_goes`), arp-ui's `the_modifier_shows_where_it_pushes`, and the app's (`the_mod_wheel_pushes_the_armed_tracks_arp`, `a_learned_knob_pushes_the_modifier`, `the_modifier_box_opens_its_options`, `the_sustain_pedal_latches_the_arp_when_prefs_say_so`, `key_pressure_pushes_a_modifier_on_pressure`, `pad_pressure_rolls_note_repeat` (the manual's drum example), `latch_add_and_the_pedal_latch_are_kept`, `the_modifier_is_kept_with_the_project`).

## Making custom patterns

Three ways, all producing the same `Pattern`:

1. **Tap on the step grid** (above).
2. **Record on the pads** (`mpfree_arp::Recorder`, engine side done): pads 1-9 write the 1st to 9th held note, several of them pressed together a chord (`*`), 13 the next note (`x`), 14 random (`?`), 15 a tie, 16 a rest; pads 10-12 do nothing yet. A hit at or above the accent velocity (110 by default) writes an accent. Two modes, as in the record mockup:
   - **Step**: each press writes the next step, with no clock; Undo removes the last one; it stops when the pattern is full.
   - **Free time**: the pattern keeps playing and each press lands on the nearest step (`Engine::position()` gives where the pattern is), overwriting what was there; holding a pad past the middle of the next step writes ties.
3. **Text**, typed in Prefs or kept in a **pattern library file** (`mpfree_arp::Library`): one `Name = pattern` line each, `#` comments, written in any editor. Every bad line is reported with its line number, token and reason. [`rust/mpfree-arp/patterns/example.txt`](../../../rust/mpfree-arp/patterns/example.txt) has eight original patterns and a summary of the syntax. Hear a whole file on the workstation with `mpfree-arp-render --patterns FILE`.

Later: import a MIDI file the owner made (notes become note picks by their rank in the file's chord); export what the arp played as a MIDI file or to a sequencer track.

Patterns save to `/data/mpfree/patterns/` as text files once MPFree has persistent storage; on the RAM-only test build they live in memory.

## Hearing it on the workstation (`mpfree-arp-render`)

`mpfree-arp-render` is a workstation-only program in the same crate. It runs the real engine in 128-frame periods at 44.1 kHz, the same way the pad player will, and plays its notes through a small built-in synth (two detuned saws, a low-pass filter and an envelope) or, for drum demos, 16 simple drum sounds made in code (pad 1 kick, 2 snare, 3 closed hat and so on). It needs no samples and no MPC, and its output is the same on every run.

```sh
# Every order, every starter pattern, drums, lanes, scale, strum, mutate, nudge, ramp and random lanes: 32 WAVs
cargo run --manifest-path rust/Cargo.toml --release --locked -p mpfree-arp --bin mpfree-arp-render -- --demo

# Every pattern and phrase in a library file of your own (copy rust/mpfree-arp/patterns/example.txt to start)
cargo run --manifest-path rust/Cargo.toml --release --locked -p mpfree-arp --bin mpfree-arp-render -- --patterns rust/mpfree-arp/patterns/example.txt

# MPFree's 64 starter phrases, each from one held note: twice through, then from the fourth above, and back
cargo run --manifest-path rust/Cargo.toml --release --locked -p mpfree-arp --bin mpfree-arp-render -- --phrases

# One of your own: a chord per bar, looped
cargo run --manifest-path rust/Cargo.toml --release --locked -p mpfree-arp --bin mpfree-arp-render -- \
    --chords "57,60,64,67;53,57,60,64" --mode pattern --pattern "1 3 2 3 x:2 . 4 x | oct 0 0 1"
```

It writes to `~/mpfree-work/arp-render-<UTC>/` (or `--out DIR`): the WAVs, a `.mid` file of each (the same notes, at the job's tempo: drum pads on channel 10 from C1 up, other notes on channel 1 as played) plus a `report.txt` that lists each file with its length, note count, peak level, dropped events and two hashes: `events` (the notes played; the same on every computer, so it can be compared with Claude's run) and `audio` (the WAV bytes; repeats on one computer, but the synth's maths comes from the system library, so it can differ between computers). Run it through `tools/mpc-one/results.py run`, and the report goes into the results zip; the WAVs stay in the work folder for listening. Options: `--mode`, `--order`, `--pattern` or `--builtin NAME`, `--chords`, `--notes` or `--pads 1,3`, `--bpm`, `--rate 1/16T`, `--octaves`, `--gate`, `--swing`, `--strum`, `--scale d_dorian`, `--ramp 30:16`, `--retrigger`, `--bars`, `--seed`, `--name`.

## Decisions (owner, 2026-11-10)

The owner accepted all four proposals, and they apply to every live test from round 5 on:

1. **Latch defaults to Reset:** a press with nothing held starts a new chord; Toggle is an option.
2. **Latched pads keep repeating** after NOTE REPEAT is released, until Latch is turned off.
3. **The generator starts on the press**, not on the next beat, until a transport exists; then it becomes a setting (`Settings::start`: Step, Beat or Bar, default Step).
4. **Record-on-pads layout:** pads 1 to 9 are the 1st to 9th held note, several at once a chord, pad 13 the next note in the order, 14 random, 15 tie, 16 rest.

## Decisions (owner, 2026-11-11, from the design doc)

1. **No Arp tab** (see "Where the arp lives").
2. **One arp per track:** a latched arp keeps running when focus moves to another track. Built as one `ArpHost` per track slot in `mpfree-play` (`arps.rs`), in front of the track host.
3. **One per-track switch, "Clips through the arp" (`Track::through_arp` in `mpfree-seq`):** Off (drum tracks) records the notes you hear, so rolls become notes; On (melodic tracks) records the chord you played and the clip plays back through the track's arp. CAPTURE follows the same rule. Turning it while a clip's notes sound leaves none hanging: each note-off goes where its note-on went (refactor A2, [playback.md](playback.md#endings-always-get-through-refactor-a2)).
   Since the instrument redesign's step 5d an arp works in notes when its track's synth plays notes, and a clip of pad hits through it goes in as its pads' notes ([instruments.md](instruments.md#5d-as-built-one-path-through-the-arp)).
4. **Holding NOTE REPEAT shows the quick band** (done in round 6, as the slim strip).
5. **STOP silences a latched arp and keeps the latch;** STOP again while stopped clears it. PLAY or a pad press plays the kept latch again (`Engine::hush`, `Engine::resume`).
6. **Q-Link 3 is Swing,** following the transport's swing until it is turned.
