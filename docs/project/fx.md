# FX: the effects, the insert chain and the mixer

**Status:** 2026-11-11, host-only and tested. The code is [`rust/mpfree-fx`](../../rust/mpfree-fx), built on the shared [`mpfree-node`](../../rust/mpfree-node) interface ([synths.md](synths.md) describes it). Nothing here is wired to the pad player or the GUI yet, and nothing has run on the MPC. The mixer's MPC parity review (what the MPC 3.6 mixer and routing can do, and how MPFree covers each part) is in the doc "MPC mixer and routing, and the MPFree mixer". The full design (what makes FX sound great or cheap, what users ask for, other devices, libraries, the architecture, the roadmap and the decisions for Jay) is in the design doc "MPFree FX and filters design"; the skill tree and the mockups are in mpfree-claude (`skill-tree/fx-skill-tree.html`, `fx/mockups/`).

## Clipping

Deliberate clipping is a feature; clipping nobody asked for is not. No effect clamps a signal unless that is its job (Drive, Crush, the Delay's feedback saturation, Master safety), and nothing fails, stops or mutes because a level is high. Every output is meant to end in **Master safety**, which keeps the converter from clipping unless the user picks its Raw mode, and counts what it caught.

## The effects

Every effect is a `Node`, so it takes sample-timed parameter events (automation, p-locks, macros), describes its parameters for a generic GUI page, and keeps the node rules: no allocation, lock or panic in `process`, and the same output, sample for sample, for any block size.

- **Multi filter:** zero-delay-feedback state-variable filter after Andrew Simper (Cytomic): low-pass, high-pass, band-pass and notch at 12 or 24 dB/octave, a bell and two shelves, resonance up to a long ring, and optional drive into a soft saturator before it. Cutoff sweeps glide in the log domain and never zip or blow up.
- **DJ filter:** one knob, low-pass to the left (20 kHz down to 80 Hz), high-pass to the right (20 Hz up to 8 kHz), and bit-for-bit off in the middle.
- **Drive:** Soft (tanh), Hard, Tube (biased), Foldback, Fuzz and Rectify, run inside 2x (Eco) or 4x (HQ) oversampling with linear-phase half-band filters, then a DC blocker, a tone low-pass, mix and output. Auto-gain keeps the level where it was, so more drive means more grit, not just louder. 55 frames of latency in both qualities, so switching never moves the sound. The tanh curves use a rational fit (within 2e-7 of tanh) instead of the C library's tanh, which is slow on the MPC's ARM.
- **Crush:** bit depth (1 to 16) and sample-rate reduction (200 Hz to 48 kHz) with optional TPDF dither. Aliasing on purpose.
- **Delay:** free (1 ms to 4 s) or synced to the tempo (1/32 to 1 bar, dotted and triplet), ping-pong, and a feedback path through a high-pass, a tone low-pass and a soft saturator, so repeats darken and roughen like tape. Feedback goes to 110 %: the saturator keeps self-oscillation bounded. Time changes glide (tape-style pitch bend, no clicks). Freeze holds the buffer as a loop.
- **EQ:** four parametric bands, each a bell, a low shelf or a high shelf (frequency, ±18 dB, Q), low and high cuts at 12 or 24 dB per octave (Butterworth), and an output gain, on the same state-variable filters as the Multi filter. At its defaults it is the input, bit for bit, and a band at 0 dB or a cut that is off rests and costs nothing. A shelf's Q stops at 2 (a 5 dB bump past the corner at full gain; at Q 18 it would be 24 dB). A slope change crossfades over about 20 ms; a band type change eases the band's gain to 0 dB, where every type passes the input exactly, swaps and eases back, so neither clicks. `Eq::response_db` gives the curve the settings are heading to, exact to the filters (a sine measures within 0.05 dB of it), for drawing on an EQ page.
- **Chorus, Flanger, Phaser:** share an LFO that runs free (in Hz) or synced to the tempo (1/16 to 8 bars a cycle) and restarts in time with the bar when the transport starts; Width moves the right side's LFO a quarter cycle on at 100 %, and 0 % keeps a mono input mono. The LFO is a polynomial sine and the delay reads are cubic, so a swept delay stays bright. **Chorus:** up to four voices at fixed spread phases (adding a voice never moves the others, and voice gains crossfade), 2 to 40 ms, and a Tone low-pass on the wet signal. **Flanger:** 0.1 to 10 ms swept up to two octaves each way (so the comb moves evenly), feedback from -95 % (hollow) to +95 % (metallic). **Phaser:** 2, 4, 6, 8 or 12 trapezoidal first-order all-pass stages swept up to two and a half octaves around a centre, with feedback; a stage-count change crossfades between taps, and unused stages rest.
- **Lo-fi:** worn tape and dusty vinyl under one **Amount** knob, so it can be a whisper of age or a broken machine. Tape: wow and flutter (a delay swept by two pairs of sines at unrelated rates, up to 0.4 % and 0.15 % of pitch) and saturation (soft, slightly asymmetric, up to 18 dB of drive). Vinyl: crackle (random clicks and the odd duller pop) and hiss, which ride the music: they rise with the input and fade over about a second after it stops, so a stopped track is silent and a bypassed slot rests. Tone: low cut, high cut, width. Amount 0 takes everything to nothing and opens the cuts to 20 Hz and 20 kHz. The tape delay costs a fixed 2 ms of latency, reported for compensation, and the dry path is delayed to match, so Mix never combs. Presets: Worn tape, Dusty vinyl, Cassette, Old radio.
- **Compressor:** feed-forward, soft knee, gain computed and smoothed in decibels (Giannoulis, Massberg and Reiss, JAES 2012), stereo-linked, makeup, parallel mix, a sidechain input for ducking, and a gain-reduction meter.
- **Cloud reverb:** a Rust port of the MIT [Cloud Seed](https://github.com/GhostNoteAudio/CloudSeedCore) reverb core by Ghost Note Audio (the notice is in [`LICENSE-cloudseed.txt`](../../rust/mpfree-fx/LICENSE-cloudseed.txt); MPFree is not made or endorsed by Ghost Note Audio). Cloud Seed's path: input cross-mix, low and high cut, pre-delay, a multi-tap early pattern, a chain of modulated all-pass diffusers, then up to 12 modulated feedback lines, each with its own diffuser, shelves and damping in the loop, every length and rate from a seeded random series that differs per side, so the tails are decorrelated. Its 45 parameters become 15: a **Character** (Plate, Room, Hall, Cloud, Echoes) picks the hidden structure, and the controls are Size, Decay (50 ms to 60 s), Pre-delay, Diffusion, Modulation, Mod rate, Damping, Low cut, High cut, Width, Early, Mix, Freeze and **Quality**. Quality Light runs half the lines (at least 4), half the stages in each line's diffuser, half the early diffuser and half the early taps, with the same seeds, lengths, EQ and decay: the same size, colour, level (within 1.5 dB) and decay time (within 6 %), a little less dense, for about half the cost (a third for the Cloud character). Full is the default; Light is there for the MPC if Full costs too much on it. Five factory presets (Dark plate, Small room, Big hall, Endless cloud, Echo chamber). What changed in the port: feedback per frame (so the output does not depend on the block size; Cloud Seed feeds back one buffer late), deterministic modulator phases, cubic reads and whole-frame base delays (so Modulation 0 is lossless and Freeze holds without dulling), the diffusers counted in the loop length (Decay is the time to fall 60 dB: within 6 % at the default diffusion at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz; at Diffusion 100 the tail runs up to about 30 % long), an O(1) reset, and every control glides. A Character or Quality change dips the wet signal for 30 ms while the structure swaps, and the lines and stages that start running again start from silence. Freeze mutes the input and holds the tail; the tail estimate is endless while frozen, so a bypassed frozen reverb keeps ringing.
- **Master safety:** trim, a 5 Hz DC blocker, then Safe limiter (default), Soft clip or Raw. The Safe limiter looks 1.5 ms ahead; its gain is the smallest any frame in the window needs, released and averaged over the window, so it is always down before the peak arrives and the output never goes over the ceiling (-0.3 dBFS by default). Every mode counts overs and frames that leave above 0 dBFS. The latency is the same in every mode.

## The insert chain

`Chain` runs effects in series, the way a track, bus or master will:

- **Bypass is click-free and keeps tails:** switching a slot off fades its input out over 20 ms and its dry signal in, so a delay keeps ringing (spillover) and a drive crossfades to dry. A bypassed slot keeps running on silence for exactly its tail, then rests and is reset.
- **Latency never moves:** each slot's dry path is delayed by the slot's latency, on or off.
- **Events:** each slot has a queue of sample-timed events (256 per block); a full queue drops and counts.
- **A NaN guard:** a node that puts out a non-finite sample is reset, its slot plays dry for that block, and the chain counts it.
- **Block-size independent,** including where a tail starts and ends. The one exception: a node with an endless tail (a frozen delay) that is bypassed and then unfrozen picks up its new tail at the end of that block.

## The mixer

`Mixer` (in `src/mixer.rs`; the module docs have the details) is the one place audio is summed. It covers what the MPC 3.6 mixer can route, in fixed stages so nothing can feed back:

1. **Inputs** (2 by default, up to 8): the line input as a stereo pair or either side as mono (Source), heard through the strip's inserts when Monitor says so (Off, On, or Auto: heard while the track is armed).
2. **Pad channels** (a pool of 32 by default, up to 128): pads that need their own inserts, sends or output. The sampler renders those apart; every other pad stays in its kit's track.
3. **Tracks** (16 by default, up to 64): each instrument adds into its track; inputs and pad channels can be routed in.
4. **Submixes** (8): sum what is routed to them; a submix can go on to a higher-numbered one or the master.
5. **Returns** (A to D): every strip above has four sends, each before or after its fader.
6. **Master**: inserts and fader, then Master safety, always last.

Every strip has an insert chain (8 slots by default) and a fader (volume -80 to +6 dB, balance pan, mute, solo); every strip but a return and the master has an output: the master, Sub 1 to 8, a track (inputs and pads), or none. Its parameters are a `ParamInfo` list like any effect's, so the GUI and the automation see them the same way.

- **Solo in place:** soloing a strip keeps it, every strip it feeds and every strip that feeds it; returns and the master are never cut, and mute wins.
- **Sidechain keys per insert slot:** a compressor on the bass can listen to the kick, a gate on the pads to the hats. The key is the other strip after its inserts and before its fader and mute, so a muted ghost kick still pumps the mix. A key may come from an earlier stage or the same stage (the mixer orders each stage so the key is ready, and refuses a loop); it costs no delay.
- **Latency compensation:** every path into a track, submix, return and the master is delayed to line up with the slowest path that could reach it, so routing and fader moves never shift anything in time. Live inputs are the exception: they are never held back, so playing through the mixer stays as immediate as it can be. Compensation stops at 50 ms (a setting); delays crossfade over 20 ms when an effect with latency comes or goes.
- **Click-free:** controls glide over 4 ms; an output change crossfades from the old destination to the new one; adding, removing, swapping or moving an insert while playing (`Edit`, built on the control thread) ducks that strip for 10 ms around the change while the rest plays on, and what it takes out comes back to be dropped off the audio thread.
- **Capture points** for the recorder: any strip before its inserts, before its fader or after it, or the master after Master safety: resampling, the looper, recording an input dry or through its effects, stems. `capture_latency` says how far to slide a take.
- **Sample-timed and block-size independent,** including mute, solo and output changes that affect other strips: the same output, sample for sample, at any block size.
- **Guards:** a non-finite sample in any input silences that strip for the block and is counted; Master safety catches a hot sum.
- **Cheap when quiet:** a strip silent for longer than its delays, with nothing moving, skips its work; one nothing wrote to this block does not even scan its input.
- **Profiling:** `set_profiling(true)` times the whole mixer, each strip and each insert slot into `mpfree-prof` probes (off by default; two clock reads each).

**The mixer screen** is [`rust/mpfree-mixer-ui`](../../rust/mpfree-mixer-ui): the panel TRACK MIX (or the mixer icon at the top bar's right edge) opens over the current page, as the layout spec places it. Views for Tracks, Pads (SHIFT + TRACK MIX), Inputs, and Submixes with returns; pages of ten strips with the master always on the right. Each strip has its name and colour, four insert rows (a tap opens the slot, "+ Insert" adds one, "SC" marks a keyed slot), sends A to D (drag; a tap on the letter takes it before the fader), pan (drag; a tap centres it), a fader beside a stereo meter (drag, relative, with a 0 dB detent; a tap on the level sets 0 dB), Mute and Solo (and Arm on an input, whose meter shows what comes in), and its output, which opens a picker of only the allowed destinations. The master shows Master safety's mode, ceiling, what it caught and its limiting. While profiling, a chip shows the mixer's share of a period and each insert row a cost bar. A meter-only update redraws only the meters. While the mixer is open the four Q-Links work on the selected strip: Volume, Pan, Send A and Send B (`mpfree_mixer_ui::qlinks` gives each card's label and text, and turns a knob into a parameter change; SHIFT gives fine steps). `cargo run --release -p mpfree-mixer-ui --bin mpfree-mixer-preview` draws the sample screens with a real mixer playing behind them. Wiring it into mpfree-hello is the Hello thread's, which owns the layout.

What it does not do yet: the wiring into mpfree-hello (the sampler's `render_routed` for pad channels, the tracks, the mixer screen), the recorder that uses the capture points, saving the mixer with a project, and the "print FX" option for recording through inserts.

## Tests

`cargo test -p mpfree-fx` (84 tests):

- every parameter description valid, ids equal to indices, defaults reported
- the fast tanh (Drive's curves) is within 1e-6 of tanh from -12 to 12, never past ±1, and rises steadily to within its accuracy
- every effect gives bit-identical output at block sizes 1, 7, 128, 1000, 4096, 8192 and random sizes, with automation on every parameter; so does a seven-slot chain with bypass switching (1, 64, 333, 4096, 8192 and random)
- every parameter alone at its minimum and maximum, and all of them together, on hot noise, DC and impulses: finite and bounded
- the Delay at 110 % feedback self-oscillates but stays bounded; synced time lands exactly on the grid and follows a tempo change; ping-pong alternates
- filter slopes (24 dB/oct: -66 to -78 dB three octaves up; 12 dB/oct: -33 to -39 dB), the bell's gain, the shelves; the DJ filter is bit-for-bit off in the middle
- Drive latency is 55 frames in both qualities and they line up to well under a frame; Mix 0 is the input, exactly, 55 frames late
- **aliasing** of a 5 kHz sine through tanh at +18 dB, worst alias in the audible band against the fundamental: **-26 dB** plain (no oversampling), **-59 dB** Eco 2x, **-97 dB** HQ 4x
- the Compressor reaches its static curve (to 0.05 dB) and ducks from a sidechain
- Master safety never passes the ceiling (44.1, 48 and 96 kHz; ceilings 0, -0.3 and -6 dB; trim up to +12 dB; a step to +15.6 dBFS), counts overs, clips nothing; it is transparent below the ceiling; Soft clip stays under; Raw passes overs and counts them
- the Cloud reverb: Decay is the time to fall 60 dB (Schroeder integration) for each character at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz and at large sizes, within 20 %; the tail estimate covers the ring-out to -80 dB; Freeze holds the tail within 3 dB, ignores loud new input and decays again after release; full Width is decorrelated and Width 0 is mono; Mix 0 is the input exactly; a reset forgets everything; a Character change dips and recovers; Light keeps every character's decay within 20 % and level within 3 dB of Full, and lines it wakes start silent; every preset is in range and plays; the bulk buffer reads match one-at-a-time reads across the wrap
- the EQ: bit-for-bit flat at its defaults and with any band at 0 dB; the curve `response_db` reports matches a measured sine within 0.05 dB for four settings at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz; the cuts are Butterworth (-3 dB at the corner, 36 and 72 dB three octaves out); type and slope changes under a boosted low sine leave no spike in its second difference
- chorus, flanger and phaser: Mix 0 is the input exactly; the chorus delays by its Delay and Width 0 keeps a mono input mono, full width decorrelates; the flanger's feedback makes a comb whose echoes fall by the feedback each pass, inverted when negative; four phaser stages notch at 0.414 and 2.414 times the centre (below -40 dB) and pass the centre; voice and stage changes stay far below a hard switch's spike; the tail estimate covers the ring-out at 95 % feedback; a synced LFO lands on the bar when the transport starts
- lo-fi: Mix 0 is the input exactly 2 ms late, matching the reported latency; Amount 0 is clean and on time; full wow bends a 1 kHz sine by 0.2 to 0.6 %, none by under 0.02 %; crackle and hiss are silent before the music, play with it and fade below -80 dB within the tail after it; the presets play at a sensible level
- chain bypass makes no click, keeps the latency, and gives the input exactly once the tail has rested; a delay keeps echoing after bypass and then dies away; the NaN guard; the full queue
- the mixer (`tests/mixer.rs`): tracks sum at unity with the balance law, against a standalone Master safety; submixes nest and returns take sends before and after the fader; an output change crossfades with no dip or click; mute and solo land on their frame and glide; latency is compensated to the frame (and live inputs are not held back); a muted kick keys a compressor on another track; each capture point takes what it says; inputs follow their source and monitoring; the same output at block sizes 1, 7, 64, 128, 333 and random with everything moving; edits while playing make no click and hand back what they take; bad input is silenced and counted; quiet strips wake on their first frame; every strip's parameters are described; profiling records only when on
- `tests/no_alloc.rs`: a counting allocator watches 4000 blocks through a chain of every effect plus each effect on its own, with random parameter moves, bypass switching, freeze, sidechain, resets and NaN, and sees no allocation or free; and 3000 mixer blocks with random strip events, edits while playing, key and capture changes, profiling switched on and off, resets and NaN pads, likewise.

## Listening and timing

- `cargo run --release -p mpfree-fx --example render [out.wav]` writes `~/mpfree-work/fx-demo.wav`: 12 bars at 90 BPM where each effect gets two bars through chains in 128-frame blocks (dry; Fuzz drive into a resonant filter sweep on the bass; crushed drums and kick-ducked chords; ping-pong delay; the delay bypassed and ringing on under DJ filter sweeps; a last hit echoing out). The mix runs hot on purpose into Master safety; it prints per-bar levels and limiting, the overs caught, and how many frames a Raw master would have clipped, and checks that nothing clips and the end is quiet.
- `cargo run --release -p mpfree-fx --example cloud_demo [out.wav]` writes `~/mpfree-work/cloud-demo.wav`: 12 bars at 90 BPM with five Cloud reverbs on sends, one per preset (two dry bars, then the small room on the drums, the dark plate on the snare and chords, the big hall, the endless cloud frozen for a bar and released, the echo chamber on the last hit, and a bar ringing out). It prints the same levels and limiting table, each reverb's return level in the last 100 ms, and checks that nothing clips and the end is quiet. `cloud_demo out.wav light` renders every reverb at Light quality (both are in `/mnt/project-files/fx/demo/`: `cloud-demo-full.wav`, `cloud-demo-light.wav`).
- `cargo run --release -p mpfree-fx --example mod_demo [out.wav]` writes `~/mpfree-work/mod-demo.wav`: 15 bars at 90 BPM with the effects as inserts whose Mix is automated by sample-timed events (dry; chorus on held chords; a synced flanger on the drums and chords; a synced 8-stage phaser on the fuzz bass and chords; Lo-fi's "Dusty vinyl" creeping in on the master; a build where the master EQ's 24 dB low cut sweeps to 700 Hz under a rising resonant bell; the drop with everything on; a last hit into a Cloud reverb send). Same table and self-check as the other demos.
- `cargo run --release -p mpfree-fx --example mix_demo [out.wav]` writes `~/mpfree-work/mix-demo.wav` and the drum submix as a capture point took it (`mix-demo-drum-bus.wav`): 14 bars at 96 BPM routed the way a user would on the MPC's mixer (kick and a kit track fed by two pad channels into a drum bus with glue compression; a bass whose compressor is keyed by the kick; chords sending to a Cloud reverb and a ping-pong delay). The bass comes in, then pumps under the kick; the chords come in; the drum bus is soloed in place (the reverb stays); the chords' output moves to a submix with a drive, crossfaded; the kick is muted and the bass still pumps; a last hit rings out. It prints per-bar levels, the pumping depth (about -15 dB with the kick heard or muted), what Master safety caught and the mixer's cost per block, and checks that nothing clips, the bass pumps with the kick muted and the solo silences it.
- `cargo run --release -p mpfree-fx --example mixer_cost [tracks] [pads]` prints the mixer's time per block and its slowest strips.
- `cargo run --release -p mpfree-fx --example insert_cost [light]` runs the bench's song (12 tracks, 8 pads, a song's inserts) with profiling on and prints every insert's mean and p99, dearest first. On the build host: the mixer 252 µs a block (8.7 % of a period), 216 µs of it inserts: the Hall reverb 106 µs, the Drive 18, the Delay 14, each compressor 7 and each EQ 4.5. With the reverb at Light the mixer takes 187 µs (6.4 %).
- `cargo run --release -p mpfree-fx --example bench` times each effect on 128-frame blocks. On the build host (an x86 desktop core) Drive costs 0.7 % of real time in Eco and 0.9 % in HQ, the Delay 0.4 %, the Compressor 0.2 %, the EQ 0.6 % with every band and both 24 dB cuts working, the Chorus 0.55 % (two voices) to 1.0 % (four), the Flanger 0.35 %, the Phaser 0.45 % (it works out its coefficients every 16 frames and glides between them, instead of an exp2 and a tan every frame), Lo-fi 0.5 %, Master safety 0.17 %, the filters 0.05 to 0.15 %, and the Cloud reverb 4.3 % (Plate), 2.8 % (Room), 3.7 % (Hall), 5.3 % (Cloud) and 1.3 % (Echoes) at Full, 2.0, 1.5, 1.9, 1.9 and 1.1 % at Light. The MPC One's Cortex-A17 is slower; it needs its own run before slot limits are set. The Cloud reverb is the first effect where that matters: if one instance at Full costs too much there, Light is the fix. Turning NEON on for the device build does not vectorise float code by itself (see [profiling.md](profiling.md)), so the device run is what counts.
- **The mixer's cost**, from `mpfree-bench --filter Mixer` (128 frames at 44.1 kHz, the mixer alone, on the build host; the VM is noisy, so means): idle with 63 strips 4 µs a block (0.15 % of a period), 12 tracks and 8 pad channels with sends and submixes 23 µs (0.8 %), 16 tracks and 32 pad channels 55 µs (1.9 %), and the 12 and 8 with a song's inserts (EQ and compressor on 6 tracks, drive and glue on submixes, a Cloud reverb, a delay, a master EQ) 230 to 280 µs, almost all of it the inserts (the Hall reverb alone is about half). The mix budget is 1 % of a period on the MPC (29 µs), set before the mixer had sends, submixes and compensation; without NEON the A17 runs this kind of summing many times slower than a desktop core, so the 12 and 8 case may well cost 3 to 6 % on the device. The device run decides; if it is over, the summing loops (a multiply, a multiply-add, a max and a copy) are the place for hand-written NEON.
